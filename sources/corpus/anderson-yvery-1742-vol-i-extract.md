# Yvery / Harpetre-Gournay Extract - *A Genealogical History of the House of Yvery*, Vol. I

**Citation:** Anderson, James. *A Genealogical History of the House of Yvery: In its Different Branches of Yvery, Luvel, Perceval, and Gournay*. Vol. I. London: H. Woodfall, Jun., 1742. Internet Archive item: `genealogicalhist01ande` (https://archive.org/details/genealogicalhist01ande).

**Pair:** This file covers Vol. I material relating to the Yvery/Perceval house and the cadet line that adopted the surname Gournay. The continuous Book VII Gournay descent (Vol. II, printed pp. 474-533) is in `sources/corpus/agenealogical-history-gournay-extract.md`.

**Scope of this extract:** discrete passages bearing on (a) Ascelin Gouel de Perceval's children and surnames, (b) the Harpetre->Gournay surname transition, (c) the Somerset land cluster of Robert de Yvery and Ascelin, (d) heraldic assignments, (e) the Stephen-period siege of Kary and Harpetre. Not a continuous chapter extract; a curated set of named-page passages.

**Method note.** Cleaned transcripts derived initially from a deep-research preliminary read of the PDF (`sources/media/anderson-yvery-1742-vol-i/genealogicalhist01ande.pdf`); page references are to the printed page (book page) with the PDF scan page in parentheses. ## p. 4 (PDF p. 28) - Ascelin's seven sons; John de Harpetre

> "He left seven sons, and a daughter, married to Radulfus Rufus, a Norman nobleman. Of the sons we have recovered only the names of Robert, William and John. Of which the youngest, John, was portion'd by his father in the manor of Harpetree, of which he assumed the name, which he afterwards changed to Gournay; and from him descended the Barons of Harpetree-Gournay before-mentioned."

## p. 21 (PDF p. 107) - Surname progression Yvery / Gouel / Percheval / Lupus -> Harpetre -> Gournay

> "Ascelin, son of this Robert, is known under several titles in ancient history: by that of Yvery, that of Gouel, and that of Percheval; and himself and his son by that also of Lupus, or Lupellus; and a younger son by that of Harpetre, from a manor of that name, in the county of Somerset, which he had for his portion, and which his descendants changed to Gournay."

## p. 39 (PDF p. 125) - Heraldry attributed to John de Harpetre

> "John, surnamed de Harpetre, younger son of Ascelin Gouel de Perceval, and ancestor to the House of Harpetre Gournay, is said to have borne two sceptres cross'd, per saltier ... but from that time it is certain, that they bore, Paley of six, Or and Azure."

## p. 78 (PDF p. 164) - Somerset land cluster of Robert de Yvery and Ascelin

> "Among others, the following lands appear to have been given to Robert de Yvery and Ascelin his son, in the county of Somerset ... Kary, Ferentone, Brismartin, Easton, Weston, [Laneverei?], the two Harpetres, Hetune, Tillebere, Biscopewide, Millescot, Babington, Stacewell, and Estide, besides a portion of land of Yvil, now known by the name of Ilchester."

## pp. 191-193 (PDF pp. 277-279) - Children of Ascelin Gouel de Perceval and Isabella de Breteuil

> "Children of Ascelin Gouel de Perceval, Earl of Yvery, and of Isabella de Breteuil, his wife.  
> I. Robert, eldest son, Lord of Yvery.  
> II. William, called also Gouel de Perceval, surnamed Lupellus.  
> III. Roger, the third son, surnamed Balbus.  
> ... John, surnamed de Harpetre, from whom the Barons of Harpetre descended, who at length assumed the name of Gournay.  
> Three sons more issued from this marriage of Ascelin with Isabella de Breteuil ... but of these we do not certainly know the names. It is only presumed, that John de Yvery ... was one of them. Hugh de Yvery ... is likewise supposed to be another of them."

## pp. 199-200 (PDF pp. 287-288) - Stephen-period siege of Kary and Harpetre

> "This William Lupellus, and William, son of John de Harpetreu ... each manned his castle, the one of Kary, the other of Harpetre ... from whence, for a time, they ravaged the neighbouring country ... [King Stephen] advanced against the castle of Kary ... After which he became master of the castle of Harpetre, partly by surprise, and partly by assault."

## Refined independent reads of three completed artifacts

### R1 — `page_00725_marye_relationship_token_magnification_sweep.png` (Marye relationship token)

The prior AI's note records "does not cleanly resolve to daughter; treat as unresolved." My independent read at the magnified resolution is firmer:

- **The opening letter of the relationship token is not a "d".** A daughter-style opening would show a closed lower loop with an ascender at the top right; the magnified panel shows neither. The opening reads as an "n"-class form: two short stems with a connector, no closed-bowl loop.
- **The token body is short: 4–5 character widths.** The word `daughter` requires 8 character widths in this hand. The visible body does not fill that span.
- **The token has secretary-hand strokes consistent with `niece`/`neece`/`nephew`.** In 17th-century English parish hands, `nephew` was sometimes used loosely without strict gender — `niece` for a female `Marye` is the most semantically plausible reading.

I would record the relationship token as: **refutes `daughter`; class-level reading is niece/nephew family; specific spelling not adjudicated.** This is one step firmer than the prior AI's "unresolved" wording and matters for the case-file family chart: the 00725 Marye is most likely Francis Gurney's **niece**, not his daughter.

### R2 — `page_00725_marye_month_token_magnification_sweep.png` (Marye month token)

The prior AI's note records "compatible with `January` but interpretive". My independent read at the magnified resolution disagrees in one direction:

- **The token is short: 4–5 character widths.** `January` is 7 characters; in this hand `January` would be longer than what is shown.
- **The token is at least as compatible with `Iuny`/`Iuly` (June/July) as with an abbreviated `Iany`/`Jany` (January).** Both options fit 4 character widths. The terminal stroke I can resolve is closer to a y-descender than to a `-ry` ending.
- **No long medial `-an-` cluster is visible.** A spelled-out `January` would show this cluster cleanly between contrast states; it does not appear here.

I would record the month token as: **short 4-char token; compatible with `Iuny` / `Iuly` (mid-summer) or with an abbreviated `Iany` / `Jany` (January); not compatible with a full-spelled `January`.** This re-opens a question the prior interpretation closed:

- If the burial month is `Iuny` or `Iuly`, page 00725 is **not** year-crossing. It is a mid-year burial entry, and the 00725 page covers a single calendar year of burials with this entry near the middle, not the tail.
- If the burial month is an abbreviated `Iany` / `Jany`, the year-crossing reading the prior AI used is still defensible.
- The page-year inference for 00725 changes accordingly: mid-year burial → page-year is the burial year itself; January burial → page-year is the year that ended in late January (one year less than the next section).

### R3 — `page_00736_marye_1618_source_mismatch_context.png` (00736 heading)

The prior AI confirmed 00736 is a 1620 page. My independent read of the supplied image goes one step further and surfaces a register-structure finding that re-frames everything downstream.

**The full decorative title reads:**

> "Billes Indented of all the Christnings, Marriages and Burialls in East Dereham 1620"

followed by named churchwardens, then a section heading:

> "Christnings Anno Dom 1620"

**Structural implication.** This register is organized as **combined annual returns** — each "Billes Indented" page bundles christenings, marriages, **and** burials for a single year. Pages with this layout (00726/00727, 00735/00736, and similar layouts elsewhere) are not separate-section starts; they are **year-block starts**, each covering one full year of all three event types.

This re-frames several earlier findings:

- The "Mariages" header I spotted on page 00729 is **not** a standalone marriages section — it is the marriages portion of an annual return whose christenings sit above it and whose burials sit below it. The full annual return for that year is therefore visible across the page, not split across separate sections of the register.
- The 00726/00727 pair likely opens a single-year annual return for the year immediately after 00725's burial year. The heading sweep requested in Ask 2 should report not only the year but also confirm the "Billes Indented of all the Christnings, Marriages and Burialls" wording, which would lock down both the year **and** the format.
- The page 00725 burial cluster is the **burial subsection** of a prior annual return, not a standalone burial page. Whatever year the 00726/00727 annual return opens (let us call it Y), the 00725 burials are the year Y-1 burial entries (if late-January year-crossing) or the year Y-1 mid-year burials (if the month token resolves to `Iuny`/`Iuly` per R2). Either way the year Y-1 anchor is set by the 00726/00727 heading.

## 1. The numbering dispute (Eudes / Renaud / Hugues I/II/III/IV/V) — fully diagrammed

Potin 1842 (pp. 69–73) is the locus classicus where the disagreement among 18th–19th-century French historians on Hugues numbering is laid out and adjudicated. Reproducing verbatim with translation, because the dispute directly bears on the repo's Pedigree Question #2 (Senex / Le Vieux / Le Vieil Huon attribution) and on the structural Pattou-vs-FMG-vs-repo disagreement.

### 1.1 The four positions

(Potin 1842, pp. 70–71, French verbatim)

> "M. Guilmeth met deux Hugues avant Girard de Gournay. Hugues I qui, d'après son opinion, allait soutenir, en Angleterre, les droits du roi Edouard; qui, en 1055, assista à la bataille de Mortemer; qui, en 1066, combattit à Hastings, et, en 1074, fut blessé à la journée de Kardiff, où il reçut des blessures dont il mourut à son retour en Normandie, et Hugues, mari de Basilée et moine à l'abbaye du [Bec].
>
> Jean Pillet n'en admet qu'un; cela est impossible. Je crois même qu'il y a eu trois Hugues avant Girard, et, du Hugues I de M. Guilmeth, je pense qu'il faut faire deux Hugues, car sans cela, Hugues Ier, fils de Renaud, aurait eu une vie fabuleusement longue."

Translation:
> "M. Guilmeth places two Hugueses before Girard de Gournay. Hugues I, who, in his opinion, went to support in England the rights of King Edward; who in 1055 took part in the battle of Mortemer; who in 1066 fought at Hastings; and in 1074 was wounded on the day of Cardiff, where he received wounds of which he died on his return to Normandy. And Hugues, husband of Basilée and monk at the abbey of [Bec].
>
> Jean Pillet admits only one; this is impossible. I [Potin] even believe that there were three Hugueses before Girard, and from Guilmeth's Hugues I, I think two Hugueses must be made, because otherwise Hugues I, son of Renaud, would have had a fabulously long life."

(Potin 1842, p. 70, French verbatim, the chronological argument)

> "Renaud vivait du temps de Rollon, mort en 917 ou en 932, et il est impossible que Hugues, son fils, ait vécu jusqu'en 1074, ce qui me confirme dans cette opinion, c'est que, le catalogue des grands seigneurs Normands qui passèrent la mer et combattirent pour le duc Guillaume, quand il conquesta l'Angleterre, contient non-seulement Hue de Gournay; mais après, le sieur de Gournay. C'est le sieur de Gournay qui me semble devoir être Hugues III et le père de Girard, lequel ne mourut pas en 1074, puisqu'il se fit moine à l'abbaye du Bec en 1080."

Translation:
> "Renaud was living in Rollo's time (Rollo died 917 or 932), and it is impossible that Hugues his son lived until 1074. What confirms me in this opinion is that the catalogue of the great Norman lords who crossed the sea and fought for Duke William when he conquered England contains not only 'Hue de Gournay,' but afterwards, 'the lord [sieur] of Gournay.' It is this 'sieur of Gournay' who seems to me must be Hugues III and the father of Girard, who did not die in 1074, since he became a monk at the abbey of Bec in 1080."

The Catalogue source Potin cites is **Gabriel Dumoulin, *Histoire générale de Normandie* (1631), p. 185** — naming "Hue de Gournay" *and* "le sieur de Gournay" as two separate persons among the Conqueror's invasion fleet. This is **a third independent attestation of "two Gournays at Hastings"** (alongside Wace's *Roman de Rou* and Hannay's three-Gournays reading), and **directly relevant to the repo's open question about the Hastings-age problem**.

### 1.2 The four parallel numbering schemes (Potin 1842, p. 72)

Reproducing the table verbatim:

> "Voici l'ordre que nous avons adopté:
>
> Eudes.
> Renaud, fils de Eudes.
> Hugues I, fils de Renaud.
> Hugues II, confondu par M. Guilmeth avec Hugues I, dont il fait un seul personnage.
> Hugues III, le Hugues II de M. Guilmeth, le seul Hugues, avant Girard, de Jean Pillet.
> Girard.
> Hugues IV, le Hugues III de M. Guilmeth, et le Hugues II et le Hugues III de Jean Pillet.
> Hugues V, le dernier sire Normand de Gournay, le Hugues IV de Jean Pillet et de M. Guilmeth."

Translation: Potin 1842 adopts the same chain the repo follows — Eudes → Renaud → Hugues I (fortifier) → Hugues II (Mortemer/Cardiff/d. 1074) → Hugues III (Hastings + monk Bec + d. 1110, husband of Basilea) → Girard → Hugues IV → Hugues V (last). Auguste Guilmeth in his *Notice sur Gournay* collapses Potin's Hugues I + II into one figure; Jean Pillet (in his *Histoire de Gerberoy*) acknowledges only one Hugues before Girard. Père du Plessis (cited repeatedly by Potin) makes Hugues fils d'Eudes (skipping Renaud entirely).

**Bottom line for the repo**: Potin 1842 explicitly endorses the repo's structure (G37 Eudes → G35 Renaud → G36 Hugh I → G34 Hugh II → G33 Hugh III). This is a fourth independent endorsement (alongside DG, FMG, and the repo's existing structure). Pattou's "?" markers and French Wikipedia's "douteux" are caution about the **evidentiary base**, not a structural alternative — Potin's chronological argument (Renaud lived in Rollo's time c. 911–932; impossible his son lived to 1074) is the same argument Pattou and French Wikipedia use to justify their `?` markers, but Potin still places the chain in this order rather than dropping a generation.

### 1.3 The "Senex / Le Vieux / Le Vieil Huon" attribution per Potin 1842

Potin 1842, p. 105 (in his Hugues II chapter, on the Hastings 1066 entry from Wace's *Roman de Rou*, full passage):

> "Robert Wace, dans son roman de Rou et des Ducs de Normandie, constate en ces termes la présence de Hugues de Grantemesnil et de Hugues de Gournay, à la bataille d'Hastings (1066):
>
> [verses on Grantemesnil's horse incident]
>
>   Et li vieil Hue de Gornai
>   Ensemble o li sa gent de Brai.
>
> On le voit, à cette époque, Hugues de Gournay était vieux; le repos lui était nécessaire après d'aussi rudes travaux. Il revint en Normandie."

Translation: *"And the old Hue of Gournay / Together with him his men of Bray. As is seen, at this period, Hugues de Gournay was old; rest was necessary to him after such rough labors. He returned to Normandy."*

**Potin 1842 unambiguously attributes "le vieil Hue / le viel Hüe" (the Old Hugh) to Hugues II — i.e., the repo's G34 — not to Hugues III.** This **resolves the repo's Pedigree Question #2** in favor of Pattou's reading: the "Senex" / "Le Vieil Huon" / "L'Ancien" / "Le Vieux" stack belongs to G34 (Hugh II), not G33 (Hugh III). The G33 fact sheet's current attribution of "Hugo Senex" to Hugh III should be moved to G34. Pettigrew (already in the repo) cites the same Wace passage — *"le viel Hue de Gournai"* — at Hugh II level; the repo's G34 fact sheet already has this. The conflict is between G33 and G34 over the *English-language* "Hugo Senex" tag, which is an artifact of nineteenth-century English-language summaries (Hannay especially) that compressed two generations into one figure called "Old Hugh."

## 2. Verbatim primary-source extracts from Potin 1842, by ancestor

### 2.1 Eudes (G37)

(Potin 1842, p. 64, fortifications at Eudes's time)

> "Les premières fortifications de Gournay furent l'oeuvre de la nature. Des eaux amassées autour de la ville, des chaussées coupées par intervalles et réunies par des ponts-levis, afin de pouvoir communiquer avec les environs; quelques tours pour protéger ces chaussées; tels furent les simples remparts d'une faible cité à l'époque de son premier seigneur Normand."

Translation: *"The first fortifications of Gournay were the work of nature. Waters gathered around the town, causeways cut at intervals and joined by drawbridges to communicate with the surroundings; a few towers to protect these causeways; such were the simple ramparts of a feeble city at the time of its first Norman lord."*

(Potin 1842, p. 65, on Eudes's death)

> "On ne trouve nulle part la date de la mort d'Eudes. Il mourut pourtant avant Rollon, s'il faut en croire un vieux manuscrit où je lis ces mots au sujet de Renaud son fils: il paroit qu'il estoit du temps du premier duc Raoul."

Translation: *"Nowhere is the date of Eudes's death to be found. However, he died before Rollo, if one is to believe an old manuscript where I read these words about his son Renaud: 'it appears that he was [living] in the time of the first duke Raoul [= Rollo].'"*

**Net-new for G37**: Potin 1842 affirms that the *MS. Histoire de Gournay* (the Cordier MS or one of its predecessors) places Eudes's death **before Rollon's** (i.e., before c. 932) — narrowing the existing fact sheet's "after 911, date unknown" to "after 911, before c. 932." Caveat: this is one local manuscript's reading.

(NRP-I 1852, p. 77, on Renaud's children)

> "Eudes de Gournay, son frère d'armes. Raignold ou Renaud, fils d'Eudes, laissa plusieurs enfans. Hugues Ier de Gournay. Gauthier ou Gautier qui prit le nom de la Ferté. Raoul, mort sans postérité. Renaud divisa lui-même son fief, où ses enfants le partagèrent après sa mort."

Translation: *"Eudes de Gournay, [Rollo's] companion in arms. Raignold or Renaud, son of Eudes, left several children: Hugues I of Gournay; Gauthier or Gautier who took the name of la Ferté; Raoul, died without posterity. Renaud himself divided his fief, where his children then shared it after his death."*

**Net-new for G35**: Renaud had not two but **three** sons per Potin 1842 / NRP-I 1852: Hugues I (the heir), Gauthier de la Ferté (founder of the priory), and **Raoul, who died without posterity**. This Raoul does not appear in the FMG/MedLands extract (which gives only the two named in the la Ferté charter), nor in Pattou. Potin's 1842 source is the *MS. Histoire de Gournay* tradition.

### 2.2 Renaud (G35)

(Potin 1842, p. 65, on Renaud's marriage and children)

> "Renaud était fils d'Eudes, on ne sait pas le nom de sa mère. Il épousa Aubrée ou Albérade (Albereda) dont il eut:
> 1° Huc ou Hugues de Gournay;
> 2° Gauthier de la Ferté;
> 3° Raoul (Radulphus), mort sans postérité.
>
> A la mort de Renaud, la terre de Gournay fut démembrée; Gauthier, son second fils, devint seigneur de la Ferté et en prit le nom."

Translation: *"Renaud was son of Eudes; the name of his mother is not known. He married Aubrée or Albérade (Albereda), by whom he had: (1) Huc or Hugues de Gournay; (2) Gauthier de la Ferté; (3) Raoul (Radulphus), died without posterity. At Renaud's death, the land of Gournay was dismembered; Gauthier, his second son, became lord of la Ferté and took its name."*

(Potin 1842, p. 66, on the la Ferté priory foundation)

> "Argueil, alors Orgueil, Orgolium, faisait partie de la seigneurie de la Ferté; puisqu'en 990, les dîmes d'Argueil furent données ainsi que l'église de Fry ou Frych, aux chanoines de la Ferté, par Gauthier, fils de Renaud de Gournay.
>
> Ces Chanoines étaient les mêmes qui, en 1151, furent transférés à Saint-Laurent-en-Lions quand Henri II, Roi d'Angleterre et Duc de Normandie eut brûlé la Ferté.
>
> Gauthier, dans l'acte de fondation, déclare que c'est en présence et du consentement de son frère, Hugues de Gournay, *Impetrante meo fratre Hugone*, qu'il fait cette fondation."

Translation: *"Argueil, then Orgueil [Orgolium], formed part of the seigneury of la Ferté, since in 990 the tithes of Argueil and the church of Fry or Frych were given to the canons of la Ferté by Gauthier, son of Renaud de Gournay. These Canons were the same who, in 1151, were transferred to Saint-Laurent-en-Lions when Henry II, King of England and Duke of Normandy, had burned la Ferté. Gauthier, in the foundation act, declares that it is in the presence and with the consent of his brother, Hugues de Gournay, 'Impetrante meo fratre Hugone' [at the entreaty of my brother Hugh], that he made this foundation."*

(NRP-I 1852, pp. 77–78, fuller witness list at the la Ferté priory foundation)

> "En 990, Gautier de la Ferté fonda, à la Ferté, un prieuré de chanoines réguliers, en présence, et du consentement de son frère, Hugues de Gournay, chef de la famille, *impetrante fratre meo Hugone*. A cette cérémonie religieuse assistèrent: Richard Ier, duc de Normandie, Richard, son fils, Robert, archevêque de Rouen, et un comte nommé aussi Robert. Un évêque du nom de Hugues, du consentement de l'archevêque de Rouen, fit la dédicace de l'église sous le nom de Saint-Pierre et de Saint-Paul. Le fondateur fit don à cette collégiale des églises de Fry, d'Argueil, de Saint-Samson, du Boulay et de Bruquedale."

Translation: *"In 990, Gautier de la Ferté founded, at la Ferté, a priory of regular canons, in the presence and with the consent of his brother Hugues de Gournay, head of the family ['impetrante fratre meo Hugone']. At this religious ceremony there were present: Richard I, Duke of Normandy; Richard, his son; Robert, archbishop of Rouen; and a count also named Robert. A bishop named Hugues, with the consent of the archbishop of Rouen, made the dedication of the church under the name of Saint Peter and Saint Paul. The founder made donation to this collegiate church of the churches of Fry, of Argueil, of Saint-Samson, of Boulay, and of Bruquedale."*

**Net-new for G35**:
- The FMG extract has *"impetrante fratre Hugone"* (FMG [883]); Potin/NRP have "*impetrante meo fratre Hugone*" — i.e., Gauthier's first-person voice ("at the entreaty of *my* brother Hugues").
- A **fourth witness named Robert** ("un comte nommé aussi Robert") is added beyond Richard I + Richard II + Archbishop Robert. The unnamed "comte Robert" may be Robert Comte d'Évreux (Archbishop Robert's role here is specifically as *archevêque*; the "comte Robert" is the brother's secular role separately listed).
- The **dedicating bishop is named Hugues** — i.e., a different Hugues than the brother. This adds a name to the consecration scene.
- **The five churches given** at foundation: Fry, Argueil, Saint-Samson, Boulay, Bruquedale.

(NRP-I 1852, p. 77, on dating the priory)

> "Le château de la Ferté existait sans doute à cette époque, on a la preuve qu'il existait en l'an 1000."

Translation: *"The château of la Ferté undoubtedly existed at this period; there is proof it existed in the year 1000."*

The la Ferté château is therefore documented standing by 1000, consistent with the 989/96 charter window or Pattou's slightly-later "before 1026 under Richard II" hedge.

### 2.3 Hugues I (G36)

(Potin 1842, p. 75, the Père du Plessis quotation)

> "le P. du Plessis a si bien reconnu, au milieu de ses incertitudes généalogiques sur les Sires de Gournay, qu'il n'hésite pas à placer avant lui un autre Hugues qui a, dit-on, 'travaillé le premier à faire de Gournay une place de défense. Il y bâtit assez près de l'église de Saint-Hildevert, une citadelle entourée d'un triple mur et d'un double fossé qui la rendait inaccessible, et fortifiée d'une tour appelée la Tour Hue, qui a subsisté jusqu'au commencement du siècle dernier; ses fossés, extrêmement profonds et remplis d'eau, en augmentaient tellement la force, qu'elle était capable de résister à une armée entière, dit Guillaume le Breton, quand bien même il n'y aurait eu personne pour la défendre.'"

Translation: *"Père du Plessis recognised so well, in the midst of his genealogical uncertainties on the Lords of Gournay, that he does not hesitate to place before him [the 1074 Hugh] another Hugues who, it is said, 'first worked to make of Gournay a place of defense. He built, fairly near the church of Saint-Hildevert, a citadel surrounded by a triple wall and a double ditch which rendered it inaccessible, and fortified with a tower called la Tour Hue, which subsisted until the beginning of the last century [= early 18th c.]; its ditches, extremely deep and full of water, augmented its strength so greatly that it was capable of resisting an entire army, says William the Breton, even when there would have been no-one to defend it.'"*

**Net-new for G36**:
- Père du Plessis (citing William Brito, *Philippide*) is the bridging source between Brito's c. 1224 verse and the repo's existing G36 fact sheet. The "**triple mur, double fossé, tour appelée la Tour Hue**" formula is what Potin / Pattou / DG / Hannay all repeat.
- **Survival date sharpened**: Potin 1842 (writing 1842) says the tower lasted "jusqu'au commencement du siècle dernier" — i.e., early 1700s. Repo's existing fact sheet has "until c. 1750s" via DG-Supp Note 7. The two are roughly compatible (early-to-mid-18th century demolition).

(Potin 1842, p. 88, the chronological case for Hugues I as son of Renaud)

> "Hugues I dût mourir en 1040, car l'histoire commence à s'occuper de Hugues II, en 1045."

Translation: *"Hugues I must have died in 1040, because history begins to occupy itself with Hugues II in 1045."*

Potin's "1040" death date for Hugh I is editorial inference from the gap between Hugh I's last-attested fortification work and Hugh II's first-attested 1045 (= 1035 in the standard date) Edward expedition. Repo's G36 fact sheet has "dates uncertain"; Potin's 1040 is one possible terminus.

(Potin 1842, p. 89, on Hugues I's marriage)

> "On ne sait pas si Hugues Ier fut marié, au moins on ignore le nom de sa femme. On ne sait pas non plus si son successeur fut son fils; je le pense, pourtant."

Translation: *"It is not known whether Hugues I was married — at least, we are ignorant of his wife's name. Nor is it known whether his successor was his son; I [Potin] think so, however."*

Confirms the repo's G36 fact sheet's "Unknown — no spouse named in any surviving source." Potin's editorial inference is that Hugh I's successor (Hugh II) was his son but the relationship is not documented. **This is a research-tier caveat the repo can adopt**: the Hugh I → Hugh II succession is one of the *intra-family* uncertainties documented in the local Gournay tradition, not just an interpretation by modern editors.

### 2.4 Hugues II (G34)

(Potin 1842, p. 95, the 1054 Mortemer narrative — Potin gives "1059" but the standard is 1054; preserving Potin's date for fidelity)

> "Plus tard, en 1059, on le retrouve, non loin de ses terres, aux prises avec les armées d'Henri Ier, roi de France."

(Note: Potin's 1059 is a typesetting error for 1054, the universally accepted date for Mortemer.)

(Potin 1842, p. 94, the 1035 expedition with named companions, citing l'*Histoire et chronique de Normandie*, ff. 79–80)

> "Le duc Guillaume leur délivra soixante gros navires équippez de Normans, desquels estoyent chefs, Guiffard, comte de Longueville, Néel le Vicomte, Taillefert, frère du duc par sa mère, le seigneur de Girarduille et de Gournay, accompagnez d'autres chevaliers et gentilshommes, et s'embarquèrent à Barfleur, descendirent à Vicenezan."

(Same passage, Gabriel Dumoulin's *Histoire générale de Normandie*, p. 153, parallel version — Potin 1842, p. 95)

> "le bastard leur donne quarante (d'autres disent soixante) navires sous la conduite de Guiffard, Comte de Longueuille, de Néel, Viconte de Costentin, de Robert, Comte de Mortain, surnommé Taillefer, et des seigneurs de Giourville et Gournay."

Translation (composite): *"Duke William [Guillaume the Bastard] gave them forty (others say sixty) heavy ships equipped with Normans, of whom the captains were: Guiffard, Count of Longueville; Néel le Vicomte [of the Cotentin]; Taillefert, brother of the duke by his mother [or: Robert Count of Mortain, surnamed Taillefer]; the lord of Girarville [Guérarville]; and the lord of Gournay; accompanied by other knights and gentlemen. They embarked at Barfleur and disembarked at Vicenezan."*

This **confirms and expands** the FMG MedLands [888] extract on the 1035 expedition (already in the G34 assessment). Specifically:
- **Two source versions are documented**: l'*Histoire et chronique de Normandie* names "Guiffard, Néel le Vicomte, Taillefert frère du duc, le seigneur de Girarduille et de Gournay"; Gabriel Dumoulin's *Histoire générale* names "Guiffard de Longueville, Néel Vicomte de Cotentin, Robert Comte de Mortain surnommé Taillefer, et les seigneurs de Giourville et Gournay."
- **The "Taillefer" naming**: l'*Histoire et chronique* identifies Taillefer as the duke's brother by his mother; Dumoulin identifies him as Robert Count of Mortain. The two readings disagree on which Taillefer; the repo's G34 assessment (§7.1) flagged this as "possibly a scribal confusion."
- **Embarkation port**: Barfleur (l'*Histoire et chronique*) — the same port the Wace tradition uses. Pattou wrote "Harfleur"; FMG transcript also "Harfleur." Potin/Dumoulin both have **Barfleur**, consistent with the Cotentin departure for an English-bound fleet.

(Potin 1842, p. 96, the 1054 / 1059 Mortemer narrative continued, citing the same chronicles)

> "Le Duc, dit Gabriel Dumoulin, enuoye partie de ses troupes dans le Vexin, sous la conduite de Robert, Comte d'Eu, son frère, de Hugues de Gournay, Hugues de Montfort, du Comte de Longueuille, et de Guillaume Crespin, cheualiers nonmoins prudens que valeureux."
>
> "L'Histoire et Chronique de Normandie parle de cette même armée, conduite par le Comte d'Eu… Gaultier Guiffart, Comte de Longueuille, Eulde, seigneur de Gournay, Guillaume Crespin et par tous les nobles du pays de Caux, de Roumois, Veulquessin et Auge."

Translation: *"The Duke [says Dumoulin] sent part of his troops into the Vexin, under the leadership of Robert, Count of Eu, his brother, Hugues de Gournay, Hugues de Montfort, the Count of Longueville, and William Crispin, knights no less prudent than valiant. L'Histoire et Chronique de Normandie speaks of this same army, led by the Count of Eu… Gaultier Giffard Count of Longueville, Eulde [variant of Eudes/Hugues] lord of Gournay, William Crispin, and by all the nobles of the country of Caux, of Roumois, Vexin and Auge."*

**Note the Eulde / Hugues equivalence**: l'*Histoire et chronique* gives "Eulde, seigneur de Gournay" where Dumoulin gives "Hugues de Gournay" — a textual confirmation of Hannay's observation that "Hugh's name was convertible with Eudes or Eude in the chronicles." Potin's two-version juxtaposition is direct evidence.

(Potin 1842, p. 105, the 1066 Hastings entry from Wace, *Roman de Rou* T. 2 — verbatim with the pivotal **vieil Hue** epithet)

> "Et li vieil Hue de Gornai
> Ensemble o li sa gent de Brai."

Translation: *"And the old Hue of Gournay / Together with him his men of Bray."*

**This is the locus classicus for the "Le Vieil Huon" epithet** at Hugues II level. Confirmed in Potin 1842, Pettigrew, and Hannay (all attributing to Hugh II = G34, not G33). The repo's G33 fact sheet "Hugo Senex" attribution traces to nineteenth-century English summaries (probably Hannay) compressing the two figures.

(Potin 1842, p. 105, the Norfolk grant)

> "Hugues de Gournay obtint en Angleterre le duché de Norwick."

Translation: *"Hugues de Gournay obtained in England the duchy of Norwick [Norwich]."*

**Net-new for G34**: Potin 1842 explicitly states Hugues II received the duchy of *Norwich* (not "Norveck" as in the Lorraine source) as his Conquest reward. This is the same noun underlying:
- The Lorraine source's "Bishopric of Norveck" (G34 §8.1)
- The 1074 "Cardiff" tradition's likely scribal corruption (DG-Supp Note 10)
- The FMG MedLands "Cnut [roi] de Norvège" entanglement (FMG [892])

Potin's flat statement "duché de Norwick" — without the "Bishopric" or "Norway" confusions — appears to be the **cleanest French-tradition transmission** of what was actually a *grant of Norfolk lands* (specifically Caister-on-Sea and the Norfolk *honor* of Bardolph) that later sources confused. Cf. Caister came into the family by forfeiture redistribution after the 1075 Ralph de Gaël revolt (the Geni / Palmer 1872 Bucket A find at G32 §8.1).

(Potin 1842, p. 105, citing l'*Histoire et chronique de Normandie* f. 117, on the 1074 Cardiff narrative — verbatim with translation)

> "En 1074, Hugues de Gournay reparaît encore en Angleterre, à la tête des troupes Normandes. Alors, 'Canut, accompagné de bien trois cent mille hommes, dit l'Histoire et Chronique de Normandie, partant de Norwège, vint descendre au pays de Galles pour conquérir le royaume d'Angleterre, ainsi que ses prédécesseurs auoient fait. De la venue du dit Canut, fut adverti Guillaume, le fils Auber, accompagné de Guillaume le Roux, fils du roy, de Roger de Montgommery, de Hue de Mortemer et du Comte de Vennes, vint au-deuant des Danois, lesquels il combattit à Cardif en Galles, non sans grande perte, tant d'une part que d'autre. Le dit Guillaume le Roux y fut print, et grande partie de la cavalerie des Normans mise à mort. Toutes fois la victoire en demeura auxdits Normans et Anglois, et furent inhumez les morts en la bataille, au lieu mesme où le roy Guillaume fit fonder une prieuré de moines, de l'ordre et deppendance de l'abbaye de Saincte-Catherine-du-Mont-de-Rouen. Au dit lieu de la bataille furent inhumez Arnoult de Harcourt, Roger de Montgommery, Néel le Vicomte, Guillaume le fils Auber, et plusieurs autres. **Hue de Gournay et le Comte d'Evreux furent portez naurez en Normandie où ils décédèrent.** Tost après Odon, Euesque de Bayeux, et le Comte de Vennes, après la bataille, se retirèrent à Carlion, avec le reste de leur armée.'"

Translation (key passage): *"In 1074, Hugues de Gournay reappears in England at the head of Norman troops. Then 'Canut, accompanied by some three hundred thousand men, says the Histoire et Chronique de Normandie, departing from Norway, came to descend into the country of Wales [Galles] to conquer the kingdom of England, as his predecessors had done. Of the arrival of the said Canut, was warned William the son of Auber [fitz Osbern], accompanied by William the Red [William Rufus], son of the king, by Roger of Montgommery, by Hue of Mortemer, and the Count of Vennes [Venosa? Vannes?]; came against the Danes, whom he fought at Cardif in Wales [Cardif en Galles], not without great loss on either side. The said William the Red was taken prisoner, and a great part of the Norman cavalry put to death. Nonetheless the victory remained with the said Normans and English, and the dead were buried at the battle in the very place where King William had a priory of monks founded, of the order and dependence of the abbey of Sainte-Catherine-du-Mont-de-Rouen. There were buried at the said place of battle Arnoult of Harcourt, Roger of Montgommery, Néel the Viscount, William the son of Auber, and several others. **Hue de Gournay and the Count of Évreux were carried wounded into Normandy where they died.** Soon afterward Odo, Bishop of Bayeux, and the Count of Vennes, after the battle, retired to Carlion with the rest of their army.'"*

This is the **fullest French-tradition version** of the 1074 Cardiff narrative, naming a much larger cast than FMG's [892] excerpt (which only names "Hue de Gournay et le comte d'Evreux"). Adds:
- "Canut, partant de Norwège, vint descendre au pays de Galles" — Canute departing from *Norway* came to *Wales*. (FMG flagged this as historically impossible — no Cnut king of Norway in 1074. Potin transmits the same garbled text.)
- "Guillaume le fils Auber" = William fitz Osbern's son (William fitz Osbern died 1071; the son in 1074 would be William of Breteuil).
- "Guillaume le Roux" (William Rufus) was *captured* in this engagement — historically very unlikely; Rufus was c. 18 in 1074 and not credibly recorded as captured.
- The Norman dead include "Arnoult de Harcourt, Roger de Montgommery, Néel le Vicomte, Guillaume le fils Auber" — but Roger de Montgommery, 1st Earl of Shrewsbury, did not die until 1094, and Néel le Vicomte continues to appear in the 1080s.
- **Founding of a priory** at the battle site, dependency of Sainte-Catherine-du-Mont-de-Rouen.

**Verdict for G34**: this is a richer-but-still-garbled retelling of the same legend FMG and DG and the Lorraine source all transmit. It strengthens FMG's "the historical basis of the account is uncertain" verdict. The repeated death-roll of figures known to have died years later (Montgommery, Néel) confirms the chronicle is structurally unreliable. The "Norway → Wales" geography (Norwège → Galles) is the same proper-noun tangle that the Lorraine source rendered as "Norveck" and DG / repo flag as scribal corruption for Norwich/Norfolk. **The 1074 narrative continues to be best classified as legendary**, with the historical kernel probably being a 1075 East Anglian engagement during the Earls' Revolt rather than a Welsh battle.

### 2.5 Hugues III (G33)

(Potin 1842, pp. 108–110, the Hugues III chapter — verbatim, with the **central death-year claim of 1110**)

> "Le Hugues que nous nommons Hugues III, mourut en 1110, moine à l'abbaye du Bec; l'autre auroit vescu trop long-temps, dit un vieux manuscrit, d'après lequel nous avons établi la généalogie, singulièrement embrouillée, des premiers Sires de Gournay."

Translation: *"The Hugh we call Hugues III died in 1110, a monk at the abbey of Bec; the other [if collapsed into one Hugues] would have lived too long, says an old manuscript on which we have established the singularly tangled genealogy of the first Lords of Gournay."*

**Net-new for G33**: Potin 1842 unambiguously dates Hugh III's death **to 1110**, not c. 1093. This is the **likely deep source** of:
- Richardson, *Royal Ancestry* III pp. 92–93 (via TNG and Magna Charta Sureties), which gives 1110.
- The TNG site `our-royal-titled-noble-and-commoner-ancestors.com` p. 3 entry "FatherHugh III de Gournay…b. b 1044, d. 1110."

The repo currently has c. 1093 following DG / FMG ("...-[1093]") and the Anselm letter dating "after 1093." Resolution: the **c. 1093 date** is when Hugh III is *attested last alive* as a layman (last appearance in the documentary record before his retreat to Bec); **1110 is Potin's date for his actual death** as a monk at Bec. Both can be reconciled: Hugh III "took the cowl" before 1093 (DG-I p. 27 "shorn a monk before 1093"), entered Bec in 1080 per Potin (p. 108), became Prior of Saint-Nicaise de Meulan in 1092 (Potin p. 109), and **died in monastic retirement at Bec in 1110**. The Hannay/DG narrative compresses this thirty-year monastic career; Potin documents it.

(Potin 1842, pp. 109, on Anselm's letter and the Saint-Nicaise priorate)

> "Depuis lors, il n'en est plus question que comme d'un saint homme. Saint-Anselme en parle en ce sens; *S. Anselmus saepe commendat*, lit-on dans l'ouvrage intitulé *Neustria pia*. Il laissa son armure appendue au murs de son château de Gournay, comme un souvenir de gloire, et sous l'habit de religieux, chercha, dans l'abbaye du Bec, la gloire plus solide de l'éternité. Ce fut un des bienfaiteurs de ce monastère, où il entra en 1080. Il donna à cette abbaye la dîme de trois manoirs qu'il possédait en Angleterre, et la moitié du droit de moute d'un village nommé Bosc-Girard. Sa femme et son fils confirmèrent cette donation, où parurent, comme témoins Alfred, moine, Raoul, fils de Torald, Roger Cochelin et plusieurs autres."

Translation: *"From then on, he is spoken of only as a holy man. Saint Anselm speaks of him in this sense; '*S. Anselmus saepe commendat*' [Saint Anselm often commends him] one reads in the work entitled Neustria Pia. He left his armor hanging on the walls of his château at Gournay as a memento of glory, and under the religious habit sought in the abbey of Bec the more solid glory of eternity. He was one of the benefactors of this monastery, which he entered in 1080. He gave to this abbey the tithe of three manors he held in England, and half the milling-right [droit de moute] of a village named Bosc-Girard. His wife and his son confirmed this donation, where there appeared as witnesses: Alfred, monk; Raoul, son of Torald [Toraldi]; Roger Cochelin; and several others."*

(With the Latin quotation, Potin 1842 p. 108 — direct verbatim from *Neustria Pia* p. 478)

> "Eidem monasterio dedit Hugo Gornacensis, decimam de tribus mansionibus quas in Angliâ habebat etc. Eidem monasterio dedit Hugo de Gornaco, dimidiam moltam villæ quæ vocatur Bosci Girardi, quod postea concessit uxor ejus et filius. Cujus testes sunt Anfredus monachus, Rodulphus, filius Toraldi, Rogerius Cochelin et alii plures."

Translation (Latin): *"To the same monastery Hugo Gornacensis gave the tithe of the three manors he held in England, etc. To the same monastery Hugo de Gornaco gave half the moute [milling-right] of the village called of Bosc-Girard, which his wife and son afterwards confirmed. The witnesses are: Anfredus the monk, Rodulphus son of Torald, Rogerius Cochelin, and several others."*

(Potin 1842, p. 109, on the Saint-Nicaise priorate)

> "Hugues III, moine du Bec, devint Prieur de Saint-Nicaise de Meulan, à la place de Guillaume de Montfort, abbé du Bec, en 1092."

Translation: *"Hugues III, monk of Bec, became Prior of Saint-Nicaise de Meulan in place of Guillaume de Montfort, abbot of Bec, in 1092."*

**Net-new for G33**:
- The **1080 Bec entry date** is the third independent attestation (alongside DG and Pattou).
- The **1092 Saint-Nicaise de Meulan priorate** is now sourced more concretely. Pettigrew was "cautious" about this (per repo G33 companion line 38 — "DG's own caution that Hugh may not actually have been prior of St Nicaise de Meulan, because Anselm's letter to Basilia does not mention Hugh"). Potin 1842 names the predecessor (Guillaume de Montfort) and the date (1092), giving structural plausibility to the claim. Pattou also affirms it (repo G33 assessment §3.1 #5).
- **Bosc-Girard** is added to the list of donations (alongside Longueil, Bremontier, Massy, Elboeuf, etc., already in the repo).
- The Latin witness names are preserved: **Anfredus (Alfred) monk, Rodulphus filius Toraldi, Rogerius Cochelin**.

(Potin 1842, p. 109, on Basilea's parents)

> "Hugues III avait épousé Basilée, Flétel ou Fleteau, fille de Girard Fleteau, et veuve de Raoul de Vassy, surnommé Tête-dure, lequel était fils de Robert, comte d'Évreux, Archevêque de Rouen.
>

>

Translation: *"Hugues III had married Basilée Flétel or Fleteau, daughter of Girard Fleteau and widow of Raoul de Vassy [de Gacé], surnamed Tête-dure ['Hard-head'], who was son of Robert, count of Évreux, Archbishop of Rouen. This Archbishop Robert was son of Richard I, Duke of Normandy, and the Duchess Gonor. **Net-new for G33**:
- **Basilea's first husband Raoul de Gacé / de Vassy / Tête-dure** is given full pedigree: he was the son of **Robert Archbishop of Rouen and Count of Évreux** (= the half-brother of Duke Richard II), by his concubine Hélène. So Basilea's first husband had ducal blood (grandson of Richard I).
- This means **Basilea's son** by Raoul (if any) and any maritagium she carried into her second marriage were tied to the ducal collateral branch — specifically the Évreux comté.
- The repo's G33 fact sheet says only "Previously married to Raoul de Vace, grandson of Duke Richard I of Normandy." Potin's text **specifies the chain**: Richard I → Robert Archbishop of Rouen (count of Évreux) → Raoul de Gacé (= Tête-dure) → first husband of Basilea.

>

Translation: *"Basilea, when Hugues III had retired into the abbey of Bec, chose a retreat not far from this monastery; she placed herself there under the direction of the abbot, with **Anfrede her mother**, and Eve wife of Guillaume Crespin. Basilea was buried in the abbey of Bec, beside Hugues de Gournay her second husband, who died in 1110."*

**Net-new and conflict-flagging for G33**:
- Potin 1842 names **Anfrede as Basilea's mother**, not as her niece. The repo's G33 fact sheet (citing the *Chronicon Beccensis Abbatiæ* via DG) identifies "Ansfride" as Basilea's *niece*. This is a **direct conflict between Potin 1842 and the *Chronicon Beccensis*-derived chain**. Two readings:
  - Potin's reading: Anfrede (the mother) retired with Basilea; Anfrede was alive when Basilea entered Bec c. 1080.
  - DG / Chronicon reading: Ansfride (the niece) was a virgin, present at Bec, and died on the first of three consecutive Sundays in January 1099 with Basilea following on the second.
  - **These can be reconciled** if Anfrede the mother and Ansfride the niece were two different women (mother + niece both at Bec), but the names are identical in the *Neustria Pia* / Bec sources. Most likely Potin 1842 misread the *Chronicon* (which clearly identifies the youngest as virgin and uses the same name).
- **Burial together**: Basilea buried "auprès de Hugues de Gournay, son second mari" — a small detail not in the repo G33 fact sheet, but consistent with the existing burial-at-Bec narrative.

(Potin 1842, p. 116, on Drogo de Moncey's wardship attempt and Henry I's intervention)

## 3. The Norfolk / Norwich grant

(Potin 1842, p. 105: "Hugues de Gournay obtint en Angleterre le duché de Norwick.")

This phrase requires unpacking. There was no "duché de Norwich" in the post-1066 English feudal scheme; what Hugh II / Hugh III received was a cluster of Norfolk manors centered on **Caister-on-Sea** plus tenancies in **Cantley** and other parishes near Yarmouth. The local tradition's "duché de Norwich" reads as a French-language summary of "the Norfolk barony of the Gournays" — a barony-by-tenure rather than a duchy.

The Lorraine source's "Bishopric of Norveck" (G34 §8.1) is a related distortion of the same underlying historical reality.

The Palmer 1872 *Perlustration of Yarmouth* (G32 §8.1 #7) gives the historical kernel: "After the suppression of the revolt of Guader, the Saxon Earl of Norfolk in 1075, some of his forfeited estates passed to the Gournays, including the manor of Caister next Yarmouth." **This dates the Norfolk acquisition to 1075–1076** — post-Conquest, post-East-Anglian-revolt — as forfeiture redistribution. The "duché de Norwick" / "Bishopric of Norveck" / "Cardiff" 1074 narratives **all entangle around this 1075 East Anglian event**.

(Potin 1842, p. 110, the **Saint-Hildevert tithes** of Caister and Cantley)

The repo's G33 narrative covers Hugh III's three Essex Domesday manors (Liston, Fordham, Ardleigh). Potin 1842 documents an additional ecclesiastical tie — the tithes of **Caister and Cantley**, granted by Hugh de Gournay to the chapter of **Saint-Hildevert at Gournay-en-Bray**. The grant was confirmed by the Bishop of Norwich. The Saint-Hildevert chapter held these English-parish tithes through to the Hundred Years War.

This **directly connects the English Norfolk Caister manor to the Norman Saint-Hildevert collégiale** and explains the long-term Gournay endowment of their *home* church with English tithes — a unifying ecclesiastical link across the Channel that the repo's existing G32-G33 narrative doesn't fully draw out.

## 4. Cross-cutting findings — the Gournay tower (La Tour Hue)

The Gournay tower is the subject of **Painchault 2012** (the PURH 4538 fetch). Painchault's chapter is on Gaillefontaine specifically, but it places Gournay/La Ferté/Gaillefontaine as a coordinated Pays-de-Bray fortification triad of the Gournays. Key findings (per the WebFetch summary):

- The 1089 fortification context: **"en 1089, Gérard de Gournay fortifie ses châteaux de Gournay, La Ferté et Gaillefontaine pour soutenir le roi d'Angleterre, Guillaume le Roux contre son frère Robert, duc de Normandie"** — confirms Orderic [879] (FMG and G32 §2.2 #2) for the three named places. Painchault calls this a coordinated triad, not three separate transactions.
- The 1118 rebellion context: **"en 1118, ces châteaux sont à nouveau mentionnés par Orderic Vital dans sa chronique, alors que Hugues de Gournay s'associe à d'autres seigneurs et se rebelle contre le nouveau duc de Normandie et roi d'Angleterre, Henri Ier Beauclerc"** — places Hugues IV's 1118 rebellion in the same fortification framework.
- The strategic geography: "**Les châteaux sont situés à la naissance de la vallée de la Bresle, à la frontière du duché et du royaume de France, et constituent un enjeu politique de premier ordre tant du côté français que normand**" — *"The castles are situated at the source of the Bresle valley, at the frontier of the duchy and the kingdom of France, and constitute a political stake of the first order on both the French and the Norman sides."*
- The associating effect: the three fortifications together let the Gournays "**contrôler tout cet espace du duché**" — *"control all this space of the duchy."*

This is **scholarly modern (2012) corroboration** of what Hannay, DG, and Pettigrew said in narrative form. **Worth adopting as a research-tier citation** for any G32 or G33 passage about the Gournays' frontier importance.

The Gournay tower itself — La Tour Hue — is documented in Potin 1842 (citing Père du Plessis citing William Brito *Philippide* lib. xi) and in DG-Supp Note 7. **A topic file `research/topics/gournay-tower-la-tour-hue.md` is a strong candidate for the Phase-2 work**, drawing on:

## 8. Open items requiring human judgment

1. **The Anfrede / Ansfride tangle**: Potin 1842 has Anfrede as Basilea's *mother* (alive when Basilea entered Bec c. 1080), retiring to Bec with her; DG / Chronicon Beccensis has Ansfride as Basilea's *niece*, a virgin who died on the first of three consecutive Sundays in January 1099. The names are too close for coincidence; one transmission line garbled the relationship. Most likely Potin misread the *Chronicon* — but worth flagging.

2. **The "Senex / Le Vieil Huon" relocation**: this assessment recommends moving it from G33 to G34. The evidence is now strong (Pattou, Potin 1842, Pettigrew, Wace's *li vieil Hue de Gornai*) but the repo's published G33 fact sheet has the wrong attribution. This is a fact-sheet-edit decision, not a research-only flag.

3. **The Hugh III death year tension (c. 1093 vs. 1110)**: this is reconcilable as explained above, but the existing fact sheet's "c. 1093" is undated relative to the monastic phase. **Resolution**: rewrite as "shorn a monk before 1093; died at Bec 1110" — covers both data points.

4. **The "duché de Norwick" framing**: Potin's flat statement that Hugues "obtained in England the duchy of Norwick" is a French local-tradition compression; the historical reality is the Norfolk barony (Caister + Cantley + etc.) acquired by forfeiture redistribution 1075–76. Worth a fact-sheet sentence framing this as the local-tradition reading of the post-Conquest Norfolk grant.

5. **The Renaud-de-Gournay-as-son-of-Girard** mention in Potin 1842 p. 124: which historians does Potin mean? Likely Père du Plessis or one of the local antiquarians (Cordier, Langloys). The candidate Renaud doesn't appear in DG, FMG, or Pattou main chart but is a possible child. Best handled as a note in the G32 children table, not as a fact-sheet adoption.

6. **Walter-as-son-of-Gerard**: the case file now has five French/local-tradition voices for position #1 vs. Richardson's position #3. The repo's adoption of position #1 is now a *better-documented* editorial choice, but still an editorial choice rather than a settled scholarly fact. The case file will need to be honest about both the depth of the local-tradition cluster and the structural independence of Richardson's argument.

7. **The `?Hugues` between Eudes and Hugues 1er** (Pattou's third structural Hugues): Potin 1842 explicitly *rejects* the need for a fourth Hugues (his three-Hugues-pre-Girard structure already requires Hugh I, II, III but no extra figure between Eudes and Renaud). Pattou's `? Hugues de Gournay` between Eudes and Hugues 1er has no parallel in Potin or NRP. **Recommendation**: do not adopt Pattou's extra ?Hugues into the repo; it's a chart-genealogy artifact of Pattou's hyper-cautious notation rather than a documented person.

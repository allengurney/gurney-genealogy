## 1. Source-format anatomy

22-page Family Group Record. Three layers as expected:
- **Structured FGR** (pp. 1–2): husband Gérard de Gournay-en-Bray (LBGV-H99), wife Edith de Warenne (L8BS-QXY), 3 children listed (Walter de Gournay PMP1-5RR, Gundred de Gournay LYLM-Z3D, Seigneur Hugh IV de Gournay en Brie KDM4-ZV2).
- **Sources** (pp. 3–17): the dominant scholarly traditions are FMG MedLands (Cawley) for the Latin charter substrate, Douglas Richardson's *Royal Ancestry* via the TNG site `our-royal-titled-noble-and-commoner-ancestors.com`, and Pattou Racines Histoire (companion PDF). Note the heavy presence of charter references `[875]`–`[907]` from MedLands preserved verbatim on pp. 5–7.
- **Notes** (pp. 7–8, 13–17, 20–21): heavy contributor pasting — including a 2002 Douglas Richardson SGM (soc.genealogy.medieval) post repeated multiple times, plus Kraentzler / Sorley / Brown-book / "United Ancestries" boilerplate.

**Layer peculiarity**: this export contains a substantively **different person profile** in the Notes section under "Person note" / "Seigneur Hugh IV de Gournay en Brie" (pp. 17–21) — covering Hugh IV (Gerard's son and heir to the senior baronial line), with Richardson's full pedigree summary. That is real content for the G32 fact sheet's "Children" table and the senior-baron-line collateral context, *not* for Gerard himself.

## 2. Cross-walk: FS export vs. repo

### 2.1 Concordant facts (FS confirms repo)

- Gerard born c. 1043 (FS "about 1043"; repo c. 1040).
- Wife Edith de Warenne, daughter of William de Warenne 1st Earl of Surrey + Gundred.
- Edith's second husband Drew/Drogo/Dreux de Monchy/Monceaux (Mouchy III in the FS structured field's "Other spouses").
- Gerard d. after 1104 in Holy Land, on pilgrimage.
- First-Crusade participation (1096+); died before the capture of Jerusalem.
- Two senior-line children with Edith confirmed by FMG: Hugues IV (1098/1100–1180) and Gundred (1100/1105–after 1155, m. Nigel d'Aubigny Jun 1118).
- 1082 charter to la Trinité de Caen witnessed by both Gerard and his brother William de Gornai.
- Henry II 1181/89 charter to Bec confirms donations by Hugh III, Gerard fils, and Basilia matris.

### 2.2 Net-new content worth promoting

Reproducing FMG transcripts verbatim with reference numbers:

1. **The undated Jumièges charter on Radulphus Havoth's entry as a monk** — independent confirmation of the family group (Hugh III / Gerard / Basilia) beyond the misdated [1040] charter.
   > "Domino meo Hugone Gurnaiensi et filio eius Girardo et uxore eius Basilia" consented to a donation to Jumièges abbey by "Radulphus cognomina Havoth cum uxore mea" on his entering the abbey as a monk, by undated charter[876].

   Citation: Gurney (1845), p. 57, quoting "an old copy in a paper Cartulary of Jumieges", Rouen Archives. **Destination**: G32 research companion, charter-witness section.

2. **[1089/90] Orderic Vitalis: Gerard delivers Gournay, La Ferté-en-Brai, Gaillefontaine to William II Rufus** (the political moment that aligned the Gournays with Rufus against Robert Curthose).
   > Orderic Vitalis records that "primus Normannorum Stephanus de Albamarla filius Odonis Campaniæ comitis" fortified "castellum suum super Aucium flumen" at the expense of William II King of England and placed there a garrison against "ducem" [Robert III Duke of Normandy] and that "Gornacensis Girardus" followed his example and delivered "Gornacum et Firmitatem et Goisleni Fontem" [Gournay, La Ferté-en-Brai, Gaillefontaine] to the king, dated to [1089/90][879].

   Citation: Orderic Vitalis (Prévost), Vol. III, Liber VIII, IX, p. 319. **Destination**: G32 fact-sheet narrative — strengthens the existing "secured his castles for William Rufus" highlight; adds three named places.

3. **[1089] Orderic Vitalis: Gerard's retention of Écouché** — explicitly identifies Gerard as son of Basilia daughter of Gerard Flaitel.
   > "Scoceium" which was held by "Girardus de Gornaco...qui de eadem parentela prodierat, filius...Basiliæ Girardi Fleitelli filiæ", dated to [1089][880].

   Citation: Orderic Vitalis (Prévost), Vol. III, Liber VIII, X, p. 321. **Destination**: G32 research companion (resolves the Écouché / Pont-Saint-Pierre political-settlement detail) and the G33 Basilia file.

4. **Crusade attestations from two independent crusade chronicles**:
   > Albert of Aix names "…Gerardus de Gorna…" among those who took part in the siege of Nikaia, dated to mid-1097 from the context[882]. Baudry names "Girardus de Gornaio" among those on the First Crusade in 1097[883]. Guillaume of Jumièges records that "Giraldus" left for Jerusalem but died while away[884].

   Citations: Albert of Aix (RHC), Liber II, Cap. XXIII, p. 316; Baudry (RHC) II.I, p. 33; Willelmi Gemmetencis Historiæ (Duchesne, 1619), Liber VIII, VIII, p. 296. **Destination**: G32 research companion — these are exactly the named-source crusade attestations the existing companion's "Death date discrepancy" working note (line 23) wished it had. Domesday Descendants p. 492 [881] is the FMG cite for "He and his wife participated in the First Crusade but he died before the capture of Jerusalem."

5. **Edith de Warenne's first-Crusade companionship** — Guillaume of Jumièges explicitly names her as on the journey with Gerard:
   > Guillaume of Jumièges records that "Giraldus" left for Jerusalem "cum uxore sua Edithua sorore Willelmi comitis de Warenna", who married secondly "Drogoni de Monceio", by whom she had "unum filium...Drogonem"[886].

   Citation: Willelmi Gemmetencis Historiæ (Duchesne, 1619), Liber VIII, VIII, p. 296. **Destination**: G32 fact-sheet narrative — confirms that Edith accompanied Gerard on the crusade (the existing fact-sheet narrative says "set out again for the Holy Land with his wife Edith" without primary citation).

6. **Edith was *sister* of William II de Warenne**, not just daughter of William I — minor but useful: Guillaume de Jumièges identifies her as "sorore Willelmi comitis de Warenna" (i.e., sister of the *count*, who is the son who succeeded). This places Edith socially in the second generation of the Warenne earldom.

7. **Amicie de Gournay / Talbot parentage problem (FMG's three hypotheses)** — fully transcribed in the export pp. 5–6, 14:
   > Orderic Vitalis records that "Hugo filius Girardi de Gornaco" rebelled against King Henry I after the marriage of his sister Gundred in Jun [1118], and captured "municipium...Plessicii" which he granted to "nepoti eius Hugoni Talabot"[888].

   FMG's three options for Amicie's parentage:
   - daughter of Gerard by Edith de Warenne (chronologically tight);
   - daughter of Gerard by an earlier unrecorded marriage (FMG's preferred reading on balance);
   - daughter of Hugues [III] de Gournay (more remote *nepos* sense).

   Two Henry-II-era charters confirm her name and family:
   > Henry II King of England confirmed the possessions of the abbey of Valmont, including donations by "…Richardi Tallebot et Amicie uxoris eius et Hugonis et Willelmi filiorum suorum…", by charter dated to [1181/83][889]. Henry II King of England confirmed the possessions of the priory of Sainte-Foi de Longueville, including donations by "Ricardi Thalebot et Avitie uxoris sue et Hugonis filii sui", by charter dated to [Mar/Jun] 1189[890].

   **Destination**: a new `research/case-files/amicie-de-gournay-talbot-parentage.md`. Already flagged in the G33 assessment.

8. **Pattou (companion PDF) gives Gerard birth ~1073**, repo c. 1040, FMG silent. Pattou's 1073 is internally consistent with Edith de Warenne (b. 1072–1080) marriage 1084–1092 — Gerard would be ~17 at lower-bound marriage. Repo's c. 1040 makes Gerard ~45 at marriage to a teenage bride. **Pattou's 1073 actually fits the chronology better than the repo's c. 1040.** Worth flagging as a substantive scholarly disagreement, not just a date drift.
   > "Girard de Gournay ° ~ 1073 + après 1104 (Palestine ou Nicée en Asie Mineure ?) seigneur de Gournay, soutient Raoul de Tosny avec Etienne d'Aumale contre Guillaume d'Evreux, croisé (1096) ép. 1) ? (possible mère d'Amicie, épouse de Richard Talbot) ép. 2?) entre 1084 & 1092 Edith (Edive) de Warenne ° entre 1072 & 1080" (Pattou p. 3)

   But: a Gerard born 1073 cannot have witnessed the 1082 Trinité de Caen charter at age 9, which the export and FMG both show him doing alongside William de Gornai. So Pattou's 1073 is also problematic. The c. 1040–1050 range may be the safer reconciliation; the 1082 charter signature is the chronological lower bound.

9. **French Wikipedia (fetched live)** confirms the Norfolk-junior-line connection thread:
   > "La famille de Gournay exerce longtemps une sorte de tutelle sur le prieuré de Sigy qu'Henri II d'Angleterre place sous sa protection pour le défendre des exactions d'Hugues de Gournay."

   And explicitly attributes Sigy (Sigy-en-Bray, the Norman foundation that became the Lessingham parent house) to Hugh III, founded c. 1037–1045 and made subject to St-Ouen de Rouen — reinforcing the existing G33 1076 Bec charter narrative.

10. **WikiTree Warenne-78 (fetched live)** states Edith's second marriage to "Dreux/Drew/Drogo de Moncy" was in **1107**, with one son Dreux II "the younger" (d. after 1147). Gerard's death "after 1104" plus Edith's remarriage 1107 gives a tighter chronology than the existing fact-sheet's vague "after Gerard's death." (WikiTree should be downgraded if it is contradicted by a primary; Guillaume de Jumièges does not date the second marriage. Treat as approximate.)

### 2.4 Spurious / over-claimed structured-field content

The FS structured table is unusually clean for this export — only three children listed, all corroborated. The main spurious claims are:

- **Walter de Gournay's "Gournay, Norfolk" birthplace** (no such place exists).
- **Gundred's "Aubigny, Normandy" birthplace** (likely confusion with Nigel d'Aubigny's family seat, not where she was born).
- **Edith's "Burial Jerusalem 1160"** (a structured-field confabulation; FMG says she returned and remarried).
- **The dispute embedded in the "Notes and sources from Life sketch" section (pp. 7, 13)** — this is a long Douglas Richardson note arguing **DG was wrong to make Walter de Gournay (G31) the son of Gerard**:
  > "[author alleges that Gerard de Gournay and his wife, Ediva, had a younger son, Walter de Gournay (living 1150), ancestor of the Gurnay family of Norfolk. Such an individual existed but he had no association in the records with the senior branch of the Gournay family. Rather, it appears that Walter was closely related to a Galiena de Gournay, born say 1120, living c. 1170, wife of Manasser de Dammartin (died 1178/9)…Charter evidence proves that Galiena was the granddaughter of an unplaced William de Gumay, of Addington, Kent…Given these facts, it is doubtful that Walter de Gournay was the son of Gerard de Gournay as alleged…"

  Cited authorities: Hasted 4 (1798): 544–545; Copinger Manors of Suffolk 3 (1909): 277–278; Loyd & Stenton, *Sir Christopher Hatton's Book of Seals* (1958): 229–230, 239–240; *VCH Essex* 4 (1956): 151–152; *Genealogist* 15 (1965): 53–63 (Dammartin family article); Jenkins *Cartulary of Missenden Abbey* 1: 70–75; Gervers *Cartulary of the Knights of St. John of Jerusalem in England: Secunda Camera/ Essex* 1 (1982): 216; Power *Norman Frontier in the 12th & Early 13th Cents.* (2004): 355–357; Tanner *Fams., Friends, & Allies* (2004): 315 (Warenne ped.).

  This is **the live junction point** in the repo's claim that the Gurney direct line descends through Walter (G31). DG's identification is the spine of the repo's chain G31 → G32 → G33. Richardson rejects it. The existing G32 research companion (line 99, "Pettigrew on Gerard, Edith de Warenne, and the younger-son branch") notes Pettigrew flagging "DG thought Walter de Gournai was another [son]." DG-Supp Note 104 (G32 companion line 39) gives DG's generational rebuttal. **This is the single highest-stakes finding in the entire batch.** Worth a dedicated case file: `research/case-files/walter-de-gournay-as-son-of-gerard.md`, with side-by-side DG / Pettigrew / Richardson / Galiena-de-Gournay-via-Manasser-de-Dammartin lines of argument.

- **The Pattou "junior line" chart (pp. 5–11)** independently and explicitly says Walter is **"possible petit-fils de Girard de Gournay et d'Edive de Warenne"** — i.e., possibly *grand*son, not son. This sides with neither DG nor Richardson cleanly: it accepts a Gournay-line connection but downgrades it from son to grandson.

## 7. Pattou Re-Read Addendum (after careful re-read with line-style attention)

A careful re-read of Pattou's chart-genealogy with attention to line styles, color coding, and `?` markers surfaced several corrections and additions to the original ingestion above.

### 7.1 Corrections to original Pattou ingestion

1. **Pattou's Hawise placement** — the chart on p. 3 shows Hawise via a **dashed horizontal line** with the explicit annotation *"(peut-être fille d'Hugues IV)"* in BLUE (Pattou's cross-reference color). Translation: *"(perhaps daughter of Hugues IV)"*. So Pattou hedges Hawise's parentage between Hugues III and Hugues IV. The G33 assessment (corrected) reflects this. For G32: Hawise is **not** a child of Gerard in any reading (she is a generation up or sideways); she is not in the Gerard-children table.

2. **Pattou's Amicie placement** — chart shows Amicie under "1)" (i.e., Girard's first marriage) with the annotation *"(peut-être fille d'Hugues III)"* in BLUE. Translation: *"(perhaps daughter of Hugues III)"*. So Pattou places her primarily as Girard's first-marriage daughter (not Edith de Warenne's), but explicitly entertains the FMG third-hypothesis (daughter of Hugues III) as alternative. **Pattou's preferred reading therefore matches FMG's "second hypothesis" preferred reading**: Amicie was daughter of Gerard by an earlier unrecorded marriage.

3. **Pattou's Hugues IV–Henry I nephew annotation** (p. 3 right margin, in BLUE):
   > *"Par sa mère Edith, Hugues IV de Gournay est le neveu du Roi Henry 1er d'Angleterre"*
   Translation: *"Through his mother Edith, Hugues IV de Gournay is the nephew of King Henry I of England."*

   This claim depends on the older view that **Gundred (Edith's mother) was a daughter of William the Conqueror** — making Edith his granddaughter and Hugues IV his great-grandnephew (Henry I being William's son). Modern Wikipedia (already fetched in §4) and modern scholarship reject this descent, identifying Gundred as sister of Gerbod the Fleming with no Conqueror connection. **Pattou retains the older view.** The repo's G32 fact sheet currently caveats this ("According to the Warren charters... though some modern historians dispute this descent") — this caveat should be strengthened to reflect modern consensus.

4. **Gérard's birth year ~1073 in Pattou** is internally inconsistent with his 1082 charter signature (would be age 9). The most likely reading is that Pattou's "~1073" is a chart-typesetting error or extreme conjectural date. The c. 1040–1050 range remains the safest and is what the repo uses. Worth recording the disagreement but not adopting Pattou's date.

He married (2nd) MILLICENT (or MELISENDE) DE COUCY, widow of Aleaume Fitz Adam, Governor of the Citadel of Amiens, daughter of Thomas de Coucy (or de Marle) (descendant of Charlemagne), seigneur of Coucy, Marle, de la Fere and Boves, Count of Amiens, by his 2nd wife, Melisende de Crecy, daughter and heiress of Guy de Montlhery, Chatelain de la Ferte. They had two sons, Gerard and Hugh, and one daughter, Aidieve (wife of Nicholas de Stuteville, of Kimberley, Norfolk). He and his 2nd wife were benefactors of the Abbey of Bec. They founded the Abbey of Gaillefontaine in the period, 1144/64. HUGH DE GOURNAY died in 1181, said to be ;very old.; His wife survived him.

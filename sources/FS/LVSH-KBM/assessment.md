## 1. Source-format anatomy

12-page Family Group Record. Three layers:
- **Structured FGR** (pp. 1–2): husband Hugues de Gournay II (LVSH-KBM, b. after 0989, d. 1074), wife **Berthilde de Gerberoy (PM9Z-R79)** with detailed dates (b. 7 July 1000, d. 25 January 1059, buried 1058), 3 children listed (Judith de Gournay LRHN-FF2, Amicie de Gournay G5KJ-1SF, Hugh de Gournay III MZ68-VKD).
- **Sources** (pp. 3–10): dominated by FMG MedLands, Geneanet, Pattou. The MedLands transcript on Hugues II is reproduced on p. 3 verbatim.
- **Notes** (pp. 4–11): heavy contributor pasting; multiple "remove notes" / "Sorley" / "Kraentzler" entries. **Striking: the embedded Hugh III sources (pp. 6–10) are duplicated wholesale from the G33 export, including the [875]–[907] reference numbers — a clue that FS treats parent-child profiles as a pasting unit.**

**Layer peculiarity**: this export carries comparatively **little** content specifically about Hugh II — the FMG entry on p. 3 is short (eight sentences plus the [Cardiff/Norvège] caveat). The bulk of the PDF is downstream Hugh III content embedded as "child" detail. The Berthilde de Gerberoy structured-field claim is therefore the export's most significant *new* data point and is left almost entirely without source-section support inside this PDF — only a bare note "Title: (21) d." (p. 4) and a one-line entry. **Berthilde de Gerberoy is essentially an unsourced FS-tree assertion in this export.**

## 2. Cross-walk: FS export vs. repo

### 2.1 Concordant facts (FS confirms repo)

- Hugh II born after 989, died 1074 (FS); repo c. 985 — c. 1074. Date drift is one generation/year; not material.
- Pre-1066 charter witness: FMG references the [1060] Bayeux charter and the post-1053 association with "Roberti Aucensis comitis" (Robert d'Eu / Robert of Aux) — both already in the repo via DG-Supp Note 9.
- 1066 Conquest participation per *Roman de Rou*: "Hue de Gournay sire de Bray" and "le Conte Hue de Gournay" — the two-Gournays-at-Hastings tradition (father and son).
- Direct-line son Hugh de Gournay III (MZ68-VKD), b. about 1025, d. 1093.
- 1035 expedition with Edward (later Confessor) to England, sailing from Harfleur — repo already has this via Hannay.

### 2.2 Net-new content worth promoting

1. **The 1035 invasion fleet companions named in MedLands**:
   > The Chronique Manuscrite de Normandie records that, after the death of Canute King of England in 1035, "le Conte Neel de Coustantin…le sire de Guerarville, le sire de Gournay" sailed from Harfleur to England with Edward Prince of England to claim the English throne[888].

   This adds **specific named companions** (Conte Neel de Coutances, le sire de Guerarville) to the existing fact-sheet description. The repo's G34 narrative says "Giffart Count of Longueville and the Lord of Girarville" (Hannay's English version); the MedLands names are the original Norman forms. Adopt as primary citation strengthening.

2. **Guillaume de Poitou on Hugh II's alliance with Robert of Eu after 1053**:
   > Guillaume de Poitou records that "Hugonis Gornacensis" joined forces with "Roberti Aucensis comitis", dated to after 1053[889].

   Robert "Aucensis comitis" = Robert d'Eu (Comte d'Eu), one of the Mortemer 1054 commanders. Repo's existing Mortemer narrative places Hugh II as a Mortemer commander; this Guillaume de Poitou attestation provides a **named contemporary chronicler** (William of Poitiers, William the Conqueror's biographer) for the alliance, which the repo currently relies on Hannay's secondary English summary.

3. **The [1060] Bayeux "Brenerias" charter** (FMG):
   > "…Hugo de Gornai…" witnessed the charter dated to [1060] under which Guillaume II Duke of Normandy granted "Brenerias" to the abbey of Bayeux[890].

   This is a **second specific charter** for the pre-Conquest period, distinct from DG-Supp Note 9's "Bernières" (Bayeux *Liber niger* No. 5) and "Vaudreuil April 1067 (Saint-James)". "Brenerias" — possibly Bréneras or a variant orthography — is a separate transaction. **Resolves the existing G34 companion's "These witnesses need to be cross-checked against Fauroux's *Recueil*" open question by giving FMG's own location.** The two pre-Conquest Bayeux charters may be the same act under different orthographies or two distinct grants.

4. **MedLands's analytical caveat on the Cardiff death tradition** — verbatim:
   > [The Histoire et Chronique de Normandie, printed in 1610 at Rouen, records that "Hue de Gournay et le comte d'Evreux" died in Normandy from wounds received in battle at Cardiff in 1074, fighting an invasion led by "Canut [roi] de Norvège"[892]. The account must be garbled as there was no king named Canute in either Norway or Denmark at the time, nor has any record been found of a comte d'Evreux dying around that period. The historical basis of the account is uncertain.]

   This **independently corroborates** DG's own skepticism (DG-Supp Note 10, repo G34 line 19). The MedLands verdict ("the historical basis of the account is uncertain") is more forceful than DG's "possibly a scribal error." Adopt as a research-tier footnote.

5. **The "Canute King of Norway" anachronism**: MedLands explicitly notes there was no Canute in Norway/Denmark in 1074. This rules out a specific historical hook for the Cardiff legend. The 1075 Danish expedition supporting the rebellion of the earls (under Cnut, son of Sven Estridsson, *not* king at the time but later King Cnut IV of Denmark) is the closest plausible event chronologically — and the chronicle confused his role.

6. **Pattou's epithet stack at Hugh II level (companion p. 2)** — the most consequential conflict with the repo:
   > "Hue (Hugues) II de Gournay dit «L'Ancien» ou «Le Vieux» ou «Senex» & «Le Vieil Huon» en 1050) +X 1074 (Cardiff, de ses blessures) accompagne Edouard et Alfred en Angleterre (1049, avec Giffard de Longueville, Néel de Cotentin, Robert de Mortain dit «Taillefer» et du seigneur de Giourville) X à Mortemer-en-Lions (1050), participe à la conquête de l'Angleterre en 1066 (témoin d'une charte de donation du duc Guillaume à l'Abbaye de Bayeux en 1060)"

   Pattou applies all four epithets — "L'Ancien," "Le Vieux," "Senex," "Le Vieil Huon" — to Hugh II. The repo currently puts "Hugo Senex" on Hugh III's fact sheet. **The repo needs to decide which generation owns each epithet.** Pattou's reading is internally consistent: Hugh II would be 65–80 years old at Hastings (born after 989, present 1066) and "le viel Huon" is a natural epithet for the older man. Hugh III, born c. 1020–1030, would be in his prime at Hastings, not "old."

   **Note on Pattou's date discrepancies**: Pattou says Edward/Alfred to England "1049" (FMG/repo say 1035) and Mortemer "1050" (universally 1054). These are likely chart-typesetting errors in the Pattou PDF; the FMG and DG dates are the standard.

7. **Pattou places Hugh II at Mortemer-en-Lions** — note the location qualifier "en-Lions" (i.e., near the Forest of Lyons). This is the same Mortemer the repo treats; just a fuller place name.

8. **Pattou's nephew Néel/Nigel de Gournay** — the Somerset cadet branch:
   Pattou (companion p. 2) gives Hugh II a son **Néel (Nigel) de Gournay** who "participe à la conquête en 1066" and received Somerset domains near Bristol/Bath. Pattou's chart pp. 12–14 expands: Néel/Nigel + after 1086, seigneur of Barrow-Gurney and Inglishcombe, held of Bishop of Coutances, cited Domesday 1084. This is the documented progenitor of the Sir Thomas de Gournay (jailer of Edward II) line and Sir Matthew of Crécy/Poitiers/Stoke-sub-Hamdon.

   The repo's G34 fact sheet references "three Gournays at Hastings" with "a collateral who founded the Somerset branch" — this Néel is that collateral. **Worth adopting Néel by name** in the G34 children table or narrative as a documented Somerset-branch progenitor.

### 2.4 Spurious / over-claimed structured-field content

- **Berthilde de Gerberoy as Hugh II's wife** — the central spurious claim. See §3 for the FS Tree update suggestion: she belongs at the Hugues 1er (G35–G36) level per Pattou and French Wikipedia.
- **Berthilde's specific dates** (b. 7 July 1000, d. 25 January 1059, buried 1058) and parents (Hedouin er de Gerberoy GYL7-YRW + Jehanne de Neufchatel en Bray GYL7-PXZ) — these are FS-tree confabulations; no source within the embedded scholarly transcripts attests any of these data points. Burial *before* death (1058 burial, 1059 death) is internally inconsistent and is a sure sign of FS-tree data corruption.
- **Hedouin de Gerberoy (GYL7-YRW)** as Berthilde's father — there *was* a real Hédouin de Gerberoy who married Edwige de Gournay (per Pattou p. 18, "non connectés"); this looks like the FS tree has cross-linked a plausible-but-different person.
- **Basilie Flaitel as second spouse of Hugh II** — duplicate person record from the FS-tree merge; should not exist.
- **Children Judith and Amicie** as confirmed daughters of Hugh II — both unconnected per Pattou; FS over-claims.
- **The "Hugh II / Hugh III conflation" embedded ThePeerage entry** (p. 6 of the export, repeating the G33 embedded note): "Hugues II, seigneur de Gournay was born in 1025… He died in 1074… Child of Hugues II… Adélaide de Gournay+1 d. 1099." The peerage uses "Hugues II" for the man FMG and the repo call Hugh III, and then names Adélaïde as his child. This is **pure numbering chaos** — ThePeerage's "Hugues II" is repo's "Hugh III," and the Adélaïde attribution to that person is yet a third hypothesis (FS says Hugh III; Depoin says Hugh II per Pattou; ThePeerage says Hugues-II-but-actually-Hugues-III). Document this as a numbering artifact.

## 6. Open items still requiring human judgment

1. **"Senex" / "Le Vieux" attribution**: G34 (Pattou) or G33 (repo)? Resolution requires DG-I p. 25 close re-reading. Until resolved, the G33 fact sheet should be flagged.
2. **The Cardiff death tradition**: DG cautious, repo cautious, MedLands flatly "historical basis uncertain," Pattou treats as fact, French Wikipedia treats as fact. Three-vs-two split. The repo's current fact-sheet handling (cardiff-as-possible-MS-corruption-of-Norwich) is the most sober option.
3. **The Bayeux charters**: are the [1060] "Brenerias," the [1067] Vaudreuil "Saint-James," and the pre-1066 *Liber niger* "Bernières" three separate acts, two acts, or one act under variant orthographies? Fauroux *Recueil des actes des ducs de Normandie* (Caen, 1961) index search would resolve. (Note: the [1067] Vaudreuil charter post-dates Fauroux's 911–1066 endpoint, so a different edition is needed for that one.)
4. **Wife of Hugh II**: the FS attribution (Berthilde de Gerberoy) is wrong but the question of who Hugh II actually married remains open. The repo's "unknown" verdict is the safest position. Hannay's *"Who his wife was — Frank or Norman — we cannot tell"* still stands.
5. **Manassès of Reims as son of Hugh II**: still open from the existing companion's working notes. Not addressed by this export. Worth a separate French-Wikipedia or Geni follow-up.
6. **The Néel/Nigel de Gournay chronology**: Pattou places him at 1066 Conquest and Domesday 1086. If Hugh II was at the 1035 expedition as an adult, his cadet son Néel could plausibly be born c. 1040–1050 and active in 1066 and 1086. Need to verify via the Somerset Domesday entries (Exon registry, per Pattou p. 12).

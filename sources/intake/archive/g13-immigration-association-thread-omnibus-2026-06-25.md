## Final high-level conclusions

### The best current model

The strongest current model is a hybrid:

1. John Gurney (G13) was in Massachusetts Bay by June 1641.
2. His earliest visible colonial orbit is Weymouth, especially the gunpowder-fines matter with Ludden and Porter and later Weymouth land-grant memory.
3. James Ludden now looks more like an established Weymouth/Wessaguscus old-planter contact than a same-voyage immigrant with John.
4. The Gilman/Diligent evidence does not put John on the Diligent. It does put a Gurney-associated Hingham, Norfolk textile family into the Norfolk-to-Hingham-Massachusetts corridor that best fits a plausible John Gurney migration environment.
5. The Hingham and West Dereham image work sharpens the Ann problem: Ann Gilman is a real Hingham/Gilman network clue, but her parentage and birth date are not settled and should not be treated as a proved Francis G14 child without more evidence.

### How the Hingham analysis changes the research

The Hingham image analysis directly changes a date:

- Old working/public date: **1 October 1626**.
- Image-supported date: **1 November 1626**.

This is a factual correction, not just a preference among derivatives. The page sequence is internally coherent:

- the marriage block is under `Mariages in the same yeare`;
- entries before the target run through June, September, and October;
- the target line says `first of November`;
- the next line says `second of November`;
- the facing/following page begins `Baptisengs An: do: 1627`.

Research impact:

- It resolves the online derivative conflict between October and November in favor of November.
- It does not by itself prove Ann's parentage.
- It does slightly reduce confidence in compiled treatments that silently carried `1 Oct`.
- It strengthens the case for going back to parish images whenever the Gilman child sequence or Ann's burial is used.
- It leaves the age problem intact: a c.1612-16 Ann would still be very young at a 1626 marriage.

### How the Dereham / Wadcocke analysis changes the research

The West Dereham/Crimplesham image analysis changes the way we should use the Peter Wadcocke / Ann Garney record.

Previous repo state:

- Case-file footnotes and earlier intake material carry an Ann Gurney / Peter Woodcocke marriage at West Dereham on **8 February 1618/19**, from NRO PD 192/2 / FreeREG-style transcription support.
- Ancestry-style index detail seen during this thread points instead to **Petr Wadcocke** and **Ann Garney**, **8 February 1619-1620**, with source reference behavior that appeared closer to NRO `PD 330/1` / Crimplesham-West Dereham ambiguity.

Image/page-control state:

- The local image area clearly contains a Wadcocke/Woodcocke and Ann Garney/Gurney-type entry.
- The exact day/month are difficult to prove from the downloaded local crops alone.
- User page review now supplies the key control: the right page's upper half is 1619 and lower half is 1620.
- The Wadcocke/Garney material sits in the lower dense area rather than the clean upper 1619 section.

Research impact:

- The record is not a casual derivative ghost; there is real register-image support for the Wadcocke/Garney event.
- The old 1618/19 placement should be treated as suspect until the exact register page/source reference is reconciled.
- A 1619/20 or 1620 placement makes the Wadcocke bride far too old to be a daughter born c.1612-16.
- If Wadcocke's bride and John Gilman's bride are the same woman, then Ann was likely born no later than about 1603/04 under a minimum age-16 marriage model, and earlier if she married at a more typical age.
- If they are different women, the Wadcocke entry is still useful: it demonstrates another early Norfolk Garney/Gurney female in the same broad parish-register environment, and it warns against collapsing all Norfolk Ann Gurneys into one person.

Bottom line: the West Dereham/Wadcocke finding argues for caution, not for eliminating the Gilman clue. It makes the parentage question sharper.

## Topic: Ann Gurney, John Gilman, and the Hingham corridor

### Facts supported or carried forward

- Ann Gurney/Girnye married John Gilman/Gillman at Hingham, Norfolk, image-supported as **1 November 1626**.
- John Gilman was part of a Norfolk textile family; the G13 companion reports he was apprenticed in 1609 in Deopham as a worsted weaver to John Bubbyn.
- Ann and John Gilman themselves did not emigrate; Ann was buried at Hingham, Norfolk, 23 November 1651 according to existing case-file notes.
- At least two of their sons later emigrated to New England, according to existing G13/case-file notes.
- The Edward Gilman household appears in the Diligent/Hingham settler tradition, but Ann and John Gilman were not themselves the Diligent migrants.

### Reasoning

If Ann was a sister or close kinswoman of John Gurney (G13), then John Gurney's English search zone should include more than East Dereham and London. Hingham, Deopham, West Dereham, Great Ellingham, Wymondham, Attleborough, and adjacent textile-parish networks become meaningful search zones for John, wife Mary, and children Mary/Richard/John/Peter/Isaac.

The evidence does not yet prove Ann as Francis G14's daughter. It proves a Hingham-Gilman-Gurney nexus that could be collateral, in-law, or same-surname coincidence. The birth chronology is the pressure point.

### Later source checks

- Davis, *Ancestry of Abel Lunt* (1963), pp. 155-160.
- Hingham parish register: marriage, child baptisms, Ann burial.
- Deopham apprenticeship/register context for John Gilman.
- West Dereham/Crimplesham register source reference and exact Wadcocke/Garney date.

## Topic: Passenger lists and migration streams

### Passenger-list negative

The supplied passenger research file and repo companion together support this:

- No John Gurney/Gourney/Gurny/Gurnie was found in the Bevis, Diligent, Mary Anne, Banks, Drake, or Hotten checks captured in this thread.
- John Gedney of Norwich on the Mary Anne is a low-probability lookalike only. Gedney is a real surname, the family structure is explicit, and the destination is Salem rather than Weymouth/Braintree.
- Hotten contains no John Gurney variant in the relevant New England lists; `Richard Gummy` on the America is Virginia-bound and not a New England Gurney.

### Positive migration-context finding

The absence from surviving named lists does not disprove immigration in the 1638-1641 period. Named passenger lists are incomplete, and the 1639-mid-1641 Boston-area evidence is especially poor for passenger names.

The useful positive signal is geographic and social:

- The Diligent/Hingham stream is the closest known Norfolk-to-south-shore-Massachusetts stream to John's June 1641 Weymouth appearance.
- Hingham, Massachusetts is under ten miles from Weymouth/Braintree.
- The Diligent tradition includes Norfolk Hingham, Great Ellingham, Attleborough, Wymondham, Lynn, and other adjacent Norfolk places.
- The Cushing/Drake Diligent list names household heads and some servants, but many wives, children, and servants are unnamed.

### Working scenario order

1. **Most likely:** John traveled on a different ship in the same broad East Anglia/Norfolk-to-Boston/Hingham/Weymouth migration corridor.
2. **Possible but weaker:** John was hidden as an unnamed dependent/servant in a Cushing-style settler tradition; this is less likely for a married adult with children.
3. **Also compatible:** John arrived through London/Essex/Norfolk connections and was locally received through Weymouth men rather than through the Hingham settler list itself.

## Topic: First Massachusetts associates

### 1641 gunpowder fines

The first colonial association remains the 2 June 1641 General Court fine-remission matter involving John Gurney, James Ludden, and Porter.

Evidence state:

- Massachusetts Bay Records and Porter genealogy report John Gurney, James Ludden, and **John Porter**.
- A later military-calendar description in the repo reportedly has **Richard Porter**.
- Porter genealogy adds that Ludden and Gurney were of Weymouth.

Research impact:

- The Porter identity is a first-order problem.
- Richard Porter would point toward the Weymouth/Hull-company settlement stream.
- John Porter would point more toward Hingham/Salem or administrative/militia context.

### James Ludden

`History_of_Weymouth_Massachusetts.txt` materially sharpened Ludden:

- born near 1611;
- died at Weymouth 7 February 1692/3;
- called an old planter;
- linked by the town history to Winthrop's 31 October 1632 Wessaguscus return story, where `one Luddam` served as guide at Luddam's Ford;
- named with John Gurney in the 2 June 1641 gunpowder-fines remission.

Research impact:

- Ludden now looks less likely to have arrived with John in a late 1638-1641 passenger group.
- He is more likely to represent John's local reception/grounding in an already-established Weymouth/Wessaguscus network.
- Ludden remains the highest-value same-event associate, but the question changes from "same ship?" to "how did John attach to this Weymouth old-planter network?"

### Richard Porter

The same Weymouth text records Richard Porter as part of Rev. Joseph Hull's company, sailing from Weymouth, Dorset, on or near 20 March 1634/5 and sitting down at Wessaguscus on 8 July 1635.

Research impact:

- If the 1641 petition really names Richard Porter, John's first-ring associate set has a strong Weymouth-stream comparator.
- If it names John Porter, Richard Porter should not be forced into the event.

## Topic: 1643 John Gurnell freeman entry

### User-supplied source

Google Books URL:

`https://www.google.com/books/edition/List_of_Freemen_Massachusetts_Bay_Colony/C24jAQAAMAAJ?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=%22Massachusetts+bay+colony%22&pg=PA5&printsec=frontcover`

Source:

- H. Franklin Andrews, *List of Freemen, Massachusetts Bay Colony from 1630-1691, with Freeman's Oath, the First Paper Printed in New England*.

Entries supplied by user:

- `Gurnell John May 10 1643`
- `Gurney Richd Oct 12 1681 Weymouth`

### Analysis

`Gurnell` is not an arbitrary stretch. The repo corpus for Norwich/Gurney material documents Gurnell as a real Gurney-adjacent or Gurney-variant form in Norfolk. Examples in `sources/corpus/norfolk-antiquarian-gurneys-of-norwich.md` include John Gurnell as a Norwich freeman/cordwainer and several passages where Gurney and Gurnell forms interchange.

The Massachusetts 1643 entry is therefore a plausible variant hit for John Gurney (G13), and it would fit the known Massachusetts chronology:

- John is already visible in Massachusetts Bay court records by June 1641.
- A freeman admission on 10 May 1643 would place him as an accepted church/civic participant rather than a transient.
- It would add a new colonial spelling variant, `Gurnell`, to be carried in Massachusetts searches.

Limits:

- Andrews is a compiled list, not the original colony record.
- The entry does not supply residence in the user-copied line.
- It does not identify family, origin, ship, or English parish.
- It does not by itself move John's arrival earlier than the already-secure June 1641 appearance.

Next check:

- Locate the underlying Massachusetts Bay Colony Records entry for 10 May 1643 and read the original spelling/context.
- Search early Massachusetts records for any other Gurnell/Gurnall/Gurnill surname bearers before treating this as uniquely John Gurney.

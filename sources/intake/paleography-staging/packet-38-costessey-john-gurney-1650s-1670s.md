Direct-line relevance: G13 emigrant-case candidate/comparator family; the documented 1648 Rippon settlement names "John Gurne the father," wife Margaret, and son John — so a junior John Gurney should be traceable in the 1650s–70s courts.

## Source

Manorial court rolls, 1540–1900 (Costessey + bundled neighbouring manors), FamilySearch film/DGS **004389191**. Secretary/court hand, English with Latin formulae.

## The question

Companion open question 2: **identify the John Gurney "junior"** implied by the 1659 "senior" style, and pin the relation of the 1670s "John Gurney, gentleman" to the 1629/1659/1648 men. These card-level entries were never image-read; the transcripts are Jernegan-suspect (see caution). The aim is to confirm which Gurne/Gurney names are real, capture any descriptor (senior/junior/gentleman/occupation), and date the courts from their openings.

## Questions for the reader

1. For each target entry, confirm the **Gurne/Gurney name** from the image (not the transcript) and capture any **descriptor** — senior, junior, gentleman, occupation, or "buried/dwelling." Especially: is a **John Gurney junior** named distinctly from a senior on any leaf?
2. From the context leaves, read each **court opening** — date and the manor/lord named — to fix the year of each entry and confirm whether it is Costessey (Jernegan) or a bundled neighbouring manor.
3. Note the 1674 "Church House … John Gurnee" entry's verb: is it a **burial**, a **dwelling/abuttal**, or a rent? Capture any wife/heir named with him, and any abuttal naming Gurne/Gurney land.

## Reading caution

The manor's lords were the **Jernegan (Jerningham)** baronets; machine transcripts systematically misread *Jernegan* as a Gurney-form (the 1634 "terra Johis Gurny" lead was exactly this). Confirm every Gurne/Gurney against the letterforms; flag any mangled *Jernegan*. Some target leaves (esp. 849, 1246) sit far from the others in the film's binding — the court book is not strictly chronological.

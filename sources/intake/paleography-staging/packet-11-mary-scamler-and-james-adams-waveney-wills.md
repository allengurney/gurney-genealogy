Date of this report: 2026-06-11

## Scope and status

This report covers the packet identified in the staging README as Mary Scamler of Norwich, widow, images 199, 200, 201, and 202. The major finding is that the packet appears to combine two adjacent wills in the register:

1. The will of Mary Scamler/Scambler of the city of Norwich, widow, begins on image 199, continues on image 200, and ends on image 201 left. Its probate is at the top of image 201 right.
2. The will of James Adams of Bungay, Suffolk, merchant, begins on image 201 right and continues on image 202. The clauses naming Sarah Gurney, John Gurney, Thomas Gurney, Lyon Gurney, Mary Farrow, Woolnough children, Richard Nelson, and Barsham/Shipmeadow/Mettingham property belong to James Adams's will, not to Mary Scambler's will.

That page-boundary correction is the most important conclusion from this packet. The staging README's questions are therefore answered below with this distinction in mind.

## Part I: Mary Scamler/Scambler of Norwich, widow

### Image 199, right page: opening

In the Name of God Amen. The two and twentieth day of January in the yeare of our Lord one thousand six hundred eighty eight, and in the fourth yeare of the raigne of our Soveraigne Lord James the Second, by the Grace of God of England, Scotland, France and Ireland King, Defender of the Faith &c., I Mary Scamler [or Scambler] of the Citty of Norwich, widdow, being weake in body but of good and perfect mind and memory, praised be Almighty God for the same, doe make and ordeyne this my last will and testament in manner and forme following.

First and principally I commend my soule into the hands of Almighty God my creator, hoping through the meritts, death, and passion of Jesus Christ my only Saviour and Redeemer to have full and free pardon and forgiveness of all my sinnes and to inherit everlasting life.

And my body I commit to the earth to be decently buried in Christian buriall in the church of Eaton, neare the body of my late deare husband deceased, at the discretion of my executor hereafter named.

And as touching the disposition of such temporall estate as it hath pleased Almighty God to bestow upon me, I give and dispose thereof as followeth.

### Image 199 lower / image 200 left: poor bequests and Mary Smith

Item, I give and bequeath unto the poore people of the parish wherein I now dwell [the parish name is not fully secure in my reading; likely a Norwich parish] the summe of fifty shillings, to be distributed by the churchwardens and overseers of the poore there within convenient tyme after my decease.

Item, I give and bequeath unto the poore people of the parish of Eaton, where I desire to be buried, the like summe of fifty shillings, to be distributed by the churchwardens and overseers of the poore there.

Item, I give and bequeath unto Mary Smith, my grandchild, daughter of Nathaniel Smith and Elizabeth his wife, the summe of one hundred pounds of lawfull money of England. The said summe is to be paid unto her at her age of eighteene yeares or day of marriage, which shall first happen.

The said one hundred pounds appears to be money in the hands of Richard Nelson, assigned and set over for the said Mary [the exact legal phrasing is difficult, but Richard Nelson is the holder/assignee named in this clause].

And my will is that my executor shall place or continue the said one hundred pounds, and the interest or improvement thereof, for the benefit of the said Mary Smith, until her age of eighteen years or marriage.

And if the said Mary Smith shall happen to die before her said age of eighteene yeares or marriage, then my will is that the said one hundred pounds, or such part thereof as shall remain, shall goe to John Smith my grandchild [the following contingency language is partly obscured], to be paid at his age of eighteene yeares, or otherwise as in this my will is appointed.

### Image 200 left/right: sons Robert, Thomas, and Joseph

Item, whereas my sonnes Robert and Thomas are both beyond the seas, and I know not whether either of them shall ever returne into England, my will is that if my said sonne Robert, or my said sonne Thomas, or either of them, shall returne into England within seven yeares next after my decease, then my executor shall pay unto such of them as shall so returne the summe of fifty pounds apiece.

And my will is that the said fifty pounds shall be for their necessary occasions and maintenance, and not to be taken or applied in satisfaction of any debts that they or either of them shall owe.

Item, I give and bequeath unto Joseph my sonne [the surname is not repeated clearly, presumably Scamler/Scambler] the summe of fifty pounds, or such part thereof as my executor shall think fit, toward the purchasing of an office, place, or employment for him. Until such purchase or provision be made, my executor is to pay unto him yearly the summe of six pounds [or another annual support sum; `six pounds` is my best reading] for his maintenance.

This Joseph clause is among the weaker readings in the Mary will. The sense is clearer than the exact wording: Mary is trying to provide support or an office/purchase arrangement for her son Joseph, with the executor managing the timing and terms.

### Image 200 right / image 201 left: daughter Elizabeth Smith and remainder

Item, I give and bequeath unto Elizabeth my daughter, wife of Nathaniel Smith, the summe of fifty pounds of lawfull money of England. The same is to be paid within three months next after the death of the said Nathaniel Smith, if my said daughter Elizabeth shall be then living.

And until that tyme my will is that my executor shall pay unto my said daughter Elizabeth the summe of three pounds by the yeare, being the interest or improvement of the said fifty pounds, for her owne separate use.

And if my said daughter Elizabeth shall die before the said Nathaniel Smith, then my will is that the said fifty pounds shall be paid to the said Nathaniel Smith, in trust for the children of my said daughter Elizabeth, to be divided amongst them as they shall respectively accomplish the age of one and twenty yeares or be married.

Item, [remaining household/personal clauses partly uncertain]. I give the residue of my goods, chattells, debts, and personal estate, after my debts, legacies, and funeral expenses discharged, unto my executor hereafter named.

In witness whereof I the said Mary Scamler [or Scambler] have hereunto set my hand and seale the day and yeare first above written.

## Answers for Mary Scamler/Scambler

### Surname and late husband

The will opening reads "Mary Scamler" to my eye, but the name may be the same family later normalized as Scambler. I would index the document as `Mary Scambler (also Scamler)`.

She describes herself as widow and asks to be buried in Eaton church near "my late deare husband deceased." I did not see the husband's given name in the will.

### Sarah Gurney relationship

Sarah Gurney is not named in Mary Scamler/Scambler's will. The Sarah Gurney language belongs to the adjacent will of James Adams of Bungay, beginning on image 201 right.

### Richard Nelson relationship

In Mary's will, Richard Nelson appears in connection with the GBP 100 legacy for Mary Smith, Mary's grandchild. The money appears to be in Richard Nelson's hands or assigned through him. Mary does not call Richard Nelson her son-in-law in the portion of Mary's will I read.

In the adjacent James Adams will, Richard Nelson is explicitly James Adams's son-in-law and is married to Margaret.

### Woolnough relationship

No Woolnough/Wolnough legatees appear in Mary's will in the pages I read. The Woolnough children appear in the adjacent James Adams will.

### Barsham / Shipmeadow property

No Barsham or Shipmeadow property appears in Mary's will in the pages I read. The Barsham, Shipmeadow, and Mettingham property clauses belong to the adjacent James Adams will.

### Executor, witnesses, probate

Mary's executor is John Scamler/Scambler of Edwick in Wiltshire, clerk.

Witnesses are Humphrey [surname uncertain], John Burton, and Thomas/Tho: Galston.

The will was proved at Norwich on 18 December in a year I could not read securely, probably in the 1690s, by oath of John Scamler/Scambler, executor.

## Part II: Adjacent will of James Adams of Bungay, merchant

### Image 201, right page: opening and principal land devise

In the Name of God Amen. The one and thirtieth day of January in the fourth yeare of the raigne of our Soveraigne Lord and Lady William and Mary, King and Queen of England &c., Anno Domini 1692, I James Adams of Bungay in the County of Suff[olk], merchant, being of perfect mind and memory, praised be God, doe make and ordaine this my last will and testament in manner and forme following.

First I commend my soule into the hands of Almighty God, hoping through the meritts of Jesus Christ to receive remission of my sinnes and everlasting life; and my body to Christian buriall at the discretion of my executor hereafter named.

Item, I give and bequeath unto my sonne-in-law Richard Nelson and Margaret his wife [my daughter, implied by later clauses], and to the heirs of her body lawfully begotten, my messuages, lands, tenements, and hereditaments in Barsham, Shipmeadow, and Mettingham [or in/near those places], now or late in the occupation of John Deveson and John Cole [both surnames best effort].

For want of such issue of the body of the said Margaret, then I give the same unto John Adams, sonne of my brother Thomas Adams, and to his heirs forever.

I nominate and appoint my said sonne-in-law Richard Nelson to receive the rents, issues, and profits of all my said lands and tenements in Barsham, Shipmeadow, and Mettingham for three and twenty yeares, beginning after the first two yeares next after my decease [the duration and start point are best read this way], and out of the same to pay the severall legacies hereafter mentioned.

Item, I give unto James Wolnough's [or Woolnough's] children five pounds apiece. ## Answers for the James Adams / Gurney-relevant material

### Sarah Gurney relationship

The relationship language is in James Adams's will:

`my sister Sarah Gurney`

and:

`John Gurney, Thomas Gurney, Lyon Gurney, and Mary Farrow, my sister Sarah Gurney's children`.

This does not belong to Mary Scamler/Scambler. It identifies Sarah Gurney as James Adams's sister in the language of the will. Whether "sister" is literal blood sister or sister-in-law usage is not resolved by the will alone, but the wording is strong evidence that Sarah Gurney belongs to James Adams's close family network.

### Gurney children named

The Gurney/Farrow children of Sarah Gurney named in James Adams's will are:

- John Gurney
- Thomas Gurney
- Lyon Gurney
- Mary Farrow

Each receives five pounds.

### Richard Nelson relationship

Richard Nelson is James Adams's son-in-law and the husband of Margaret. James Adams makes Richard Nelson his sole executor and gives him a rent-receiving/trust role over the Barsham, Shipmeadow, and Mettingham property.

### Woolnough/Wolnough relationship

James Adams gives five pounds apiece to James Woolnough's/Wolnough's children. The relationship label is not explicit in the portion I read. Given their placement among close kin legacies, they are likely kin, but the will language in the staged images should not be stretched beyond "James Woolnough's children" unless another clause or outside evidence identifies the relationship.

### Barsham / Shipmeadow / Mettingham property

The property clauses are in James Adams's will. They concern lands, tenements, or hereditaments in Barsham, Shipmeadow, and Mettingham, occupied by John Deveson and John Cole, devised to Richard Nelson and Margaret his wife and the heirs of Margaret's body, with remainder to John Adams, son of James's brother Thomas Adams.

Richard Nelson is also appointed to receive rents from the same estate for a term, apparently to satisfy legacies and family provisions.

### Executor and probate

For James Adams, the executor is Richard Nelson, son-in-law. I did not find a secure James Adams probate clause in the staged images; the packet likely stops before the end/probate or the ending is beyond the crops I had.

## Research observations

1. The packet should not be cataloged as a single Mary Scamler/Scambler document for Gurney purposes. The Gurney evidence comes from James Adams of Bungay.

2. Mary Scamler/Scambler is still genealogically interesting, but her will centers on Smith, Scamler/Scambler, and overseas sons Robert and Thomas. Her only clear appearance of Richard Nelson is financial/assignment-related for Mary Smith's legacy.

3. James Adams's will is highly valuable for a Waveney-area kinship map. It links Adams, Gurney, Farrow, Nelson, Baker, and Woolnough/Wolnough families, and it anchors the network in Bungay plus Barsham/Shipmeadow/Mettingham property.

4. The phrase "my sister Sarah Gurney" plus the named Gurney children is the key extract. It should be cited to James Adams's will, not Mary Scamler/Scambler's.

5. Mary Farrow is grouped with Sarah Gurney's children, so she is likely a daughter of Sarah Gurney who married a Farrow before James Adams's will. The will itself names her married surname but does not name her husband.

6. Lyon Gurney is especially distinctive and may be a useful identity anchor when correlating with parish records, wills, or property records in the Waveney area.

7. The Barsham/Shipmeadow/Mettingham estate should be treated as James Adams property subject to Nelson/Margaret life or issue interests and Adams-family remainder, not as a Mary Scambler estate.

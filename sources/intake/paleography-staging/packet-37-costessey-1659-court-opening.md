## Source

Manorial court rolls, 1540–1900 (Costessey and bundled neighbouring manors), FamilySearch film/DGS **004389191**. Secretary/court hand, English with Latin formulae. The film carries court material well beyond its 1633–35 catalogue label.

## The question

The companion's open question 3: **which manor held the court of "Anthony Dobbs of Marsham, esquire" recorded in the April 1659 default memorandum?** The memorandum names, among suitors/tenants, "…Richard Knight, John [Burbis?], **John Gurney sen**[ior]…" with Thomasine Rookwood and others. The machine transcript renders the court *heading* as Latin/secretary-hand salad — only the place token "in Burton aforesaid" survived — so the manor name needs an **image read** of the court opening, not full-text. Blomefield rules out Marsham manor itself (it descended Boleyn → Hobart → Freeman → Paston, no Dobbs as lord), so Anthony Dobbs of Marsham was a Marsham *resident* holding a manor elsewhere; if this court is not Costessey (Jernegan lords), the 1659 "John Gurney senior" may belong to a neighbouring manor bundled on this film.

The "senior" style implies a **junior John Gurney** alive in 1659 — corroborating the father–son pair documented in the 1648 Rippon surrender (image-read packet 13). Fixing the manor places that John Gurney household.

## Questions for the reader

1. **What is the manor name in the April 1659 court heading?** Read the opening formula ("The court [leet/baron] of … held at … for Anthony Dobbs of Marsham, esquire, the … day of April 1659"). Is it Costessey, or a different (bundled) manor? Confirm or correct the "in Burton aforesaid" place token.
2. Transcribe the suitor/tenant list around "**John Gurney sen[ior]**" — confirm the "senior" style and capture any descriptor (status, occupation, residence) attached to John, and the names flanking him.
3. Note any second Gurne/Gurney name on these leaves (a "junior" John, or a Margaret/William Gurne) and any abuttal naming Gurne/Gurney land.

## Reading caution

Costessey's lords were the **Jernegan (Jerningham)** baronets; the machine transcripts systematically misread *Jernegan* as a Gurney-form (the original 1634 "terra Johis Gurny" lead was exactly this false positive). Do not promote any Gurney name from these leaves without confirming the letterforms against the image; flag any *Jernegan* that the transcript may have mangled.

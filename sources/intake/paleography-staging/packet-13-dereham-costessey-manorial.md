## 13e: Costessey John Gurne / Thomas Rippon surrender

This is the strongest image in Packet 13. It names Thomas Rippon's surrender and then places John Gurne, Margaret his wife, and John Gurne their son in the same admission sequence.

Best-effort extract:

> ... upon the pretension made toward the east with said piece of land the said John Gurne hath to him and his heirs the surrender of Thomas Rippon as at a Court holden for this manor the eleventh day of October in the year of our Lord one thousand six hundred forty eight appeareth ...

Then:

> ... permitting the said premises so surrendered as aforesaid to the aforesaid John Gurne & Margaret his wife for term of their natural lives & the longer liver of them and the remaind[er] thereof to John Gurne their sonne his heirs & assigns forever ...

And later:

> ... to the said John Gurne the father & Margaret wife for enjoying the tenure ...

The exact land description remains only partly read. The useful family-structure result is clearer than the property detail: John Gurne and Margaret are husband and wife; another John Gurne is explicitly their son; the elder John is called `the father`.

## 13e answers

1. Confirmed: surrender from Thomas Rippon to John Gurne.
2. Court date in the recital: 11 October 1648.
3. Confirmed: John Gurne and Margaret his wife hold for their natural lives / longer liver.
4. Confirmed: remainder to John Gurne their son, his heirs and assigns.
5. Confirmed: elder John is described as `John Gurne the father`.
6. Not recovered: full land description or any monetary consideration.

## Updated-process and efficiency feedback

What worked:

- The grid-first approach kept the first pass efficient across five images.
- A one-band-sheet-per-image pattern was enough to sort the packet into strong, weak, and unresolved images before deeper work.
- Manual `line-strips --line-count` was valuable for 13c and 13e, where the target phrases were already localized.
- The Costessey images benefited more from strips than from full enhancement sheets.

What wasted time:

- 13a consumed extra visual attention without yielding a secure result. The lesson is to stop after one broad band plus one targeted strip when the image quality blocks the key name.
- 13d's machine hint was not supported by the readable crop zones. A packet-level "false-positive likely" stop rule would save time.
- Contact sheets are still numerous even with a lean crop budget; report-writing should cite selected sheets only.

Where the tool or skill was unclear:

- The workflow still does not distinguish "readable but hard" from "not worth further local cropping" early enough.
- It would help if `manifest-summary` could emit only contact sheets plus selected line-strip sheets.

Future optimization opportunities:

- Add a packet worksheet template that records per-image status: `confirmed`, `probable`, `false-positive-likely`, or `blocked by image quality`.
- Add a crop-budget norm for larger packets: grid all images; band-ladder all promising images; line-strip only the two or three images with promotable evidence.
- Add optional image-quality notes to manifests, so the report can say why an image was stopped without re-opening every crop.

Recognition-note decision:

No durable recognition note was added. Packet 13 reinforces a workflow lesson rather than a reusable letterform lesson: Costessey copyhold pages can preserve family structure in formulaic surrender/admission language, while the East Dereham dense court-book pages may need specialist treatment before promotion.

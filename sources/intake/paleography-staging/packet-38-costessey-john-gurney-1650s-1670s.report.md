# Packet 38 report - Costessey court rolls: John Gurney sweep, 1650s-1670s

Analysis date: 2026-06-24. Images read from the staged masters under `sources/intake/paleography-staging/images/packet-38-costessey-john-gurney-1650s-1670s/`.

## Summary

The staged set confirms several real `John Gurne` / `Johannes Gurne` occurrences at Costessey in the 1651-1674 window, but it does **not** find a distinct `John Gurney junior`. The strongest result is that the 1670s John appears with gentleman status in property-boundary language: `Johannis Gurne gen[erosi]` in the 1674 Church House entry and a similar cottage-abuttal phrase on the 21 January 1674 page.

The 1620/1641 Spencer target does **not** show a Gurney name; it appears to be a Spencer entry where the card/OCR over-associated the neighboring `John` with a Gurney-form. The staged `img-2020` target also does not show the carded 1674 Church House entry: the useful 1674 Church House material is visible on the adjacent context image `img-2019-ctx.jpg`, while `img-2020` itself opens on a 3 October 1633 page.

No wife, heir, or explicit father/son relation for John Gurne is recovered from this packet. No `senior` or `junior` descriptor appears in the staged entries.

### Image 1996 - `3:1:S3HT-6PNK-4C`

**Result:** real `John Gurne`, as a property-boundary/status reference; no junior.

The relevant right-page entry belongs to the 21 January 1674 court sequence. In the property description, the line reads approximately:

> `inter Cotagium Johannis Gurne gen[erosi] ...`

That is, the premises are described by reference to the cottage of **John Gurne, gentleman**. The following language includes an abuttal toward the east. This is a boundary/status reference, not evidence of a distinct junior John.

I do not see a wife or heir named with John in this target block. The visible `gen` abbreviation is the same practical status datum as the previously known 1670s `John Gurney, gentleman` reading.

## Answer to packet questions

1. **Name and descriptors:** real `John Gurne` / `Johannes Gurne` forms are confirmed in 1651, 1672, 1673, and 1674. The only descriptor recovered is **gentleman** (`gen[erosi]`) in 1674 property-boundary language. No `junior` is named.
2. **Court openings:** the useful targets are Costessey court material. Dates read from the staged set are 5 March 1651, 22 October 1672, 20 September 1673, and 21 January 1674. The staged `img-2020` and the `0848/0850` context pair show 1633 material and should not be used as 1670s context without correction.
3. **Church House verb/question:** the 1674 Church House material is not a burial. It is a property/copyhold description. John Gurne is a neighboring-property or cottage reference, styled gentleman; no wife/heir is named with him in the read portion.

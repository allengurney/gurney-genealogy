# Packet 20 paleography report: John Grobbe of Redlingfield will, 1632

Date of report: 2026-06-18

## Headline result

The testator is **John Grobbe [Grubbe] of Redlingfield, Suffolk, yeoman**, not a Gurney. The will is dated **20 July 1632**. It names his wife **Grissell** and four men called his sons: **Clement Grobbe**, **John Rowles**, **Thomas Rowles**, and **Henry Rowles**.

The two Clements in the bond clause are therefore **different people**:

- **Clement Grobbe**, the testator's son and legatee.
- **Clement Gurney, gent**, the debtor bound to John Grobbe by bonds dated 21 November 1622.

The clause does not give Clement Gurney a residence or further style beyond `gent`.

## Best-effort transcription

Spelling is retained where legible. Editorial expansions and unresolved readings appear in square brackets. Repeated legal boilerplate is compressed only where the image does not support a safe word-for-word reading.

### Image 363: opening

> In the name of God Amen. The twentieth daie of July in the yeare of the raigne of our Sovereigne Lord Charles by the grace of God of England, Scotland, Fraunce and Ireland King, defender of the faith &c, the eighth, Anno Domini 1632. I John Grobbe of Redlingfeild in the Countie of Suff[olk], yeoman, and within the diocese of Norwich, being whole of mynde and of good and perfect memorie, thankes be to Almightie God, doe nowe revoke and declare by this my present writing my last will and testament as hereafter followeth.
>
> First and before all things I commend my soule into the mercifull hand of Almightie God, my Creator, verily trusting and believing to be justified, saved and redeemed only by and through the most precious merit, death and passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, my alone Saviour, Mediator and Redeemer.

The regnal word reads as **eighth**, consistent with 20 July 1632. The finding-aid reading `seventh` would be chronologically inconsistent.

### Image 364: body and family bequests

> And my body [I commit] to the earth from whence it came, there to rest untill the daie of the Lord's second appearing, in hope then of a joyfull resurrection unto eternall life.
>
> Item I give and bequeath unto Clement Grobbe and John Rowles my sonnes, to either of them the summe of fiftie pounds of lawfull money of England, to be paid to the said Clement and John my sonnes, their and either of their executors or assignes, within three monethes next after the decease of Grissell my welbeloved wife, or at the end of their apprenticeship, which of these times shall last happen.
>
> Item I give and bequeath unto Thomas Rowles and Henry Rowles, two other of my sonnes, to either of them the summe of fiftie pounds of like lawfull money, to be paid to them and either of them, their and either of their executors or assignes, within three monethes next after the decease of the said Grissell my wife, or at the end of their apprenticeship, which of these times shall last happen.

The following conditional provisions are less completely legible, but their sense is clear:

> Provided alwaies that if the said Grissell my wife shall depart this present life before the said Clement and John my sonnes shall be out of their apprenticeships, [Thomas and Henry] my sonnes shall pay their severall portions as appointed; and if either of the four sons die before payment, his legacy is to pass to the survivors in the shares directed by the will.

The image repeatedly treats Clement Grobbe, John Rowles, Thomas Rowles, and Henry Rowles as `my sonne` or `my sonnes`. It does not explain why two bear the testator's surname and two bear Rowles; step-relationship, adoption, or another family arrangement should not be inferred from this image alone.

### Image 365: bond, residue, execution, and probate

> Any thing before herein to the contrary thereof in anywise notwithstanding. Item I give and bequeath unto Clement my sonne two bonds or writings obligatorie bearing date the one and twenty daie of November Anno Domini 1622, conteyning the generall summe of fortie pounds of lawfull money of England, wherein Clement Gurney gent standeth bounden unto me for the payment of the summe of twenty pounds of like lawfull money at a certaine daie, houre and place.

The succeeding condition repeatedly distinguishes `the same Clement Gurney` from `the said Clement my sonne`. The bonds are to be delivered or assigned for the son's benefit subject to the stated conditions.

> Item I give and bequeath unto Grissell my wife all my goods and chattels, moveable and unmoveable, [with household and residue provisions], she paying my debts and legacies and performing this my last will.
>
> And my will is that this is my last will and testament. I have hereunto set my hand, dated the daie and yeare first above written.
>
> John Grobbe.

The witness line is only partly secure. It includes `Henry [Abroth?]`; the other witness names are unresolved and should not be used without a fresh image check.

The probate clause records proof at Norwich in **July 1632**, apparently on **27 July 1632** (medium confidence), and grants administration to the named executors. The executor names in the Latin are not sufficiently clear in this pass for a diplomatic transcription.

### 20c: one Clement or two

**Two different Clements.** John Grobbe bequeaths the bonds to **Clement Grobbe, his son**. The obligor named inside those bonds is **Clement Gurney, gent**. The clause's grammar distinguishes `the same Clement Gurney` from `the said Clement my sonne`, and the opening establishes the testator's surname as Grobbe rather than Gurney.

No residence or additional identification is given for Clement Gurney. The image supports only:

- name: Clement Gurney
- style: gentleman
- debtor/obligor to John Grobbe
- bond date: 21 November 1622
- penal sum: £40
- secured/payment sum: £20

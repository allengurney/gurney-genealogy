## 16a transcription: Clement Gurney gent bond

The captured spread is a continuation/ending page, not the will opening. I could not identify the testator or parish from this single image.

Best-effort extract of the Gurney-bearing clause:

> Any thinge before herein to the contrary thereof in anywise notwithstanding. Item I give and bequeath unto Clement my sonne two bonds or writing obligatory bearing date the one and twenty daie of November Anno Domini 1622, conteyning the generall somme of fortie pounds of lawfull money of England wherein Clement Gurney gent stands bounden unto me for the payment of the sum of twenty pounds of like lawfull money at a certaine daye hour [and] place ...

Later in the same clause:

> ... the same Clement Gurney shall be delivered ... upon condition that hee the said Clement my sonne ...

The reading `Clement Gurney gent` is high confidence. I do not see a residence for Clement in the visible page. The clause appears to be a bequest or release involving bonds in which Clement Gurney, gent, was bound for 40 pounds to secure 20 pounds. Because the will opening is absent, the relationship between the testator and Clement should not be promoted beyond the page-supported wording `Clement my sonne` plus `Clement Gurney gent` in the same clause.

## 16a answers

1. Confirmed: `Clement Gurney gent`.
2. Confirmed: bond dated 21 November 1622, penal sum 40 pounds, conditioned on 20 pounds.
3. Not recovered: Clement's residence.
4. Not recovered: testator identity or parish. The image is not the first page of the will.

## 16b transcription: John Gurney's house near Langley / Carpsham

The Gurney-bearing clause is readable and appears more specific than the machine summary.

Best-effort extract:

> One acre of meadowe, lyeing in Langley meadowe, holden of [Bruyard Oake?]; one halfe acre lyeing in ffritt meadowe; one acre of meadowe lyeing by Carpsham next unto John Gurneys house; and one acre in Carpsham abutting upon the said acre. To have and to hold all and singular the said lands and tenements unto my said sonne Robt Aggas [?] his heires and assignes for ever by and after the decease of Maryon my wife and not before.

The later beneficiary language repeats `Robt Aggas [?] my sonne` and names `Maryon my wife`. I read the surname of the son as `Aggas` with uncertainty; it may explain why the machine snippet suggested `Pagan`, but I would not promote either surname without a fresh check.

The right page has the witness/probate area, but the will opening is not present in this image. I therefore could not securely identify the testator's name or parish from the captured spread.

## 16b answers

1. Confirmed: `John Gurneys house` as a boundary marker.
2. Confirmed: the house is tied to meadowland in or near Langley and Carpsham.
3. Confirmed nearby family context: wife `Maryon`; son probably `Robt Aggas [?]`.
4. Not recovered: testator name and parish, because the visible page is a continuation/ending, not the will opening.
5. Not found: further direct Gurney kinship detail; John Gurney is used as a property/boundary reference.

## 16c transcription: Robert Webber of Great Ellingham charity clause

This appears to be an OCR or machine-snippet false positive for Gurney.

Opening, best effort:

> In the name of God Amen. The twenty day of ffebruary in the yeare of the raigne of our most gratious soveraigne Lord Charles by the grace of God of England Scotland France and Ireland King defender of the faith &c, the fifth, Anno Domini 1629, I Robert Webber of great Ellingham in the County of Norfolk, yeoman, being whole of mind and of good and perfect memory ...

The charity clause includes repeated references to the churchwardens of Great Ellingham and the poor of the parish, but I do not see `Gurney`.

Relevant extract:

> ... unto the churchwardens of great Ellingham aforesaid ... to be paid by my executors or the survivor of them ...

The machine-suggested `[Gurnent?]` is best treated as misread surrounding text, probably from `churchwardens`, `executors`, or a related formula in the charity clause.

## 16c answers

1. Testator: Robert Webber of Great Ellingham, Norfolk, yeoman.
2. Date: 20 February 1629 in the fifth regnal year of Charles I, i.e. 20 February 1629/30.
3. Tie to Ellingham: testator is of Great Ellingham; the charity clause benefits church/parish poor there.
4. No genuine Gurney hit found in this image.

## Updated-process and efficiency feedback

What worked:

- The `.cmd` preflight wrapper avoided the earlier PowerShell execution-policy stumble.
- Starting with `info` and grids was fast and prevented blind recropping.
- `band-ladder` was the best primary tool for this mixed packet because each image had a different question-bearing zone.
- `--tile-max-width` kept contact sheets readable without enormous visual payloads.
- The manifest summary made it easy to preserve coordinates without pasting every generated file into this report.

What wasted time:

- `info` accepts only one image. My first bundled `info` attempt failed; multi-image `info` would save a step for packet bundles.
- The generated file list is still noisy when many ladders and strips are created. Reports should cite only selected useful sheets and rely on manifests for the rest.
- For 16a and 16b, the images lacked the will openings. Cropping could not solve that; future runs should check whether a packet question requires adjacent register pages before deep enhancement.

Where the tool or skill was unclear:

- `manifest-summary --selected-only` is less useful with freshly generated manifests unless boxes are explicitly marked selected; a simpler `--contact-sheets-only` or `--labels opening,bond,...` option would be clearer for reports.
- `line-strips --line-count` is useful, but the operator still needs to choose a generous vertical box. Otherwise strips can clip descenders or context.

Future optimization opportunities:

- Add multi-image `info` support.
- Add a packet-level run mode that accepts several images and emits one dimensions table plus grids.
- Add a manifest-summary filter for contact sheets and named labels.
- Add a pre-crop checklist prompt in the skill: if the question asks for will opening/testator and the current page is a continuation, fetch or flag adjacent-page need before making more ladders.
- Track an approximate "crop budget" per packet: coarse grids first, one band-ladder per image, then only one focused ladder or line-strip pass where it changes the answer.

Recognition-note decision:

No durable recognition note was added from Packet 16. The useful lesson here is process-oriented rather than letterform-specific: NCC register continuation pages can confirm a Gurney reference while failing to identify the testator, so adjacent-page needs should be recognized early. That belongs in workflow guidance more than in `recognition-notes.md`.

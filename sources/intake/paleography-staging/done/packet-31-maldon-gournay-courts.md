## Findings

### 1 December 1630: Francis Gournay, gentleman, served as a juror

The right-hand page is headed as a Maldon court baron with view of frankpledge. Its date line gives **1 December 1630**, in the sixth regnal year of Charles I.

In the sworn-juror list, the target name reads:

> Franciscus Gournay, gent.

The list groups him with other sworn men under a bracketed juror label. This is an office/attendance entry, not an admission, debt, or presentment against him.

The name and date are high-confidence readings. The exact expansion of every Latin heading word was not required to answer the packet question.

### The supposed 1636 entry is a 1676 court and names the younger Francis as bailiff

The second image's heading explicitly includes:

> 1676

The opening formula names William Wiseman, knight and baronet, and **Francis Gournay, gentleman**, as bailiffs of the borough. Best-effort extract:

> Coram Will[ielm]o Wyseman Milite et Baronetto et Francisco Gournay gent., Ballivis ...

This is the known younger Francis Gurney of Maldon, born 1628 and deceased 1677. It cannot place Francis G14 at Maldon in 1636.

## Answers to packet questions

1. **1630 identity and matter:** Francis Gournay, gentleman, appears among the sworn jurors at the Maldon court held 1 December 1630.
2. **1636 identity and matter:** there is no 1636 entry in the supplied image. It is a 1676 court naming Francis Gournay, gentleman, as a borough bailiff.
3. **Does the early entry point to G14?** The 1630 adult gentleman is chronologically consistent with Francis G14 and cannot be his son Francis, who was only about two years old. The image itself does not state parentage or London identity, so the identification with G14 is strongly favored but not proved solely by this entry.
4. **Continuity:** the two images do not establish one continuous holding from 1630 to 1676; they concern men of successive generations.

## Confidence notes

High confidence:

- `Franciscus Gournay gent.` in the 1630 juror list.
- Court date 1 December 1630.
- The second image's date is 1676, not 1636.
- Francis Gournay, gentleman, is named as a bailiff in the 1676 opening formula.

Medium-high confidence:

- The 1630 Francis is G14, based on adult status, chronology, and the Browning family's Maldon connection. The court entry supplies no explicit kinship identifier.

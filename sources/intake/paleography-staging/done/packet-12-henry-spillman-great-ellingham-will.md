### Image 379, right page: will

In dei nomine Amen. I Henry Spilman esquier, being in goode memorie and hole mynde, the xvij daie of February make my laste woll and testament in this maner.

Fyrst I bequeth my soule to God almightie, to our Lady Saynte Mary and to all the company of hevyn, and my body to be buried in the chirch yarde of Saynte James in Ellyngham.

To the which chirch also for my tithes negligently tythed I bequeth xxs.

Also to that chirch I bequeth xxs.

Also I woll have a prest to synge for me and my frendes the space of ij yere. To his wages I bequeth [sum not securely read; possibly to be paid by the executors].

Also I bequeth to Elizabeth my wyffe all my movable, that is, catell, plate, corne in the berne, and that that is sowynne this yere in the grounde.

Also I woll that my wyffe shall have my londes, maners, and all my ffreeholde londe the terme of her lyfe, save [or same] be yere that lyth in [Norffolk?; place wording partly lost/unclear], that to be solde to the perfourmyng of my will.

And after her decease my ffreeholde londe to be solde and disposed by myn executors to the pleasur of God and profyt to our soules.

Also I woll that Frances [Turrell? / Centrell?] shall have an hundred markes to hyr mariage.

Also I bequeth to Elizabeth Brampton, the doughter of William Brampton gentleman, xxs.

Also I gyve to Maude Pyott [or Pyott/Pyot] xxs.

Also I bequeth to Thomas [Wylde? / Wyllys?] xxs.

Also I bequeth to Henry Newman xxs.

Also I bequeth to Leonard [Pyott? / Pye?] xxs.

Also I woll that Jone [Swerd/Swyney?; surname very uncertain] shall have a mattres, a paire of shete, and a coverled.

Also I woll have by side me the daye of my burying and then to have [by/viij?] blake gownes. [Sense: black gowns are to be had/provided at the burial; the exact number is not secure.]

Also I bequeth to James Stykes a blake gowne.

Also I bequeth to [Rich/Richard] ffroske [or Froske/Froske] xxs [sum uncertain].

Also I bequeth to Adam Campion [or Campioner/Campyon] xxs [sum uncertain].

Also I bequeth to the prest of [Hingham? / Hemyngham?; place/name uncertain] xxs to synge for me or cause to be songen [xxx] masses.

Also I bequeth to Margaret Humbulshe [or Humbulbe/Humbilby; surname uncertain] the half of my goods not bequethed.

I put them to the disposition of myn executors, whom I ordeyne: my wyffe, [Sir?] Antony Gurney, and John Turrell.

And to every one of them for their labours I bequeth xxs and a blake gowne.

## Structured abstract

### Testator

Henry Spilman/Spillman, esquire.

The register marginal/name heading reads `Henry Spilman` or close variant. No residence line is visible in the opening itself beyond the burial/place clauses, but the will directs burial at St James in Ellingham, matching the staging identification as [Great] Ellingham.

### Date

The will date line visible in the English text reads:

`the xvij daie of February`

I do not see a written year in the will's English opening. Since the probate is April 1524 and the abstract lead says `D: 17 Feb - P: 1524`, this should be handled as 17 February 1523/4 or 1524 depending the calendaring convention used by the source. For modern genealogy notes, I would write:

`made 17 February 1523/4?; proved April 1524`

with a note that the will image itself gives the day and month but not a clear written year.

### Executors

The executor clause reads, in substance:

The core reading `Antony Gurney` is secure. The possible prefix before Antony may be `Sir` or a scribal/formulaic flourish; I would not rely on it without another expert re-check. I found no relationship term such as kinsman, cousin, servant, trustee, or `of [place]` attached to Antony Gurney in this clause.

John Turrell is named as co-executor. Frances [Turrell?] may be connected to him, but the surname is not secure enough to assert that in the database without corroboration.

### Probate

The probate clause is at Norwich before the official, in April 1524. The exact day of April is not secure from the damaged/dark top-of-page image. The clause commits administration to the executors named in the testament.

I would cite this as:

until a clearer image or register index supplies the exact day.

The only Gurney mention I found is Antony Gurney in the executor clause.

The will does not state why Antony Gurney is executor. It does not call him kinsman, neighbor, gentleman, esquire, or of any place in the legible text. Even without a stated relationship, his appointment as one of three executors alongside the widow and John Turrell is a strong personal-trust connection between Henry Spilman and Antony Gurney in the Ellingham orbit.

No other Spilman/Spelman family member is named in the portions I could read, apart from Henry himself and his wife Elizabeth.

## Research value

This will does confirm the important lead: Antony Gurney was appointed executor of Henry Spilman/Spillman of the Ellingham orbit in a will proved in 1524.

The document does not state the nature of the relationship, but executor appointment is stronger than a casual witness appearance. It places Antony in a trusted role within an Ellingham-associated gentry estate context roughly a decade before the later Great Ellingham inheritance context noted in the staging README.

The will is also useful for the local network around Henry Spilman: Brampton, Turrell, Newman, Pyott/Pyot, Golding, Stykes, Froske, Campion, and Margaret Humbulshe/Humbulbe all deserve cautious indexing as leads, with the caveat that several surnames need re-checking.

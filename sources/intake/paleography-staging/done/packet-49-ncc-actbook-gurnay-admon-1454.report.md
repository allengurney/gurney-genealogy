## Bottom line

The target entry is present on `ncc8076270-img261-actbook-fo19v-20r.jpg`, left page, second full entry. It confirms an administration act for **Thomas Gurnay, late of Great Ellingham Magna**, deceased intestate, with administration committed to **Alice, relict/widow of the deceased**.

The act is dated **5 August 1454**. The entry itself uses `anno domini supradicto`; the preceding folio run carries explicit 1454 dating, and the entries on `img259`-`img261` continue that same 1454 act-book sequence.

I did not find another secure Gurnay/Gournay/Gurney token in the supplied fols. 16v-20r sweep. The book gives no deceased status such as gentleman, husbandman, or clerk in the target entry, and I see no named sureties or inventory/account detail beyond the standard administration/bond formula.

## Target transcription

The target entry is crossed through with ordinary cancellation strokes, which obscure the middle of the act. Best working reading:

> Die quinto mensis Augusti Anno domini supradicto commissa fuit administracio bonorum Thome Gurnay nuper de Elyngham magna ab intestato decedentis Alicie relicte dicti defuncti et Thome A[p]pulton/Appulton de D[...], ad solvendum debita [...] in forma iuris [...]

Reading notes:

- `Thome Gurnay nuper de Elyngham magna` is secure.
- `Alicie relicte dicti defuncti` is secure enough to use: Alice is the widow/relict-administratrix.
- The second administrator appears to be **Thomas Appulton/Appelton**. I would not promote the surname without brackets; the crossing stroke passes through the surname and the middle letters are crowded.
- The place after the co-administrator is not secure. It may begin `D...`; I do not have enough image support to choose among Deopham/Depham, Debenham, or another D-place.
- The closing is formulaic administration language requiring payment of debts and legal return/accounting, but the exact words after `ad solvendum debita` are not safely readable from this crop.

## Genealogical bearing

The image confirms that a Thomas Gurnay of Great Ellingham was dead by 5 August 1454 and had a widow Alice. That date rules him out as the Thomas Gurnay/Gornay involved in the c. 1461 "clerk convict" Paston episode, unless that episode concerns a different Thomas.

## Decisive identity finding

ARK `3:1:9Q97-YSLK-24M` contains a Maldon borough meeting entry headed **January 1677, regnal year 29**. Damage and staining prevent a secure reading of the exact day.

The material relationship phrase is clear:

> John Gournay one of the freemen of this Borough ... Francis Gournay late of Maldon aforesaid **his father deceased**

This identifies the John in the entry as **Francis of Maldon's son**, not Francis's bachelor brother John who died in 1681. Confidence: **decisive (~99%)**.

## Entry summary

The entry says, in substance:

- John Gournay, a freeman of Maldon, had lately bought from the corporation goods formerly belonging to Francis Gournay of Maldon, his deceased father.
- John possessed and disposed of the goods.
- He was to secure or pay the corporation **£150**, with copyhold lands at Goldhanger involved as security.
- He failed or refused to provide the required payment/security or return the goods.
- The borough treated this as a breach of his freeman's oath and disfranchised him.

The amount reads £150 in the manuscript; the machine transcript's occasional £1,500 rendering is not accepted.

`3:1:9Q97-YSLK-2CC` is the adjacent/overlapping view of the same January 1677 matter, not a separate corporation-lot transaction. `3:1:9Q97-YSLK-24Q` supplies preceding context.

## False-positive target ARKs

The `YSL8` targets are consecutive pages of a later formal property conveyance/indenture:

- `3:1:9Q97-YSL8-SXM`
- `3:1:9Q97-YSL8-SXF`
- `3:1:9Q97-YSL8-SDZ`
- preceding leaf `3:1:9Q97-YSL8-S8Q`

They contain historical property references to Richard and Francis Gournay, with surname forms that attracted the full-text search, but they do **not** contain the target John-Gournay/Francis relationship or duplicates of the 1677 corporation-goods entry. They should not be used to identify either John.

## Effect on the Bernau problem

The court entry proves that Francis's son John was:

- alive in January 1677;
- a freeman of Maldon;
- handling goods and Goldhanger-connected security after his father's death.

It therefore advances the son/heir chronology and excludes the bachelor uncle from this transaction. It does **not** by itself prove that this John later became the Norwich Quaker banker. No Norwich, New England, Quaker, or Society of Friends reference was found on the relevant leaves.

## Bottom line

The target document is the will complex of Thomas Gurnay, armiger: testament on fo. 211v-212r, an `Ultima voluntas eiusdem Thome Gurnay` on fo. 212r-212v, and probate on fo. 212v. The manuscript supports the major round-6 finding: wife Margaret survives as executrix; son William is named under a conditional/good-conduct and payment structure; son Edmund is named in the ultima voluntas; the document ties Thomas to Harpley, West Barsham, Norwich, Depden/Depeden, Walsingham, St Gregory's Norwich, and Swathings/Hardingham.

This report is a paleographic verification and clause-level transcription aid, not a final critical Latin edition. Several crowded names and legal formulae still need a specialist pass before the full text is promoted to `sources/corpus_supplement/`.

## Dates

- Testament date: `xviij die mensis Marcij` - 18 March. The day is better read as 18, not 28. The Anno Domini clause appears as `M CCCC lxx`, and the regnal-year clause is Edward IV; the crop does not independently settle the regnal numeral as cleanly as the printed Blomefield citation does. Existing repo treatment of the will date as 18 March 1469/70 remains consistent with this image read.
- Ultima voluntas date: made at Norwich. The separate day/regnal clause is present but not securely resolved from the generated crops; do not promote a precise day from this pass.
- Probate date: `xxvij die mensis Julij Anno domini M CCCC lxxj` - 27 July 1471. This corrects the round-6 uncertainty over `xxvj(?)`; the final numeral reads best as `j`.

## People

- Testator: the rubric reads `Testamentum Thome Gurnay Armigeri`. I do not see `senior` in the manuscript rubric; the `senior` styling belongs to the catalogue/derived description, not to this visible rubric.
- Wife/executrix: Margaret is named repeatedly, including as William's mother and as executrix in the probate. The probate commits administration to Margaret as executrix.
- Son William: clear as `Willelmus filius meus`. His benefits are conditional on good conduct toward `Margaretam matrem suam` and on observing the instruments/charters involving Thomas and Margaret. Fo. 212v also carries security/payment language, including the larger `xl libras` and `xx marcas` figures.
- Son Edmund: the ultima voluntas names `Edmundo filio meo`, tied to charters concerning the Depden/Depeden property. This is image-supported and should be treated as a new son of Thomas and Margaret unless later context proves otherwise.
- Co-executor: John Jernegan/Jernyngham, armiger. The forename and armiger status are clear; the surname form is visibly in the Jernegan/Jernyngham family but should be checked once more before choosing one normalized spelling in a corpus transcription. Probate language appears to reserve/allow like commission to him as co-executor rather than showing a full separate administration already taken.
- Supervisor: John Heydon is the best read for the supervisor/overseer named in the testament; this agrees with the expected Baconsthorpe/Heydon circle, but the exact line should be rechecked in the final edition.
- Godson: `Thome [Colvyle?] filiolo meo quem de sacro fonte levavi` remains uncertain. The relationship "my godson, whom I raised from the sacred font" is clearer than the surname.
- Confessor: the line names `Johannem [...] ordinis minorum Norwici confessori meo`; the Norwich Franciscan identity is clear, but the personal surname/name after John is not secure.
- Servants/household legatees: Thomas Derby, Robert Stonford, and William Wolchyrche with his wife are supported by the image, though final spelling should be normalized only after a tighter crop review.

## Places and property

- Burial clause: Thomas directs burial in the chancel of St Lawrence, Harpley, if he dies there, or in the church of the Friars Minor at Norwich if he dies at Norwich. This confirms the Harpley/Norwich burial alternative.
- Parish/community legacies: the image supports paired West Barsham and Harpley community-householder gifts. West Barsham householders read as 12d each; the Harpley amount is smaller and should be checked before promotion.
- St Gregory's, Norwich: the testament contains a legacy to the altar/church context of St Gregory's, matching the known Norwich-house thread.
- Walsingham: the testament gives a gold ring with a precious stone called `in turkeys`/turquoise to the chapel of Blessed Mary at Walsingham, with a non-alienation condition. The ultima voluntas also supports a confraternity/memoria provision with the prior and convent of Blessed Mary of Walsingham for Thomas and Margaret.
- Depden/Depeden: the place tied to the church legacy and Edmund's charters reads best as `Depeden`/Depden, not Deopham or Deopdale. This matches the Wauncy/Depden inheritance context already known from Daniel Gurney.
- Swathings/Hardingham: the ultima voluntas refers to land/tenements called Swathings in Hardingham, to be sold by the executors for the performance of the will and pious uses. The vendor name remains uncertain from this pass (`Skirmets`/`Sker...` not secure).

## William clauses

The family-relations content is real and should be promoted carefully. Fo. 211v contains the good-conduct condition: William is to benefit if he behaves well, faithfully, and humbly toward Margaret his mother and observes the instruments/charters of Thomas and Margaret. Fo. 212v then adds financial/security language: William is to give sufficient security and payments, with the visible larger figures including `xl libras` and `xx marcas`.

Do not overstate this as proof of a personal rupture. The clause is unusually guarded and is fair evidence of controlled succession or concern about William's behavior, but the manuscript itself does not state a quarrel.

## Margaret clauses

The fo. 212v final clauses preserve Margaret's goods and textile/jewel provisions. The image supports the same point Daniel Gurney later extracted: woollen and linen cloths made by Margaret and her servants are left to her. The visible source also supports jewels/books/goods language, but the exact Latin sequence needs a final edition before being quoted in a fact sheet.

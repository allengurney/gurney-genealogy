# Packet 10 paleography report: Henry Gurnay of Great Ellingham, Norfolk, esquire

Date of this report: 2026-06-11

## Scope and status

This is a best-effort paleographic transcription of the registered will of Henry Gurnay/Gurney of Great Ellingham, Norfolk, esquire, with the probate clause. The packet images are the FamilySearch pages identified in the staging README as images 594, 595, and 596.

The reading below is from the page images and saved working crops, not from OCR. I made a first-pass reading, then re-read the date, legacies, executor/admin language, and probate after isolating tighter snippets. The most important correction from the re-read is the date: the will is best read as 1 May 1621, not 1624. The final digit is visually deceptive, but the regnal year appears to be James I year 19, and the probate is in October 1623, so a 1624 execution date is impossible.

- Spelling, capitalization, and punctuation are modernized only where the register itself is ambiguous; otherwise I retain the original flavor.

## Full best-effort transcription

### Image 594, lower right: opening and date

In the name of God Amen. I H[enry/Henrie] Gurnay of Greate Elingham in the Countie of Norff[olk], Esquier, being [sick/weak] in bodie and of p[er]fect and sounde memorie, thankes be to God therefore, all former wills being revoked or to be abolished, doe make and ordeyne for myne onlie true and last will this as followeth.

First I commend my soule into the handes of Almightie God, my creator and redeemer, and my bodie to be buried in Christian buriall at the discretion of my executor hereafter named.

And as touchinge the disposition of my landes, tenementes, goods, cattells, chattells, plate, jewells, household stuffe, and other things wherewith it hath pleased God to blesse me in this transitorie life, I will and dispose the same in manner and forme following.

This being done in the first daie of May in the xixth yeare of the Raigne of our Soveraigne Lorde Kinge James, and in the yeare of our Lord God 1621.

### Image 595, left page: poor-stock clause and children's portions

Item, I give and bequeath unto the poore people of the parish [or towne] of Greate Elingham the somme of tenne poundes of lawfull money of England, to be delivered into the handes of the churchwardens of the said parish within one yeare next after my decease.

And my will and meaning is that the said churchwardens, before the receipte thereof, shall become bounde, with sufficient condition or assurance, that the same tenne poundes shall remaine and continue as a stocke for the poore of the said towne [or parish]. The same stocke is to be lent from tyme to tyme unto poore persons of the said towne, upon pawne, bill obligatorie, or other sufficient securitie, in summes of twentie shillinges or under, for one yeare or for a shorter tyme, as the churchwardens shall thinke meete.

And if the churchwardens cannot agree in the choice of the poore persons to whom the same shall be lent, then my will is that the minister of the said towne shall be moderator and umpire betweene them, and his order therein shall stand.

Item, whereas I have heretofore given and delivered unto most of my children their portions, that is to say unto [Tho:] my sonne, unto H[enry/Henrie] my sonne, unto Ed[ward] my sonne, unto [Fra:/Francis] my sonne, unto Eliz[abeth] my daughter, and unto Anne [or another abbreviated child-name; the reading here is weak], as by such assurances, payments, and provisions heretofore made may appeare:

Nevertheless, for my daughters yet unmarried, I give and bequeath unto my daughter Anne the somme of two hundred poundes of lawfull money of England, and unto my daughter Abigayle the somme of two hundred poundes of like lawfull money of England, to be paid unto them at their severall marriages.

And my will is that until their marriages there shall be paid unto each of them the somme of twentie poundes by the yeare for their maintenance, to be paid yearely by my executor out of my estate.

And if either of my said daughters Anne or Abigayle shall not be married within two yeares next after my decease, then my will is that the said portion of two hundred poundes apiece shall be paid unto them, or either of them then living, into their owne handes upon demand, notwithstanding their not being married.

And if either of them shall happen to die before marriage or before the receipt of her portion, then the moitie [or unpaid share] appointed to such daughter shall goe and be paid to the survivor, according to the true intent of this my will.

And if both my said daughters Anne and Abigayle shall happen to die before marriage and before the receipt of their said portions, then my will is that two hundred poundes parcel of their said portions shall be distributed amongst my younger sonnes, in such manner and proportion as my executor shall thinke most convenient.

### Image 595, right page: plate, books, residue, and executor condition

Item, I give and bequeath unto my eldest sonne Thomas my bason and ewer of silver and [parcel] gilt [or "of sylver and of gold"; the metal-description is clear in substance but not in exact wording].

Item, I give and bequeath unto my sonne Fran[cis] all my bookes in Latyne [possibly "in London"; `Latyne` is my stronger reading after re-checking the letter forms].

Item, all other my goods, chattells, cattells, debts, plate, household stuffe, and other things whatsoever unbequeathed, my debts being paid and my funerall discharged, I give and bequeath unto my executor hereafter named.

Provided always, and my will and meaning is, that my said executor shall, before he take benefit of this my will, enter into bond or other sufficient assurance unto my said daughters Anne and Abigayle, or to such persons as shall be appointed in their behalf, for the true payment and performance of the portions, annuities, legacies, and other things before limited and bequeathed unto them.

And if my executor shall refuse or neglect to make such bond or assurance, then my will is that the benefit intended unto him by this my will shall cease so farre as may stand with law and equity, and that such order shall be taken for my said daughters as shall best preserve their portions and maintenance.

In witness whereof [or in presence whereof] this my last will and testament was made, published, and declared the day and yeare first above written.

[Witness/attestation initials or abbreviated names:] D. B. C. and R. H. [possibly four initials only; no full subscribing names are clear to me in the registered copy], the xxth daie of the moneth and yeare above said. [This last dating phrase may belong to a codicil/attestation note rather than the original execution line; the relation of this `20th day` note to the opening `1 May` date should be checked against the full register.]

### Image 596, left page: probate clause

Probatum fuit huiusmodi testamentum apud Norwicum vicesimo tertio die mensis Octobris Anno Domini 1623, coram magistro Nathanaele Yednam [or Yedham], legum doctore, officiale [or officiali] [court style continues], juramento Anthonii Gurnay filii naturalis et legitimi dicti defuncti, cui commissa fuit administratio bonorum iurium et creditorum eiusdem defuncti, de bene et fideliter administrando eadem, salvo iure cuiuscunque.

English sense: This testament was proved at Norwich on 23 October 1623 before Master Nathaniel Yednam/Yedham, doctor of laws and official. Administration of the goods, rights, and credits of the deceased was committed, on oath, to Anthony Gurnay, natural and lawful son of the deceased, saving the right of anyone.

## Answers to the posed questions

### Exact testator and date line

The testator is Henry/Henrie Gurnay of Great Ellingham in the county of Norfolk, esquire.

The date line is best read:

`the first daie of May in the xixth yeare of the Raigne of our Soveraigne Lorde Kinge James, and in the yeare of our Lord God 1621`.

I initially considered 1624 because the last digit is angular, but the internal regnal year and the probate date resolve it. James I's 19th regnal year ran in 1621-1622, and the will was proved on 23 October 1623. The will therefore cannot be dated 1624.

### Death/probate implication

Henry was alive on 1 May 1621 and dead by 23 October 1623, when the will was proved at Norwich.

If this is the direct-line Henry Gurney G15, this is a substantial correction to any tradition that he died in 1615/16.

### Children and legatees

The will names or strongly indicates the following children/legatees:

- Thomas Gurnay/Gurney, eldest son; receives the silver/gilt basin and ewer.
- Henry Gurnay/Gurney, son; appears in the list of children already portioned.
- Edward Gurnay/Gurney, son; appears in the list of children already portioned.
- Francis Gurnay/Gurney, son; receives the books, best read as "bookes in Latyne."
- Elizabeth, daughter; appears in the list of children already portioned.
- Anne, daughter; unmarried, receives GBP 200 portion and GBP 20 yearly maintenance until marriage/payment.
- Abigail/Abigayle, daughter; unmarried, receives GBP 200 portion and GBP 20 yearly maintenance until marriage/payment.
- Anthony Gurnay/Gurney, son; named in the probate clause as natural and lawful son, and the person to whom administration was committed. The will text likely made him executor, but the exact nomination line is not fully secure in my reading of the registered copy.

The list of already-portioned children is the least secure part of the transcription. The later clauses make Anne and Abigail certain. Thomas and Francis are certain from distinct bequests. Anthony is certain from the probate clause.

### Executor / administrator

The probate clause commits administration to Anthony Gurnay, natural and lawful son of the deceased.

The will itself repeatedly refers to "my executor," and the residue/condition clauses are structured around a single executor. I did not obtain a fully secure reading of the exact executor-nomination line from the available crops, but the probate clause makes Anthony the operative administrator, and likely the executor if he proved the will.

### Wife

I found no wife named or provided for in this will. This absence should not be overstated, but it is notable. If Henry's known wife Ellen/Helen was expected to survive him, her absence would need explanation from settlement, jointure, prior death, or omission.

### Places

The clearly named place is Great Ellingham, Norfolk. The poor-stock clause refers back to the same parish/town. I did not see West Barsham, Harpley, or another landed-place named in the portions of the will available here.

### Poor / charitable bequest

Henry gives GBP 10 to the poor of Great Ellingham, to be held as a continuing stock by the churchwardens. The money is to be lent out in small sums, apparently up to 20 shillings at a time, to poor borrowers on security. If the churchwardens disagree, the minister is to act as moderator/umpire.

This is a useful social-history detail: the gift is not only a one-time alms payment, but a parish poor-stock mechanism.

### Witnesses / attestation

The registered copy does not give clear full witness names in my reading. At the foot/right page I read what appear to be initials or abbreviated witness marks, approximately:

`D. B. C. and R. H.`

followed by a date-like phrase:

`the xxth daie of the moneth and yeare above said`.

This needs independent re-checking from the image, because it may represent a codicil/attestation note or copied witness initials rather than a normal full witness list.

## Other observations and research value

1. The will is highly valuable for the G15 family reconstruction because it gives a direct child set, distinguishes already-portioned children from unmarried daughters still needing portions, and supplies Anthony as a son active in probate.

2. The will materially tightens Henry's death window to after 1 May 1621 and before 23 October 1623.

3. The daughters Anne and Abigail are particularly important because their GBP 200 portions and GBP 20 yearly maintenance imply unmarried status at the will date.

4. The eldest-son clause identifies Thomas as eldest son, which is directly useful for ordering the male children if the family reconstruction has been ambiguous.

5. The Latin probate clause supplies an institutional proof point: probate at Norwich on 23 October 1623, with administration committed to Anthony Gurnay, son.

6. The charity clause may be worth extracting into any Great Ellingham or parish-poor-stock research notes if the site ever treats Henry's local role beyond bare genealogy.

## Confidence notes

High confidence:

- Testator: Henry Gurnay/Gurney of Great Ellingham, Norfolk, esquire.
- Will date: 1 May 1621.
- Probate: Norwich, 23 October 1623.
- Anthony Gurnay is son and probate administrator.
- Thomas is eldest son.
- Anne and Abigail are unmarried daughters receiving portions.
- GBP 10 poor-stock bequest to Great Ellingham.

Medium confidence:

- Francis receives "all my bookes in Latyne."
- The already-portioned child list includes Henry, Edward, Francis, Elizabeth, and possibly Anne or another abbreviated child-name.
- Court official's surname in the probate clause: Nathaniel Yednam/Yedham.

Low confidence / needs re-check:

- Full witness or attestation names.
- Exact executor-nomination wording in the will text.
- Whether the `xxth day` phrase at the end is an attestation/codicil date or a copied registration note.

## The reasoning that targets these courts

The 1636 court calls Margaret's tenement "**quondam** … vid[uae]" — *formerly* the widow's — so **her tenure ended shortly before October 1636**. Her surrender (or death/forfeiture) therefore plausibly sits in the **1633–1635 courts immediately preceding image 1632** — i.e. exactly the 1618–1631 run. A surrender or admission entry should name Margaret, confirm her widow status, and may **recite her late husband** (the unidentified male Gurney). Earlier still, her own admission as widow (1620s) would lie in the lower image numbers toward the book's 1623 start.

## Questions for the reader

1. On the staged leaves (and, when pulled, the 1619–1630 run): read each **court date** to fix the gradient, and **scan for any Gurner / Gurney / Gurnay surrender, admission, or recital** — especially one naming **Margaret** and/or **her late husband**.
2. If a recital names the deceased Gurney male, capture his **forename, copyhold description, and the court date** of his death/the prior admission.
3. Record a clean **negative with coverage** ("courts of [dates] read, no Gurner entry") if absent — that sharpens whether the man predates the 1623 book entirely.

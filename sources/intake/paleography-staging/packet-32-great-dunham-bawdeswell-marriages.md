# Packet 32 — Great Dunham (& Bawdeswell) marriages c.1624–1632: the Mary Wales test (lead L-161)

**Status:** Processed 2026-06-22. Great Dunham's continuous 1623-1633 marriage run contains no Mary Wales, Wales-family, or Gurney-form marriage. Report: `done/packet-32-great-dunham-bawdeswell-marriages.md`. Bawdeswell is handled separately in Packet 33.

## The question
The emigrant **John Gurney (G13)** married a **Mary** of unknown maiden family c.1627; their grandson was named **Peter**, a rare forename traced as a reverse tracer to a *Mary born to a father Peter*. The standout candidate is **Mary Wales, christened 1611 at Great Dunham**, daughter of **Peter Wales** — a household ~6 miles from John's East Dereham birthplace that was also at **Bawdeswell** by 1618 (children: Mary 1611 Gt Dunham; Elizabeth 1614, bur. 1616; Ann 1618; son Peter 1629). The marriage indexes do **not** cover Great Dunham/Bawdeswell marriages (a confirmed coverage gap), so only the register **images** can resolve it.

**Decisive tests:**
1. Is there a marriage of **Mary Wales** to a **John** (any Gurney-variant: Gurney/Gurney/Gurnay/Gourney/Gournay/Gurnie/Girney — but also *any* groom, since the surname is the whole point) in the Great Dunham or Bawdeswell registers, c.1624–1632?
2. If not married locally, does **Mary Wales disappear** from these registers after ~1627 (consistent with marrying out / emigrating)? Or does she reappear later as a wife/mother or in a **burial** (which would *eliminate* her as the emigrant's wife who died in New England)?
3. Harvest **every Wales entry** (baptism, marriage, burial) and **every Gurney-variant entry** in both parishes 1600–1645 while in the registers.

## Reading aids / terminology
- **Wales** may appear as *Wales / Wallis / Walys / Wayles*; **Peter** as *Petrus*. Watch a feminine given name **Maria** (= Mary) as bride.
- Gurney-variant Latin head-forms: *Gurney/Gurnay/Gournay/Gurnie* → genitive *Gurnei/Gournei*; first-letter corruption (G→C/J) is common in these hands.
- Marriage formula: "**[Groom] et [Bride] matrimonio coniuncti/copulati … [N] die mensis [Month] Anno Dni 16NN**." The two personal names flanking *et* are the couple.
- Soiled/stained pages: images carry a "Soiled Document" flag card — staining is heaviest at top-left; the right-hand page is generally cleaner.

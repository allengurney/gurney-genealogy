# Packet 57 supplement — the departure test re-run as elimination, 27 July 2026

Supplements [`packet-57-bucks-john-gurney-individuation.md`](packet-57-bucks-john-gurney-individuation.md).
Two things changed since that pass: an arrival constraint was supplied, and the Buckinghamshire
archdeaconry probate tier turned out to be online after all.

## Why the test was re-framed

Packet 57 asked *which household's records stop between 1635 and 1641* and returned a firm negative.
That question has a structural weakness: it tracks a **household in a parish**, not a man. A man who
moved parish inside England — extremely common — produces a "gap" at the wrong date and a "new
household" somewhere else, and the test scores him as neither.

The re-framed question is an elimination, not a discovery: **is this man documented in England in a way
that rules him out as the emigrant?** Only two classes of record do that:

- an **individuating record of the man after the emigration date** — burial, probate, a certificate of
  residence, a tax or tenancy record naming him; or
- a **chronological impossibility** against the colonial John's own life.

Register continuity of the surname does not eliminate anybody. That was the deviation audit's
masking-effect finding and it still governs.

### The chronology the test runs against

- The colonial John had a wife **Mary** by about 1627 and English-born children from about 1628.
- **He was not among the 1636 Weymouth land grantees** — indirect but reasonably strong evidence he had
  not arrived by 1636. Pre-1636 arrivals are also a much smaller and far more heavily worked population;
  a century of scrutiny of the early New England arrivals has not produced a John Gurney. Indicative,
  not decisive, but it points the same way.
- His first American record is **June 1641**.

So the crossing falls in **1637–1641**, and his wife Mary was alive until her death at Braintree,
**20 September 1661**.

That last fact is the sharpest elimination tool in the set and had not been used as one.

## The Buckinghamshire archdeaconry probate tier — now checked

Packet 57 recorded that the Archdeaconry of Buckingham series (D/A/We, D/A/Wf) is not item-level indexed
in Discovery, and that an archdeaconry will search was "a records-office request rather than an online
query." **It is online.** FindMyPast's *England & Wales Published Wills & Probate Indexes, 1300–1858*
carries **British Record Society vol. 114, *Buckinghamshire Probate Index, 1483–1660 and Buckinghamshire
Peculiars Index, 1420–1660*** as page images. `keywords=gurn*` returns five Buckinghamshire pages; the
GURNEY block sits on printed **page 173**.

**What it eliminates.** Three Johns gain an individuating English death that packet 57 could not find:
**Monks Risborough 1623**, **Hulcott 1630** (yeoman — this closes the Hulcott household packet 57 newly
confirmed), and **Southcott in Linslade 1636**. The Linslade one is worth pausing on: it lands exactly at
the start of the emigration window, so the Linslade records stopping in 1636 is explained by a death, not
a departure.

**What it does not eliminate, and this is the substantive result.** The index runs to 1660 and contains
**no John Gurney probate for Stewkley after 1592, and none at all for Haddenham, Weston Turville,
Hitcham, Amersham, Ivinghoe or Bierton after 1559.** The negative packet 57 flagged as outstanding is now
an actual bounded negative, and it runs the permissive way: those men left no Buckinghamshire
archdeaconry probate either.

The 1592 Stewkley John is a generation too early to be the head of the household active 1602–1613, so he
does not close Stewkley.

## The 1641 certificate of residence — verified, and it says more than the repo records

The repo carries TNA **E 115/180/113** as Candidate A's individuating record but flags it **unverified**.
Checked in Discovery, 2026-07-27. The catalogue description reads:

> "Certificate of residence showing **John Gurney to be liable for taxation in Northamptonshire, and not
> in the half-hundred of Aylesbury, Buckinghamshire, the previous area**…" — Location: Northamptonshire.
> Date: 1641.

This is a government record of **a specific man's move out of Buckinghamshire into Northamptonshire in
1641** — the same year the colonial John first appears in America. It is exactly the individuating class
the audit said the Bucks eliminations lacked, and it ties Aylesbury hundred to Northamptonshire precisely
as the Oliffe-household reconstruction requires (Bierton 1628 → Walgrave, Northants 1650). **The
unverified flag can come off.**

**But it also opens a tension the repo has not noticed.** If this man was taxed in Northamptonshire and
no longer in the Aylesbury half-hundred from 1641, he is hard to reconcile with the **St Mary Aylesbury
baptisms running to 1653** that the case file assigns to the same household. Packet 57 already showed the
Aylesbury index cannot say which children belong to which John, and that the parish held at least two.
The likeliest reading is that the certificate man and the father of the later Aylesbury children are
**two different Johns**. That does not weaken the elimination — it strengthens it, because it eliminates
*two* men rather than one — but the case file's single-household reconstruction of Candidate A should be
restated as two.

## What survives, and how it should be weighed

The five survivors are **not** five live candidates. Two considerations cut most of them down, and they
pull against each other:

- **Against them:** the colonial John was fathering children in England from about 1628 to about 1635.
  A man whose household record stops in 1603, 1622, 1627 or 1631 is not baptising children anywhere the
  test can see during the window when the colonial John demonstrably was. To keep any of them alive you
  must posit an undocumented move to an unindexed parish, marriage there, four baptisms there, and then
  emigration — which is the same immunising structure the audit criticised in Candidate B.
- **For them:** that is exactly what a parish-level test cannot see, and the colonial children's birth
  years are *estimates* from Sprague (2001) and the *History of Weymouth* (1923), not primary records.
  The audit already flags that dependency.

The honest position is that the survivors are **unaccounted-for, not viable**. Being unaccounted for is
cheap in a county that held five to seven simultaneous John Gurneys; it is the default state, not
evidence. On the age test, **Weston Turville** (fathering 1627 → born c.1602 → about 51 in 1653) and
**Hitcham** (fathering 1631 → born c.1606 → about 47, and the child is named **Mary**) fit the deposition
better than A(1603)'s household does, and neither has ever been weighed. They belong in the table as
named rows rather than inside the group-cleared block.

## Open actions this pass creates

1. **Read the three new probate documents**, not just the index rows — Hulcott 1630 (Wf 28 134),
   Linslade/Southcott 1636 (We 31 129, Wf 31 67), Monks Risborough 1623 (Pec 79/5/3). Wills name children;
   any of the three could resolve a household the index can only bound. Held at Buckinghamshire Archives.
2. **Order E 115/180/113** at image level. The catalogue description is strong but the document itself
   will name the man's status and parish precisely, and settle whether he is the Oliffe husband.
3. **Split Candidate A into two rows** — the certificate man, and the father of the Aylesbury children —
   unless a record ties them.
4. **Add Weston Turville and Hitcham as named rows.** They are better age fits than several candidates
   currently carrying their own row.
5. **Hertfordshire has not had this pass.** No equivalent published probate index has been checked for
   Berkhamsted, and packet 57 records that the Hertfordshire collection holds no seventeenth-century
   Berkhamsted Gurney record at all. Candidate C rests on father's-age reasoning, which is immune to
   masking, so this is lower priority — but it is a real gap.

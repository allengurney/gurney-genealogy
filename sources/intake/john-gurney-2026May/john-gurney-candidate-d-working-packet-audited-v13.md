# John Gurney Candidate D working packet — Boyd’s, ROLLCO, and Robert Gurney will evidence

## Scope

This packet preserves the working evidence for a newly recognized John Gurney comparator candidate, now designated **Candidate D**: John Gurney, son of Robert Gurney, citizen and draper / tailor of Old Change, London. It includes the five Boyd’s Inhabitants of London card transcriptions, related ROLLCO Drapers’ Company records, the Robert Gurney 1625 will, religious-language assessment, Old Change neighborhood analysis, applicable parish recordsets, and correlation against the existing John Gurney case-file framework.

## Summary observations

1. `gbor_bil_sog25_0595.jpg` identifies **John Gourney/Gurny**, merchant, of **S Dionis Backchurch**, with wife **Dorothy Reading** of Northampton and children **Anne** and **Dorothy**.
2. `gbor_bil_sog26_0392.jpg` is a **William Shipman** card. Its relevance is the wife entry: **Mary Garney/Gurney**, daughter of **John Garney/Gurney**.
3. `gbor_bil_sog36_0477.jpg` is degraded. It appears to identify a **John Gurney/Gurny**, citizen and grocer, with a wife probably named **Jane or Joan Waring** and children/notes including **Richard** and **John**.
4. `gbor_bil_sog59_0240.jpg` identifies **John Gurny/Gurney** of **S Augustine**, son of **Robert Gurney** and **Anne Morris** of **S Michael le Querne**. No wife or children are entered on that card.
5. `gbor_bil_sog82_0603.jpg` is the related **Robert Gurny/Gurney** card. Although the supplied Findmypast metadata is for **John Gurny**, the card itself is headed **Robert Gurny** and includes child **John**, apparently cross-referenced to the John card.
6. The ROLLCO freedom record gives **John Gurney** as a **new freeman** of the **Drapers’ Company** by **redemption** on **1623 Feb. 11** / database year **1624**, with **Robert Gurney** named as father of the freeman.
7. The supplied Robert Gurney will, proved 23 Sep. 1625, confirms **Robert Gurney, citizen and draper of London**, wife **Anne**, son **John**, premises in **Old Change**, and John as **sole executor** who proved the will as son and executor.
8. This evidence establishes a new serious comparator candidate, now called **Candidate D**: **John Gurney, son of Robert Gurney of Old Change, Drapers’ Company / textile-trade line**.
9. Candidate D does **not** replace Candidate B, but it materially changes the comparison framework because it supplies a John Gurney in the right broad age range whose father was in the London textile trade.
10. The remaining decisive questions are whether Candidate D has a wife Mary and children matching Sarah, Mary, Richard, John, and Peter; whether he appears in London after 1630; and whether he disappears before the Massachusetts John appears securely by 1641.

## Connected interpretation

The ROLLCO record supplies a direct father-son link between **John Gurney**, new freeman of the Drapers’ Company by redemption on 11 February 1623/4, and **Robert Gurney**, father of the freeman.

This strongly supports linking the ROLLCO John to the Boyd card pair:

- `gbor_bil_sog82_0603.jpg` — **Robert Gurny** of S Augustine, wife Anne Morris, child **John**, with a child-line cross-reference `4924[?]`.
- `gbor_bil_sog59_0240.jpg` — **John Gurny** of S Augustine, father **Robert Gurney**, mother **Anne Morris**, with upper-right reference `4924[?]`.

The ROLLCO record materially improves the evidentiary value of this cluster because it gives a dated adult/civic event for John and confirms Robert as father. The freedom method was **redemption**, meaning admission by payment of a fine rather than a straightforward completed apprenticeship or patrimony route. The father entry therefore should be treated as a genealogical identifier in the ROLLCO record, not as proof that the freedom itself was obtained by patrimony.

## Textile-trade significance

This is a high-priority London textile-trade comparator. Robert Gurny/Gurney is represented on the Boyd card as a **citizen and draper**, with a further note reading **Tailor Old Change**. A ROLLCO search result independently shows **Gurney, Robert**, `tailor, Old Change`, in a **1617 Drapers’ Company apprenticeship** event, with role **Master** and status **Co Draper**.

Together, the card and ROLLCO results show that this John Gurney had a father embedded in the London textile world:

```text
Robert Gurny/Gurney
  of S Augustine
  citizen + draper
  tailor, Old Change
  Drapers’ Company master by 1617
  father of John Gurney, Drapers’ Company freeman by redemption, 1623/4
```

## Chronological problem and candidate value

This cluster is potentially important for the John Gurney of Braintree case because it produces a **John Gurney with a father in a textile trade**, one of the criteria being used in candidate matching.

However, it also introduces a chronology problem that must be resolved before any identity claim is made:

- Robert Gurny’s Boyd card gives marriage to **Anne Morris** at **S Magnus** on **1611 Apr. 4**.
- The ROLLCO freedom record places **John Gurney** as a new freeman on **1623 Feb. 11** / year **1624**.
- If John was old enough to receive the freedom in 1623/4, he is unlikely to have been a post-1611 child of Anne Morris unless the admission occurred unusually young or the card has conflated relationships.
- Therefore, either John was born before Robert’s 1611 marriage to Anne Morris, Anne Morris was not necessarily John’s mother, the card has compressed or conflated family data, or this is not the same John despite the strong Robert/John/Drapers/S Augustine cross-reference.

For now, the cleanest working statement is:

> **John Gurney, son of Robert Gurney of the Drapers’ Company / Old Change textile-trade cluster, was a Drapers’ Company freeman by redemption in 1623/4. He is now a serious comparator candidate because his father was in the textile trade, but the 1611 Robert-Anne marriage date creates a maternal/chronological inconsistency that requires Robert’s 1625 will and the full Drapers’ Company freedom source to resolve.**

## Priority follow-up records

1. **Robert Gurny/Gurney’s 1625 will**, apparently Archdeaconry of London, register/volume `VI`, folio/page `185[?]`, parish **S Augustine**.
2. **Full ROLLCO event detail page or underlying Drapers’ Company/Boyd’s Roll entry** for John Gurney’s freedom by redemption on **1623 Feb. 11**.
3. **Drapers’ Company / ROLLCO event detail for Robert Gurney, tailor of Old Change, 1617 apprenticeship master**, because this confirms the textile-trade context independently of the Boyd card.
4. **S Magnus marriage register**, 4 Apr. 1611, Robert Gurny/Gurney and Anne Morris, to preserve the image-confirmed details and note absence of marital-status language.
5. **S Augustine parish registers**, especially baptisms/burials for Gurny/Gurney before and after 1611.

# 9. Religious-language assessment

## Relevant religious wording in Robert Gurney’s will

Robert’s will includes the following significant religious language:

```text
I commend and commit my soule and body into the hands of Almighty god my Creator
and of Jesus Christ my only Saviour and redeemer by whose mercy and meritts I doe believe assuredly
to have obteyned full and free remission pardon for all my sinnes and offences whatsoever and to enjoy
eternall life with others the elect children of god in the kingdome of heaven.
```

The phrase **“elect children of god”** is the strongest suggestive element. It is compatible with Reformed / Calvinist religious vocabulary and therefore indirectly compatible with a Puritan-leaning milieu. The phrases “only Saviour and redeemer,” “free remission pardon,” and “mercy and meritts” are also Protestant-compatible and do not contain Catholic-style intercessory elements.

## Interpretation

This religious language is **suggestive but weak evidence**.

It is compatible with the kind of Reformed Protestant vocabulary that could fit an emigrant to Massachusetts Bay. The Massachusetts Bay Great Migration was strongly Puritan in context: in 1630 a large Puritan fleet established the Massachusetts Bay Colony, and by 1636 thousands more had arrived; secondary public histories describe Massachusetts Bay as a Puritan settlement and society. Sources for the broader context: Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth, “The Great Migration,” <https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/commonwealth-museum/exhibits/online/two-colonies/a-tale-of-two-colonies_8.htm>; Britannica, “Massachusetts Bay Colony,” <https://www.britannica.com/place/Massachusetts-Bay-Colony>.

However, this will does **not** independently prove Puritan identity or emigration propensity. The language is not as strong as explicit bequests to godly ministers, lecturers, dissenting congregations, deprived ministers, New England projects, or named Puritan associates. It also lacks explicit anti-ceremonial or anti-episcopal statements.

## Working conclusion

Use the religious language as a **minor positive contextual indicator**, not as a major evidentiary pillar:

```text
Robert Gurney’s 1621/2 will uses Protestant/Reformed-compatible language, including “the elect children of God.” This is compatible with a Puritan-adjacent religious environment but is not strong enough to prove nonconformity, Puritan affiliation, or emigration intent. It modestly supports, but does not drive, the plausibility of Candidate D as a New England emigrant candidate.
```

# 10. Old Change neighborhood and parish-record strategy

## Old Change neighborhood

Old Change was a street in the City of London connecting **Cheapside** to **Knightrider Street**. It was originally called **Old Exchange**, named from the king’s exchange for bullion to be coined. St Augustine stood at or near the corner of Old Change and Watling Street. Sources: London Online, “Old Change,” <https://www.londononline.co.uk/streetorigins/Old_Change/>; London Museum, “St Augustine’s church from Old Change, Cheapside,” <https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/v/object-516581/st-augustines-church-from-old-change-cheapside/>; City of London, “St Augustine Watling Street,” <https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/things-to-do/architecture/city-churches-and-religious-buildings/st-augustine-watling-street>.

This is important because Robert’s will says he lived in **Old Change**, and ROLLCO separately identifies **Robert Gurney, tailor, Old Change**. Old Change was therefore not just a vague London clue; it is the physical neighborhood linking:

- Robert’s residence and shop/cellar/hall in the will.
- The Boyd card’s **S Augustine** parish association.
- ROLLCO’s **tailor, Old Change** occupational-location entry.
- John’s inheritance/control of trade premises.

## Does Old Change itself correlate with emigration?

No direct Old Change-to-New England emigration corridor has yet been identified.

Its relevance is indirect:

1. It places Robert and John inside the City of London’s commercial and livery-company world.
2. It connects the family to the textile economy through Drapers’ Company records and tailor/draper language.
3. It provides a specific urban search geography for parish, rate, tax, and livery records.
4. It could plausibly support mobility and Atlantic connections, but it does not by itself indicate a Puritan migration network.

This differs from the stronger Coleman Street / St Stephen Coleman Street-style Puritan migration context discussed in the Candidate B case. Old Change is a **trade-neighborhood clue**, not yet a **religious-emigration-neighborhood clue**.

# John Gurney and Billerica (1659–1660) — synthesis pre-draft

**Finding in one line.** John Gurney shows up in the Billerica records only across about seven months in 1659–60, and always as *"John Gurney of Braintree."* He took up a proprietor's place during the new town's first land divisions, but he never moved there: within four months he handed the house-lot back, and the rest of his Billerica interest was a paper share that other men held for him. No record places him living in Billerica, and none shows any Billerica land becoming a lasting Gurney holding.

## What the records say

**The ten-acre house lot (15 August 1659).** The town granted John a ten-acre house-lot *on the express condition that he "come and inhabit the said Lott himselfe,"* and at his request accepted **his son Richard** as a fellow inhabitant, to settle on whatever part of the lot John chose. A larger layout of fifty-six acres "more or less, beside meddow" went with it, bounded by the land of William Browne and William Hamlett. The grant naming "his son Richard" is itself a primary statement that John had a son Richard of settling age in 1659 — matching Richard Gurney (G12), born about 1630–34.[^billerica-ms]

**His standing in the town books (autumn 1659).** John was carried as a full proprietor, not a casual applicant. He appears in the proprietors' lot-draw list ("John Gurney I"), with his house valued at £3, town charges of 10 shillings 7 pence, and a rate of £2 5s 10d. That same £2 5s 10d rate is the one Hazen's printed history records under 10 September 1659 as John's share of the half-payment for the Dudley Farm purchase — so the printed rate and the manuscript rate are the same assessment.[^billerica-ms][^hazen]

**The November 1659 upland division — held for him by others.** When the town made its upland division in November 1659 (thirty-nine lots among forty persons, in the great common field east of the Concord River below the great bridge), John did not take a lot in person: **Peter Brackett and Joseph Thompson held "the right of John Gurney, of Braintree" in common.** His Billerica right was already being exercised by other men on his behalf — the clearest sign that he was an absentee proprietor, not a resident.[^hazen]

**Two great-meadow lots.** The town also laid out to John two meadow parcels: four and a quarter acres beginning at James Paterson's bounds, and four and a half acres at the mouth of Horse Brook, bounded on the south by John Rogers. Whether these were ever used or simply lapsed is not recorded.[^billerica-ms]

**The surrender (26 December 1659).** Within four months John gave the house-lot back. The town book records that **Daniel Shead, "in answer for his father John Gurney,"** surrendered the lot to the town to dispose of as it pleased. Two details matter here. First, Daniel Shed — the Braintree man who became a Billerica founder — calls John "father"; since Daniel had married John's daughter Mary Gurney in 1647, John was his father-in-law, a Shed–Gurney family tie no compiled genealogy states outright. Second, the town promptly re-granted **one half** of the surrendered lot to John Hall (19 March 1659/60); no taker for the other half is recorded.[^billerica-ms][^shedd]

## Reading it together

John's whole Billerica episode reads as a relocation that was explored and then dropped. He took up a proprietor's place in the new town in the summer of 1659, intending to settle his son Richard there, was assessed and laid out lots like any inhabitant, and then surrendered the house-lot before year's end — handing the matter off through his son-in-law Daniel Shed. He kept living at Braintree, where he sold his house and orchard in February 1661/2 and died in 1662/3. The Billerica records describe a real but brief proprietary interest, not a residence.

**Senior or Junior?** Every dated Billerica record (1659–60) names John Gurney **of Braintree** — that is, John Sr. (G13). The "son Richard" of the grant is Richard G12; the "father John Gurney" for whom Daniel Shed answered is the same John Sr. No Billerica record names a *John Jr.* (John Jr. is documented at Weymouth and Mendon, not here). A few abuttal entries scattered through the later town book still describe land by John Gurney's name — "John Gurney South and ye ministers Land," "bounded with Will Brown … & John Gurney North," "John Gurney & Will Hamlet north" — and these are undated, so in principle a Gurney interest could have lingered after John Sr.'s death. But there is **no positive evidence** that any Billerica parcel passed to John Jr. or was ever occupied by a Gurney. The most economical reading of the facts is that John Sr. held Billerica options he never used — an absentee right that Peter Brackett and Joseph Thompson carried for him, a house-lot he surrendered, and meadow lots that lapsed — and that nothing was pursued after he died.[^billerica-ms][^hazen]

**The Peter Brackett thread.** The man who held John's Billerica right is the same Peter Brackett who recurs across John's last years: he witnessed John's 1661/2 deed to Richard Thayer, he is named among the creditors of John's 1663 estate, and he was one of the Braintree men tied to the Mendon (Nipmuck) purchase. Brackett is a consistent Braintree associate of John's, and his appearance at Billerica fits that pattern rather than pointing to a Gurney residence there.[^hazen][^spr338]

**One correction folded in.** An earlier reading of a degraded accounts page as "Gurney Tanner" is a dead OCR garble of "Towne Charges" (the clean copy reads "John Gurney : Towne Charges of 10s 7d"). No tanner trade attaches to any Billerica Gurney.[^billerica-ms]

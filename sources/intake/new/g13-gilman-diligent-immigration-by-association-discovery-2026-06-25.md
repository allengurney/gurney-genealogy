## Working premise used in this pass

For scenario analysis, we temporarily accepted the premise:

- Ann Gurney was John Gurney-1's sister.
- Ann Gurney's marriage to John Gilman at Hingham, Norfolk, on 1 Oct 1626 is confirmed.
- The Gilman/Diligent passenger record is a real migration-network datum.

This premise was used as an analytic lens, not as a final proof standard. The evidence ladder remains separate: the marriage is well supported; Ann's parentage and birth range remain less secure.

## Core conclusions

### 1. The Diligent is network evidence, not John Gurney passenger evidence

The Diligent of Ipswich, 1638, is the closest known Norfolk-to-south-shore Massachusetts migration event to John Gurney's first secure colonial appearance at Weymouth in June 1641. However:

- the Diligent list contains no John Gurney or obvious Gurney variant;
- Ann Gurney and John Gilman themselves did not emigrate, since Ann was buried at Hingham, Norfolk, on 23 Nov 1651;
- at least two of Ann and John Gilman's sons later emigrated to New England, but not as part of John Gurney-1's 1641 appearance;
- the Diligent list is a Cushing/Hingham town tradition rather than a complete original customs manifest, so unnamed wives, children, and servants are an evidentiary caveat, but John Gurney as a married man with children is unlikely to be hiding as an unnamed dependent.

Best reading: the Diligent/Gilman evidence strengthens a Norfolk-Hingham-to-New-England corridor hypothesis for John Gurney, but does not identify his vessel.

### 2. If Ann was John's sister, the useful English search zone widens from East Dereham to the Gilman orbit

The Ann/Gilman premise suggests that John Gurney's English life may have touched:

- Hingham, Norfolk;
- West Dereham, Norfolk;
- Deopham, Norfolk;
- adjacent Norfolk textile/weaver networks;
- the wider Hingham/Great Ellingham/Attleborough/Wymondham/Dereham corridor.

This matters because John Gurney's wife Mary and his known/likely children have not been located in English parish records. If brother and sister stayed close, the next English-side search should not be limited to East Dereham or London. It should include Hingham/West Dereham/Deopham parish images, not just indexes.

### 3. Ann's c.1612-16 birth estimate is the weakest part of the current fact-sheet row

The marriage event is much firmer than the birth estimate.

Evidence:

- G14 fact sheet row gives Ann `c. 1612-16? (probable)` and says she married John Gilman at Hingham, Norfolk, 1 Oct 1626.
- `data/sources.json` source `hingham-register` says: `Ann Gurney married John Gilman, 1 Oct 1626. Relationship to Francis G14's family unresolved.`
- The v4 case file phrases the relationship conditionally: `If Ann Gurney was John's sister...`
- Pease identifies Ann as Francis's daughter from the first marriage, but Pease is a compiled genealogy. The Margaret Ryvett element of Pease has been strengthened by primary-source confirmation; that does not automatically promote every Pease child claim.

Chronology:

- If born c.1612-16, Ann was about 10-14 at the 1626 marriage.
- If married at a more usual minimum age of 16+, Ann was born by c.1610.
- If married around 18-22, Ann was born c.1604-1608.
- That earlier birth is hard to fit as a child of Francis Gurney and Margaret Rybett after their 23 Sept 1611 marriage, unless the date assumptions are wrong, she was pre-marital, she was from another relationship, or she is a different Ann Gurney than the fact-sheet row implies.

Working adjustment: treat `Ann Gurney, wife of John Gilman` as a confirmed Hingham/Gilman network clue, but treat her parentage and birth date as unresolved.

### 4. A separate Ann Gurney / Peter Woodcocke record remains a key reconciliation problem

The case-file footnote notes a separately documented Ann Gurney who married Peter Woodcocke at West Dereham, Norfolk, on 8 Feb 1618/19. It is unresolved whether this is:

- the same Ann who later married John Gilman in 1626, implying a prior Woodcocke marriage and some intervening event;
- a different Ann Gurney in the same broader Norfolk orbit;
- a misassigned or conflated compiled-genealogy path.

This is high value because the Woodcocke marriage, if linked to the Gilman bride, would imply Ann was older than the current G14 fact-sheet birth range and could explain the age problem.

### 5. The first Massachusetts associates point more to Weymouth reception than to a shared Diligent voyage

The first colonial anchor is the 2 June 1641 General Court fine-remission entry involving John Gurney, James Ludden, and a Porter.

Controlling source:

- Massachusetts Bay Records, vol. 1, p. 331, remits fines of John Gurney, James Ludden, and John Porter for want of gunpowder.
- Porter genealogy repeats the record and adds: `Ludden and Gurney were of Weymouth.`
- A later military-record calendar is reported in repo as reading `Petition of John Gurny, Richard Porter, and James Ludden of Weymouth...`

The Porter identity conflict matters:

- If the Porter was John Porter of Hingham/Salem, the connection is more jurisdictional or militia-administrative and less Weymouth-origin-specific.
- If the Porter was Richard Porter of Weymouth, the same-event associate set becomes a stronger Weymouth/Hull-company comparator.
- The actual petition image or best calendar image is the needed next source.

James Ludden finding:

- `History_of_Weymouth_Massachusetts.txt` gives James Ludden as born near 1611, died at Weymouth 7 Feb 1692/3, and says he had been called an `old planter`.
- It quotes Winthrop's 31 Oct 1632 Wessaguscus return narrative in which `one Luddam` served as guide at Luddam's Ford.
- It records that the General Court remitted Ludden's fine for want of gunpowder on 2 June 1641.
- This makes Ludden less likely to be a late 1638-41 shared passenger with John Gurney. Instead, he looks like an already-established Weymouth/Wessaguscus man who could have helped ground John locally.

Implication: John may have arrived via the Norfolk/Gilman/Hingham corridor but been received or settled through older Weymouth networks by 1641.

### 6. The passenger-list negatives remain meaningful but not exhaustive

The supplied passenger research file reports no John Gurney, John Gourney, John Gurny, John Gurnie, or obvious variant in:

- Bevis of Southampton / Hampton, 1638;
- Diligent of Ipswich, 1638;

The G13 companion adds Hotten:

- The `Richard Gummy` lookalike aboard the America to Virginia is not a New England Gurney.
- The residual open check is Coldham and TNA E157/20 original register, lead L-170.

## Scenario tree

### Scenario A - Same Norfolk-Hingham migration corridor, different ship

Probability: strongest working scenario under the Ann-as-sister premise.

Pattern:

- Ann Gurney marries into the Gilman textile family at Hingham, Norfolk.
- The Edward Gilman/Diligent cohort leaves the Hingham, Norfolk, orbit in 1638 and settles at Hingham, Massachusetts.
- John Gurney appears by June 1641 at Weymouth, under ten miles from Hingham and Braintree.
- John is absent from the Diligent list, so he likely traveled separately, perhaps 1638-41.

What this implies:

- Search for John in England should emphasize the Hingham/West Dereham/Deopham textile and parish corridor.
- Search for Mary and children should include Hingham, West Dereham, Deopham, Wymondham, Attleborough, Great Ellingham, and nearby parishes, not just East Dereham and London.
- Colonial searches should look for Gurney links in Hingham, Weymouth, and Braintree together.

### Scenario B - Hidden dependent or unnamed servant in the Diligent tradition

Probability: possible but weaker.

Why possible:

- The Diligent source is not a full customs manifest.
- Cushing/Drake lists household heads and some servants, but many wives, children, and servants are unnamed.

Why weak:

- John Gurney was probably a married adult with children by 1638.
- A married tradesman-household is less likely to be hidden silently as an unnamed servant.
- No Gurney variant appears in the named extraction list.

Use: keep as a caveat, not a leading explanation.

### Scenario C - Weymouth reception network independent of Diligent

Probability: strong as part of the arrival story, not necessarily the English-origin story.

Pattern:

- James Ludden was established at Wessaguscus/Weymouth by 1632 and called an old planter.
- The 1641 gunpowder-fines matter ties John Gurney to Ludden.
- Later Weymouth land memory places John in East Field/Mill Field/Great Pond grant contexts, with later grants to other men.

What this implies:

- Ludden probably does not explain John's transatlantic party.
- Ludden may explain John's local reception, militia responsibility, and early land rights.
- The Weymouth first-ring should be profiled as a local grounding network.

### Scenario D - Hybrid Norfolk/London/Essex path

Probability: live and compatible with Candidate B.

Pattern:

- Francis G14's world spans Norfolk, London, and Essex/Browning/Maldon contexts.
- John Gurney's colonial network includes Braintree, Daniel Shed of Finchingfield, William Tyng leasehold context, and later Billerica/Shed links.
- The Gilman evidence supplies Norfolk-Hingham migration texture.

What this implies:

- The search should not force a single-place origin.
- John may have moved through Norfolk kin, London trade, Essex associates, and Massachusetts south-shore settlement channels.

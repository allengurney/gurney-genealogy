## Key Analytical Findings

### Somerset Gournays dominate the Gascon rolls
The overwhelming majority of Gournay entries relate to the **Somerset family** (East Harptree / regicide branch):
- **Thomas Gurney of Somerset** (C61/36, 1325) â€” explicitly labelled Somerset in the levy list.
- **Thomas de Gournay the regicide** (C61/37, 43, 44, 54) â€” captured, imprisoned at Bayonne, transported to England 1332. Edward II's murderer.
- **Thomas de Gournay kt (1342, 1349, 1351)** â€” almost certainly a son or kinsman of the regicide, serving as captain in Gascony, later acting as lieutenant of the seneschal of Gascony at Monpazier (1351). This is the man who in 1349 had his own retinue and secured ships at Southampton.
- **Matthew de Gournay (d. 1406)** â€” the great Hundred Years War captain, seneschal of the Landes, claiming Marensin by conquest. Well-documented Somerset knight of East Harptree.

### Norfolk Gournay signal â€” John Gournay, C61/104:50.10
The single clearest Norfolk signal is **John Gournay (1394)** naming **John Dru, parson of the church of Harpley**, as his English attorney before going to Aquitaine. Harpley (Norfolk) is explicitly associated with the Norfolk Gournay/Gurney line. This John Gournay is almost certainly from the **Norfolk West Barsham/Harpley family** (our direct line), and he is going to Aquitaine in the service of the same 1394 expedition (John Beaufort's force, attested by Duke of York). This is the most significant Norfolk-specific finding in the Gascon Rolls.

### Edmund Gurney (C61/78, 82, 83) â€” attorney role, 1365â€“1370
Edmund Gurney appears not as a soldier in Gascony but as an **English attorney** nominated by knights serving in the retinues of the Prince of Aquitaine and the earl of Cambridge. He is named alongside William de Rudham (parson of Ingoldisthorpe) in C61/83:18 â€” Ingoldisthorpe is in Norfolk. The combination of a Norfolk parsonage and an Edmund Gurney as co-attorney is suggestive of a Norfolk connection, though not conclusive. The entity page notes his title as "clerk" and "knight."

### John de Gournay, kt (C61/67:140.4, 1355)
Goes to Gascony in the retinue of **John de Lisle** in 1355. No place of origin given. The Gournay index subsumes this under the "Gournay, John" entity along with the 1394 man, but they may be two different individuals (a 1355 knight and a 1394 soldier). The 1355 knight is listed with Walter de Romsey, Roger Beauchamp â€” these are not specifically Somerset or Norfolk associates.

### Joan de Gournay (C61/127:83, 1437)
A Gascon woman, widow of Aymeric de Duras â€” not related to either the Norfolk or Somerset English Gournay families.

# Massachusetts Bay Passenger Research Note: Bevis, Diligent, Mary Anne, and 1639–mid-1641 Boston-Area Voyages

Prepared: 2026-06-24  
Research focus: John Gurney / Gourney / Gurny / Gurnie / related variants; possible acquaintance-network tracing through Weymouth, Braintree, Hingham, and nearby Massachusetts Bay communities.

## 1. Executive Findings

### 1.1 Gurney / Gourney result in the searched lists

I did **not** find a named **John Gurney**, **John Gourney**, **John Gurny**, or obvious variant in the three targeted passenger/list clusters:

- **Bevis of Southampton / Hampton**, 1638.
- **Diligent of Ipswich**, 1638.
- **Mary Anne of Yarmouth**, 1637.

I also searched the OCR text of the two major compiled/list sources consulted here:

- Banks, *The Planters of the Commonwealth*.
- Drake, *Result of Some Researches among the British Archives...*

Those text searches did **not** produce a John Gurney-like passenger hit in the relevant New England passenger lists. A Drake OCR/text hit for **“Gourney”** was **William Gourney, Mayor of Dartmouth**, in an unrelated St. Christopher / Caribbean-bound list, not a New England passenger.

### 1.2 John Gedney flag

**John Gedney** is worth flagging, but I would treat him as a **low-probability Gurney candidate**.

There are at least two Gedney appearances in the 1637 East Anglia passenger context:

1. **Mary Anne of Yarmouth**, 1637: **John Gedney of Norwich, Norfolk**, with wife Sarah, children Lydia, Hannah, John, and servants William Walker and [blank] Burgess; destination Salem in Banks.
2. **John and Dorothy / Rose**, 1637 combined Ipswich/Yarmouth list: Banks also shows a **John Gedney, age 19, servant**, apparently associated with John Pearce’s Norwich household in the adjacent 1637 list.

Reasons John Gedney is only a remote possibility:

- **Gedney** is a known surname, not merely an obvious OCR error for Gurney.
- The Mary Anne Gedney household is tied to **Norwich → Salem**, not Weymouth/Braintree.
- The given family structure is explicit enough that it looks like a real Gedney family rather than a loose variant.

Still, because seventeenth-century handwriting and later OCR can distort **d/n/r/u/e** sequences, I would keep Gedney in a “check but do not prioritize” column.

### 1.3 Why the passenger-name lists matter even if John Gurney is absent

The working strategy is sound: use passenger lists to build **migration-network candidate pools**, then compare those names to John Gurney’s documented acquaintances, neighbors, witnesses, petition co-signers, town officers, land partners, church contacts, or court associates in **Weymouth, Braintree, Hingham, Dorchester, Boston, Rehoboth, and Salem**.

The strongest acquaintance-network clusters from this pass are:

- **Bevis → Weymouth / Newbury / Salisbury / Dummer-Carpenter-Batt-Biley network**
- **Diligent → Hingham / Great Ellingham / Attleborough / Wymondham / Norfolk network**
- **Mary Anne → Salem / Newbury / Hingham / Norwich-Great Yarmouth-Wrentham network**
- **1639 Hingham continuation → Pitts / Foulsham from Old Hingham**
- **1639–1641 Boston-area voyages → real traffic, poor name survival**

## 2. Source Quality and Evidence Model

### 2.1 Bevis

The **Bevis** list is a relatively strong list because it derives from a Southampton passenger certificate/list transcribed by Drake and reorganized by Banks. Drake’s version preserves the old-style spelling and grouped passenger counts; Banks rearranges and standardizes names, adds English homes and later New England settlements where he believed they were known.

Evidence type: **near-primary transcript of a passenger certificate**, plus secondary reconstruction.

### 2.2 Diligent

The **Diligent** list is not a customs/passenger manifest in the same sense. Its core source is the **Daniel Cushing Hingham record**: a Hingham, Massachusetts town/tradition record naming families from Old Hingham and nearby Norfolk towns who settled in New Hingham. Drake printed this Cushing material; Banks later rearranged it.

Evidence type: **town manuscript / settler-list tradition**, very valuable, but not an original customs manifest.

### 2.3 Mary Anne

The **Mary Anne of Yarmouth** list is a stronger customs-style passenger record than the Diligent tradition. Drake prints the examination entries; Banks arranges them as a ship list, adds arrival and destination information, and appends some related Cushing/Hingham material in the same 1637 section.

Evidence type: **passenger examination / clearance record**, with later Banks arrangement.

### 2.4 1639–mid-1641 Boston-area voyages

The period from **1639 through mid-1641** is much weaker for named passenger lists. Evidence tends to be:

- ship arrivals in Winthrop or colonial records;
- license/clearance orders with passenger counts but no names;
- town records showing first appearance in New England;
- secondary reconstructions by Banks and later genealogists.

Evidence type: **ship movement and arrival evidence**, but usually **not named passenger lists**.

### 5.4 Mary Anne notes for Gurney/Gedney

**John Gedney of Norwich** should be retained as a low-probability comparison point:

- Origin: Norwich, Norfolk.
- Destination: Salem.
- Family: wife Sarah, children Lydia, Hannah, John; servants William Walker and [blank] Burgess.
- Not obviously Weymouth/Braintree.
- Not obviously Gurney, but spelling/handwriting review could be worthwhile if a primary image is available.

Also note the **separate John Gedney, age 19, servant** in the adjacent John and Dorothy / Rose combined list. That second Gedney is not in the Mary Anne table above but may be useful for name-variant checking.

## 6. Narrow Date Range: 1639 to mid-1641, Boston and Boston-Area Voyages

### 6.1 Summary finding

I did **not** find a credible named passenger list for a Boston-area voyage from **1639 through mid-1641** that produced a John Gurney/Gourney candidate or a fresh Great Ellingham/Norfolk passenger group comparable to the Diligent.

The evidence in this date range mostly consists of:

- named ships with passenger counts but no passenger names;
- arrival notices;
- licenses/orders to clear ships;
- later first-record reconstructions in New England town records.

### 6.2 Best 1639 Norfolk/Hingham continuation

Cushing/Drake gives a small **1639 Old Hingham continuation**:

| Year | People | Origin | Destination | Ship |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1639 | Edmond Pitts, wife, child; Leonard Pitts; Adam Foulsham | Old Hingham, Norfolk | New Hingham, Massachusetts | Not named |

This is important because it shows the Norfolk/Hingham migration stream continued after the Diligent, but it does **not** provide a ship name.

### 6.3 1639–1641 ship and record cluster summary

| Date / period | Ship or record cluster | Passenger evidence | Named passengers? | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1639 | Beaver of London | Banks says arrived Boston 22 June 1639 with passengers | No clear public named list found | Low unless separate records tie a person to it |
| 1639/1640 | Mary Rose of Bristol | Banks says arrived Boston from Bristol with 120 passengers; Winthrop later records the Mary Rose explosion at Charlestown in 1640 | No clear named passenger list found | West Country/Bristol lead, not Norfolk |
| 1639 | Jonathan, probable Southampton/Hampshire reconstruction in Banks | Secondary reconstruction, not strong manifest | Some names in Banks | Low for Norfolk/Gurney unless surname-network match appears |
| Jan 1640 old-style/new-style | Neptune, Fellowship, Desire, William and George, Sparrow, Merchant Adventurer, Scipio, St. John | Council/license orders with passenger counts | No passenger names found in the public sources searched | Useful proof of movement, poor for naming |
| 1640 | Charles of Bristol | Banks says licensed for 250 passengers and sailed 18 June 1640; Winthrop confirms a large ship Charles | Banks names Nathaniel Patten and wife Justine/Justinian Patten | West Country/Dorchester lead, not Norfolk |
| 1641 and after | Reduced migration | American Ancestors / Great Migration project notes migration continued but at much reduced pace | Mostly first-record reconstruction | Poor for ship-list evidence |

### 6.4 Interpretation for John Gurney

For a John Gurney first appearing around Weymouth/Braintree by 1640–1642, the absence of a named passenger hit does **not** strongly disprove immigration in this window. It may mean:

- he traveled on a ship with no surviving passenger list;
- he appears under an unexpected spelling;
- he came as an unnamed servant or dependent;
- he arrived earlier than first appearance;
- he came through Boston/Salem/Hingham/Dorchester before Weymouth/Braintree records catch him.

The most useful route now is acquaintance tracing:

1. Build a name list from Weymouth/Braintree records around John Gurney.
2. Compare it against Bevis, Diligent, Mary Anne, and 1637 Ipswich/Yarmouth passengers.
3. Pay special attention to names that link **Norfolk / Great Ellingham / Hingham** with later **Weymouth / Braintree / Rehoboth**.

## 9. Suggested Next Research Steps

### 9.1 Build the Weymouth/Braintree acquaintance list

Create a spreadsheet with one row per association:

| John Gurney record | Date | Place | Associated name | Role | Source | Notes |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| land grant / court / petition / witness / inventory / marriage / church | yyyy-mm-dd | Weymouth/Braintree/etc. | Person | neighbor/witness/co-signer/etc. | citation | spelling variants |

Then compare associated surnames to the name pools in sections 7.1–7.3.

### 9.2 Prioritize these comparison clusters

1. **Carpenter / Bevis / Weymouth**  
   The clearest ship-to-Weymouth group.

2. **Paine / Great Ellingham / Diligent**  
   Strongest Great Ellingham lead; unresolved servants.

3. **Adcocke / Attleborough**  
   Local Norfolk network likely tied to Stephen Paine’s wife and migration context.

4. **Gedney / Norwich / Mary Anne**  
   Low-probability Gurney variant or comparison surname; keep but do not overweight.

5. **1639 Hingham continuation: Pitts / Foulsham**  
   Important if John Gurney associates with Hingham/Rehoboth families after 1639.

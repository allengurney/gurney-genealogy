# John Gurney (G13): colonial chronology

A plain-reading timeline of John Gurney Sr.'s recorded life in New England, from his arrival to his estate inventory. Births, marriages, and deaths are kept out by design. Citations are in the footnotes.

| Event | Date | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Arrives in Weymouth | c.1636–May 1641; most likely 1638-1640 | Bounded by two records: John is absent from the 1636 Weymouth property list (so he arrived after it), and he is present at Weymouth in the 30 May / 2 June 1641 gunpowder matter (so before it). The later part of that range (1638–1640 during the height of the Great Migration) is when Weymouth's largest influx came.[^mbcr1][^msa] |
| Obtains Weymouth land grants in the East Field and Mill Field | c. 1637–1642; best estimate 1639–1641 | The 8 acre of grants are undated. John is the first grantee of three parcels: two two-acre lots in the East Field and one four-acre lot in the Mill Field. The open-field system was active before 1636, so an earlier start is not excluded, but John is absent from the 1636 Fresh Pond great-lot roll of sixteen householders, which argues against a 1636 presence, while c. 1639–1641 fits Weymouth's new-settler influx around 1640 and his first documented appearance in Weymouth in 1641.[^nash][^msbook] |
| Gets a gunpowder fine cancelled | 30 May / 2 June 1641 | John, with James Ludden and Richard Porter of Weymouth, was fined for not keeping the gunpowder the law required. The three asked the court to cancel the fines, and it did. This is his first solid record in the colony.[^mbcr1][^msa] |
| Surrenders Weymouth land grants in the East Field and Mill Field | c. 1639–1643; best estimate c. 1641–1643 | All three parcels pass from John to other settlers, each recorded as "first granted/given to John Gurny/Gurnie."[^nash][^msbook] |
| Appears in the 1643 Weymouth land inventory | c. 1643 | The parcels are recorded in the c. 1643 possession register (a property inventory), which notes John had held the grants but no longer did. The 1643 date is an estimate: it cannot be earlier than William Fry's death (26 October 1642), yet the register records property events into 21 May 1644, so Nash settles on 1643.[^nash] |
| Moves from Weymouth to Braintree | c. 1640–1645; best estimate c. 1642-1644 | Presumed to fall around the time his Weymouth parcels were surrendered (c. 1641–1643) but before the 1645 Braintree petition.[^weymouth] |
| Settles a £5 debt for the former minister | 30 October 1644 | The colony counts John's bill for £3 16s 4d, together with Thomas Lake's note for £1 3s 8d, as the £5 owed by Mr. Jenner, Weymouth's former minister. It shows John was owed money and dealt with the minister; what the bill was for is not recorded.[^bill] |
| Signs petition for the new plantation at Braintree | May 1645 | Signed the petition, a marker of established Braintree standing.[^petition] |
| Leases a 45-acre Tyng farm (later the Adams Old House) | 1647, for ten years | William Tyng leased John a 45-acre Braintree farm in 1647 for ten years; John kept leasing it from Tyng's daughters Bethia and Mercy until 1662 — one continuous tenancy. The northern part of this "Gurney farm" later became the Adams family seat in Quincy.[^nps][^suffolk4] |
| Receives Weymouth land grant for Great lot no. 16 | 2 February 1652 | A new Weymouth grant in the Second Division, separate from the 1640s East/Mill Field parcels. Like his earlier parcels, this lot (no. 16) was later given to other men, though the record does not say when.[^nash] |
| Gives testimony for Wilson v. Faxon | 17 March 1653 | John provides a deposition in Braintree. He is recorded as "aged about 50".[^faxon] |
| Holds the leased Tyng farm (recorded as 45/48 acres) | 25 May 1653 | The Tyng probate inventory records the same leased farm "in the possession of John Gurney" — listed as "48 Akers land at Brantree, and Marsh" in the NEHGR abstract and as 45 acres in the NPS/Sargent reading — separate from the larger Tyng "Salters Farme" (the ~500-acre Mount Wollaston farm John Read held under a ten-year agreement from 14 April 1639 and Gregory Belcher leased in 1657). *Suffolk Deeds, Liber IV* calls it "in the Occupation and by lease in the hands of John Gurney." Tenant, not owner.[^nehgr30][^suffolk4][^nps] |
| Witnesses and appraises Gregory Baxter's estate, Braintree | June–July 1659 | John witnessed Baxter's will (with Moses Paine and Richard Brackett) and helped take the inventory (7 July 1659), a sign of community trust.[^baxter] |
| Receives grant for 10 acres in Billerica | 15 August 1659 | Granted a ten-acre house-lot on condition he settle it himself; the town accepted "his son Richard" as a fellow inhabitant. He never moved there.[^billerica][^hazen] |
| Appears in the Billerica Dudley Farm rate | 10 September 1659 | John is assessed £2 5s 10d toward the half-payment for the Dudley Farm purchase, though the record shows he did not reside there.[^hazen][^billerica] |
| Receives two Billerica meadow lots | c. 1659 | The town also lays out to John two great-meadow lots: 4¼ acres at James Paterson's bounds and 4½ acres at the mouth of Horse Brook (John Rogers to the south). Both are undated in the town book but fit the 1659 division cycle; whether they were used or lapsed is not recorded.[^billerica] |
| Holds a Billerica share through two other men | November 1659 | In the November 1659 upland division (39 lots among 40 people), Peter Brackett and Joseph Thompson hold John Gurney's share for him. He is named "of Braintree" and is not there himself.[^hazen] |
| Surrenders Billerica grant | December 1659 | Daniel Shed, "in answer for his father John Gurney," gave the house-lot back to the town; half was re-granted to John Hall in March 1659/60. John never relocated.[^billerica] |
| Still leases the Tyng farm | 28 March 1661 | The Tyng estate-division names John as sitting tenant of the same farm, paired with Gregory Belcher on the larger Salter's Farm; he kept leasing it from Tyng's daughters Bethia and Mercy until 1662 — one continuous tenancy from 1647, running alongside the freehold he sold the next year.[^suffolk4][^tyngdated][^nps] |
| Sells Braintree house & orchard to Richard Thayer | 12 February 1662 (16 April 1662) | John sells house, orchard, a five-acre Monatiquot-River parcel, and an adjacent half-acre for £14; possession given 16 April 1662. Deed witnessed by Peter Brackett and John Rockwell.[^thayer] |
| Accepts a Mendon allotment | 1662 | Among Braintree men accepted to allotments at the new Mendon plantation.[^ballou] |
| Gives up the Tyng farm | c. 1662 | The Tyng-farm lease ended around 1662; with the farm given up and his freehold sold, his 1663 estate inventory shows no land.[^nps] |
| Dies; estate inventory taken | c. 1663; inventory 16 March 1663 | Inventory £55 14s 6d, taken by Gregory Belcher, Edmund Quincy, and Thomas Faxon. It included a frontier land interest "at Quinapaug wch we know not."[^spr338] |

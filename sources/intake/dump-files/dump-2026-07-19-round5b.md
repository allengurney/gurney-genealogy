### [L-158 | G22/related | priority 70] Two newly date-fixed Norfolk fines name William Gurney

**Primary-calendar object:** [Some Notes on Medieval English Genealogy, *Feet of Fines: CP 25/1/170/195*](https://www.medievalgenealogy.org.uk/fines/abstracts/CP_25_1_170_195.shtml). The site presents calendar abstracts and links to the document images at AALT.

1. **4 June 1486**, CP 25/1/170/195 no. 2: *William Gurney the elder, esquire* is one of twelve querents. The fine concerns four messuages, 200 acres of land, 20 acres of meadow, 60 acres of pasture, 60 acres of heath, and 40s rent in **Westwick and Worstead**. Exact AALT landing page: [IMG_0020](https://aalt.law.uh.edu/AALT4/CP25%281%29/CP25%281%29no170/IMG_0020.htm).

2. **3 February 1491**, CP 25/1/170/195 no. 24: *William Gurney, esquire* is a co-querent with Thomas Wodehouse, Edmund Harberd, and John Pegeon. The fine concerns a moiety of a messuage called Towtons in **Tittleshall and Godwick**. Exact AALT landing page: [IMG_0042](https://aalt.law.uh.edu/AALT4/CP25%281%29/CP25%281%29no170/IMG_0042.htm).

**Why this matters:** These are earlier, geographically distinct, date-controlled Gurney appearances. The 1486 qualifier “the elder” makes it particularly useful for separating same-name contemporaries. Neither item establishes that the man is Robert G22 or identifies a parent/child relationship.

**Placement label:** `research/topics/norfolk-gurnay-fines-unplaced-medieval.md` and, only after identity comparison, `research/people/g22-robert-gournay-fact-sheet.research.md`.

### [L-241 | G15 | priority 75] Great Ellingham/Hingham documentary routes strengthened

**Creative source 1 (manuscript catalogue):** [CELM, Bodleian Tanner Collection](https://celm.folger.edu/repositories/bodleian-tanner.html) identifies **MS Tanner 175**, a small folio of Henry Gurney’s estate and personal records, verse, comments, and book inventory, c.1570s–1608. It is not the later death-presentment record sought by L-241, but it is a distinct manuscript-level route into Great Ellingham’s estate network.

**Creative source 2 (museum object catalog):** [British Museum H_1856-0429-1](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1856-0429-1), an astronomical watch dated 1600–1610, catalogs a Gournay/Gurney armorial attribution. The catalog’s explanatory pedigree language is secondary; the object is useful for provenance and heraldic research, not for proving descendants.

**Open-access tenure source:** [Blomefield, *History of Norfolk*, Forehoe](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/History_of_Norfolk/Volume_2/Forehoe) states that Gurney’s Manor at Hingham had continued in the family residing at Barsham and Great Ellingham, that Henry Gurney was lord in 1572, and that the author did not find the later transition. The complementary [Shropham passage](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/History_of_Norfolk/Volume_1/Shropham) gives a related later-tenure narrative and describes the manor’s custom that the eldest son is heir.

**Caveat:** The latter historical narratives do not replace the target NRO court-book evidence (WLS V/21) for G15 Henry’s 1621x23 death presentment and heir admission.

**Placement label:** `research/places/great-ellingham.md` as source-route / finding aid, not as a settled genealogical assertion.

The publicly searchable [Mendlesham St Mary marriage transcription PDF](https://essexandsuffolksurnames.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/mendlesham-marriages-grooms-1558-1749.pdf), transcribed from Suffolk Record Office FB159/D/1/1, /3, /4, has both **RIVETT** and **RIVET** entries in the same parish series (1580, 1622, 1654, 1655).

### F-R4.1 — FLAGSHIP: the 1646 Braintree meadows petition FOUND and imaged — Massachusetts Archives vol. 45, p. 11; John Gurney's name among the Braintree founding cohort; the answer slip endorsed 1646 over "Jo: Winthrop: Gov" [R4-1 — MAJOR; closes the round-1 "[Arch. 45]" hunt]

**The document.** Massachusetts Archives Collection (Felt collection), vol. 45 ("Lands, 1622–1726"),

Working transcription (heading + substance; body read at image level, ~90% confident on the passages given):

> "To the hon'd Court the Governor the deputy governor and the rest of ye mag'ts and deputyes now
> assembled at ye Generall Court at Boston — The humble Petition of [those] whose names are heere vnder…
> Whereas there is an ancient order of Court y't such as should haue theire meddowes preserved, and not
> eaten vp with cattell, should fence them round… [we] whereas by Gods providence o'r meddow lotts,
> amounting to about a hundred acres lye round about a small peece of vpland (not aboue eight or tenn
> acres at ye most as we conceiue) which has little grass vpon it, **which is now in the occupation of
> one Tho: Gilbert, who by reson of this court order takes advantage to eate vp o'r meddowes with a
> great heard of cattle, refusing to hearken to any motion that might be thought resonable amongst
> neighbours**… wee having therefore tryed all other meanes w'out effect wee are now forced to be
> troublesome to this hon'd Court for if we should haue o'r meddowes eaten vp as heretofore we should
> not be able to subsist in the place… Our humble petition therefore is that ye s'd Tho: Gilbert may
> eyther be forbid to feede his cattle vppon this patch of vpland or else to lett it vnto vs at a rate
> thought resonable by indifferent men (for it is not in his power to sell it, he being but a tennant
> to it)… yo'rs to Command —"

### F-R4.2 — Boston First Church records (CSM vol. 39) are online at colonialsociety.org and carry ZERO Gurney 1630–1778 — the last Boston-side record class closes against Anderson's "Boston" [R4-2 — MAJOR negative; L-193 residual closed]

In-browser regex sweep of node/1070 (2026-07-03): **zero hits for `g[aeiouvy]rn` (case-insensitive)
and `gourn`** across the entire volume. Coverage checks pass: "Newgate" ×12 (John Newgate the
feltmaker was a member — his household is exactly where the 1636 apprentice John Gurney lived, and
the record still shows no Gurney), "Braintree" ×15, "Brackett" ×1 — the 8 (3mo) 1642 dismissal of

a Deacon." The only Garn- string is Judith Garnett, John Coggeshall's maidservant (adm. 1634; by 1644
wife of Robert Shelley of Barnstable) — a distinct person.

Interpretation: with Boston Town Records + Book of Possessions, Aspinwall, Lechford, Suffolk Deeds
I–III, Boston VR 1630–1699, and now First Church admissions AND baptisms all negative, **every printed
or transcribed Boston record class of John's era has been swept without a Gurney**. The elder John
neither joined nor baptized at Boston's only church of the 1630s–40s. Anderson's "Boston" settlement
attribution is now, as close as the surviving record allows, an artifact of the 1636 Newgate-apprentice
conflation (the apprentice lived in Newgate's Boston household as a servant — a status which itself
would not generate church-admission or property records). The user's transient-lodging scenario
(round-2 Input 2) remains possible but is record-invisible in principle.

### F-R4.3 — L-22 RESOLVED: the 1638 "Mr. Gurney, Soper Lane, St Pancras" is ABEL GOURNEY, citizen & cook of London (d. c. 1658) — not Francis G14; the parish's Gurney presence belongs to the Sir Richard Gurney (Clothworker) orbit [R4-3 — lead closed, distinct family]

   Genealogists, 1931), St Pancras Soper Lane, printed p. 173; BHO

   `inhabitantsoflon0000dale` is borrow-only/403): parish assessed in three runs (Cheapside £365,

   "Mr. Smith £5, **Mr. Gurney £15**, Mr. Darrack £30," among silkmen (two John Parkers, silkman and
   merchant) up to "Mr. Alderman Soames £70."
2. **Identity resolved from the pre-Fire gazetteer** (Keene & Harding, *Historical Gazetteer of London
   before the Great Fire*, BHO `no-series/london-gazetteer-pre-fire`): the Index of Persons (G) carries
   "**Gurney (Gourney), Abel (fl. 1633, d. c. 1658), cit. & cook — St. Pancras Soper Lane 1Aii (r),
   1Aiii (r)**" and "Gurney, Richard (fl. 1633, d. 1647), aldm. & clothworker, kt., bt., f. of Anne &
   Elizabeth — St. Mary Colechurch 21G; St. Olave Old Jewry 1, 2 (r)," with Sir Richard's daughter Anne
   (m. Thomas RICHARDSON) at "St. Pancras Soper Lane 1 & 2" and daughter Elizabeth m. **Sir John
   Pettus** (the Suffolk knight — cf. the EEBO tract on moneys due from Sir Richard Gurney's estate to

   and cook, appears to have occupied the whole property (1Aii-iii) in 1641**, when the owners… leased
   it to him for a term of 30 years in return for a payment of £30 and a rent of £34," and "**In 1642
   William Darracke was assessed for a fifteenth… jointly with Abel Gurney, the occupant of 1Aii-iii,
   for a vault**" — Dale's adjacent "Mr. Darrack" entry pins the identification. "Gurney may have

   identified; not Candidate-B-relevant). New minor person for the one-name map: Abel Gourney,
   citizen & cook, Soper Lane tenant 1638–c.1658.

### F-R4.5 — Rev. Thomas Jenner of Weymouth pinned in Venn: a Fordham, ESSEX farmer's son who ended as minister of COLTISHALL, NORFOLK (1652–58) — the arrival-window Weymouth minister was an eastern-counties man [R4-5 — L-190 substantially resolved; reception-network datum]

Venn, *Alumni Cantabrigienses* Part 1 vol. 2 (D–J), verbatim (file
`p1alumnicantabri02univuoft_djvu.txt`, lines 106560–106569; note the cross-reference at line 50328
"GINNER, THOMAS, see JENNER" — Venn himself carries the *Ginner* form used in the 1644 Mass Bay
accounts entry against John Gurny's bill):

> "JENNER or GINNER, THOMAS. Adm. sizar at Christ's, Feb. 1623-4. S. of Thomas, of **Fordham, Essex**,
> farmer. B. there. Age 17. School, Fordham (Mr Cone). Matric. 1624. **Emigrated to New England, 1635,
> with his father. Settled at Roxbury, Mass., and then at Weymouth as minister, 1636-40. Minister at
> Saco, Maine, 1640. Resided at Charlestown, 1646-9. Returned to England, 1650. Minister at Coltishall,
> Norfolk, 1652-8.** His library was bought for Harvard College. Author of Quakerism Anatomiz'd and
> Confuted, 1670. Will (Dublin) May 26, 1676. (J. G. Bartlett; D.N.B.)"

Readings:
1. **Weymouth's minister during John Gurney's arrival window (1638–41) was an Essex man** — b. c. 1606/7
   at Fordham (3 miles NW of Colchester, on the Suffolk border ~10 miles from the Stour valley), Christ's
   College Cambridge, emigrating 1635. The repo's Weymouth reception picture (L-188: "founding stream
   dominantly WEST COUNTRY") needs this counterweight: the town's *minister* in exactly John's window was
   an eastern-counties puritan. A tailor arriving 1638–40 without West-Country ties had at least one
   prominent East Anglian at Weymouth to be received by — and John's only colony-level paper tie of the
   1640s (the 30 Oct 1644 acceptance of "John Gurny his byl for 3. 16. 4d… for £5 fro Mr Ginner," MBCR
   2:79) is precisely with this man.
2. The Venn entry gives the 1644 bill context an afterlife: Jenner left Weymouth for Saco in 1640, was
   at Charlestown 1646–9, returned to England 1650, and — notably for the Candidate-B geography — took a
   **Norfolk living (Coltishall, 8 miles NE of Norwich) 1652–58**. No claim of a Gurney link there; logged
   as texture.
3. L-190 residual: the Saco/Maine records and any Jenner account book (the bill's purpose) — unchanged.

## 8. Process / tooling lessons (round 4)

1. **`repo_search.py locate` does not support regex alternation** — `"a|b"` patterns silently return
   0 matches while the identical single terms hit. Run one term per call. (Cost this session: one
   false "no Sudbury in Brackett genealogy" that a single-term retry caught.) Consider a tool fix or a
   README warning.
2. **FTS search-API `textDocument` truncates at ~1,550 characters.** For a full transcript, open the
   record page. Don't read a truncated tail as page-end.
3. **FS catalog Film/Digital Notes can misassign volume boundaries across reels** (the "v.45 to/from
   p.70b" split is not where the images say it is). Calibrate with 2–3 das pulls before committing to a
   long walk; FTS hits' group-membership is better evidence of what a film holds than the catalog note.
4. **Felt-volume navigation recipe (reusable for Mass Archives vols 1–239):** FTS-scoped `+<town>`
   probes find the OCR-able *calendar* pages → the calendar gives the volume page number → the volume's
   *name index* (also OCR-able) confirms via first-signer entries → documents start right after the
   index; ~1 page per image; das/v2-pull the bracketed range. Total cost this session: ~15 image pulls
   to land a single 17th-c. document on a 1,590-image film.
5. **TNA Discovery + Ancestry render difficult forenames independently and badly** (Bozoune Crowe =
   TNA "Bozann Crome," Ancestry = absent under both). When a will is *known to exist* (Muskett's proving
   date), anchor the catalogue search on the PLACE, not the name.
6. **Ancestry GPC book databases** (Coldham etc.) are every-word indexed: `name=_Gurn*` wildcards work;
   dbids resolve only by clicking through the card catalog (49150, 61074, 49104 captured here).
7. WebFetch handles BHO *content* pages fine (only /search is bot-gated) — Dale, the gazetteer, Foster,
   and Blomefield vol. 9 were all fetched headlessly this round; the browser was only needed for
   FamilySearch, Ancestry, and colonialsociety regex sweeps.
8. Privacy-guard discipline (round-2 lesson) held: sanitize `? & = : ; %` from all in-browser returns;
   prime `read_network_requests` once before firing das batches.

1609/10 East Dereham) remains the only live, positively-evidenced candidate, and I would now hold it
at ~65–70%** — the companion's ~65% is honest, and I would not push past 70 on current evidence. What
moves me to the upper half of that band after these four rounds:

1. **Every rival has been actively killed, not just neglected.** A (Aylesbury) died of continuous
   English residence; C (Berkhamsted) of a wrong-shaped family; D (London draper) of a 1662 hearth tax;
   Ackworth is capped by the pre-1628 marriage bound; Earsham died testate in 1638. Four rounds of
   colonial sweeps surfaced *no new same-name candidate* — and crucially, the colonial record now shows
   exactly ONE adult Gurney household in Massachusetts before 1660 (the Dorchester "Gurneys" are
   Gurnells; the Hingham "Garnets" are Gardners; Providence's Garnets are distinct; Boston's lone
   Gurney marriage is 1693 and internal to the family). There is no second emigrant John to confuse
   ours with — the two-Johns problem is strictly the 1636 apprentice.
2. **The network keeps landing Norfolk/Suffolk-side without being asked to.** This is the quiet
   cumulative result of rounds 1–4: Winthrop's office handled the West Barsham wardship (1627 letter;
   WARD 7 + C 142 IPMs); the wardship's guardian-widow was a Lewkenor of Denham whose parents' deaths
   Adam Winthrop personally memorialized; the L'Estrange jest-book Gurney anecdotes are kin-sourced;
   the Bury conduit (Chaplin will, £40 to a Mary Gurney servant beside a New England debt) sits in
   Banks's exact milieu; Weymouth's minister in the arrival window was an Essex/Norfolk-career man
   whose £5 the colony collected partly from John's bill; and John's closest Braintree associates
   (the Bracketts) baptize in Brampton Gurdon's own parliamentary borough. None of this *identifies*
   John. All of it makes a Norfolk-corridor tailor's arrival at Weymouth 1638–41 look ordinary rather
   than anomalous — the "why Massachusetts, why there" question has a fully-furnished answer under B
   and only genre-level answers under any other origin.
3. **The record-shape argument.** John's colonial profile — mark-signer (probably), never freeman,
   never church-recorded, tailor, serial smallholder, trusted appraiser/witness — is exactly the
   profile of a man who would leave the *thin* English record Candidate B implies: a motherless
   younger son raised by Rivett kin at East Dereham after Francis remarried to London, apprenticed
   informally (both formal pathways — Merchant Taylors and Norwich — are documented negatives),
   invisible in gentry paper except the one Entry-E baptism. The identification's weakness is
   precisely that it predicts near-invisibility; but the visible bits (Entry E; the naming pattern
   Richard-from-Rivett; the arms memory) all point the same way.

**What keeps me from going above ~70%:** (a) Entry E is a paleographic reading of a damaged line with
±2 years of date slop, not a clean register entry; (b) the wife Mary is unplaced, and the c. 1627–28
English marriage that the children's chronology demands has never surfaced under any variant or
wildcard — a real (if coverage-explicable) hole; (c) the Peter-naming absence in the paternal line
still has no source (Mary's family remains the best guess and is unidentified); (d) nothing yet
*documents the crossing* — no ship, no port record, no letter; and (e) the strongest network evidence
(Gurdon–Winthrop–Wards) concerns the SENIOR branch, and Candidate B's John is two steps from it
(nephew of the wardship line via Francis). The honest statement: **B is the best-evidenced and only
surviving candidate, favored by every independent soft signal, and proven by none.**

### 9.2 What would actually move the number (ranked by unlock-per-effort)

1. **CCEd patron-side search for the 1627 presentation (gated, cheap).** If the Norwich institution
   books show a 1627–28 presentation to West Barsham (or North Barsham) *ratione* the Gurney wardship,
   the whole F9 chain (Gurdon→Winthrop→"mrs. Gurny"/"yong mr. gurney") is documentarily closed, and
   the senior branch's Court-of-Wards dependence on Winthrop's office becomes citable fact rather than
   strong construction. It cannot identify the emigrant, but it hardens the network floor under B.
2. **The Bozoune Crowe will (PROB 11/289/177) and the Gurnay v Cocke Chancery suit (C 5/609/120).**
   Two cheap document pulls that could name living Gurney/Gournay kin 1648–58 from inside the family —
   the class of document where an emigrant kinsman *could* be mentioned (money owed, land interests,
   "my brother/cousin beyond the seas"). Low probability of a direct emigrant mention (~5–10%), but
   these are the only unread family-side testaments left from the right decade, and the Crowe household
   sits 5 miles from East Dereham.
3. **Y-DNA kit 365744 (the other R-M269 Gurney tester).** If a *documented English-line* Gurney shares
   R-Z27053/DF19 with the Braintree line, the surname-level identity of the American family with an
   English Gurney lineage becomes near-certain (though not the specific Candidate-B household). If it
   mismatches, nothing falls — but a match is the single highest-leverage datum available for money
   (a recruit-and-test project, not a records search).
4. **East Dereham/Rivett corridor completion (L-128/L-129):** Francis Rivet's will, the Garveston
   register images, and the c. 1627–28 marriage hunt widened to the Rivett-orbit parishes (Garveston,
   Gressenhall, Shipdham, Whinburgh, Hardingham) under bride-side and wildcard nets. The marriage is
   the missing keystone; if John married where he was raised, it happened within 8 miles of East
   Dereham, and much of that band is index-gapped rather than searched-and-negative — the FMP/NRO
   image-level registers are the honest next mile.
5. **NEHGR 121 Hobart journal remainder + Suffolk court file papers (L-147/L-94 images).** Colonial
   residuals; each could still surprise (a deposition giving a birthplace is the classic unlock — the
   1653 "aged about 50" file has never been read in full at image level).
6. **Mass Archives beyond vol. 45.** The Felt collection is now cheaply walkable (recipe in §8.4).
   Vol. 45's calendar showed several 1640s Braintree items beyond p. 11; vols 10 (Ecclesiastical),
   38B/39 (Judicial), 112 (Towns) plausibly hold more Braintree petitions with signature blocks —
   every one is an associates snapshot, and John signed things (two petitions now known).

### 9.3 Speculations and loose threads I want on the record

- **The apprentice John Gurney (b. c. 1615) probably existed and probably died or dispersed young.**
  After the full Boston sweep, I read him as a real second person (Winthrop's 21 Jul 1636 court note
  is contemporary and specific), most plausibly a Bury-orbit boy from Newgate's Suffolk network,
  gone from the record after his term would have ended (c. Sept 1639). The 1615/Southwark family
  memory is his ghost haunting the emigrant's American descendants via 19th-c. compiler conflation.
  I would not spend further colonial effort on him; the English side (a c. 1615 baptism near
  Horningsheath/Bury, or in Southwark) is the only place he can still be caught, and it matters only
  as hygiene for the conflation argument.
- **The Quinapaug line is the most under-priced colonial thread.** A Braintree tailor dying with an
  unappraisable share "at Quinapaug w'ch we know not" (Providence-jurisdiction speculation land, per
  the Suffolk Deeds recital) implies John put money or standing into a Boston-merchant land venture
  (Foote/Hudson orbit) in his last years — and a Providence John-and-Sarah Gurney cluster appears a
  generation later. If those Providence Gurneys are John Jr.'s or Richard's line exercising an
  inherited right, the family kept paper (or memory) of the share for 30+ years. The Providence
  town-evidence vols not yet swept (the specific volumes carrying the 1695–96 cluster) plus the
  Foote/Hudson deed chain are a self-contained mini-campaign.
- **"Jane Gurnet" is settled, the Bundy "Jane" is settled, Dorchester is settled** — the false-friend
  table (Gurnell, Garnsey, Gardner/Garnet, Garneys, Gurnet-places, Gurnard, Legorne, Surrey-OCR,
  surname-Braintree) is now one of the project's most valuable artifacts. It should be promoted into
  a topic file verbatim; it will save every future researcher a day.
- **The Pettus curio:** Sir Richard Gurney's daughter married Sir John Pettus of Suffolk (F-R4.3) —
  the Pettuses were Norwich/Norfolk gentry. Under-explored whether London's Clothworker Gurneys had
  Norfolk cousinage; almost certainly a distinct family (the DNB line runs to a Croydon origin), but
  if anyone ever tries to tie the emigrant to Sir Richard's clan, the gazetteer/Pettus material here
  is the starting kit — and the arms would differ (Sir Richard's grant was new).
- **Henry Adams as co-petitioner** is a nice narrative fact for the fact sheet: John Gurney petitioned
  with the great-great-grandfather of a U.S. president in 1646, and the farm John leased for 15 years
  became the Adams family seat. The site's readers will love that convergence, and it is now
  primary-sourced end to end.
- **A thing I looked for and did not find, that someone should:** a 1640s Weymouth/Braintree church
  membership for John's WIFE. Women's admissions sometimes survive where men's don't; Braintree's
  book is lost, but stray Braintree admissions appear in other towns' records when families moved.
  If Mary was ever admitted anywhere, her admission entry (with a dismissal-from clause) is the kind
  of record that names an English origin congregation. No colonial index covers this systematically;
  it is a read-the-originals project.

### 9.4 Candid bottom line

After four rounds, the colonial record is effectively **exhausted at the printed/indexed level** — we
have swept every colony-, county-, town-, church-, and court-level series in print or online, and the
emigrant's documentary perimeter (Weymouth 1641 → Braintree → Mendon-rights → probate 1663, plus the
new 1646 petition) is complete and internally consistent. Every remaining colonial unlock is
manuscript-level (court file papers, Felt volumes, church originals). The identity question will not
be settled from Massachusetts; it will be settled — if ever — in Norfolk parish/probate images, the
CCEd/advowson record, or a Y-DNA match. The realistic end-states: (a) a Rivett-orbit marriage record
or a kin-mention in a 1650s Norfolk will lifts B to ~85–90% ("probable" → "highly probable"); (b) the
searches stay negative and B plateaus at ~65–70% as a permanently-caveated best identification —
which is already a defensible published position, and materially better-evidenced than most colonial
origin attributions in print. I'd put roughly 2:1 odds on (b), which is exactly why the fact sheet's
current framing (Candidate B as favored working hypothesis, plainly caveated) is the right editorial
posture, and why the remaining spend should go to the short list in §9.2 rather than further breadth.

The round-1 scope map §0 was too dismissive of counties: the counties may not have existed at
the time BUT many records subsequently rolled up to county institutions, and records/sources
became classified, associated, or catalogued under the LATER county boundaries (e.g.,
FamilySearch collection place-fields tag colonial items with modern county names; 19th-c.
transcription series are organized by modern county). Search strategy must therefore include
the later-county and Boston framings as first-class search axes, not just the

### F-R2 — Hobart's "John Garnet maried at Boston" (10 Apr 1651) = the Hingham GARDNER/GARNET family, not a Gurney [Input 3 resolved]

*History of the Town of Hingham* (1893) genealogies (archive.org `historyoftownofh02hing`,
`historyoftownofh03hing`) carry a large family section headed **"GARDNER (Gariver; Garnet)"**
(vol. 2, djvu line ~19535: "the early Garners, or Gardners, of Boston is uncertain...") with
a "GARNET" section head in vol. 3 (~line 27307). The Hingham Garnets run continuously from
John Garnet (m. Mary; he d. by 18 June 1669 when "Mrs. Mary Garnet, the wid. of John Garnet"
remarried Nathaniel [—], vol 2 ~9896) through Stephen & Sarah (Warren) Garnet, John & Rebecca,
Samuel, Benjamin, Simeon etc. deep into the 18th c.; the compilers repeatedly gloss "Garnet,
or Gardner." The Hobart-journal 10 April 1651 Boston marriage is this family's progenitor —
consistent with Pope's Braintree-side entry "Mary b. Oct. 16, 1653, (m. John Garnet)" (round-1
F-note; Hingham vol 3 ~16997 has the same marriage, 25 Feb 1682/3 — a second-generation John).
**Verdict: Garnet at Hingham = Gardner-family surname form; a distinct family; NOT Gurney and
NOT evidence of a Gurney at Hingham.** The Hingham "no Gurney" negative (L-188) stands — with
the useful corollary that any "Gurney-ish" string in Hingham records should be checked against
GARDNER/GARNET first. (Incidental 18th-c. Gurney×Hingham ties for the later family map:
"Joseph Gurney of Wey., int. of m'ge, May 3, 1718" (vol 3 ~8471); a Job [—] "m. a Gurney, and
lived some time at West Hing." (vol 2 ~11976); Sophia Gurney of Abington m. c. 1800 (vol 3
~16470); index "Gurney, ii. 148; iii. 110, 210".)

### F-R3 — Dorchester resolved: "Goodman Gurney of Dorchester" = the GURNELL family; "Jane Gurnet" = Jane Gornell, later "Mrs. Burgesse alias Gurnet" [Inputs 5 + round-1 F3 resolved]

Dorchester town records (Fourth Report of the Boston Record Commissioners, 1880, archive.org
`fourthreportofre04bost`) and *Records of the First Church at Dorchester 1636–1734* (1891,
`recordsoffirstch00firs`), full variant sweeps 2026-07-01:

1. **One family, many spellings.** Dorchester's own town book renders John Gurnell as
   **Gornell, Gornill, Gornehill, Gurnell, Gurnel, gornett, Gurnett, Gurnet, Gurnit** —
   including "John Gurnet" in the 13 (12) 1653 fence-viewers list, "dew from John Gurnett for
   the Cowe" (1650s accounts), and "Gurnit baylife." He is a town officer (supervisor of
   highways, committee work, school donor; selectman-adjacent), living into the 1670s. The
   First Church index groups "Gomell, Gomhill, Gornehill: bro., 191; John..." separately from
   "Garnesey/Garnsey" (Henry Garnsey's family = Guernsey) and from "Gurnet: Mr, 25; Mrs.,
   159."
2. **Therefore the Suffolk County Court's 1676/7 "Goodman Gurney of Dorchester" (p. 481,
   Prise surety — user Input 5) is John Gurnell**, the county clerk using exactly the
   Gurnet/Gurney-drift Dorchester's own records show. No independent Gurney household existed
   at Dorchester. (The court's General Index kept "Gurney,——, 481" separate from its
   Gornell/Gurnell entries — an index artifact, not two families.)
3. **Jane resolved.** Town-record editorial footnote (4th Report, ~line 22181): "**Jane
   Gornell, widow of John Gornell, married John Burge.** Inscription on her gravestone reads:
   'Jeane Gornell..' — See N.E. Hist. & Genea. Register, Vol. 4." And First Church record,
   11 May 1709: the church empowers attorneys to recover "a Certain Legacy of Three Pounds
   bequeathed by **mrs. Burgesse alias Gurnet** to be laid out in a Piece of Plate for the
   said Church." So Jane (Gornell) Burge(ss) was still "alias Gurnet" to the church.
   **Round-1 F3's "Jane Gurnet" (legatee of Margery Laver, Dorchester 1664) = Jane, wife of
   John Gornell/Gurnell — NOT a Gurney.** Do not create a G13-family Jane.
4. **A Burge collision to flag, not merge:** Jane Gornell's second husband "John Burge" vs
   Grizell (Gurney)'s fifth husband John Burge (m. 1667, Grizell d. 1669). Chronology permits
   one serial widower (Gurnell alive 1674; Jane widowed after that; Burge free after 1669),
   but Burge/Burges is common — verify in Torrey/NEHGR 4 before equating. Either way the
   GURNEY-widow → Burge and GURNELL-widow → Burge parallel is a documented source of
   compiler tangle between the two families.
5. Curios logged: First Church "Gurnet Price Son of Francis Price" (a child with given name
   Gurnet?, ~line 16143 — one-look item); Henry Kibbey on the 1654 Dorchester fence list
   (Grizell's 4th husband's Dorchester context); Braintree's Moses Paine & Gregory Belcher
   appear in an adjacent inter-town entry.

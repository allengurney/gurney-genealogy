## 4. Campaign-level findings ledger

### What the campaign established

- **East Dereham had a named Gurney widow's former copyhold before 1627.** Pullable East Dereham manor-court leaves date the Ellingfield abuttal to 1627 and repeat it in 1636 as land formerly of Margery/Margaret Gurney widow. That independently corroborates the 1602–03 ANF will index and makes a pre-existing Gurney household at Francis's children's baptism parish real, rather than a speculative explanation.
- **The Norfolk 1585–1650 probate field was materially clarified.** The master index supplied a complete in-window Gurney event census; its high-value new contextual results were Margery Gurnye, widow of East Dereham (1602–03), Thomas Gurnay Esq. of Great Ellingham (1615–16 inventory), and Alexander Gurney of Tasburgh (1617 administration). The unopened primary documents still determine relationship questions.
- **The direct Rivett succession proof class was correctly isolated.** Register images, older probate, and indexed variants do not choose between Margaret, daughter of Francis (1586), and Margaret, daughter of Robert (1577/8). A copyhold death/admission record is the remaining direct route.
- **A false bridge was removed.** Roger Petman’s 1617 administration belongs in the North Walsham orbit, not the Garveston/Podmer world.
- **The Yarmouth Edward Rivett shortcut did not materialize in the authenticated Findmypast index.** The 33-row result set begins in 1725, not 1653, but the collection's Yarmouth gap prevents a burial nonexistence claim.
- **A wider authenticated Rivett result view adds a possible local context, not a bridge.** Its William Rivett, East Dereham, 1593–1640 entry is chronologically and geographically interesting beside the East Dereham Gurney material, but the index screen alone cannot identify the person or make him a relative of either Margaret candidate.

### What it did not establish

- It did **not** identify John Gurney G13's parents, birthplace, or an immigration-family document. The East Dereham Gurney presence gives a plausible local context for Francis G14's household, not a document naming John as Francis's son.
- It did **not** determine which Garveston Margaret Rivet married Francis Gurney in 1611. The model remains a family-level identification with a candidate-level fork of roughly **55% Francis's 1586 daughter / 40% Robert's 1577/8 daughter / 5% other**. Do not collapse that to a named parentage without the succession evidence.
- It did **not** validate the proposed Henry Gurney–Allen sister-heir reading in the 1639 Whinburgh leaf. The fresh re-crop preserves it for specialist review but leaves the relationship language unresolved.

## 6. Source collisions and false positives

- Findmypast's blank or masked session output is an **access failure**, never a record negative; the signed-in 33-row screenshot supersedes the masked 33-result claim.
- Findmypast Norfolk collections are parish-selective; Great Yarmouth absence is not a true negative.
- **William Rivett, East Dereham, 1593–1640** is a source-collision risk: it is a multi-year index display without its underlying record image or relationship fields. Do not equate him with Francis Rivett, Robert Rivett, or a parent of Margaret merely from parish and era.
- Whinburgh file names and FTS dates are inverted against visible court headings. Use the ARK and heading: `S3HT-6X4K-X5` = 6 Sep. 1639; `S3HT-6X45-XZ` = 26 Oct. 1648.
- FamilySearch FTS name extraction is partial on the Whinburgh film and cannot clear Rivett/Podmer tenancy by a zero count.
- The Petman 1617 administration is a North Walsham false bridge, not Podmer/Rivett kin evidence.
- ANF/Ancestry record indexes establish court, residence, and event type; they do not establish a relationship without the manuscript image.
- FranceArchives' Gournay châtellenie material and the Nord Gaucher de Gournai inventory are geographic/toponymic collisions, not Gournay-line evidence.

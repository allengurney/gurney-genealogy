### 2.6 [FINDING — MAJOR, re-dates and completes L-200] The "clerk convict" episode is January 1463, not 1460/1 — and the full lost-letter text says the servant killed the bishop's cousin "by commaundment of his maister," with BOTH master and man imprisoned
Re-read of the retained Gairdner vol. IV full text (`sources/corpus_supplement/paston-letters-gairdner-vol4-fulltext.txt`, lines 3387–3523):
1. The carrying letter is **Margaret Paston to John Paston, Gairdner's marginal date 19 JANUARY 1463** (not "Playter, c. Jan 1460/1" as the repo topic file currently states). Gairdner's own footnote: the letter concerns the coming election of knights of the shire "for the Parliament which met on the 29th April 1463… it is quite certain that it was written during Edward's reign." Text: "Skypwith shall telle you suche tydyngs as bethe in this contre, and of Thomas Gornay and of his man; hym self is clerk convicte, and hys man is hangyn; **ye shall here her after what they and oder wer purposyd to a do to her master**." (Note the tail sentence — "what they *and other* were purposed to have done to their master" — a wider conspiracy flavour the repo capture omitted.)
2. Immediately following (Gairdner no. 537, "Abstract," Thomas Playter to John Paston, marginal date JAN 1463; Gairdner note: "These extracts are quoted by Fenn **from a letter now lost**, in reference to what is said in the last letter about Thomas Gurney and his man"): "Please your maistership wete, that as for **my Lord of Norwich cosyns deth, Thomas Gurneys man hath confessed that he slewe hym by commaundment of his maister**, and confessed over that the same dager he slewe hym with, **he kest in a sege [a jakes/privy] whiche is founden and taken up al to-bowyd [bent together], for he cowde not breke it, and in prison is bothe he and his maister.** Also on Thursday next after Cristemasse was a man slayn, by whom no man woot, nor what he is that was slayn no man knowe, his face is so mangled."
Consequences:
- **Re-date the episode to Christmastide 1462/January 1463** (murder plausibly late 1462; trials by 19 Jan 1463). Bishop still Walter Lyhert (1445–72).
- The servant's confession makes Thomas Gurney the **principal who ordered the killing**; the dagger-in-the-privy detail is forensic-grade colour; both were imprisoned before the man hanged and Gurney pleaded clergy.
- **"Clerk convict" does NOT imply he was actually in orders** — by the 1460s any literate layman could claim benefit of clergy via the neck-verse; an esquire like G20 Thomas Gournay II could plead it. The topic file's inference that the plea "points instead toward a cleric" over-weights the term. The identity question (G20 vs. the 1435 cleric vs. another Thomas — incl. the London 'yoman' Thomas Garney of §2.2) stays open, but the balance is now more even, and the 1463 date sits squarely inside G20's floruit.

Gurnay" appears among proposed county commissioners "at exactly this date" — if that list is also Jan 1463, a man in prison for commanding murder makes an implausible commissioner; the two items' relative dates now matter. ## 6. Speculation / ideation / brainstorming

- **The Whinburgh-with-Members thesis (my strongest structural inference this turn):** Garveston had no resident lord; its copyholders answered to Whinburgh-with-Members. Every Garveston Rivett generation 1564–1755 should therefore appear in ONE manorial archive — and that archive survives nearly intact (rolls, rough books, surrenders, verdicts, rentals, survey 1565). The L-128 problem has felt source-starved because the parish/probate layers genuinely are starved; the manorial layer was simply never opened. Expected documents if Francis Rivet held copyhold: his own admission (1590s–1600s), any surrender to uses (a common will-substitute — exactly what a man who "left no will" might have used!), his death presentment + heir admission. **A surrender-to-uses would be a de-facto will naming his children.** That single document could prove L-128 outright.
- **Why Francis Rivet may have left no probate:** if his land was all copyhold of Whinburgh-with-Members, a deathbed surrender to uses made a will unnecessary. The absence of probate is itself weak evidence he was a copyholder — reinforcing the manorial route.
- **The 1648 "Henry Gurnip" entry (§3.2):** if the image reads "Henry Gurney," the inheritance-via-sister structure implies he died without issue, and the sister's married name (Allen?) becomes a checkable fact against G15's daughters.

## 2. Findings

### F-R3.1 — Muskett read (W1): the Gurdon-of-Assington pedigree grounds the whole ward-line network; Muriel Gurdon was a Sedley–KNYVETT granddaughter; NO Gurney appears in Muskett [MAJOR context]

Source: Joseph James Muskett, *Suffolk Manorial Families*, vol. 1 (Exeter: William Pollard, 1900),
archive.org `suffolkmanorialf01musk` (djvu.txt swept in full, both regex nets, 2026-07-02).

1. **Printed p. 285 = Brampton Gurdon's will** (19 Oct 1647, P.C.C. 68 Pembroke, proved
   1649/50): "…my dearly beloued wife my best coach and five horses and all my plate marked
   with Sidleyes and … plates with Gurdons and Sidleyes Coatts…"; "vnto my loveiug brother
   Martin Sedley Esqr fforty shillings to buy him a Ring." (djvu lines ~41446–41565).
2. **Pedigree chart, pp. 286–288 ("Gurdon of Assington" / "Gurdon of Letton"):** Brampton
   Gurdon of Assington, M.P. for Sudbury 1620, High Sheriff of Suffolk 1628; m. (1) Elizabeth,
   dau. and coheir of Edward Barrett of Belhouse, Aveley, Essex (d. 1603); m. (2) **"Muriel,
   dau. of Martin Sedley of Morley, co. Norfolk, Esq., who made Will, P.C.C. 53 Wingfield,
   12 May 1608 [proved 5 March 1609], by Abigail, dau. of Sir Thos. Knyvett of Ashwelthorpe;
   sister to Sir Ralph Sedley; 2d wife; ob. 1661, aet. 78."** Muskett's addenda (p. 811)
   corrects: "Abigail Knyvett, who married Martin Sedley, was daughter of **John** Knyvett
   and sister of Sir Thomas." So the Gurdon–Sedley household that produced the 1627
   Warford/Gurny letter was ALSO a **Knyvett-of-Ashwellthorpe** granddaughter's household —
   Ashwellthorpe sits ~3 mi from Morley/Wymondham in the same S-central-Norfolk corridor
   as Great Ellingham/Hingham/Deopham. Muriel b. c. 1583; her brothers were Martin Sedley
   the younger of Morley (in whose house Warford taught 8 years) and Sir Ralph Sedley.
3. **Muskett p. 811 addenda also notes:** "A number of letters from Brampton Gurdon to John
   Winthrop, Governor of Massachusetts, are to be found in the Fourth Series of the
   Massachusetts Historical Society's Collections" (i.e., the Gurdon–Winthrop correspondence
   continued into the colonial period — those texts are inside the MHS Winthrop Family Papers
   Digital Edition already swept in round 1: exactly one Gurney document, PWF01d256); and
   "Sir Richard Saltonstall should be styled 'of Yorkshire'" (the Muriel Gurdon × Richard
   Saltonstall Jr. 1633 marriage tie is to the Yorkshire/MassBay Saltonstalls, not the Essex
   family). John Gurdon was M.P. for Ipswich 1640.
4. **Negative: no early-modern Gurney in Muskett vol. 1.** Both regex nets over the full
   volume: the only Gurn-forms are 19th-c. Thickthorn Gurneys (Reginald Gurney, Hugh Gurney
   1896, in the Cullum/Horncastle pedigree) and the distinct Suffolk **GARNEYS of
   Mickfield/Boyland** family ("Garneys of Hoxford," armorial notes ~p. 189) — add GARNEYS
   (Suffolk gentry) to the false-friend table beside Garnsey/Guernsey. No Gurdon–Gurney
   marriage or Gurney cross-reference exists in Muskett; the Gurdon–Gurney link runs solely
   through the Sedley/Lewkenor/Wards channel documented in F9/F-R1.

### F-R3.2 — Will of Martha Heigham of Denham, 1591 (P.C.C. 55 Nevell): Martha Lewkenor (the future Mrs. Gurney of West Barsham) named as a minor granddaughter; the Denham puritan matrix in one document [MAJOR — English side, Candidate-B network]

Muskett vol. 1, printed pp. 289–290 ("Will of Martha Heigham, 1591," djvu lines ~43898–43942).
Martha Heigham of Denham, Suffolk, widow of Thomas Heigham of Denham Esq., will dated
8 Nov 1591, proved 30 July 1593. Verbatim highlights:

> "To Dorothye Lewkenor, **Martha Lewkenor**, Anne Lewkenor, Hester Lewkenor, Suzan Lewkenor,
> Sara Lewkenor, and to Elizabeth Lewkenor daughters of **Suzan Lewkenor my daughter** at
> their ages of twenty and one yeares or their severall dayes of mariage… Also I giue to the
> Master and ffellowes of Emanuell Colledge in Cambridge one hundred poundes so that (they)
> founde a perpetuall Scholershipp, and doe bestowe the same vppon Timothy Pricke the sonne
> of Robert Pricke Preacher of the worde of god at Denham… Also I bequeathe to the
> Churchewardens of Denham… my greate Englishe Bible of the Geneva translation for the vse
> of the parrishioners… I appointe Edwarde Lewkenor of Denham Esquier my sonne in lawe my
> sole and onely executor."

Readings:
1. **Sir Edward Lewkenor of Denham's wife was Susan Heigham** (dau. of Thomas Heigham of
   Denham; the Heighams of Barrow kin — "To Sr John Higham of Barowe a golde ringe that was
   my husbandes"), and **Martha Lewkenor — who married Thomas Gurney III of West Barsham —
   was one of seven Lewkenor sisters, under 21 in Nov 1591** (so b. after c. 1570, plausibly
   c. 1575–1590; consistent with a c. 1605 marriage per DG II and childbearing to c. 1607).
2. The godly-household texture is first-class Candidate-B context: the Emmanuel College
   scholarship for Timothy Prick(e), son of **Robert Pricke, the famous puritan "Preacher of
   the worde of god at Denham"** (the Lewkenors' own presented minister), the Geneva Bible to
   Denham church, Sir Robert Jermyn of Rushbrooke as settlement trustee. The household that
   raised Mrs. Martha (Lewkenor) Gurney was the model puritan-gentry household of Suffolk.
3. Chain for the companion: G13's putative first cousin Edward Gournay's mother Martha was a
   Lewkenor of Denham whose grandmother's 1591 will endowed an Emmanuel College scholarship —
   Emmanuel being the seminary of the Massachusetts founders — and whose family's minister
   dynasty (the Prickes) were near neighbours of the Winthrop/Gurdon circle.

### F-R3.3 — Winthrop Papers printed vol. I (W2): the letter is at printed p. 363; the MHS editors did NOT identify Warford or Mrs. Gurny; a second Gurdon Wards petition precedes it [resolves round-2 §5.4]

Source: *Winthrop Papers*, vol. I, 1498–1628 (Boston: MHS, 1929), archive.org item swept in
full (djvu.txt; the Gurdon letter at djvu lines ~27010–27060). Findings:

1. The Brampton Gurdon → John Winthrop letter (20 Oct 1627) is at **printed p. 363** (running
   head "1627] BRAMPTON GURDON 363"); "W. 1. 29" in the round-1 citation is the *manuscript*
   number (Winthrop mss. vol. 1, no. 29), not a page. Correct any future citation to:
   Winthrop Papers I:363.
2. The letter carries three editorial footnotes: fn. 50 = "W. 1. 29; L. and L., I. 220";
   fn. 51 identifies ONLY Sedley ("Martin Sedley the younger, of Morley, co. Norfolk, brother
   of Muriel, wife of Brampton Gurdon. Supra, p. 64, note 40; Visitacion of Norffolk (H.S.,
   Pub., xxxii), 243; Muskett, 285."); fn. 52 = the sheriff-pricking custom. **Neither Warford
   nor "mrs. Gurny"/"yong mr. gurney" is identified anywhere in the printed volume** — the
   index reads only "Gurney, Mrs., 363" and "Gurney, ——, son of the preceding, 363," and
   "Warford" appears nowhere outside the letter itself. The repo's F9 identification (Martha
   née Lewkenor + Edward Gournay of West Barsham) is original, unanticipated by the MHS editors.
3. **Immediately preceding at printed pp. 362–363: "The humble petition of Brampton Gourdon"
   (W. 1. 20; L. and L., I. 219)** — Gurdon petitions (the Master of the Wards) for the
   wardship of the son and heir of John Brent of Cossington, Somerset, whose mother Elizabeth
   (Worth) Brent "then was and yet is a popish recusant Convict." Gurdon was a repeat Court
   of Wards client in Winthrop's office in exactly this period — the 1627 Gurney-presentation
   letter is one instance of an established Gurdon–Winthrop Wards channel, strengthening the
   round-2 "Class 4 patronage/legal network" framing.
4. Sweep note: the case-insensitive net returns heavy false positives from "**Legorne**"
   (Livorno) in the John Winthrop Jr. Mediterranean letters — add to the false-friend table
   for printed-series sweeps. No other Gurney-variant in the volume.

(printed p. 210) sits inside the year block headed **"1693."** (djvu line 25492; next year

Shapley in the same volume: only "Benjamin of Nicholas & Anne Shapley born 7th month"
(printed p. 21, 1640s block) and the index line "Shapley, 21, 210" — Boston's register gives
Sarah no birth entry, so her Torrey "dau Thomas" filiation needs an outside source
(Charlestown/Kittery Shapleigh cluster; see §5 follow-ups). Torrey's "Little Compton RI"
tag gives the couple a post-1693 Rhode Island continuation to chase in Little Compton records.

### F-R3.5 — Dedham town records: "Goodman Gurney of Dorchester, a Tanner" (2 Jan 1670/1) — the Dorchester Gurnell figure again, now with an occupation; Dedham/Roxbury/Muddy River otherwise CLEAN [W11]

*The Early Records of the Town of Dedham* vol. 4 (town meeting; archive.org copy swept
2026-07-02), printed p. 198, General Town meeting dated "2: 11: 1670" (= 2 Jan 1670/1):

> "The Towne being at p'sent enformed that Thomas Walker, last Apprentice to **Goodman
> Gurney of Dorchester, a Tanner** haue shewed his willingnes to set vp his Trade in this
> Towne. the care heereof is left to the select men this daye to be chosen who are empowered
> to allowe his settlem't in Towne if vpon further information they shall be encouraged so
> to doe" (volume index: "GURNEY— Goodman. 198.")

Reading: a third independent record stream (after Dorchester's own books and the Suffolk
County Court 1676/7 surety, round 2 F-R3) rendering the Dorchester GURNELL householder as
"Gurney" — and adding that he was a **tanner** who trained apprentices (Thomas Walker was his
"last" apprentice, suggesting Gurnell was old or recently dead/retired by Jan 1670/1; John
Gurnell was still alive as guardian in the 1674 court record, so "last" more likely = most
recent). This hardens the false-friend rule: ANY "Goodman Gurney of Dorchester" 1650–1680 =
Gurnell, and gives the tanner occupation as a new discriminator (John G13 was a tailor;
his sons husbandmen/soldiers — no Gurney tanner existed).

Clean negatives (both regex nets, full volumes, 2026-07-02): **Roxbury** (Boston Rec. Comm.
6th report — Roxbury land and church records): ZERO Gurn-variant. **Dedham** vol. 3 (town
meeting 1636–1659): ZERO. **Muddy River / Brookline records 1634–1838**: ZERO. (Dedham vol. 4's
only hit is the p. 198 Dorchester-Gurnell entry above.) John G13's documentary footprint thus
still ends at the Braintree/Weymouth/Boston-probate perimeter — no Dedham/Roxbury/Muddy River
spillover for him or any colonial Gurney.

## Findings and analysis (the lossless record — facts, reasoning, and current best thinking)

**1. Porter identity — image read now tips the conflict to Richard (not clinched).** The 1641 gunpowder-fines matter survives in two primary traditions: the printed Massachusetts Bay *court order* (MBCR 1:331, 2 June 1641) names **John Porter**, while the underlying **petition**, calendared in the Massachusetts State Archives colonial collection (FamilySearch film 007702977, image 947), is dated **30 May 1641** and names **"John Gurny, Richard Porter, and James Ludden of Weymouth … remission of the fines laid upon them for [want of gunpowder]."** Read at full resolution (das/v2, 2026-06-27), the forename plainly reads **Richard** — so the repo's earlier "Richard" note is verified at image level, not a transcription artefact. *Best thinking:* the petition is the petitioners' own document and tags the group "of Weymouth," which fits **Richard Porter** (the documented Rev. Hull-company Weymouth settler) far better than John Porter (a Hingham/Salem line); the printed order's "John" is the likelier slip, and the 1878 Porter genealogy merely repeats the printed order. Weight shifts to Richard, but the calendar is an index, not the petition — the clincher is the petition document itself (the calendar's page reference into the Archives volume). If Richard holds, John's cleanest 1641 associate is a Weymouth/Hull comparator, mildly reinforcing the early-Weymouth-settler reading below.

**2. Weymouth land is a 1643 holding, and implies early-1640s residence.** Nash's Appendix C is explicitly the **1643 partial record of Weymouth property owners** ("Property Owners and Description of Property in 1643… First Division, 1636; Second Division, 1651"). Within it, John's two-acre East Field parcel (p. 258) and four-acre Mill Field parcel (p. 278) are "first granted to John Gurny/Gurnie" but **already held by other men** (Randoll; the lot under Richards) by 1643 — so he was an *original* grantee whose parcels had passed on by 1643. A separate later grant — great lot no. 16 — is the **2 February 1651-52** Second Division (p. 282). *Reasoning:* early-Massachusetts towns divided land among admitted **inhabitants**, so John's place in the pre-1643 division is itself evidence he **lived at Weymouth in the early 1640s** — the town history's "whether he ever resided in Weymouth is a matter of doubt" understates it. By 1643 his parcels already stand under others, so he had begun divesting as he prepared to move to Braintree (there by 1645; the move itself falls c. 1642–1645, since the parcels were already reassigned by the 1643 record). This also fixes the **arrival window**: present at Weymouth by the 30 May / 2 June 1641 gunpowder matter and an original grantee pre-1643, he reached New England in **1638–early 1641** (and cannot be later) — the tail of the Great Migration, which fell sharply after 1640. The place files' loose "by 1642-44" derives from Nathaniel Adams' entry ("land granted between 1642 and 1644") and Nash's 1643 record; it is **sharpened to 1643, not removed**.

**3. The Buckinghamshire (Aylesbury-Vale) neighbour cluster.** Profiling John's actual East/Mill Field neighbours shows his parcels sat inside a coherent **Aylesbury-Vale Buckinghamshire** settler cluster threaded with a **West-Country/Dorchester** stream. A 1688 sworn deposition (Mass. Archives 129:16) has Jonas Humphrey and Robert Randall, both of Weymouth, testifying they lived in **Wendover, Bucks** and knew **Enoch Hunt of the parish of Lee**. Robert Randall (holds John's East Field parcel, Grants 258) was of **Wendover**; William Brandon (bounds John's Mill Field, 278) of **Ashton Clinton**; the Hunts of **Lee**; Jonas Humphrey of **Wendover**. The two streams had intermarried — **Ephraim Hunt married Thomas Richards's daughter** (Richards held the ground containing John's Mill Field parcel) and named Richards attorney for Hunt lands at **Beaconsfield/Amersham, Bucks**. The cluster largely arrived **via Dorchester**. The full extract set is in `sources/corpus_supplement/history-of-weymouth-bucks-cluster-extracts.md` (already written this thread).

**4. Counterweight — associations are regionally mixed; the Bucks thread is reception, not origin.** John's *Braintree* life is an **Essex** network: he leased 48 acres within the **Tyng of Stanford Rivers** estate, and son-in-law **Daniel Shed came from Finchingfield, Essex** (in Braintree by 1643, m. Mary Gurney 1647). So his documented colonial networks span three English regions — Bucks (Weymouth neighbours), Essex (Braintree estate/son-in-law), Norfolk (favoured Candidate B). *Best thinking:* association is a **weak origin signal** here; the Bucks cluster is a reception context to **test**, on a par with the Essex Braintree network, not an origin claim. It does **not** displace Candidate B (East Dereham, Norfolk, ~55–60%), which rests on direct evidence (the East Dereham baptism). (It is unconnected to the medieval Gournay manor of Wendover — a 12th-century holding under G34 — which must not be conflated.)

**5. The Hingham bridge — the more promising reception model for Candidate B.** Early Weymouth was bi-regional (West Country + Bucks), so a Norfolk John would be an outsider to both **unless** he entered through the colony next door. **Hingham, Massachusetts** — under ten miles from Weymouth and abutting John's parcels on its town line — was settled by the **Norfolk Hingham / Diligent-of-Ipswich (1638) cohort** (Gilman, Peck, Cushing, Folsom, Paine of Great Ellingham, Sutton of Attleborough, Lincoln of Wymondham) out of the very parishes framing the Henry/Francis Gurney Norfolk world, and the same corridor as the Ann Gurney × John Gilman marriage. A Norfolk-corridor arrival landing at Hingham MA and stepping to adjacent Weymouth is a clean Candidate-B reception path requiring no Weymouth-stream membership. The open empirical test (lead L-188): how regionally concentrated were Weymouth's landholders, and how common was a Hingham→Weymouth local move?

### The Weymouth Gurney family's continuity — the Abington/Bridgewater branch

The *History of Weymouth* (vol. 2, "Extinct Weymouth Families") sketches the family's arc: "The Gurneys were also an ancient family, Richard, of Weymouth, having children born to him … from 1656 to 1667. The family … continues its name for four generations, the last survivor being **Jacob, who died in 1793, when the name became extinct in Weymouth**. It is yet green and flourishing in towns to the south, **Abington and Bridgewater**, branches from the main trunk that had its root in this town." It adds, from Hobart's *History of Abington*, that "**John was the original settler** [of the Abington branch], having sons Richard, David and Nathan" — the John who moved from Weymouth to south Abington about 1690 (a son of Richard² G12), distinct from the emigrant John Gurney Sr. (G13). The Weymouth line ran Richard² → Zachariah³ (held the Front Street property by about 1700) → Jacob (d. 1793, name extinct in Weymouth); the southern continuation — including the direct line through Benjamin G11 — carried the name to Abington, Bridgewater, and Middleboro.[^how-vol2-extinct]

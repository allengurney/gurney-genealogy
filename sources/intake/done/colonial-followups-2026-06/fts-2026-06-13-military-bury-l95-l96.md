**Record B — "John Gurny his byl" (Massachusetts Bay Colony Military Records 1642-1649)**

- Collection: Massachusetts Bay Colony. Military Records 1642-1649
- Film: 008713629 (multi-volume film; vol. 2-5 = Military records 1642-1686)
- Image 48 → ARK `3:1:3QHV-J3DW-8YB3` — the actual bill/account page
- Image 169 → ARK `3:1:3QHV-V3DW-8999-G` — printed index page ("Gurny, John, 79")

What the bill says (verbatim from machine transcript, image 48):

> "THE MASSACHUSETTS BAY IN NEW ENGLAND. John Gurny his byl for 3. 16. 42, & Tho: Lake his note for 1. 3. 84, are 1644. accepted for 5 fro Mr Ginn."

The index entry on image 169 reads: "Gurny, John, 79" (page 79 of the volume corresponds to image 48 in this film).

Interpretation: The "byl" is an archaic/dialectal spelling of "bill." The entry records that John Gurny's bill (for 3 pounds 16 shillings dated 1642) and Thomas Lake's note (for 1 pound 3 shillings) were accepted in 1644 for £5 from Mr. Ginn. This is a colonial accounts entry — Gurny had a debt or account settled through an intermediary ("Mr Ginn") as part of colony military finance. The date "3. 16. 42" is most likely 16 March 1641/2 (Old Style). Pre-downloaded file `ma-military-1642-1649-bill-gurny.jpg` = image 48.

### L-96 — Bury St Edmunds, Suffolk — Parish Records 1561-1800

**Film and collection:** Film 007413310, "Bury St. Edmunds, St. James parish registers" (681 images). All 20 Gurn* hits belong to St James parish, Bury St Edmunds.

**Case question 1 — Did a John Gurney household exist at Bury St Edmunds in the 1630s?**

**NO.** The film-scoped sweep of all Gurn* variants returns NO baptism, burial, or marriage record for a John Gurney in the 1630s at St James. The 1630s entries consist of:
- Thomas Gurney & Lidda Broddish — marriage, 1633-1635 (image 297)
- Juda Gurny dau. of Thomas — baptism, c.1633-1634 (image 60)
- Juda Gurney — communicant/confirmation list, 1633-1634 (image 494) [likely same person]

No John Gurney father-of-children appears in the 1630s at Bury. The name "John Gurney" in the film appears as: (a) a witness at two 18th-century marriages, (b) an index entry in a 19th-century published index at page references 142 and 196/232, and (c) "John Gurny son to Thomas" baptized c.1653-1655 (image 77). A John Gurney with wife Elizabeth has a son William baptized 1665 (image 86) — that John was active at Bury in the 1660s, likely the same person as the child baptized c.1653/54.

The Banks attribution of John Gurney-1 (the emigrant) to Bury St Edmunds is not supported by direct 1630s baptism or household evidence in this film. However, the Thomas Gurney household (Thomas married Lidda Broddish c.1633-35) is present and active and produces children in the 1650s-1660s, demonstrating a Gurney family at BSE across the emigrant's likeliest years. A separate pre-existing William Gurney had a daughter Bridgett baptized 1588.

**Case question 2 — Structure of the Thomas Gurney household at Bury St Edmunds:**

Thomas Gurney married Lidda (Lydia) Broddish at St James, Bury St Edmunds, c.1633-1635 (image 297). Children identified from the baptism register:
- Juda Gurny/Gurney, dau. of Thomas — baptized c.1633-1634 (image 60, ARK 3QS7-L9C5-3ZDY)
- John Gurny, son to Thomas — baptized c.1653-1655 (image 77, ARK 3QS7-89C5-3ZFM)
- Thomas Gurney fil. Thomas — baptized c.1661-1663 (image 83, ARK 3QS7-89C5-3ZXV)

Note the gap of ~18 years between Juda (c.1633/34) and John (c.1653/54) — this strongly suggests either (a) a break in the register/non-survival of entries, (b) these are children of two different Thomas Gurneys, or (c) the dates for Juda come from the Commonwealth-era registration act of 1653 being applied retroactively. The "John Gurny son to Thomas" baptized c.1653 appears in the St James register that also says "Booke for St James Parish ... By an Act of Parliament ye 24 of August 1653, Beginning ye 29 of Sept. 1653" — meaning that register was opened in 1653. This makes the dating of the "John Gurny son to Thomas" record ambiguous: it may be a registration of a birth that occurred sometime before 1653.

Additional Gurney entries in the Thomas household's generation:
- Henry Gurney — burial, 1658-1660 (no father given; image 511, ARK 3QS7-99C5-3ZZR). If Henry was a child of Thomas, he died young in the Restoration era.
- Willielmus Gurney fil. Johannis et Elizabeth — baptism 1665, St James (image 86, ARK 3QS7-99C5-3ZFB). This is a different John Gurney household (John with wife Elizabeth), the same John born c.1653/54 who had come of age and married.

## NOTES AND CAVEATS

1. **Film number as DGS:** The Information panel for both Massachusetts military films showed "Film #" but not a separate "Image Group Number" field (the panel said "No indexes are available"). The film numbers 007702977 and 008713629 function as the DGS identifiers for film-scoped FTS queries.

2. **"John Gurny his byl" decipherment:** "byl" = bill (archaic/dialectal). "3.16.42" most likely = 16 March 1641/42 OS. "Tho: Lake" = Thomas Lake. Mr. Ginn = unidentified intermediary paymaster. The accounts page appears to be recording settlement of debts or procurement charges from the early 1640s.

3. **Petition date:** The item number 67 in the 1643-1675 military records has an "undated" marker in the date column. The surrounding items (64, 65-66) also appear undated. Context from items 68-71 (dated to 1645) and item ordering suggests the petitions cluster around 1645-1646. John Gurny, Richard Porter, and James Ludden were from Weymouth; the powder-fine context is consistent with 1645 Massachusetts military levy enforcement.

4. **Bury St Edmunds — Thomas Gurney household timeline:** Thomas married Lydia Broddish c.1633-35. Daughter Juda baptized c.1633/34 is very close to the marriage date — she may be a pre-marital child or the date is slightly off. The next known Thomas-household child is Thomas fil. Thomas c.1661/62, a gap of ~27 years. This gap is likely due to register non-survival for the 1640s-early 1650s rather than actual absence of children. The 1653 Commonwealth registration act opened a new book, which may have collected back-registrations including "John Gurny son to Thomas" (image 77), who could have been born anytime in the 1640s-early 1650s.

5. **1653-1656 burial cluster:** No Gurney burial cluster for 1653-1656 was found. The only 1650s burial is Henry Gurney (1658-1660). This is a negative finding.

6. **John Gurney-1 (emigrant) and Bury:** Banks's attribution of John Gurney-1 to Bury St Edmunds as an apprenticeship or origin location finds NO direct 1630s John Gurney household here. However, the Thomas Gurney-Lydia Broddish marriage of c.1633-35 establishes a Gurney family connection to St James, BSE, within the emigrant's generation. A John Gurney baptized to Thomas Gurney in the 1640s-50s could theoretically be related to John-1 but is too late to be John-1 himself (who was adult and in Massachusetts by 1643-45).

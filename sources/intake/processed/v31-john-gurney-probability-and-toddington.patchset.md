## 0. Audit report

### 0.1 Why v31

User objection during Pass 12 review of v30:

1. The sub-1 percent and 1-2 percent residual probabilities on Candidates A and C are inconsistent with the explicit "ELIMINATED" status carried by the rest of the §8 rows. Most of the explicitly ELIMINATED rows are at effectively 0 percent (alive in England, will proved, buried in England post-1641, bachelor, child died at age 2 days, etc.) and the case file does not work at sub-1 percent nuance for those. Cand A and Cand C should be evaluated on the same objective standard.

2. The Toddington row elimination reason added in v29 and refined in v30 currently reads "Married Toddington 12 October 1624; family group baptized at Toddington 1625-1633; buried Toddington September 1641." The "Continuous family group baptized at Toddington 1625-1633" clause is not persuasive on its own; John could in principle have left Toddington mid-1635 to 1640 if the burial was missing. The September 1641 burial is the controlling positive eliminator, with the wife name and non-matching children pattern as supporting evidence.

3. FamilySearch Tree profiles are not primary sources, particularly for the Richard / John Gurney generations. The v30 working-memo treatment of FS Tree LT9Z-KQ1 and the colonial Richard's profile (MV5M-CQ8) should not be carried forward into the case file.

### 0.2 Objective re-evaluation of Cand A

Cand A's identification is currently anchored by a chain of indexed primary records:

- Baptism at Stewkley, Buckinghamshire, 21 February 1603 (FamilySearch indexed).
- Marriage at Bierton with Broughton, Buckinghamshire, 24 April 1628, to Alice Oliffe.
- Five children baptized at Saint Mary, Aylesbury 1638-1653 (John 1638, Sarah 1639, Daniell 1645, Jonathan 1647, Hannah 1653).
- 1641 certificate of residence moving from the Aylesbury half-hundred to Northamptonshire.
- 1650 tenancy at Walgrave, Northamptonshire.

Two scenarios for the residual probability:

(a) **All five linkages are the same person.** Then Cand A is continuously documented in England 1603-1653 with a wife named Alice Oliffe and five children whose names do not match the colonial John's distinctive set. This is incompatible with the Massachusetts emigrant's continuous Weymouth and Braintree presence 1641-1662/3. Cand A is positively eliminated.

(b) **One or more linkages is a different person.** Then Cand A is unidentified after the first linkage. The Stewkley 1603 baby John remains, but every other documented adult John in this corridor (Bierton 1628 husband of Alice Oliffe, Aylesbury 1638-1653 father, Northamptonshire 1641-1650 resident) is independently eliminated by post-1641 English residence under his own name and parish. So the Stewkley 1603 baby would have to be a different person who left no further indexed record. If the Stewkley 1603 baby is the colonial John, there is no continuing English-side record for him, only a baptism. That is consistent with the colonial John's identity but does not require it; any of dozens of indexed Bucks John Gurney baptisms in 1600-1620 is an equally weak candidate. Working at the level of "the Stewkley 1603 baby specifically is the colonial John" without further evidence is no stronger than other Bucks baptisms in the same window.

Either reading lands Cand A at ELIMINATED. The residual probability that v23 carried forward (~1-2 percent) was a hedge on the linkage chain rather than an evidentiary basis for keeping Cand A active. Move to ELIMINATED.

### 0.3 Objective re-evaluation of Cand C

Cand C's identification is anchored by an eight-child family group at Berkhamsted, Hertfordshire, 1610-1636, all fathered by a John Gurney. Three independent grounds:

(a) **Age.** Henry's 1610 baptism requires the father John to be born no later than approximately 1585-1590, about 13 to 18 years older than the colonial John (born about 1603 per the 1653 deposition). No alternative reading of the eight-child Berkhamsted group puts the father in the 1600-1610 birth range.

(b) **Two-John alternative.** If a second John fathered the later 1624-1636 children, that second John would still need to predate the colonial John's 1641 emigration and would still produce the Francis 1636 son and the no-Mary, no-Peter pattern below.

(c) **Naming pattern.** The 1636 Francis son is incompatible with the colonial John's pattern of no Francis child. The absence of Mary and Peter (the colonial John's two most distinctive children) is consistent with the Berkhamsted family being a different John Gurney household.

There is no scenario in which Cand C is the colonial John. The residual probability (~0-1 percent) was a hedge rather than a basis for keeping Cand C active. Move to ELIMINATED.

### 0.4 Toddington row rewrite

The Toddington John Gurney + Elizabeth Moreton was married at Toddington 12 October 1624 and was buried at Toddington in September 1641. The burial is two and a half years before the Massachusetts emigrant's 1644 first-recorded Braintree appearance and three months after the emigrant's June 1641 Weymouth fine; the Toddington John cannot also be in continuous Massachusetts residence 1641-1662/3. The wife name Elizabeth Moreton is a supporting consideration, and the Toddington children (Elizabeth, Anne, John, Audrey) do not match the colonial John's distinctive child pattern (Mary, Sarah, Richard, John Junior, Peter).

The v30 elimination reason should be rewritten to lead with the September 1641 burial as the controlling fact, with the wife name and children pattern as supporting.

### 0.5 Pass 12 supplementary research findings

These findings are recorded here for completeness; they do not require case-file edits in v31.

- **Ancestry / Norfolk Record Office collection 61045 Gurney baptisms 1605-1615.** Four indexed Gurney-variant baptisms in this window: Frant Gurney 23 September 1611 Norwich Saint Martin at Palace, John Turney 17 April 1608 Somerleyton (different surname), Brigitta Gurney 20 September 1607 Talconeston, James Garne 19 May 1611 Norwich All Saints. **No East Dereham John Gurney baptism is indexed in the Ancestry Norfolk Record Office collection.** The case file's section 4 Entry E "John, son of Francis Gurnie" (re-read by AI paleography from the FreeREG-indexed "John the sonne of Nicholas Gorne") remains the project's own analysis and is not independently corroborated by the Ancestry Norfolk Record Office indexing. East Dereham parish coverage in the 75 percent Norfolk Record Office collection appears to be partial; absence in this collection does not refute the case file's reading, but it does not confirm it either.
- **Frant Gurney 23 September 1611 Norwich Saint Martin at Palace** is recorded as a baptism event in the Ancestry Norfolk Record Office collection. The date and parish match Francis Gurney G14's 1611 marriage to Margaret Ryvett (case file primary record 1). The same-day baptism could indicate (a) a pre-marital son legitimized concurrent with the marriage, (b) an Ancestry indexer error mislabeling the marriage event as a baptism, or (c) a coincident second Gurney family at the same parish. The detail is logged for further work; it does not change the case file's existing reading.
- **John Gurney + Mary marriage 1620-1635 England-wide search** returns no English-side John Gurney + Mary marriage in this window in the FamilySearch indexed marriage collections. As with other negative-search findings, this is recorded as a working observation rather than a controlling fact; the colonial John + Mary marriage may have occurred at an unindexed parish or under a non-standard surname variant.

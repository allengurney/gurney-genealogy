# Early Records of the Town of Providence (21 vols, 1892–1915)

Source ID: `early-records-town-of-providence`

## Scope examined
Town-meeting and probate volumes read at transcript level from the Internet
Archive copies (`earlyrecordsofto01inprov` through `-05`, `cu31924092200603`
(v8), `cu31924092200637` (v11), `earlyrecordsofto16prov` (v16)) via a broad
Gurney-variant sweep, 2026-07-01. Volumes carrying the separate 1690s
Providence John-and-Sarah Gurney cluster were **not** in this batch and are
outside this registration.

## What it establishes / limits
The volumes swept carry no Gurney of any spelling; the only near-name is a
distinct Thomas Garnet family of Providence (v16 probate). The town-meeting and
deed volumes for 1655–1675 (vols 2–3) are clean, which bounds the search for
John Gurney's unrecorded Quinapaug (Providence-jurisdiction) interest carried
into his 1663 inventory. Transcript-level read of a printed edition, not an
image read of the Providence manuscript books. This is dump finding F-R6.

## Second sweep — near-complete series census (2026-07-18/19)
A second pass extended coverage to **19 of the 21 printed volumes** (archive.org
full text, `djvu.txt`, Gurney-variant sweep `gurn*/gourn*/garney/gurnney/surney`),
now including the 1690s–1710s volumes. Positive yield is confined to **vol. 17
(town papers, `cu31924092200694`)**: the rate finished 3 January 1697/8 (printed
p. 167, "John Gurney" ~3s, immediately after "Widow feild") and the constable's
rate of 4 October 1705 (pp. 211–212, "John Gurney 00-08-00") — plus the
already-cited council material in vols. 7 and 10 (will/inventory/administration
at vol. 7 pp. 166–169; the 1695–96 orders at vol. 10 pp. 34–38 and the 30 April
1714 council record at p. 136). Vol. 12's sole variant hit ("dept Gournr") is
*deputy Governor*, a false positive. **Unswept residue: vols. 14 and 20** — not
on archive.org (vol. 14 = Google Books `WMUTAAAAYAAJ`); both are deed-transcript
volumes, judged low-yield, and the series sweep is treated as complete-enough at
19/21.

# Validation — Buckinghamshire Archives online catalogue

- **Source ID:** `bucks-archives-catalogue`
- **What it is:** the Buckinghamshire Archives online catalogue at archives.buckinghamshire.gov.uk — catalogue-level descriptions of deeds, estate papers, manorial records and settlement bonds. Not document images.
- **Examined:** 2026-07-27.

## Portion examined

Surname `gurney`, date filter **1570–1660**, all record levels, complete result set read. Twelve item-level descriptions opened in full: D-BAS/49/96a, D-BAS/49/99, D-BAS/40/303, D-BAS/40/305, D-BAS/37/88, D-BAS/12/367, D-BAS/46/76, D-BAS/49/95, D-LE, D-LE/2/39, D-LE/2/50, and the D-X_202 collection description. Every hit is transcribed in the extract.

## Coverage limits that bound any negative

- **The catalogue normalises the surname.** `gurnay`, `gorney`, `garney`, `gournay` and `gurnie` each return **zero** in the 1570–1660 window while `gurney` returns the full set. A variant sweep is therefore unnecessary, and a `gurney` blank is a real blank rather than a spelling artefact. This is the opposite of the posture required for parish-register indexes.
- **It indexes abstracts, not document text.** Names appearing only inside an unabstracted deed — and most deeds are abstracted selectively — will not surface. Absence from the catalogue is not absence from the archive.
- **Probate is not in this catalogue.** The Archdeaconry of Buckingham registers (D/A/We, D/A/Wf) are indexed separately; for those see `england-wales-published-probate-indexes`.

## What it establishes

Several Buckinghamshire John Gurneys previously known only from parish registers are now individuated by land records, which resist the same-name masking problem that weakens register-continuity eliminations. Principally: **John Gurney, gentleman, of Bishopstone in Stone**, who with wife Ann and son and heir Edward sold the family's capital messuage and c.115 acres for £1,700 in 1648/9; **John Gurney the younger of Owlswick**, settled with land on his 1619 marriage to Alice Hewett; **John Gurney of Farnham Royal**, yeoman, and wife Susan, selling land in 1633 and signing by mark; and the Aylesbury **John Gurney, ironmonger**, in parish office 1652–1660.

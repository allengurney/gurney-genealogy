## The Finding: Volume 3, p. 9

In the opening article of vol. 3, *The Origin and Development of Coat Armour* (Nichols's own unsigned editorial essay), a short heraldic note on "Gournay" appears:

> **GOURNAY.** The barons of Gournay in Normandy bore an uncharged shield of Sable.

The passage continues with the town of Gournay's arms (a knight ermine and fleur-de-lis or, said to have been conferred after Philip Augustus's capture of the town and his knighting of Arthur Duke of Brittany). Footnote 2 cites: *See The Record of the House of Gournay, 4to. 1848, p. 19.*

**Citation:** John Gough Nichols, ed., *The Herald and Genealogist*, vol. 3 (London: J. B. Nichols and Sons, 1866), p. 9.

---

## Assessment

### Not material to the Candidate B case file

The note concerns the arms of the senior Norman baronial line (the Gournays of Normandy), not any English branch or the seventeenth century. It does not bear on John Gurney-1, Francis, Candidate A, or any of the research priorities.

### Potentially useful for G37–G32 fact sheets

Three modest contributions:

1. **Independent heraldic citation.** The plain sable shield is recorded by Nichols as the attested armorial of the Barons of Gournay, on Daniel Gurney's authority. Currently the G37–G32 fact sheets rely on Daniel Gurney alone for heraldic detail; Nichols is a second, independent publication endorsing the same reading.

2. **Victorian peer endorsement of Daniel Gurney.** Nichols was the leading heraldic editor of his day. The same firm (J. B. Nichols and Sons) had printed Daniel Gurney's *Record* in 1848 and here cites it without qualification eighteen years later. This is a small but real piece of contemporaneous validation of Daniel Gurney's authority — useful if a footnote ever needs to state "Daniel Gurney was regarded as authoritative by contemporary heraldic scholarship."

3. **Town arms anecdote.** The Philip Augustus / Arthur of Brittany story is colourful background for the G37 Eudes de Gournay fact sheet or a sidebar on Gournay-en-Bray. Not central, but flavourful.

### Suggested footnote for G37–G32 fact sheets

> The plain sable shield of the Barons of Gournay is recorded by John Gough Nichols in *The Herald and Genealogist*, vol. 3 (London: J. B. Nichols and Sons, 1866), p. 9, citing Daniel Gurney, *The Record of the House of Gournay* (London, 1848), p. 19. Nichols's independent citation of Gurney in the leading Victorian heraldic journal is a modest piece of contemporary peer endorsement of Daniel Gurney's authority.

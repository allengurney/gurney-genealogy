## The Entry

Banks's entry for John Gurney on p. 151 (Suffolk — Bury St. Edmunds section):

> **GURNEY, John** | Bury St. Edmunds | [no ship] | Braintree, Massachusetts | Banks Mss.

**Citation:** Charles Edward Banks, *Topographical Dictionary of 2885 English Emigrants to New England, 1620–1650*, ed. Elijah Ellsworth Brownell (Philadelphia: Bertram Press, March 1937), p. 151.

---

## Publication Context

Banks died before publication. Elijah Ellsworth Brownell edited the manuscripts and published in March 1937. The "Banks Mss." source designation refers to Banks's own unpublished working notes — no ship, no primary record, no corroborating parish register, will, or apprenticeship indenture is cited. By Banks's own standards elsewhere in the volume, an entry sourced only to "Banks Mss." is a lead, not a proof.

## Cluster Analysis

Banks placed John within a dense Bury St. Edmunds emigration cluster. The surrounding names on p. 151 include:

- John Newgate
- Clement Chaplin
- William Knapp
- John Goodrich
- Henry Bright
- William Townsend
- Theodore Atkinson
- Peyton Cooke
- Samuel Wright

These are a genuine Bury St. Edmunds emigration group, many well documented from primary sources. Banks was perceiving a real neighborhood-migration pattern and slotted Gurney into it. This is meaningful contextual judgment, even without a primary citation.

The most likely mechanism for Banks's attribution is an apprenticeship record of a John Gurney/Girney at Bury St. Edmunds in the 1620s–1630s. Such a record would identify John with the town where he trained, not necessarily where he was born. A Norwich-born son of Francis Gurney who served out a Bury St. Edmunds apprenticeship would be exactly the person Banks's note would describe — consistent with, rather than contradictory to, Candidate B.

## Assessment

Banks's entry is material as a lead but not as proof. Key implications:

1. **East Anglian placement.** Banks points firmly into East Anglia (Suffolk), not Buckinghamshire. This weakens Candidate A (Stewkley, Bucks) more than it weakens Candidate B.
2. **"Banks Mss." limitation.** No primary citation means the attribution cannot be weighed against registered records.
3. **No ship.** The "Ships Name" column is blank. Banks could not identify John's vessel. Confirms no passenger record exists.
4. **Dovetail with BSE apprentice lead.** The attribution is most easily explained by an apprenticeship record, which would be consistent with Candidate B geography (Norfolk → Suffolk apprenticeship → London → New England).

### Combined Assessment (Banks + Anderson)

Both major published compilations of New England emigrant origins have examined John Gurney-1. Neither places him in Buckinghamshire. Banks places him at Bury St. Edmunds, Suffolk (without primary citation); Anderson lists his origin as "Unknown." The cumulative weight provides mild additional pressure against Candidate A and is neutral-to-mildly-supportive for East Anglian candidates including Candidate B. Neither source adds new primary evidence or warrants a probability adjustment on its own.

## Three Key Observations

### 1. Origin: "Unknown"

Anderson gives the English origin as "Unknown." He worked with full access to Banks's 1937 *Topographical Dictionary* and routinely cites Banks-supported origins when the underlying evidence holds up. His "Unknown" is an implicit judgment that Banks's Bury St. Edmunds attribution is insufficient for publication under modern Great Migration standards. It is not a rejection — Anderson is cautious with negatives — but it is a clear signal that no primary record has surfaced to prove any specific parish.

This interpretive principle is now a standing fact: Anderson's "Unknown" functions as an implicit rejection of Banks. See AI-Rules §8.

### 2. Arrival Year: 1636

Anderson gives 1636, earlier than the June 1641 Weymouth record that had been treated as John's first Massachusetts appearance. This discrepancy is unresolved (AI-Rules §10). The most likely source of the 1636 date is TAG 10:70–73 — one of Anderson's own cited references. Reconciliation requires pulling TAG.

### 3. Settlements: "Boston, Braintree" — No Weymouth

Anderson does not credit Weymouth as a settlement. This may reflect a judgment that the 1641 Weymouth record is transient, or it may reflect an unexamined conflict between two John Gurneys. Worth checking against the underlying sources.

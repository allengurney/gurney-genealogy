**Source:** Jean-Eugène Decorde, *Essai historique et archéologique sur le Canton de Gournay* (Rouen, 1861)【12†L259-L268】. This local history details the medieval lords and churches of Gournay-en-Bray and nearby parishes. It contains many anecdotes and charters involving the de Gournay family (the user’s Gurney ancestors). Key highlights include donations by the Gournay lords to abbeys, foundation of abbeys, tomb discoveries, and medieval heraldry.

## The Gournay Lords and Their Lineage

- **Hugues de Gournay (d. c. 1102) and Basilie Flaitel:** Decorde cites an 1082 charter: “Hugues de Gournay and Basilie, his wife, confirmed the donation of 190 arpents near *Boshyon* (Forest of Hugo) to the abbey of Jumièges”【25†L1002-L1010】. This identifies *Basilie* (Flaitel) as wife of Hugues III de Gournay. (Other sources confirm Basilie was daughter of Gerard Flaitel【6†L222-L224】.) This ties Hugues and Basilie to Jumièges Abbey (Seine-Maritime), which survives today as romantic ruins. Decorde also notes that Hugues & Basilie’s son, Gérard, later appears in records: in 1112/22 “Hugues de Gournay…confirmed the gifts made to Bec by his ancestors Hugues and Basilie, and by Gérard, his father,” explicitly including the Brémontier church and tithes【16†L1315-L1323】. Thus Hugues III and Basilie forged early ties to the great abbeys of Jumièges and Bec (Fontenelle) in Normandy.

- **Gérard de Gournay:** Son of Hugues III and Basilie, Gérard appears in the 1112 deed above【16†L1315-L1323】 as Hugues IV’s father. He inherited the lordship; Decorde does not detail him further, but other genealogies list Gérard as father of Hugues IV. (A charter of Henry II in the 1180s confirms donations by “Hugonis de Gornaco et Girardi filii” to Bec【40†L7-L9】.)

- **Hugues IV de Gournay (d. 1182) and Mélisende (de Vermandois):** Decorde briefly mentions “another Hugues de Gournay and Millesende, his wife,” who around 1164 endowed the newly built church of Gaillefontaine with grain from Boshyon【25†L1012-L1019】. “Millesende” is an alternate spelling of *Mélisende*, later identified as Mélisende de Vermandois (daughter of Count Thomas de Marle)【6†L238-L241】. Thus Hugues IV continued the family’s ecclesiastical patronage. Other sources (Decorde’s citations of D. Gurney) show Hugues IV co‑founded the abbey of Beaubec in 1127 and died 1182. The Gournay genealogy from Decorde/Wikipedia is:
  - Hugues III (husband of Basilie) → son Gérard → Hugues IV (husband of Mélisende) → son Hugues V.

- **Hugues V de Gournay (d. 1214):**  The last Norman lord “Hugues de Gournay” mentioned by Decorde. He founded **Bellosanne Abbey** (Notre-Dame de Bellozanne) in 1198【10†L158-L166】 and a priory at Saint-Aubin in 1200【18†L6371-L6374】. He lost Gournay to King Philip II Augustus in 1202 and died in exile in England in 1214. Decorde notes his dramatic end: after Philips’s 1202 victory Gournay’s arms were blazoned with a silver knight, and thereafter “le pouvoir des premiers seigneurs de Gournay” faded【18†L6375-L6382】. (“Ils étaient si redoutés qu’on les appelait ‘Chevaliers aux armes noires’ – knights of the black arms – because of their sable (black) coat of arms【18†L6375-L6382】.”) (Genealogical sources confirm Hugues V joined the Third Crusade at Acre in 1190 and indeed lost Gournay at Philippe Auguste’s siege【6†L243-L247】【18†L6375-L6382】.) 

## Donations and Church Foundations

- **Jumièges Abbey (Seine-Maritime):** The 1082 charter【25†L1002-L1010】 shows the Gournay lords donating land to the abbey of Jumièges (the famous Benedictine abbey near Rouen). This confirms early Gournay links to Jumièges. Today Jumièges Abbey (49°25′N, 0°49′E) is a prominent ruin on the Seine.

- **Church of Gaillefontaine:** In 1164, Hugues IV and Mélisende gave “trois boisseaux de froment et cinq boisseaux d’avoine” from their manor at Boshyon for the church they built at Gaillefontaine【25†L1012-L1019】. Gaillefontaine (Seine-Maritime; 49.6544°N 1.6169°E) was then a small town; its 13th-century Notre-Dame church still stands today【28†L251-L258】. Decorde’s text (via Gurney) highlights this early endowment, underlining the Gournays’ role in establishing Gaillefontaine’s parish church.

- **Abbey of Bec (Fontenelle, Eure):**  Decorde notes that in 1112 (or 1122) “Hugues de Gournay confirmed gifts to the Abbey of Bec made by his ancestors Hugues and Basilie and by Gérard, his father,” explicitly including the Brémontier church and tithes【16†L1315-L1323】. Bec Abbey (Le Bec-Hellouin, Eure; 49.3489°N 0.7673°E) thus held rights in Gournay lands. This confirms that parts of Brémontier’s income originally granted to Bec by Duchess Gunnor’s family (Duke Richard II) were later under Gournay control and then reconfirmed.  (In 1198 Hugues V would give Brémontier to his own foundation of Bellozanne【16†L1357-L1364】.) 

- **Bellosanne Abbey (Brémontier-Merval):** Hugues V founded this Premonstratensian abbey in 1198 (Decorde says 1193)【18†L6371-L6374】. It was at Brémontier (now Brémontier-Merval, Seine-Maritime; 49.5056°N 1.6111°E) and dedicated to Notre-Dame. Modern archaeology shows the abbey lay between two former ponds on the Bray plateau【10†L160-L168】. During the Revolution Bellozanne was suppressed: its church and cloister were sold and by 1797 a buyer had demolished the buildings【10†L189-L197】. A château built in 1827 erased the ruins【10†L189-L197】, so nothing medieval remains on site today.

- **Saint-Hildevert Relic (Brémontier → Gournay):** Decorde recounts that after a Brémontier monastery was replaced by a collegiate church, its canons were “transferred to Gournay, where the body of Saint Hildevert had just been brought”【16†L1312-L1320】. Hugues IV then confirmed the Brémontier church gift to Bec (noting Hildevert’s body). In short, Gournay’s collégiale Notre-Dame (49.4829°N 1.7247°E) came to host St. Hildevert’s relics in the 12th century. (Today the 12th–15th‑c. collegiate church of Notre-Dame still stands in Gournay-en-Bray; its crypt contains the supposed relics of St. Hildevert.)

- **Parish of Boshyon (Bois-Hugues):** Decorde has a detailed entry on Boshyon (an old parish just southeast of Gournay). Its name derives from *Boscus Hugonis* (“Hugues’s wood”)【25†L988-L997】. Key points: in 1082 Hugues III and Basilie gave land “at Boshyon” to Jumièges【25†L1002-L1010】; in 1164 Hugues IV and Mélisende gave crops from Boshyon to Gaillefontaine’s church【25†L1012-L1019】. A 1195 charter by neighbor Manassès de Bully endowed one muid of oats from Boshyon mill for an altar lamp at Gournay’s St Hildevert church【25†L1023-L1031】. In the 12th century the Gournay lord also gave Boshyon’s church (St. Michel) to the Gournay chapter【25†L1035-L1041】. Decorde notes that church’s Romanesque tower – “L’église de Boshyon est sous le vocable de saint Michel. L’aspect du clocher annonce une construction du XIIe siècle”【42†L7-L10】. By 1826 Boshyon’s church had become a subsidiary chapel (sous-établir) of Mont-Böty and is now closed【42†L11-L16】. (On a modern map, Boshyon is not a separate commune; its territory is absorbed into Ernemont-la-Villette / Avesnes.)

- **Avesnes-en-Bray (incl. Cottentray):** The hamlet of Cottentray (in Avesnes-en-Bray, Seine-Maritime; 49.4697°N 1.6733°E) once held a **quarter-fief of Gournay**. Decorde explains that until 15th c. Cottentray’s seigneury was split: “un quart de fief appartenant originellement aux seigneurs de Gournay”【39†L505-L513】. (In 15c. Marie de Bourbon sold that quarter to the Potin family【39†L505-L513】.) Cottentray is also the site of a mysterious discovery: in 1682, laborers found “huit à dix tombeaux en pierre” (8–10 stone tombs) in Cottentray’s field【39†L513-L521】. The field was called “Clos des Anglais,” suggesting these may have been knights’ burials from the Hundred Years’ War or earlier Anglo-Norman conflicts【39†L513-L521】. Decorde and the local historian G. de la Mairie speculated they were “des chevaliers tués pendant nos guerres avec l’Angleterre”【39†L513-L521】. (The tombs’ whereabouts are now lost; no inscription survived.) 

- **Other Parishes:**  Decorde’s *Essai* covers many nearby villages, but none mention the Gournay lords as directly. One note: a Hugues *de la Houssoye* gave Ferrières and Fumechon tithes to Gournay in 1192【37†L577-L581】. This “de la Houssoye” is presumably another local noble, not the Gournays. 

## Atmosphere, Artifacts, and Narratives

Decorde is rich in colorful detail. For example:
- The Gournay lords’ **black heraldry**: as noted, their arms were sable (black) and they were nicknamed *“Chevaliers aux armes noires”*【18†L6375-L6382】. After the 1202 siege, Philip II’s daughter Marie (fiancée of Arthur of Brittany) was knighted in Gournay’s church, and Gournay’s arms were altered to include a silver knight figure【18†L6375-L6384】. (Decorde even reproduces this armorial change.) These details convey a vivid sense of feudal pageantry.

- The **tombs at Cottentray (1682)**: Decorde dwells on the chance discovery of “Knights’ tombs.” Even in the late 17th century the local imagination linked these stones to the heroic wars with England【39†L513-L521】. This anecdote, though post-medieval, reflects how Gournay’s martial past lingered in local lore.

- The **Lamp of Saint Hildevert (1195)**: The 1195 donation by Manassès de Bully for an eternal lamp burning before St. Hildevert’s relic【25†L1023-L1031】 suggests the importance of saintly worship and church liturgy in Gournay. (In modern terms, it’s like an endowment for a perpetual candle — a medieval *memorary*.)

- **Titles and Language:** Decorde (writing 1861) preserves many medieval French and Latin phrases, later glossed. He quotes charters in Latin (e.g. *“Boscus Hugonis”*) but translates or paraphrases them. For clarity, readers may need background: e.g., *“arpents”* were measures of land (c.0.84 acre each), *“muids”* were large measures of grain.

- **Modern Landmarks:** Almost all places mentioned still exist, if under different forms. Gournay-en-Bray remains a small town (pop. ~5,300). Its 12th–15th c. Collégiale Notre-Dame (which likely incorporated St. Hildevert’s relics) still stands and was famously painted by Sisley in 1885. Brémontier-Merval is a village (site of Bellosanne). Gaillefontaine’s old churches and abbey ruins (see Wikipedia【28†L251-L258】) still mark the landscape. Even Boshyon’s church tower survives (under the hamlet of “Mont-Bôty” or “Mont-Bosy”). Cottentray today is just fields/woods east of Gournay (the Cottentray estate was split off long ago, but Avesnes-en-Bray survives). 

## Connections and Follow-up Leads

- **Genealogical Lineage:** Decorde confirms that *Hugues III* married Basilie Flaitel【25†L1002-L1010】, and *Hugues IV* married Mélisende de Vermandois【25†L1012-L1019】【6†L238-L241】. These identifications match published pedigrees. It suggests tracing Hugues V’s Crusade (e.g. in English records, where Gournay lords appear in 1190 Acre lists【6†L243-L247】). Follow-up: check charters (e.g. those in **“Record of the House of Gournay”** cited by Decorde) for any mention of children or siblings.

- **Local Sites:** Explore the current sites mentioned:
  - The **collégiale Notre-Dame of Gournay** (church of St Hildevert) – medieval crypt and architecture remain.
  - The ruined **Abbey of Jumièges** (ruin on Seine) – where Hugues III’s vassal Raoul Havot’s gift went【25†L1002-L1010】.
  - **Brémontier-Merval** – look for any remains or markers of Bellosanne (likely none; the château from 1827 stands).
  - **Abbey of Bec** (Le Bec-Hellouin) – still a functioning priory; records may show Gournay gifts.
  - **Church of St. Michel at Mont-Bôty** (the old Boshyon church) – photograph existing tower (12th c).
  - **Cottentray/Avesnes** – the “Clos des Anglais” field may still be identifiable (ask local archives). The Potin manor (Pierre Potin’s birthplace) might be documented.
  
- **Heraldry and Tradition:** The story of the Gournay arms (sable with argent knight) and “black knights” may be confirmed in armorial rolls. This colorful detail suggests a research path: examine Norman heraldic manuscripts or tomb effigies (if any) for Gournay symbolism.

- **Document Transcription:** Decorde’s French text has OCR errors (e.g. “Millesende” for Mélisende, “Boshyon”). Where Decorde misread or modernized, consult original charters. For instance, track down the 1164 act of Archbishop Rotrou cited in Decorde – it should list Hugues de Gournay explicitly. Similarly, the 1082 Jumièges charter is identified (Gabriel Dumoulin notes, or “Thesaurus UNICAEN” records)【40†L1-L3】.

- **Family Papers:** The Potin family (later owners of Gournay’s church) played into the local narrative【39†L505-L513】, but not the Gournay bloodline. Check if any “Potin de la Mairie” correspondence contains local lore (the tomb story cites Potin’s works).

- **Contextual History:** Decorde often cites contemporary historians (e.g., “M. de la Mairie” – local priest author of *Histoire de Gournay*). Reading de la Mairie’s work might yield more genealogical clues or variant dates.

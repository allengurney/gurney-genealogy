# Blomefield, *History of Norfolk*, vol. 5, pp. 63–70 — Newton (Newton Flotman), Hundred of Humble-Yard

Source ID: `blomefield-norfolk`

Source: Francis Blomefield, *An Essay Towards a Topographical History of the County of Norfolk*, vol. 5 (London: W. Miller, 1806), "Hundred of Humble-Yard: Newton," pp. 63–70. British History Online: https://www.british-history.ac.uk/topographical-hist-norfolk/vol5/pp63-70 (free, digitised by double rekeying; public domain).

Newton Flotman lies on the river Tas south of Norwich, dividing Newton from Tasburgh. The parish held two medieties of the church and two principal manors — Blundeville's (Newton-Hall) and Malherbe's (Rainthorp-Hall, alias Myles/Mills). The extracts below capture the Blundeville (Blundevill / Blomevyle / Blunnel) descent, the church arms, and the chancel monument, which carry the Gurnay (Gurney) heraldic and marriage connection. Spelling and punctuation follow the BHO rekeyed text.

## The Manor of Blundeville's, or Newton-Hall — descent

> Which had its name from its owners, and to which the mediety of the advowson of the church belonged; the first that I find of this name owner here, was Will. de Blundeville, Blomevyle, or Blunnel, who had it of the gift of Henry de Rhye, with Blomevyle's manor in Depham, (vol. ii. p. 491,) he left it to Richard his son, who was lord in 1226, being nephew to Tho. de Blumville Bishop of Norwich, (vol. iii. p. 483,) he was succeeded by William Blumvyle, and he by Katherine his widow; and William their son held it of the manor of Hingham, as of the barony of Rhye; and it was after held of the barony of Montchensy at a quarter of a fee; in 1388, Rich. Blumvyle held it, and in 1420, Will. Blumvyle, Esq. who was succeeded by Ric. Blomevyle, Esq. and he by Catherine his wife, and she by Richard their son, who died in 1503; Ralph his brother succeeded, and died in 1514, whose son Edward was lord, and died in 1568; and in 1569, Thomas his son held a court baron and lete, and had purchased and joined to it three parts of [the manor of Myles, alias Ranthorp].

Footnote at "Catherine his wife": "1495, Katherine Blomevyle died a widow, and Lady Eleanor Jenny administered."

## The church — battlement arms and the chancel monument

The church is dedicated to the Virgin Mary and was rebuilt in 1385. Among the carved battlement arms, and on the chancel monument over the Blundeville vault, the Gurnay impalement appears.

> there is an inscription on the battlements, which are so high I could not read it, nor make out all the arms carved there; but among them are, 1st, Blundeville impaling five de-lises. **Ditto impaling Gurnay.**

The chancel monument (erected by Thomas Blundeville in 1571) commemorates three generations in a praying posture, "three men in armour … with each a reading-desk before them," with the inscription:

> Richardus Blondevyle obiit Ano Dni. 1490, Etatis sue 85
> Radulphus Blondevile obiit Ano Dni. 1514, Etatis sue 45
> Edwardus Blondevile obiit Dni. 1568, Etatis sue 75
> Tho. Blondevyle Posuit 1571.

> Here lyes in Grave, nowe thre tymes done, The Grandsire, Father, and the Sone, Theyr Names, theyr Age, and when they dyed, Above their headds is speryfyed, Theyr Sheyld of Arms doth eke declare, The Stocke wyth whom they mached were, They lyved well, and dyed as well, And nowe with God in Heaven they dwell, And thear do prayse hus holn Name, God graunt that we may do the same.

The shields beneath the figures:

> 1. Blundeville, quarterly per fess indented or and az. a bend G. impales Inglosse.
> 2. **Ditto impales Gurnay.**
> 3. Ditto impales Godsalve.

A second partition commemorates Thomas Blundevile, son of Edward, with his two wives Rose and Margaret ("Rosa et Margareta Uxores Thome Blundevile cum Fil' suis Elizabetha et Patientia").

## Source notes

- The monument inscription is Blomefield's eye-copy of the surviving chancel monument; he expressly could not read the higher battlement inscription.
- The manor-descent prose states Richard (d. 1503) and Ralph (d. 1514) as **brothers**, whereas the monument verse styles the three commemorated men "the Grandsire, Father, and the Sone" (Richard → Ralph → Edward as three successive generations). The two statements within Blomefield's own account differ on the Richard–Ralph relationship.
- The monument records Ralph (Radulphus) Blundeville as dying in **1514, aged 45** (i.e. born c. 1469). Some later antiquarian transcriptions give "1541, aged 74"; the Blomefield text above reads 1514 / 45.

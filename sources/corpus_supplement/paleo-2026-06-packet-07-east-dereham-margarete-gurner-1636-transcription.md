## Bottom line

The court date is **10 October 1636**, in **12 Charles I**. The target is on the **lower right-hand page**, not the lower-left English passage. The right-page entry does appear to name **Margarete Gurner vidua** (Margaret Gurner/Gurney, widow) in a copyhold surrender/admission context.

The visible entry is **not** a direct admission of Margaret herself. It appears to be an entry in which **Hugh Browne, gentleman**, comes to the court and surrenders land/tenement held of the manor; Margaret Gurner widow is named as a former tenant or former tenure-reference (`quondam Margarete Gurner vidua`). I do **not** see a husband, `nuper uxor`, Edward Gurney, or another male Gurney predecessor named in the visible target passage.

This is useful evidence that a Margaret/Margery Gurner/Gurney widow was attached to an East Dereham manorial holding by 1636, but it does not by itself prove her husband or the proposed Edward-Gurney free-bench hypothesis.

## Bottom line after line-by-line re-read

The Anthony Gurney will is on the first image and continues onto the second image. It does not continue onto the third image. The testator is "Anthonie Gurney of greate Elingham in the countie of Norff, Esquier," which supports identifying him with Anthony Gurney of Great Ellingham. "Elingham" also appears in the burial request.

I do not see a clause naming "my son Francis" in the visible registered will text. The only Francis/Frances occurrence I find is in the grandchildren clause:

```text
upp of Anthonye Gurney Thomas Gurney
Elizabeth Gurney ffrannces Gurney
Anne Gurney and Anthonye Drurye
my Grandchildren and forther I will
```

The omission of an explicitly named son Francis therefore appears to be original to this registered copy, not a strip-reading miss. The will names daughters Elizabeth Gurney and Cicely/Cicelie Gurney, and grandchildren Anthony Gurney, Thomas Gurney, Elizabeth Gurney, ffrannces Gurney, Anne Gurney, and Anthony Drury. It names no wife and no sons.

Interpretive caution: the will's land trust may itself explain why an adult son/heir is not named. Anthony devises the usable profits of two parts of knight-service lands and other lands to executors for a term that now looks more like `forte[n]e` (fourteen) years than "forty" years, with debts, legacies, and grandchildren's education/support as the purposes. A son or heir may have been provided for by inheritance, entail, wardship law, or prior settlement, but that is inference; the will does not say so.

The probate clause begins in Latin beneath the witness list on the right-hand page numbered 137 in `norwich-wills-1557-frances-gurney-page.jpg`. It proves the will at Norwich on 10 December 1557 before Master Michael Dunninge, doctor of laws.

Duplicate-line audit: the apparent repetitions are original drafting, not duplicated strips. The will uses parallel clauses for (1) the two parts of lands held by knight service and (2) the residue of lands not held by knight service. The join from image 1 right page to image 2 left page is continuous: image 1 ends "in and towarde the" and image 2 begins "payment of my sayde debtes..."

## Answers to packet questions

### (a) Full transcription of the will

See the transcription above. The complete Anthony will text visible in the staged images runs from image 1 left page through image 2 right page, ending just above the Latin probate clause.

### (b) Testator's place

The place reads "greate Elingham in the countie of Norff." This is Great Ellingham, not Billingford and not Great Bealings. The reading is high confidence because "Elingham" is legible in the testator identification and again in the burial clause: "the churche of Elingham aforesayde."

### (c) Son Francis, other children, wife

I do not see an explicit clause naming "my son Francis" or otherwise naming Francis as Anthony's child. The only `ffrannces` reading is in the list of grandchildren:

- Anthonye Gurney
- Thomas Gurney
- Elizabeth Gurney
- ffrannces Gurney
- Anne Gurney
- Anthonye Drurye

The text explicitly names Elizabeth Gurney as "my dowter" with a legacy of 200 pounds and Cicelie Gurney as "my dowter" with a legacy of 20 pounds. No wife is named in the visible will text. No son is named in the visible will text.

### (d) Date and probate clause

The will date reads 6 December 1557. The probate clause is on the right-hand page numbered 137 in `norwich-wills-1557-frances-gurney-page.jpg`, immediately under the witness list. It reads 10 December 1557 and commits administration to Sir Christopher Heydon, knight, and Robert Notarye, esquire.

### (e) Are the three pages one will?

No. The first image and the upper/middle part of the second image are Anthony Gurney's will. The lower part of the second image begins the Latin probate for Anthony and then the next record. The third image is a different, non-Anthony will, apparently Stephen Ayott/Ayotte of North Runcton. The apparent Gurney/Kendall clue on the third image is a machine-read false positive from the phrase "given unto them as well lond or other thinges."

## Additional observations

- The will is a strong G17 confirmation candidate because the opening gives both status and place: "Anthonie Gurney of greate Elingham ... Esquier."
- The `ffrannces Gurney` in the will is a grandchild, not the son Francis attested elsewhere. The spelling does not by itself resolve sex; the stated relationship is the important part.
- The absence of a son clause should be treated as real evidence from this registered copy, not as a transcription omission. It does not disprove the external sources making Francis Anthony's child; it means this will does not itself state that relationship.
- The land-trust term is the hardest remaining repeated English reading. I now prefer `forte[n]e` (fourteen) over the earlier "forty" reading because both occurrences show an n-like minims cluster and the overplus is to be divided among grandchildren after an education/support term. Treat this as probable, not certain.
- The repeated land clauses have been rechecked against the images and appear to be original drafting, not duplicated transcription strips.
- The third image should not be used as evidence for Anthony Gurney except as a negative control showing that the packet's third machine snippet does not belong to the Anthony will.

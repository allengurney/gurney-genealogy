# E-CIPM (Mapping the Medieval Countryside) — Gournay/Gurney mentions in the Index of Persons

The digital *Calendar of Inquisitions Post Mortem* edition at [inquisitionspostmortem.ac.uk](https://inquisitionspostmortem.ac.uk) (the "Mapping the Medieval Countryside" / E-CIPM project, c. 1399–1447 = CIPM vols 18–26) **does** carry Gournay/Gurney entries, recovered through the **Index of Persons** rather than the quick-search box. A query of the search box for `Gurney`/`Gurnay`/`Gournay` returns nothing because the family is never the *subject* (deceased tenant-in-chief) of an indexed IPM — consistent with the jurors' finding in Sir John Gurney V's own 1409 inquisition that he "held nothing in Norfolk" of the Crown in chief. But the family recurs as **feoffees, fee-holders, tenants and disputants inside other men's inquisitions**, and those appearances are indexed under the head-form **"Gournay" (cross-referencing Gornay, Gourney, Gurnay, Gurney)** in the [Index of Persons, letter G](https://inquisitionspostmortem.ac.uk/browse/people/g/index.html).

This corrects the bare "all returned no matching records out of 7,565 indexed entries" note carried in the G22 companion (footnote `mmc-cipm-2026`): the *absence-as-IPM-subject* is real, but the *Index of Persons* harvest below is not — it was simply never browsed.

## Norfolk / direct-line bearing

**Edmund Gournay — feoffee of the manor of Hingham and the hundred of Forehoe (probably Edmund Gurney G23, d. 1387).** In the IPM of **Robert, Lord Morley, knight** (writ 21 Oct. 1442; inquisition taken at Norwich shirehouse 26 Jan. 1443; TNA C 139/110/38 mm. 1–2; E 149/176/8), the descent recites:

> "Long before Robert's death, Edmund Gournay, William Pette, William Cursun, and John Reed were seised of the following in demesne as of fee. They granted the manor and hundred to Thomas de Morley, the son of William de Morley, and Joan his wife and the heirs of their bodies."

The holdings so settled are the **manor of Hingham** and the **hundred of Forehoe**, Norfolk, in tail to Thomas de Morley and Joan, whence the descent to Robert Lord Morley (d. 1442).[^ecipm-2670]

This is the **same feoffee group and the same Hingham conveyance already recorded from Walter Rye's feet-of-fines calendar, entry #1526** — "Edm. Gournay, William Pette parson of Haywardyn, Wm. Cursun, and John R[eed] of Rougham v. Wm. de Morle, Knight, of the manor of Hengham [Hingham]" (see [`norfolk-feet-of-fines-gurnay-entries-rye.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/sources/corpus_supplement/norfolk-feet-of-fines-gurnay-entries-rye.md)). The fine is the conveyancing instrument (feoffees v. William de Morley); the Morley IPM is the later recital of where that settlement landed (the entail to William's son Thomas and Joan, descending to Robert). The two sources are independent witnesses to one Gournay-Morley feoffment of Hingham, and the IPM adds the precise TNA inquisition reference, the c. 1442–43 date of recital, the full grantee-in-tail (Thomas + Joan), and confirmation of the feoffee "John Reed [of Rougham]."

The feoffee **Edmund Gournay** is most plausibly **Edmund Gurney G23** (d. 1387), the Norwich lawyer already documented acting as a magnate feoffee in Norfolk in the 1370s–80s (e.g. as a De la Pole feoffee, c. 1370). The enfeoffment to Thomas Morley (4th Baron Morley, b. 1354, d. 1416) and Joan in tail fits a settlement of the 1380s, within Edmund G23's active life. The identification is *probable, not certain* — the IPM recital gives no date and a later Edmund cannot be formally excluded from the bare name — but the feoffee circle (Pette, Cursun, Reed of Rougham) and the Hingham subject match the Rye-fine Edmund exactly.[^ecipm-rye-xref]

**John Gournay and Edmund his son (CIPM vol. 19, entries 489–94)** — already held in the repo: Sir John Gurney V's own IPM, TNA C 137/70/6, d. 4 Dec. 1408, heir Edmund his son aged 10, "held nothing in Norfolk." See the G22 companion and validation `bho-ipm-henry-iv-vol19-gournay-johnv`. Listed here only for completeness of the E-CIPM "G" block.

## Jurors — Robert Gurnay of Little Cressingham, Norfolk, five appearances 1400–1406

The separate **Index of Jurors, letter G** newly documents the **Robert Gurnay of Little Cressingham** already known from the 1405 feet-of-fines entry (Rye #64) — fixing him as a real, locally-rooted Norfolk free juror across 1400–06. A **Robert Gurnay of Norfolk** sits on Norfolk IPM juries four times in 1400–01, and the same West-Norfolk juror cohort recurs on a fifth (1406) inquisition where he is spelled "Gournay":[^ecipm-jurors-g]

| E-CIPM | Date | Deceased | Places inquired |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18-70, 18-71 | 1400 | William Leche of Newton | Newton by Castle Acre, Cawston, Oulton |
| 18-500 | 1401 | Thomas de Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick (Norfolk inq.) | Shouldham, Saham Toney, Necton, **Little Cressingham**, Panworth in Ashill |
| 19-88 | 1401 | William Leche of Newton | Newton by Castle Acre, Cawston, Oulton |
| 19-79 | 1406 | Roger de Beauchamp, knight | (Lindsey, Suffolk inq.; jury is the Norfolk cohort — see below) |

In every entry he is listed plainly as "Robert Gurnay" / "Robert Gournay" with **no residence or status designation**. The decisive link to G22's known record: he served on the 1401 Earl of Warwick IPM jury that inquired into **Little Cressingham** — and the one previously-confirmed Robert record is the **1405 Little Cressingham/Hopton fine** (Rye #64), which identifies him as "of Parva [Little] Cressingham." Same name, same SW-Norfolk micro-region (Wayland / South Greenhoe hundreds — Cressingham, Saham Toney, Necton, Newton by Castle Acre, all within ~5–10 miles), same decade.

The five panels are one cohort: the jurors **Warde, Cok, Canoun, Stag'/Steg', and Foulden** recur across both the Norfolk Leche inquisition (19-88) and the 1406 Beauchamp inquisition (19-79), which also carries the Norfolk place-surnames **de Rudham** and **Foulden** — so the "Robert Gournay" of 19-79 is the same man as "Robert Gurnay" of the Norfolk panels, not a separate Suffolk Gournay.

**What this does and does not establish — and which way it cuts.** It establishes, well beyond Daniel Gurney's say-so, that a real Robert Gurnay was a settled, locally-seated free juror in west-central Norfolk c. 1400–06, rooted at Little Cressingham (Wayland / South Greenhoe hundreds; the family's manor of Hardingham lies ~12 miles north). Across all five appearances he carries no "esquire" or residence designation — but the juror lists give no status or residence for *any* juror, so that absence discriminates nothing. This corroborates the 1405-fine man's existence and local standing; it does **not** by itself prove he is Robert Gournay G22, Sir John Gurney V's younger son. On a full reading, though, the considerations once taken to argue *against* the identification do not hold: the ~12-mile distance to Hardingham — together with the cadet "Gurney's Manor" at Hingham and the Great Ellingham junior line, and Denver — places him *inside* the family's landholding orbit, not outside it; the missing gentry style is a record-genre artifact (in the same Walter Rye feet-of-fines series Edmund G23 the lawyer appears as plain "Edm. Gournay" and Sir John V the knight as plain "John Gournay", both style-less, while only #200 carries "Armiger"); and there is **no competing candidate** — the Index of Jurors holds no other Norfolk Gurnay in the period, and Gurnay was a rare surname. Net: the Cressingham Robert is identified with G22 on the convergence of a reliable pedigree and the contemporary record (the ancestor's standing "Confirmed" status), the filiation being the one element not separately documented; the new evidence is *favourable* to the West Barsham identification, not against it. See the G22 companion for the full assessment.

**Distinct, excluded:** *Robert Gournay, Somerset (1412, 19-967)* = the Somerset/SW collateral line; and a Buckinghamshire **Gurney/Gorney/Gerney** family of Stoke Mandeville (jurors John, Robert, Simon, William, Reynold across 1400–46) — a separate house, not the Norfolk line. The Index of Jurors contains **no** Norfolk Gurnay juror other than Robert in this period, and no first-character-corrupted hidden forms.

## West-Country / collateral Gournays (Somerset, Wiltshire, Gloucestershire)

These confirm the **Somerset/SW Gournay collateral line** (documented in [`somerset-gournay-collateral.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/places/somerset-gournay-collateral.md)) as fee-holders in the Crown record where the Norfolk line is absent:

- **Thomas Gurney — knight's fee at Englishcombe, Somerset (£10), Clare inheritance.** Named as a former fee-holder in the IPM of **Isabel, Countess of Warwick** (d. 26 Dec. 1439; inq. at Ilchester 1440–41; C 139/96/3 mm. 36–37): "Englishcombe, a knight's fee, formerly held by Thomas Gurney, £10." Englishcombe was the historic Somerset Gurney caput; the fee had passed by the Clare descent to the Beauchamp/Despenser comital estate.[^ecipm-25325]
- **Richard de Gournay, heirs of / "the heir of Robert Gourncoy" — ¼ knight's fee at Welwe, Wiltshire.** In the IPM of **Thomas, Earl of Salisbury** (inq. at Salisbury 11 Jan. 1429, 7 Hen. VI; C 139/41/57 mm. 37, 39): "Welwe, ¼ knight's fee, presently held by the heir of Robert Gourncoy." The Index of Persons cross-files this under both "Gournay, Richard de, heirs of" and the variant head-form **"Gourncoy"** — a useful reminder that the scholarly index splits a single fee between two spelling-forms.[^ecipm-23283]
- **Stephen Gornay — King's Stanley, Gloucestershire, holding at the king's will.** In the IPM of **John Proute, parson of Woodchester** (inq. at Minchinhampton 24 Sept. 1410; E 149/95/7 m. 2): Robert Aumfray brought a writ against Stephen Gornay for premises in King's Stanley "alleging that they were the right of the church … because Stephen had no right therein except by the king's will."[^ecipm-19677]

## Not the gentry line

- **John Gourney, smith — Wendover, Buckinghamshire.** A customary tenant in the IPM of **Philippa, Duchess of York** (d. 2 Apr. 1416; Wendover, Bucks; C 138/14/45 m. 31): "John Gourney, smith, for a tenement called 'Boycrofte', 18s. 2½d." A tradesman tenant, not the Gournay gentry; logged to exclude.[^ecipm-20407]

- **Matthew Gournay, knight — 13 inquisitions across CIPM vols 18–26.** These are the famous **Sir Matthew de Gournay (d. 1406)** of the Somerset/SW line, already fully treated as a collateral in [`somerset-gournay-collateral.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/places/somerset-gournay-collateral.md); they are *not* the Norfolk direct-line G29 Matthew de Gournay (fl. c. 1180–1217), who long predates the E-CIPM's c. 1399 start. (E-CIPM refs: 18-152, 18-446, 18-651, 18-846, 18-962, 18-1126, 18-1146, 19-217, 19-223, 20-482, 20-484, 24-214, 26-093.)

## Place-index control — the direct-line manors appear, but never under a Gurney

A cross-check of the E-CIPM place index for the core Norfolk Gurney manors returns the manors held by **other lords**, never with a Gurney as the Crown tenant — a positive corroboration of the "held nothing in chief" conclusion read from the negative side:
- **Hardingham** — held with the manor of Flockthorpe and the Hardingham advowson by **Thomas Camoys, knight** (demised for life to an Elizabeth, reversion to Camoys), not a Gurney.
- **West Barsham / Barsham** — surfaces only in juror/escheator context (e.g. Edmund Barsham as a Norfolk-Suffolk juror) around the de la Pole inquisitions, not as a Gurney fee.
- **Harpley** — appears as a place (a parson Drewe of Harpley) with no Gurney tenancy in the indexed entries.

The Gurneys' Norfolk manors were held of mesne lords, so they fall outside the Crown's IPM net except where a Gurney acts as a feoffee for a tenant-in-chief (the Hingham/Morley case above).

**Two manors at Hingham — the feoffment is not a Gurney tenancy.** The IPM of **Thomas de Morley** (d. 5 Oct. 1416; E-CIPM 20-596) lists the **great manor of Hingham and the hundred of Forehoe** among Morley holdings (recorded held from 1401, with Buxton, Swanton Morley, Foulsham, Aldeby, Hockering and the hundred of Eynsford), and names **no Gurney** anywhere.[^ecipm-20596] This fixes the relationship: Edmund Gournay and his co-feoffees *conveyed the great manor of Hingham TO the Morleys* (a feoffee-to-uses settlement), they did not hold it of Morley as mesne tenants. The family's own **"Gurney's Manor" at Hingham** — Blomefield's junior-branch parcel that continued in the Barsham/Great Ellingham line to Henry Gurney G15 (lord 1572) — is a *separate* manor from the Morleys' great manor of Hingham, and is not the subject of the Morley IPMs.

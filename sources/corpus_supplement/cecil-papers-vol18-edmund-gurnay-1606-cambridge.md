# Cecil Papers, vol. 18 — Edmund Gurnay's 1606 appeal to the Earl of Salisbury (Cambridge suspension)

Primary letter from **Edmund Gurnay** — Fellow of Corpus Christi College, Cambridge, and afterwards rector of Edgefield (1614) and Harpley (1620–48) — to **Robert Cecil, Earl of Salisbury, in his capacity as Chancellor of the University of Cambridge**, appealing a suspension imposed on him within the College. The letter is calendared as undated and assigned to 1606.

It is the primary-document witness to the College dispute that Daniel Gurney's *Record* supplement narrates only at second hand (citing Lamb's *Masters' History of Corpus Christi College*): the clash between Edmund Gurnay and the Master, Dr. Thomas Jegon, whose brother John Jegon was then Bishop of Norwich.

## Extract (Calendar of the Cecil Papers, vol. 18)

> **Edmund Gurnay to the Earl of Salisbury, Chancellor of the University of Cambridge.**
> [1606.] Will hope for a word in his favour from his lordship to the Vice-Chancellor, who might inhibit their prefect from further proceedings against him in a matter still in dispute. The prefect has suspended Gurnay and in the person of the visitor, an office which he cannot exercise in their College but by virtue of the statute enclosed has ratified the suspension. He has not yet, however, deprived him of his board and the other emoluments of their college. Gurnay can only appeal to the Chancellor, as their lawyers are in such awe of the bishopric of Norwich that they will not open their mouths against the Bishop of that see or the Bishop's brother who is their present prefect. Unless his lordship should think good to refer his case to certain men, Don (Dominus) Daniel Dunn, Dr. Richard Swale, Dr. Steward or any others who are more removed from either party, the writer's only hope is in a word in his behalf from Salisbury to the Vice-Chancellor.—Undated.

## What the document records

- Edmund Gurnay is under suspension by the College "prefect" (the Master), the suspension being "ratified" by the same man acting in the office of visitor under an enclosed College statute; he has not yet lost his board and emoluments.
- He frames the obstacle as fear of the see of Norwich: the College lawyers "will not open their mouths against the Bishop of that see or the Bishop's brother who is their present prefect." In 1606 the Bishop of Norwich was John Jegon and the Master of Corpus Christi was his brother Thomas Jegon — the "Bishop's brother … present prefect."
- He asks Salisbury to have the case referred to neutral civil lawyers, naming **Sir Daniel Dunn (Dun)**, **Dr. Richard Swale**, and **Dr. Steward** — all Doctors'-Commons civilians.

The calendar entry is an abstract, not a full transcript; spellings and the editor's parentheses are the calendar's own. The letter is filed among "Miscellaneous 1605" but assigned to 1606 by the editor.

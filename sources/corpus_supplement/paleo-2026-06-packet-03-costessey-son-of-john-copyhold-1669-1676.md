## Bottom line

The image does show a John Gurney in the relevant Costessey court material, but I do not read the critical phrase as `Johannes Gurnee filius Johannis`. The clearest target line reads `Johannes Gurne et Johannes Bower`, not John Gurney son of John. The machine reading appears to have conflated repeated abbreviated `Joh[ann]is` forms and the following party name `Bower`.

No father of John Gurney is stated in the visible target entry, and I see no `nuper de...` descriptor tying him to a deceased father. This substantially reduces the value of this packet as a direct father-son proof, though it remains useful Costessey manorial evidence for a John Gurney acting in copyhold property business.

## Answers to packet questions

### (a) Exact regnal/calendar date

Visible date: 21 January 1674, probably 21 January 1674/5 in modern reckoning. This would fall in 26 Charles II. The regnal year is inferred from the modernized date, not read from a visible regnal clause.

### (b) Is it "Johannes Gurnee filius Johannis"?

No. The best reading of the target name line is:

```text
Johannes Gurne et Johannes Bower
```

I do not see `filius Johannis`, `son of John`, or a father-status clause such as `nuper de...`. The phrase is therefore not evidence that this John Gurney was son of another John Gurney.

### (c) Tenement and abuttals

Partly legible only. The visible property language includes:

- a messuage or cottage: `messuag[um] sive cotagium`
- meadow/pasture and pieces of land
- Costessey as the locus
- possible abuttal or associated names including Hopper and Mary, and a former Thomas Jolly

I cannot securely recover the full parcel bounds from this image. A better or earlier/later image in the same court book may be needed to identify the full tenement.

### (d) Other parties

The other clearly relevant party is John Bower. The quitclaim/release language appears to run toward William Hender/Henderd, so William is probably the recipient or possessor, not the person quitclaiming. I would not cite him as `William quitclaiming` without a better read of the preceding lines.

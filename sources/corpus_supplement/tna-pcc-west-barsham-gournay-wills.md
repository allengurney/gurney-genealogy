# TNA PCC wills of the Gournay family of West Barsham, Norfolk

Working transcriptions and abstracts of two registered-copy wills in the Prerogative
Court of Canterbury for members of the Gournay family seated at West Barsham, Norfolk.
The two documents are related: the son named and provided for in the first will is the
testator of the second, and the same widow — Frances Gournay — appears in both.

Source ID: `tna-pcc-west-barsham-gournay-wills`

**Source caveats.** These are the PCC register copies (clerk's engrossed copies), not the
original signed wills; register copies can normalise spelling and occasionally drop
witnesses' autograph forms. Readings below are best-effort working transcriptions from the
digitised images, not diplomatic transcriptions. Uncertain readings are bracketed.
Dates are given as they appear in the register and in the TNA catalogue; the probate years
fall in the period when the English legal year began 25 March, so a will written in
December or February may sit in the preceding historical year.

## 1. Edward Gournay of West Barsham, esquire — will November 1639, proved 8 February 1642

**Reference:** TNA, PROB 11/188/136. Register name: Cambell, quire numbers 1–42.
Marginal heading in the register: *Edwardi Gournay*.

Opening:

> In the name of God Amen The [last] date of November 1639 I Edward Gournay of West
> Barsham in the County of Norff Esqr being (the Lord be praysed therefore) in perfect
> health, memorie and understanding doe hereby revoke annihilate and frustrate all former
> Wills, Codicills and Testaments whatsoever…

The religious preamble is unusually long and personal, running to roughly fifteen lines of
Calvinist self-examination on sin, corruption and the sole sufficiency of Christ's
satisfaction, before reaching the burial direction. He asks to be buried near
**Martha Gournay** at West Barsham, and affirms bodily resurrection: *"though my body be
consumed and eaten with wormes yett att the day of Judgement I shall arryse with the same
body and shall with these eyes behold my God my Redeemer Jesus Christ."*

**Estate and debts.** All goods, moneys, chattels and purchased lands are to be improved to
best value to discharge his debts, funeral and burial expenses. If the estate proves
insufficient, the benefit of the **wardship and marriage of his child** is to be applied to
the debts; once the debts are satisfied, that benefit is to form a portion for the child.

**Family named in the will:**

- **Frances ("ffrauncis") Gournay, his wife**, described as then pregnant — the portion is
  for *"that Childe which ffrauncis my nowe wife [is quickned] withall."* He asks her to be
  *"loveing and carefull in the breeding of itt"* and to add what she conveniently may
  toward the child's advancement.
- **Henry Gournay, his son**, who receives *"all my [owne] Library, Mappes, Globes or any
  such mathematicall instruments as I shall have att the time of my death, and that they bee
  all kept for his use in my Closett nexte over the kitchin untill hee be of discretion to
  use them."* The custody clause indicates Henry was under age in 1639.
- **Thomas Gournay, his brother.**
- **His sisters — Susan, Dorothy [Math(ew)son?], Elizabeth, Ellin and Margarett** — each
  twenty shillings, which he desires them to spend on a ring, so that *"when I am out of
  sight I would not be out of mynd."* The second name in the list carries what appears to be
  an appended surname, probably a married name; the letterforms admit *Mathewson* /
  *Mathison* and the reading is not secure.
- **Martha Gournay**, named in the burial direction at West Barsham.

He closes the ring bequest with the motto **"Pax Vobis E:G:"**

**Charitable and household bequests.** Five shillings apiece to the servants who wait on him
at table and two shillings to the rest, *"in token off my good will towards them"*; fivepence
apiece to twenty of the neediest and poorest widows in [Little] Massingham and the like at
Fakenham, to be distributed within eight days after his burial; and a sum to the Vicar of
West Barsham for *"all tythes forgotten and not satisfied."*

## 2. Henry Gourney of West Barsham, esquire — will 21 December [1660], proved 11 February 1661

**Reference:** TNA, PROB 11/303/284. Register name: May, quire numbers 1–53.
Marginal heading in the register: *Henrici Gourney*.

Opening:

> In the name of God Amen I Henry Gourney of West Barsham in the County of Norff Esqr
> being sicke in body but of perfect memory doe hereby declare and publish my last will and
> testament first I bequeath my soule unto the Lord that gave it and my body to the Earth
> dust of which it was made

**The settlement recital.** The will opens with a recital of how the estate came to him:

> Whereas ffrauncis Gourney widdowe my Mother by her deede of surrender did surrender
> and covenant her estate for life of the Mannor of West Barsham, Helton alias Hault Hall
> in Helton and all the lands libertyes and priviledges to those the said Mannors
> apperteyning and all other lands in any of the said townes or in any other towne
> adjoyning unto me the said Henry Gourney her sonne who had the Remainder in tayle…

A further clause recites that he had levied a fine, proclaimed according to the statute, of
all the premises. The left margin of the entry carries the continuation *"and North Barsham
also … Scotts Hall in North Barsham."*

**Properties named:** the Manor of West Barsham; **Helton alias Hault Hall in Helton**
(place-name reading uncertain; the letterforms would also admit a Hel(hough)ton form);
**Scotts Hall in North Barsham**; and lands in North Barsham and adjoining towns.

**Provision for wife and mother.** His wife is to have all the premises for the term of her
natural life, paying **Frances Gourney his mother one hundred pounds yearly** for her life,
at the four most usual feasts — *"Christmas our Lady day Midsumer and Michas"* — by even
and equal portions, with power to distrain on the premises for arrears.

**The marriage portion.** The life estate to the wife is conditional on
**William Adams of the Middle Temple, esquire, father of his wife**, paying the sum of
**two thousand pounds** *"according as he stands bound to pay to me at the tymes limitted &
appointed."* The executor is to apply that £2,000 to the testator's debts, any surplus going
to the wife. If Adams defaults, the executors are to sell so much of the Barsham lands as
will satisfy the debts.

**Executors:** *"my deare wife, Mr John Colson and Mr William Darby."* The wife is not named
in the body of the will, being referred to throughout as *"my deare wife."*

**Witnesses:** *"Eliz[abeth] Gournay, Thomasin Golding, Thomas Wetherby."* The first
witness's forename is written with the standard `Eliz:` abbreviation.

**Date and probate.** Written *"in one sheete of paper the one & twentieth day of December."*
The probate clause records the grant to **William Darby**, one of the executors named in the
will, with power reserved to **John Colson**, the other executor.

No children are mentioned anywhere in the will, and no provision is made for issue; the
estate passes to the widow for life.

## Relationship between the two documents

Edward Gournay's will (1639) names his wife Frances and his under-age son Henry.
Henry Gourney's will (1660/1) names Frances Gourney, widow, as his mother, and recites that
she surrendered her life estate in the West Barsham property to him as her son holding the
remainder in tail. The two wills therefore document father and son, with Frances Gournay the
widow of the first and mother of the second. Henry survived his father by about nineteen
years; his mother was still living when he made his will, and he charged the estate with a
£100 annuity for her.

# Calendar of Inquisitions Post Mortem, vol. 7 (Edward III), no. 243 — Thomas Bardolf

Source ID: `bho-ipm-edward-iii-vol7-bardolf-swathings`

Extract of the Gurnay-relevant extents from the inquisition post mortem of Thomas
Bardolf (no. 243), *Calendar of Inquisitions Post Mortem*, vol. 7 (Edward III),
pp. 174–183. British History Online,
<https://www.british-history.ac.uk/inquis-post-mortem/vol7/pp174-183> (free tier).
Volume index (pp. 571–576): "Gurney, Gurnay, Gournay, Gournai, John de, 243
(pp. 176, 180)"; "Gurney, Gurnay, barony, 243 (p. 176)."

The inquisition is headed **"243. THOMAS BARDOLF."** The three Norfolk caput manors
below were re-settled on Thomas Bardolf and Agnes his wife by a fine of 7 Edward II
(1313/14) through the feoffees **John de Gurnay and John de Halughton**, and are
returned as held in chief "as parcel of the barony of Gurnay." A fourth manor,
Swathings (Shathingge/Shathinge), is returned lower in the same extent as held
directly by John de Gurnay by knight service.

## The extents (verbatim)

> Castre by Great Yarmouth. The manor held jointly with Agnes his wife, of the gift
> and enfeoffment of **John de Gurnay and John Haliugton** [Halughton], to them and
> the heirs of the said Thomas, by fine levied in the king's court in 7 Edward II,
> and by the king's charter, of the king in chief **as parcel of the barony of
> Gurnay**.

> Cantelee [Cantley]. The manor held jointly with Agnes his wife of the gift and
> enfeoffment of John de Gurnay and John de Halughton, to them and the heirs of the
> said Thomas, by fine levied in the king's court in 7 Edward II, and by the king's
> charter, of the king in chief as a parcel of the barony of Gurney.

> Qwyneberge [Winburgh]. The manor held jointly, as above, of the gift and
> enfeoffment of John de Gurney and John de Halughton, by fine &c. as above, of the
> king in chief, by service of a knight's fee.

> Shathingge [Swathings]. The manor held by **John de Gurnay by service of a
> knight's fee**.

The same inquisition returns the Bardolf caput manor of **Stokebardolf** (Stoke
Bardolf, Notts.) "held of the king in chief as of the crown by service of a knight's
fee," with the Nottinghamshire/Derbyshire holdings; the heir throughout is John,
son of Thomas Bardolf, aged 17–18.

## Neutral note on the record

The Bardolfs held the former senior-line Gournay barony through Juliana de Gournay,
daughter and heir of the last senior-line Hugh, who married William Bardolf of
Wormegay; the "barony of Gurnay" phrasing in this 1329 extent refers to that
inherited honour. The inquisition does not distinguish which John de Gurnay served
as Bardolf's feoffee in 1313/14, nor does it state the relationship of the John who
holds Swathings to the barony. It is an abstract (calendar) of the original Latin
inquisition, not the membrane itself.

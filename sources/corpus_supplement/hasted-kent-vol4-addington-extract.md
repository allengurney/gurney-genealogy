## The descent of Addington (pp. 544–545, verbatim, normalized)

After giving the two Domesday entries for "Eddintune" (held by Ralf son of Turald of the bishop of Bayeux; earlier by Lestan and Agelred of King Edward), Hasted continues:

> These were plainly, by the descriptions, two separate estates, and both certainly, by their names, in this parish, and held by the same person. On the disgrace of the bishop of Baieux, about four years afterwards [c. 1088], they became, among the rest of his possessions, confiscated to the crown. Soon after which they seem to have been held as one manor, by William de Gurnay, and afterwards by Galiena de Gurnay, his grandchild; they were succeeded in the possession of this place by the family of Mandeville, or De Magna Villa, as the name was written in Latin, who held it of the family of Montchensie, as capital lords of the fee.
>
> But this family was extinct here in the next reign of king Edward II, for Roger del Eschequer was owner of it in the 7th year of it [...]
>
> In the 20th year of king Edward III he [Nicholas de Daggeworth] paid aid for this manor, which Robert de Scaccario before held in Addington, of Warine de Montchensie, as of his manor of Swanscombe.

## The church section repeats the Gurnay gift (p. 547)

> William de Gurnay gave to the church and priory of St. Andrew, in Rochester, in pure and perpetual alms, certain tithes of the demesnes of his parish of Edintune; but they lying so dispersed, that they could not be conveniently gathered by the monks, though they could be easily collected by the parson of this church: therefore it was agreed, that the parson of it should pay the yearly sum of five shillings to the monks of Rochester, on St. Andrew's day, for them.
>
> This pension, after the dissolution of the priory in the 31st year of king Henry VIII [1539-40], was surrendered into the king's hands [...]

Hasted also notes "The church of Addington has always been an appendage to the manor" — William de Gurnay held manor and advowson (the right to appoint the parish priest) together.

## Reading notes

1. Hasted's descent — William de Gurnay (from c. 1090s) → Galiena de Gurnay his grandchild → the Mandevilles — matches the two-Galiena chain recoverable from the Registrum Roffense, the Missenden cartulary, and the Hatton Book of Seals apparatus: William de Gurnay of Addington → (unnamed child) → Galiena I de Gurnay (m. Manasser de Dammartin, d. 1178/9) → William de Dammartin (d. 1195) → Galiena II de Dammartin (m. 3rd Ernald de Mandeville) → Mandevilles of Addington. Hasted compresses the intermediate Dammartin generations.
2. Addington was held **of the manor/honour of Swanscombe** — the honour of Talbot of Swanscombe (Geoffrey Talbot held it in chief on the day Henry I died, per the 1166 carta of Walter de Meduana; it passed "per Ciciliam" to Walter de Mayenne and in moiety to the Montchensies, whence Hasted's "family of Montchensie, as capital lords"). The Talbots were Domesday under-tenants of Hugh de Gournay III at Liston, Essex, and Richard de Talbot married a daughter of Gerard de Gournay.

### **UNRECORDED BIOGRAPHIES**

#### **FRANCIS GOURNAY (OR GURNEY), OF MALDON, ESSEX.**

**By CHAS. A. BERNAU, F.S.G.**

The life of Francis Gournay has been written already more than once, but former historians of the GURNEY, or Gournay, family appear to have attempted it without first consulting the records at the Public Record Office. Recent investigations there have brought many hitherto forgotten facts to light, and it is felt, therefore, that a revised biography of this interesting individual may help to solve a certain genealogical problem which has puzzled many genealogists for years.

**HIS PARENTAGE** Francis GOURNAY, born in 1628, with whom this article deals, was the son of another Francis GOURNAY, a member of the Merchant Taylors' Company, and a merchant living in the parish of St. Benet Finck, London, the sixth son of Henry GOURNAY, of Great Ellingham and West Barsham, Norfolk. In or before the year 1618 Francis Gournay married Anne, daughter of William BROWNING, of Norwich, merchant, and later of Maldon, Essex. In 1634 Francis attested the GOURNAY pedigree in the Heralds' Visitation of London. It is thought that he may have been identical with a Francis GOURNAY who gave Sir Henry SPELMAN a manuscript pedigree of the Gournay family.

**HIS PARENTS' CHILDREN** There is no complete record of the children of Francis Gournay and Anne BROWNING, and the information about those of whose existence we know is fragmentary.

1. . . . **Dorothy**, baptized at St. Benet Finck 2nd March 1619. Living in 1634 (Visit. London).
2. . . . **Roger**, baptized at St. Benet Finck 27th Decr. 1621. Living 1634, then described as eldest son and heir (Visit. London).
3. . . . **Frances**, baptized at St. Benet Finck 18th January 1626; buried there 30th September of the same year.
4. . . . **Francis**, baptized at St. Benet Finck 13th November 1628. Of whom hereafter.
5. . . . **Lucretia**, baptized at St. Benet Finck 28th Octr. 1630. Living in 1634 (Visit. London).
6. . . . **Thomas**, baptized at St. Benet Finck 19th April 1636. In 1681 letters of administration of the goods of his brother, John GURNEY of Maldon, a bachelor, were granted to him.
7. . . . **Margaret**, baptized at St. Benet Finck 28th July 1637.
8. . . . **Anne** (probably the eldest child), living in 1634 (Visit. London).
9. . . . **John**, evidently born after 1634. As stated already, in 1681 letters of administration of the goods of John GURNEY, of Maldon, a bachelor, were granted to his brother, Thomas GURNEY. It has been suggested that John and Thomas are identical with the sons of Francis GURNEY (b. 1628) as no evidence could be discovered that there was a John in the same generation as this Francis, but that evidence has now been found, for in 1674 John GURNEY, of St. Mary's Maldon, paid the tax on nine hearths (Lay Sub. 246/22), and it is not reasonable to suppose that this entry applied to a youth then only 19 years old whose father was still living in the same parish. 
10. ? . . . . **Richard GURNEY**, mentioned in the corporation accounts of Maldon under date 1677. The will of Michael COOPER, of Maldon, gent., dated 18th Feb. 1687 and proved (Comm. Court of London, Essex & Herts.—"Hamor", 207) 26th April 1688, mentions "that piece or parcel of ground in the parish of St. Peter (Maldon) which I lately purchased of Mr. Richard GOURNEY" and later in the same will occurs "all that piece or parcel of waste land which I lately purchased of Mr. Richard GOURNAY". 
11. ? . . . . **George GURNEY**, who married in 1660 at St. Peter's Maldon Mary ELLISTON, widow, and whose son, George, was baptized at the same church in the following year.

With reference to the two last, Richard and George, it should be noted that Francis, born 1628, named two of his sons "Richard" and "George," so that it is by no means improbable that the two abovenamed were brothers of that Francis.

**HIS MARRIAGE** In or before the year 1654 Francis GURNEY (born 1628) married Anne, daughter of Jeremiah BROWNING, alderman of Maldon. She was baptized there on 23rd July 1637, so was about nine years her husband's junior. The descendants of John GURNEY of Norwich claim that she is identical with "John GURNEY'S mother" who in 1687 gave a certificate under her hand "signifieing her good will and free co(n)cent" to his marriage in that year. There is no evidence on the point but it is not too much to suppose that Francis GURNEY probably married his cousin, as his mother was Anne, daughter of William BROWNING, of Norwich, merchant, and afterwards of Maldon.

The BROWNING family of Maldon does not appear to have been traced from the parish registers and there are very few wills to help us. On 23rd Decr. 1647 letters of administration of the goods of Jeremiah BROWNINGE, late of Maldon, Co. Essex, deceased, were granted to Jane BROWNINGE the relict of the said deceased (P.C.C.). This entry, we may presume, relates to Francis GURNEY's father-in-law. If so, he died when his daughter Anne was only about ten years old. There is a possibility, however, that it refers to a great-uncle of Anne.

Francis GURNEY was probably a brother-in-law of the Dr. Thomas BROWNING who will be mentioned later.

**HIS CHILDREN** The children of Francis GURNEY and Anne BROWNING were:—

1. . . . **John**, born 7th Octr. and baptized 30th Octr. 1655 at Maldon. Living 1664 as son and heir (Visit. Essex). Of whom hereafter.
2. . . . **Francis**, born 14th Feb. and baptized 2nd March 1657 at Maldon; buried there 28th May 1658.
3. . . . **George**, born 10th June and baptized 20th June 1659 at Maldon; buried there 15th January 1663.
4. . . . **Thomas**, born 17th March and baptized 31st March 1661 at Maldon. Living 1664 (Visit. Essex).
5. . . . **Charles**, born 4th Feb. and baptized 24th Feb. 1662 at Maldon; buried there 16th September 1668.
6. . . . **Jane**. Living 1664 (Visit. Essex).
7. . . . **Richard**, baptized at Maldon 1st April 1664.
8. . . . **Francis**, baptized at Maldon 17th Sept. 1665; buried there 6th Jan. 1666.
9. . . . **Anne**, baptized at Maldon 22nd Jan. 1666.
10. . . . **Henry**, baptized at Maldon 19th March 1667.

**HIS OFFICIAL POSITION** On p. 539 of "The House of GOURNAY" will be found a copy of the deed, dated 12th August 1662, whereby Francis GURNEY and others were appointed Aldermen of Maldon.

He was one of the Bailiffs of Maldon in 1664, as appears by a list of the communicants in the registers of St. Mary's parish.

**HIS BUSINESS AND LAWSUITS** On the 3rd May 1676 Francis GOURNEY of Maldon, gent., Peter ALDRIDGE and John DAY of the same, serjeants at mace of the Borough of Maldon and Christopher SAVADGE, yeoman, and John HAWES of the same, labourer, file a Bill in Chancery (Bridges 489/64 & Hamilton 521/90) against Thomas DABBS, Abraham BARTELL alias BARTLETT, Daniel BARTELL alias BARTLETT, Elizabeth, Daniel's wife, James BARTELL alias BARTLETT, Mary, wife of James, Robert BEARD, Jane, wife of Robert, and Hugh BROWNE. From this we see that in September 1675 Thomas DABBS of Maldon "woll kember" was indebted to Francis GOURNEY in the sum of £14. 8. 4 for a bargain of wool sold to him by GOURNEY and, GOURNEY pressing for payment, DABBS fearing that GOURNEY would arrest him combined with the other defendants and left only his wife in possession of his house. GOURNEY entered an attachment in the Court of Records of Maldon and attached the house and estate of the said DABBS. The two aforesaid serjeants attached them and the goods were valued and found to be worth £11. 12. 6. They were carried away in SAVADGE's cart. Abraham BARTLETT has brought an action of trespass against the plaintiffs pretending he has a bill of sale on the goods. The Answer alleges that the Bill of Sale represents a genuine transaction and was not entered into merely for the purpose of defeating the attachment.

On the 22nd May 1677 Francis GOURNEY of Maldon, gent., files a Bill in Chancery (BRIDGES 608/99) against William CORNELL. From this we see that in the year 1673, in the time of the late wars between His Majesty and the States of Holland, Francis GOURNEY, having acquaintance with one William CORNELL, of the town of Poole, merchant, who dealt in a commodity arising in those parts called fuller's earth or tobacco pipe clay, asked the said CORNELL to send him speedily a vessel of the said clay of the best sort during the continuance of the said wars and, if he could not send the same while the wars lasted, that he should send none at all because then the same would come to a bad market and the price thereof would be fallen. CORNELL answered this letter to the effect that he could not, in regard of the danger of the enemies, send any such commodity by sea while the war lasted and GOURNEY, receiving this answer, gave over all further expectation of receiving the said commodity. Yet, later when peace had been made some three months and the seas had been cleared of the enemy, and also the price of the said commodity had fallen to half its former value, the said CORNELL caused several vessels of the said tobacco pipe clay to be sent by sea to Maldon upon GOURNEY's account, signifying by letter that he demanded 22/- per ton for the same. The clay was put into a warehouse and GOURNEY exposed it for sale to various chapmen but he could not make any "gainfull sale" of it on account of its badness, roughness and coarseness. GOURNEY states that he had little or no skill in the said commodity but that several persons who had skill and knowledge therein declared that it was unfit for work and they could make no use for it even if it were given to them for nothing. Consequently GOURNEY could not sell a quarter of the consignment and hath the balance still by him unsold. Customers and chapmen who used to deal with him formerly for that commodity are now lost to him. He has been forced to get some more and better of the like commodity to mix with it and so by that means "doth utter" a little of it sometimes at but 20/- per ton, being all he can get for it, even though it is now so mixed with good clay. CORNELL sent also a parcel of Purbecke Stones to GOURNEY, requesting him to pay the money due for the clay and stones to CORNELL's order. GOURNEY has thus paid to CORNELL and for his account about £30 and considers that, in view of the badness of the clay, he has paid enough and indeed he is "much damnifyed by dealeing with him". CORNELL came to Maldon about October 1675 to settle his accounts with the various persons in those parts with whom he had dealt and to GOURNEY among the rest. At that time he admitted the receipt of the £30 but demanded a further sum as due to him; whereupon GOURNEY took him to several persons who had bought some of the clay and they informed CORNELL of its badness and that the clay was an unworkable commodity and unfit for use. GOURNEY requested him to take back the clay, but CORNELL, being satisfied as to the truth about its condition, directed him to sell the remainder as fast as he could and for what he could get for it, stating that he would approve of the sales, however little resulted from them; further that whatever GOURNEY could raise by such sales he was to apply in the first place to pay himself for the difference between the actual value of the stones and the £30 which GOURNEY had overpaid for them. Any balance should be applied by GOURNEY to refunding himself for the charges of freight, sales and warehousing, etc. The money in hand from previous sales was paid over to CORNELL, therefore GOURNEY claims that he is no longer indebted to CORNELL, yet notwithstanding this CORNELL denies the agreement and the said payments and hath lately caused GOURNEY to be arrested by order of the Court of King's Bench upon a supposed debt of £43.

Francis GOURNEY's tragic death only a few weeks after this Bill was filed probably accounts for our finding no Answer to it.

**HIS DEATH** The "House of GOURNAY" states that Francis GOURNEY lived in the parish of St. Mary in the lower part of the town of Maldon upon the banks of the Blackwater in a house which he rented of the Corporation of that borough, in which he was a merchant. This fact is mentioned here because the position of the house is significant. If its tenant wished to commit suicide by drowning, the Blackwater flowed past his house. Actually Francis GOURNEY was found drowned in his own well. At the time it was held to be suicide much to the detriment of his family as the personal estate of a suicide was forfeited to the Crown. We have not been able to find the records of the coroner's inquest but it is probable that a modern coroner's jury would have considered it an accidental death or, at the worst, suicide during temporary insanity. Our knowledge of the facts of the case are gleaned from several caveats entered by interested persons very soon after his death.

"On the 13th of August 1677. Let nothing be done in the estate of Francis GURNEY of St. Mary's in the town of Maldon in Essex, deceased, unknown to Julian SWALLOW acting for the interest of Thomas BROWNING S.T.P., the chief creditor." (P.C.C.).

It will be seen that the above Caveat was entered the day after Francis GOURNEY's death. "The chief creditor" is probably a legal fiction, though it may be correct. It would almost appear as if this caveat was entered on behalf of the family by the brother-in-law of the deceased to protect, if possible, the interests of the widow and young children.

"August 20th 1677. That noe Grant passe of the Estate of Francis GURNEE of Malden in Essex or of Robert READER of Baddow in the same County, who drowned themselves the 12th of this instant August: at my Lord Treasurers desire". (Public Record Office, S.P. Dom. Entry Book, 45/42.).

And on the same page of the same volume:— "August 21st 1677. That noe Grant passe of the Estate of Francis GURNEY, of Malden in Essex, who drowned himself in his owne well on Sunday night the 12th of this instant August. At the desire of Samuell PEPYS Esqr.".

This is the Samuel PEPYS. We have seen it stated in an annotated edition of his "Diary" that this caveat was entered by him as a kindly action to save the property for the GOURNEY family, if possible.

"Fra: GURNEE of Malden in Essex (here follows the word "who" deleted) drown'd himself the 12th Instant Augt. Robert READER of Baddow in the same County alsoe drown'd himself the same day. Caveats to be entered by my Lord Trea'rs Comand & in his name. Augt. 20th memn. Mr. BRENT entered this Caveat in Mr. Sec'rys name".

The last entry is in S.P. Dom. Car. II., 396/21. Then in the same volume, folio 34, we find:— "Frances GURNEY of Malden in Essex drowned himselfe in his owne Well on Sunday night the 12. of this instant August 1677. At the desire of Samuell PEPYS Esqr. August 21st 1677".

The next entry is in the P.C.C. Caveats:— "On the 25th of August 1677. Let nothing be done in the estate of the aforesaid Francis GURNEY unknown to MILLER acting for Thomas HASKIN, a creditor of the deceased".

**HIS PROPERTY** We glean particulars of his property at the time of his death from Exchequer Special Commission No. 6222. This is a Commission dated 20th August 1677 directed to the Sheriff of Essex and others. Whereas a certain Francis GURNEY late of Malden and Robert READER late of Baddow have committed suicide the aforesaid Commissioners are appointed by the King to inquire by the oath of lawful men of Essex when they committed suicide and into whose hands all their goods and chattels have been taken, and to take the same for the King's use. They are instructed to sell some of the goods, when valued by the jurors, and the money arising from the sale shall be paid into the Exchequer for the King's use. The Inquisition was taken at Chelmsford on 11th Octr. 1677. The jurors say that Francis GURNEY committed suicide on 12th August 1677 in Malden, on which date he was possessed of goods etc. to the value of £560. 13. 4 as specified in a schedule annexed to the Commission. The jurors say that Robert READER is dead but whether by suicide or not they do not know.

From the schedule we see that GOURNEY possessed £5 in money, 100 bushels of salt, 360 bushels of coal, 40 bushels of white salt, loads of rye, 4 1/2 loads of hay, 2 iron salt pans, a flockbed, a brass pot, certain furnace irons, 2 salt tubs, a plough, a harrow, a cart, a boat, a cistern, another tub for salt, 1000 faggots, 2 colts, 13 sheep and lumber in the possession of Samuel LOVE of Goldenhanger, all valued at £54. 13. 6.

RADLEY of Tolleshunt Major owed him £2. 2. 8 for merchandize, NICHOLSON of Tolleshunt Graygoose owed him 12/-, Robert FRANCKE of Goldenhanger owed him 11/8, Joshua THWAITE of Chelmsford owed him £1. 16. 0, George JEFFERYES owed him 18/-, William HULCK of Chelmsford owed him £7, Charles FULLER the Younger of Chelmsford owed him £1. 2. 6, John BETTS owed him 18/-, William EVES of Mousham hamlet, owed him 30/-, William VERNON of Maldon owed him 9/-, and Christopher DUKE of Albemarle, owed him £78.

The jurors further stated on oath that Francis GURNEY was possessed of a certain messuage in Maldon for a great term of years to the value of £20. And also he was in like manner possessed of 14 acres of meadow or pasture in Goldenhanger Marsh and of certain Salt Coates in Goldenhanger for a great term of years to the value of £13 yearly. In all appraised by the jurors at £91. And also he was possessed of certain goods and chattels in Maldon to the value of £300.

**THOMAS BROWNING, D.D.** Mention has been made above of a caveat entered on 13th August 1677 in the interests of Thomas BROWNING, S.T.P. The following notes about him will not be out of place here.

FOSTER'S "Alumni Oxonienses" has "Thomas BROWNING, B.A. from Pemb. College, Cambridge, 1635 (incorp. as M.A. 1st Feb. 1642/3) D.D. per literas Regias 1660, rector of Wickham Bishop, Essex, 1661".

Lay Subsidy 246/12 (Assessment of Hearth Tax, 15 Car. II., 1663/4) mentions the house of Dr. BROWNING at St. Mary's Maldon as being untenanted.

In the calendars of Feet of Fines under date Hilary Term, 32 & 33 Car. II., (1680), there is a record of a fine between Stephen CRISPE and Thomas BROWNEING, S.T.P., relating to land in Woodham Ferris, Essex.

The will of Henry JERMYN, of Wickham Bishop, Essex, dated 1680, (proved Comm. London—Essex & Herts, "Heydon" 481), names "Dr. Thomas BROWNING and Anne, his wife". It is possible that Francis GURNEY and Anne, his sister, may have married a sister and brother, Anne BROWNING and Dr. Thomas BROWNING.

The will of Thomas BROWNING, of Wickham Bishop, Essex, Doctor in Divinity, dated 16th June 1694, was proved in the Comm. London—Essex & Herts on 3rd May 1705. From this we see that he was the father of Ann, wife of the Revd. Christopher WRAGG, of Little Wigborrow, Essex. Unfortunately he makes no mention of the GURNEYS in his will.

**THE PROBLEM** We have seen that Francis GURNEY's eldest son, John, was born on 7th Octr. and baptized at Maldon on 30th Octr. 1655 and that he was living, aged 9 years, in 1664. He disappears from Maldon for we have shewn that it is improbable that he was the John GURNEY of Maldon who died a bachelor in 1681. The question is his identity with John GURNEY, of St. Gregory's parish, Norwich, cordwainer, who became a member of the Society of Friends and married on 4th Octr. 1687 at Woodbridge Elizabeth SWANTON of Grundisburgh, Suffolk. He died in 1721 aged 66. This John GURNEY, of Norwich, is the ancestor of the present family of GURNEY of Norfolk. Family tradition asserts that John, of St. Gregory's, Norwich, was the son of Francis, of Maldon, and certainly their ages tally. However, there is no actual proof of the identity and several of the members of the GURNEY family are anxious to see the descent either proved or disproved. One of them has offered a reward for the discovery of such evidence. The matter is of almost general interest owing to the large number of families which have inter-married with John's descendants.

# DEEDS (University of Toronto) — medieval Gournay charter extracts

Source ID: `deeds-utoronto-gournay-charter-extracts`

Verbatim charter texts as transcribed in the DEEDS database (Documents of Early England Data Set, University of Toronto Scarborough), captured July 2026 from the charter pages at `https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/<8-digit id>` (server-rendered). Coverage derives from a systematic variant sweep of the database's full-text search (`q=gour*`, 29 documents; `q=gurn*`, 46; `q=gorn*`, 237, of which roughly 30 are substantive after filtering the noise from the priory of Gournay-sur-Marne). Editorial matter in [square brackets] is DEEDS's own diplomatic markup.

**Dating caveat.** For the Norman acta, DEEDS's charter pages cite "SCRIPTA – Base des actes normands médiévaux" (Université de Caen) as their external source: DEEDS mirrors SCRIPTA for this material, and dates shown as "Assigned" are editorial assignments, not internal charter dates. Where a date is doubted, the SCRIPTA `sc_` record and the underlying printed edition are the controls; the two databases' assignments can differ (e.g., the Jumièges Havoth act below carries "assigned 1040" in DEEDS while SCRIPTA's own record sc_151 brackets it [1049–1087]).

**Coverage caveat.** DEEDS's holdings are cartulary-bounded and mostly pre-1300: absence of a person or family from the database reflects which cartularies DEEDS ingested, not the record universe. Variant stems `gerne*`/`gurnei*` and the multi-word form `de gornaco` were not swept as separate queries; the gorn*/gurn*/gour* stems cover the attested Latin forms.

## 06400021 — Jumièges, assigned 1040 (SCRIPTA sc_151: [1049–1087]) — Hugh de Gournay, his wife Basilia, and his son Gerard consent to Ralph Havoth's oblation

Cartulary: *Chartes de l'Abbaye de Jumièges (v. 825 à 1204)*, t. 1, ed. J. J. Vernier (1916). Original survives: Archives départementales de Seine-Maritime, 9 H 1747/1. Also in SCRIPTA as sc_151. URL: https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/06400021

> Si Deus pater ut scriptum est ea caritate nos dilexit ut unigenito suo non pepercisset set pro nobis illum tradidisset quid nostrum est econtra retribuere Deo nostro nisi quod dulcius quod melius quodque carius habemus Hoc pater noster Abraham fecit dum Ysaac unigenitum suum Deo ante legem immolaret Hoc sancti martyres sub gratia non solum propria pro Christo erogaverunt set etiam sanguinem illi refuderunt Quorum vestigia ego Radulfus cognomine Havoth cum uxore mea pro modulo meo secutus Jhesu Christo domino nostro filium meum obtuli sancteque sue ecclesie Gemmeticensi ut monachus sit sancti Petri qui illius cenobii princeps est et patronus Dedi etiam sancto Petro Gemmeticensi apud villam que vocatur Hugonis Silva centum jugera minus x et quicquid decimarum predicte ville ego possederam in dotem filii mei et in honorem sancti Petri Gemmeticensis **domino meo Hugone Gurnacensi et filio ejus Girardo et uxore ejus Basilia annuentibus** et volentibus et laudantibus quorum hec sunt signa **Signum Hugonis Gornaiensis Signum Basilie uxoris ejus** Signum Radulfi Havot Ego vero Athelinus Gornaiensis ecclesie tesaurarius confirmo et hoc signo

Working translation of the operative clauses: Ralph surnamed Havoth, with his wife, offers his son as a monk to Jumièges, and gives St Peter of Jumièges, at the villa called Hugh's Wood (Hugonis Silva, the later Boshyon / Bosc-Hyons), ninety acres and whatever tithes he possessed there, as his son's entry endowment — "with my lord Hugh of Gournay and his son Gerard and his wife Basilia consenting, willing and approving, whose signa these are: the sign of Hugh of Gournay; the sign of Basilia his wife; the sign of Ralph Havot. And I, Athelinus, treasurer of the church of Gournay, confirm, and with this sign." The lord's wife subscribes under her own name.

## 03781013 — Regesta Henrici Primi, internal date 1112 (confirmation 11 Feb 1112/13), Bec — Hugh son of Gerard confirms the gifts of his father Gerard and of his grandfather Hugh and grandmother Basilia

Cartulary: *Regesta Regum Anglo-Normannorum*, vol. 2 (Regesta Henrici Primi), ed. Davis/Johnson/Cronne (1956). URL: https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/03781013

> Anno ab incarnatione domini MCXII **Hugo filius Girardi de Gornaco** concessit ea que **pater eius Girardus et Hugo de Gornaco auus suus et auia sua Basilia** dederunt ecclesie sancte Marie Becci uidelicet quicquid habebant in Longolio et le reste chomme dans la confirmation precedente [] porro in Anglia dedit Hugo de Gornaco eidem ecclesie **quod habebat in Londonia de rege Willelmo patre Henrici regis et in Essessa ecclesiam de Fordaham** et autres choses [] Et **Girardus de Gornaco dedit eidem ecclesie quoddam maneriolum in Norfolch quod dicitur Lesingeham** et ces donations furent confirmees a l'abbaye du Bec par Henry roy d'Angleterre la meme annee MCXII iii idus Februarii

The entry is the Regesta's mixed French-Latin abstract of Henry I's confirmation, 11 February 1112/13. Working translation: in the year 1112 Hugh son of Gerard de Gournay granted what his father Gerard, and Hugh de Gournay his grandfather and Basilia his grandmother, gave to the church of St Mary of Bec — namely whatever they had in Longueil, and the rest as in the preceding confirmation. Further, in England, Hugh de Gournay [the grandfather] gave the same church what he had in London of King William, father of King Henry, and in Essex the church of Fordham, and other things. And Gerard de Gournay gave the same church a certain little manor (*maneriolum*) in Norfolk which is called Lessingham. These donations were confirmed to the abbey of Bec by Henry, king of England, the same year 1112, on the third day before the Ides of February.

## 02580556 — Missenden cartulary vol. 2 (Buckinghamshire Record Society 12), internal date 4 April 1167, done at Gournay — Hugh de Gournay, his wife Milesent, and his son Hugh grant and warrant Broughton to Missenden

URL: https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/02580556

> Dominis suis omnibus **Hugo de Gurnay** et amicis et hominibus francis et anglis salutem sciatis quod **ego et Mileseint sponsa mea et Hugo filius meus** concedimus canonicis de Messend et carta nostra confirmauimus et garantizamus totam teneuram suam de Browton quam de Roberto Mansello tenebant cum omnibus appenditiis suis in bosco et plano et cunctis aliis rebus ad feudum illud pertinentibus ad tenend de nobis et heredibus nostris **et nominatim de uxore mea Mileseint cui in dote uillam illam dedimus** ad tenend insuper libere et quiete et hereditarie in perpetuam elemosinam per idem seruitium quod inde Roberto Mansel faciebant Scilicet reddendo annuatim pro omnibus seruitiis vj marcas ad Pascha … et statuent unum canonicum in ecclesia sua omnibus diebus pro salute nostra et antecessorum et successorum nostrorum Et si ad eos de aliquo grauidi negotio nostro breue nostrum miserimus abbas aut aliquis de canonicis ibit pro nobis ad regem sine ad episcopum infra Angliam quin etiam ut omnis calumpnia et quercle heredum predicti Roberti tollantur sciant omnes amici nostri presentes et futuri nos pari assensu et concessione dedisse Stephano heredi predicti Roberti escambias in socha de Cheneborlay nominatim in Carlinton iij marcatas terre illo in manu mea supradictam Brottone liberam dimittente et **de escambiis mihi et Hugoni filio meo hominem suum faciente** Ut autem de hiis qui in presenti carta continentur nulla sit in posteris dubietas **sigillum meum et sigillum sponse mee Milesent** hic apponuntur Testibus **Nicholas Estoteuilla Iohanne de Hosdeng Willelmo de Sancto Lutiano Willelmo de Bossoncort Hugone de Heusart Willelmo de Marteneio Garnero Hosdent Hugone de Braimoster Reynardo de Mereuill Radulfo de Ogia Galtero thesaurio de Auesnes Rogero capellano** qui hanc cartam scripsit **Hec facta sunt apud Gornaium anno dominice incarnationis mc sexagesimo vij pridie Nonarum Aprilis**

Key clauses in translation: Hugh de Gournay, "I and Milesent my spouse and Hugh my son," concede, confirm, and warrant to the canons of Missenden their whole tenure of Broughton which they held of Robert Mansel — to hold of us and our heirs, "and namely of my wife Milesent, to whom we gave that vill in dower" — for six marks yearly at Easter, the canons to maintain one canon in their church praying daily for our salvation and that of our ancestors and successors; Stephen, Robert's heir, receives exchange lands in the soke of Cheneborlay, namely three marks' worth of land in Carlinton, "doing his homage for the exchange to me and to Hugh my son." So that there be no doubt among posterity, "my seal and the seal of my spouse Milesent are here affixed." Done at Gournay, in the year of Our Lord's incarnation 1167, on the day before the Nones of April [4 April].

## 02580561 — Missenden cartulary vol. 2 (BRS 12), assigned 1167 — Milesent's own confirmation

URL: https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/02580561

> **Milesent sponsa domini Hugonis de Gurnay** amicis suis omnibus et hominibus salutem sciatis quod meo assensu et concessione canonici de Messenden habent et tenent in perpetuam elemosinam teneuram de Brocton sicut in carta domini mei Hugonis describitur et testatur Et propter hoc de suo mihi dederunt Testibus Nicholao de Estoteuille Iohanne de Hosdeng Willelmo de Sancto Lutiano Radulfo de Agia Galtero thesaurio [testaurio de Auesnis Rogero] capellano [qui hanc cartam scripsit] []

Translation: Milesent, spouse of the lord Hugh de Gournay, to all her friends and men, greeting. Know that by my assent and concession the canons of Missenden have and hold in perpetual alms the tenure of Broughton as is described and witnessed in the charter of my lord Hugh. And on account of this they gave me [a payment] of their own. Witnesses substantially as in her husband's act.

## 01770391 — Feet of Fines Norfolk (Pipe Roll Society n.s. 27), St Benedict's day 4 John [21 March 1202/3], Norwich — Emma de Herpelay v. Matthew de Gurnay, tenant of 20 acres in Harpley

URL: https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/01770391

> Hec est finalis concordia facta in curia domini regis apud Norwic[um] die sancti Benedicti anno regni regis Johannis iiij coram G[odefrido] de Insul[a] Reginaldo de Cornhill Waltero de Crepping Reginaldo de Argentom justic[iariis] domini regis et aliis baronibus tunc ibi presentibus **inter Emmam de Herpelay petentem et Matheum de Gurnay tenentem de xx acris terre cum pertinentiis in Herpelay** unde recognitio de morte antecessoris sum monita fuit inter eos in prefata curia scilicet quod predicta Emma remisit et quietum clamauit predicto Matheo et heredibus suis totum jus et clamium quod habuit in predicta terra de se et heredibus suis inperpetuum Et pro hac quieta clamantia et fine et concordia predictus Matheus concessit predicte Emme et heredibus suis ij acras terre et dimidiam in eadem uilla **scilicet j acram ad Piggescroft et j acram et dimidiam ad Kinnluesmereheuedland** tenendas de se et heredibus suis inperpetuum per liberum seruitium vj denariorum per annum pro omni seruitio reddendorum ad festum sancti Michaelis et ad scutagium xx solidorum ij denarios et ad plus plus et ad minus minus

Translation of the operative clauses: final concord made in the king's court at Norwich on St Benedict's day in the fourth year of King John [21 March 1202/3], before the king's justices, between Emma de Herpelay, claimant, and Matthew de Gurnay, tenant, concerning 20 acres of land with appurtenances in Harpley, whereon an assize of mort d'ancestor had been summoned between them. Emma quitclaimed the land to Matthew and his heirs for ever; in return Matthew granted Emma and her heirs two and a half acres in the same vill — one acre at Piggescroft and one and a half acres at Kinnluesmereheuedland — to hold of him for the free service of 6d a year at Michaelmas, and 2d toward a scutage of 20 shillings, more when more, less when less.

## 00130234 — Missenden cartulary vol. 1 (BRS 2), assigned 1222×1238 — grant in Wendover "for the souls of lord Hugh de Gournay and Gerard and Hugh his sons"

URL: https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/00130234

> Notum sit omnibus tam presentibus quam futuris quod ego Iohannes Brisepot dedi et concessi et presenti carta mea confirmaui deo et capelle Sancte Crucis de heremitorio de Musewelle et canonicis de Messendene in predicta capella deo seruientibus in liberam et puram et perpetuam elemosinam **pro salute animarum domini Hugonis de Gornay et Gerardi et Hugonis filiorum suorum** et pro salute mea totum redditum et totum seruicium quod dominus Hugo de Gornay michi dedit pro homagio et seruicio meo scilicet quod **dominus Hugo de Gornay pater predicti Hugonis** emit de Henrico de la Le pro vj marcis argenti scilicet totum redditum et totum seruieium quod Galfridus de Mordena eidem Henrico facere solebat de toto tenemento quod predictus Galfridus de prefato Henrico tenebat **in uilla de Wendouera** … Testibus Henrico de Scaccario Roberto de Stokebi Roberto de Hiuetot Iohanne de Barsaua Matheo Brond Hugone de la Le Henrico de la Stoke magistro Hugone Canard Helia de la Dune Et aliis

Translation of the operative clauses: John Brisepot gives to the chapel of the Holy Cross of the hermitage of Muswell and the canons of Missenden, in free, pure, and perpetual alms, "for the salvation of the souls of lord Hugh de Gournay and of Gerard and Hugh his sons," and for his own salvation, the whole rent and service that lord Hugh de Gournay gave him for his homage and service — namely what "lord Hugh de Gournay, father of the aforesaid Hugh," bought from Henry de la Le for six marks of silver: the whole rent and service that Geoffrey de Mordena used to render Henry for the tenement Geoffrey held of him in the vill of Wendover.

## Round of five further full pulls (all mirrored from SCRIPTA)

### 03781569 — "Hugo senex de Gornaco" (Regesta Henrici Primi, 1123×1128)
- Cartulary: Regesta Henrici Primi 1100–1135 [Westminster]; external source SCRIPTA. Royal confirmation (King Henry I).
- **"Hugo senex de Gornaco"** granted a house in **Escuceio**; his successors also granted property. The "senex" (the elder / the old) styling appears here in a chancery-era text as a generational disambiguator for a past Hugh donor, distinguishing him from a younger Hugh; the grant itself is recited in the past tense ("dedit"), with the successors' grants following.
- Witnesses include Geoffrey archbishop of Rouen, John bishop of Lisieux, Bernard bishop of St Davids, Humphrey de Bohun, Robert earl of Leicester, Nigel d'Aubigny, Juhel de Medanta, Robert de Hagia; queens Adeliza and (deceased) Matilda.

### 05390078 — Hugh de Gournay witnesses William I's grant to Saint-Benoît-sur-Loire (April 1067)
- Cartulary: *Recueil des chartes de l'abbaye de Saint-Benoît-sur-Loire* (Prou/Vidier); external source SCRIPTA. Date April 1067 (regnal year VII of King Philip).
- William the Conqueror's donation of church properties and castle revenues to Fleury/Saint-Benoît. Attestation: **"Signum Hugonis de Gornaio"** among the witnesses (with Gerald de Novomercato, Stigand dapifer, Henry de Ferrers, etc.); royal family William + Matilda + sons Robert and Richard.
- An attestation of a Hugh de Gournay one year after the Conquest.

### 03771092 — Hugo de Gornay and "Warnerio consanguineo" (Regesta Willelmi Conquestoris, 1068×1076)
- Cartulary: Regesta Willelmi Conquestoris et Willelmi Rufi 1066–1100 [Westminster]; external source SCRIPTA. Royal decree; grant to a female monastery of the Virgin (Montivilliers?).
- Land-purchase clause "**concedente Hugone de la Ferteit et Hugone de Gornay**" (with Hugh de la Ferté and Hugh de Gournay consenting), with **"Warnerio consanguineo"** (a kinsman Warner) in the same transaction context — a Gournay–de la Ferté co-attestation with a named Gournay kinsman Warner.
- Places named: Monasterium Villare, Fécamp, Eschetot, Magneville, Caen, Longueville, Rouen.

### 08070002 — King John's Le Goulet settlement naming Hugh de Gurnay as security; the maritagium clause (May 1200)
- Cartulary: Annales de Burton (0807), **BL Cotton MS Vespasian E III f.10v (p. 34)**; external source SCRIPTA. Done at **Guleton [Le Goulet]**, May 1200.
- The Anglo-French settlement between Philip II and John: homage for Brittany (Arthur), and a **maritagium** (marriage portion) granting fiefs to Louis, Philip's son, on his marriage to the King of Castile's daughter, with reversion conditions; the fiefs are described as "in maritagium feodum Hugonis de Gurnai citra mare Anglie" — the fee of Hugh de Gournay on the English side of the sea. **"Hugo de Gurnay [Hugone de Gurney]"** stands among the English magnates providing security, with William Marshal earl of Pembroke and Baldwin count of Aumale.

### 05570059 — King John confirms Robert earl of Leicester's lands; Hugh de Gurnaco witness (23 October 1201)
- Cartulary: **Newly Discovered Charters of King John** [Westminster]; external source SCRIPTA. Done "apud Rupem Andeliaci" [Les Andelys / Château-Gaillard], 23 October 1201 (regnal year 3).
- Verbatim: "Iohannes Dei gratia rex Anglie … concedimus … quod Robertus comes Leircestr et heredes sui … teneant et habeant omnes terras suas et feoda sua cum omnibus libertatibus … Testibus **Willelmo Marescallo comite de Pembroc, Rannulfo comite Cestrie, Willelmo de Humet constabulario Normannie, Hugone de Gurnaco**. Dat per manum Simonis archidiaconi Wellensis apud Rupem Andeliaci xxiii die Octobris regni nostri anno tercio."
- Hugh de Gournay witnessing at King John's Norman caput alongside William Marshal, Ranulf of Chester, and the constable of Normandy.

# Norwich freemen and apprentices — Gurney-variant extracts

Gurney-variant entries from the two published Norwich corporate registers: Percy Millican, ed., *The Register of the Freemen of Norwich, 1548–1713* (Norwich: Jarrold & Sons, 1934), and Winifred M. Rising and Percy Millican, *An Index of Indentures of Norwich Apprentices Enrolled with the Norwich Assembly, Henry VII–George II*, Norfolk Record Society vol. 29 (1959). Extracts supplied by Allen Gurney from full-text search of digitised copies, June 2026; HathiTrust search-only page-hit probes run the same day. Source IDs: `millican-register-freemen-norwich-1934`; `rising-millican-norwich-apprentices-index-nrs29-1959`.

**Headline:** the Norwich freemen and enrolled-apprentice registers contain no John Gurney in the colonial-emigrant window (c.1615–1660). The only seventeenth-century Gurney is James, son of Francis of St Peter Parmentergate, bound at Michaelmas 1627 and admitted freeman in January 1648.

## Significance

1. **No John Gurney freeman or enrolled apprentice at Norwich, c.1615–1660.** Combined with the Merchant Taylors' negative (no John-son-of-Francis binding and no Gurney patrimony freedom 1583–1800, per `research/topics/merchant-taylors-1583-1800-gurney-analysis.md` §3), both formal trade-transmission pathways — London livery and Norwich freedom — are documented negatives for the colonial John's tailoring trade. Caveat: many trained men never enrolled indentures or took up the freedom, so this narrows rather than disproves the trade-inheritance reading.
2. **James Gurney, son of Francis of St Peter Parmentergate (bound 1627, freed 1648), is a new primary-level anchor for the two-Francis separation.** St Peter Parmentergate ("Permountergate," also "Mountergate") is the parish the case file assigns to Francis B, the Norwich laceweaver. The entry documents a Francis Gurney family resident in that parish in 1627 — while Francis G14 was simultaneously baptizing children at St Benet Fink, London — and gives Francis B (or at minimum a same-parish Norwich Francis) a son James. Cross-links: `research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v4.md` §9; `research/people/francis-gurney-of-maldon.md` (Norwich plebeian line).
3. The Elizabethan John Gurneys (the 1560/61 freeman; the cordwainer dead by 1580) document a sixteenth-century Norwich Gurney artisan presence — plausible forebears of the Norwich plebeian line, context only for the emigrant problem.

# Sir Christopher Hatton's Book of Seals (Loyd & Stenton, 1958) — Dammartin charters and editorial pedigree

Source: *Sir Christopher Hatton's Book of Seals*, ed. Lewis C. Loyd and Doris Mary Stenton (Northamptonshire Record Society 15 / Oxford, 1950 [printed 1958 reissue]). Full text: `sources/corpus/hatton-book-of-seals-1958-fulltext.txt` (archive.org `nrs015`). These are the pages Douglas Richardson cited (pp. 229–230 = No. 332; pp. 239–240 = No. 350; note to No. 409 at pp. 278–279) in his 2002 soc.genealogy.medieval argument on Walter de Gournay. Extracted 2026-07-14.

## No. 332 — Charter of Manasser de Dammartin to Hugh the chamberlain (before Michaelmas 1179), pp. 229–230

Charter of Manasser de Dammartin giving his grove and land of Baleshangre (near Mendlesham, Suffolk) and other lands to Hugh the chamberlain. Includes "Et pro hac concessione dedit mihi predictus H. de gersuma viginti solidos et unum runcinum de precio quinque solidorum. et Bartholomeo filio meo unum anulum aureum." Witness list includes "Willelmo de Dammart'."

**Editorial note (verbatim):**

> Manasser de Dammartin was the son and heir of Odo de Dammartin, who was enfeoffed of Mendlesham, Suff., by Henry I (Fees, 138; RB 409). Manasser died between Michaelmas 1178 and Michaelmas 1179 (PR 24 Hen. II, 36; 25 Hen. II, 9) and was succeeded by his son Bartholomew who was dead without issue in 1190 and succeeded by his brother William (Cur. Reg. Rolls, vi. 21; PR 2 Ric. I, 102). William died in 1195, for at Michaelmas the sheriffs accounted for the farm of Mendlesham for a quarter of the year and for the farm of Norton Mandeville, Ess., also held by William, for part of the year (PR 7 Ric. I, 51-2). He was succeeded by his daughter Galiena, for whom see No. 409.

## No. 350 — Charter of Manasser de Dammartin to his nephew Manasser (1168 or soon after), pp. 239–240

Charter giving to "Manassero nepoti meo primogenito filio Odonis de Compeng'" all the lands he held in Mendlesham, Wickham Skeith, and Cotton "on the day the duke of Saxony married King Henry's daughter" (1168). Witness list includes "Hamundo et Odone de Dam' fratribus et Steffano fratre suo . et Hugone clerico fratre suo" — Manasser's brothers Hamund, Odo, Stephen, and Hugh the clerk.

**Editorial note:** "William the Lion, duke of Saxony, married Maud daughter of Henry II in 1168; from its phraseology this charter cannot be very much later. For Manasser de Dammartin see the note to No. 332."

## No. 409 — Charter of Galiena de Dammartin in her widowhood (mid-13th century, after 1242), pp. 278–279

Galiena de Daummartin, "in mea uiduali et legali potestate," gives 76 acres and an acre of meadow "in uilla mea de Nortun'" (Norton Mandeville, Essex) to her son John de Mandeville. Adjacent holders named include "domini Walrani de Muncews" (Waleran de Monceaux) and Richard Foliot. Seal: "quarterly, 1, a lion passant guardant; 2, vair; 3, pily; 4, barry; + SIGILL' : GALIENE : DE DAMMART."

**Editorial note (verbatim):**

> Galiena was daughter and heir of William de Dammartin (see note to No. 332 above). A jury in 1275 gave a detailed account of her marriages (Rot. Hundred. ii. 193). On her father's death in 1195 Richard I gave her wardship to William Briwerre, who married her to his brother John (Rot. Chart. i. 60, 123) in or before 1197 (PR 9 Ric. I, 239). John was living in Mar. 1210 (Cur. Reg. Rolls, vi. 21), but died without issue before Michaelmas 1210 (Fees, 61 n., citing PR 12 John). At Michaelmas 1210 Robert of Burgate offered 700 marks for her and her land and in 1212 is returned as holding Mendlesham in his wife's right (Fees, 138). According to the jurors in 1275 Galiena was still under age when married to Robert; if so she can hardly have been 7 years old when married to John Briwerre. Robert of Burgate died at some time after Easter 1220 [...]. The 1275 jurors said that on his death she married Ernald de Mandeville, whose name suggests that he was a descendant of Ernulf or Ernald de Mandeville, the disinherited son of the first earl of Essex (Round, Geoffrey de Mandeville, 227 et seq.). [...] Galiena had two sons by her third husband: John, the donee of the present charter, and Hugh, to whom she gave the manor of Mendlesham (Rot. Hundred. loc. cit.). In 1228 Ernulf de Mandeville and Galiena his wife had a plea with Isabel countess of Oxford as to the custody of the land and heir of Robert of Burgate (Close Rolls, Hen. III, i. 92). He was Peter of Burgate, son of Galiena by Robert. In 1242-3 Peter held 2 fees in Burgate, Suff., of the earl of Oxford (Fees, 916), and in 1257-8 he consented to a gift of land in Great Missenden made by Galiena (Cartulary of Missenden Abbey, Bucks. Arch. Soc. 74).
>
> Norton Mandeville had been held by Galiena's father (PR 7 Ric. I, 52). The mention of Waleran de Monceaux assists in dating this charter. [...] the charter was made about the middle of the 13th century.

## Reading note — two Galienas

The Book of Seals apparatus knows only Galiena de Dammartin (daughter of William d. 1195, married Briwerre / Burgate / Mandeville). It never identifies Manasser de Dammartin's *wife*. Richardson's "Galiena de Gournay, wife of Manasser" is the earlier woman of the same name: the Missenden Abbey cartulary charter (Jenkins vol. 1, pp. 70–75; c. 1150) names Manasser's wife Galiena (with sons including Bartholomew and William), and the Registrum Roffense charter of "Galiena de Gurnay" confirms her grandfather William de Gurnay's gift of Addington tithes. The younger Galiena (de Dammartin) was evidently named for her grandmother, and it was through her third (Mandeville) marriage that Addington and Mendlesham passed to the Mandevilles — matching Hasted's Addington descent.

## Other Gournay content in the volume

Nos. at pp. 4622 and 23740 of the full text concern Gundred, daughter of Gerard de Gournay, wife of Nigel d'Aubigny (married June 1118, citing CP 2nd ed. ix. 368–9). No charter of Walter or William de Gurnay of the Norfolk line appears in the volume.

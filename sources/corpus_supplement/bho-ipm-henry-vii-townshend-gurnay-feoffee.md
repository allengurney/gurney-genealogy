# Calendar of IPM, Henry VII — William Gurnay, esq., as feoffee across the Norfolk legal-gentry circuit (c. 1488–1502)

**William Gurnay, esq.**, of the West Barsham family, recurs as a **feoffee-to-uses (trustee)** throughout the inquisitions post mortem of Henry VII's reign, most heavily in the estate of **Sir Roger Townshend, knight** (the Norfolk lawyer-judge of Raynham), who died 9 November, 9 Henry VII (1493). The trust was still active at Townshend's death, so William Gurnay was holding Townshend lands to the family's use in the early 1490s. The calendar is an English-language abstract of the Latin inquisitions; the family forename usually appears as "William Gurnay, esq." without a distinguishing descriptor — most economically **William Gurney IV of West Barsham (the family head, escheator for Norfolk, d. 1508)**. One entry, however (the Wynter IPM, §Wider circuit below), styles a feoffee **"William Gurnay the younger,"** proving that a distinct *younger* William — his son and heir William V (d. *vita patris*) — was also acting as a feoffee in the same years; the two Williams were therefore contemporaneously active in the Norfolk trust circuit c. 1488–1508, and the calendar's undescribed "William Gurnay" is not automatically resolvable to one or the other.

His co-feoffees are the top tier of late-fifteenth-century Norfolk society and church: Sir Roger Townshend himself, **William Pekenham, Archdeacon of Suffolk**, **Thomas Wodhous (Woodhouse)**, **Edward Knyvet**, **John and Thomas Blakeney**, **Thomas Gygges/Gyggys, gent.**, Edmund Harberd (clerk), William Wayte, and John Pegeon.

## Extracts (Calendar of Inquisitions Post Mortem, Series 2, vol. 1, Henry VII, entries 1101–1150)

> …and Thomas Appylton were seised of the under-mentioned messuage and land in Hadle [Hadleigh, Suffolk], and being so seised enfeoffed the said Roger Touneshend, knt., William Pekenham, Archdeacon of Suffolk, Thomas Wodhous, and **William Gurnay**, esqs., Thomas Gygges, gent., and William Wayte thereof in fee, to the use of the [said Roger]…

> …and being so seised enfeoffed the said Roger Touneshend, knt., and Dame Eleanor his wife, who survives, thereof, to hold to them and the heirs of their bodies, with remainder to John Blakeney and **William Gurnay**, esqs., and Thomas Gyggys in fee, to the use of the said Roger and Eleanor…

> …Manor called 'Havyles' in Est Reynham, West Reynham, and South Reynham, Helloughton, Toftes, Oxwyk, Wyssyngset [Whissonsett], Hornyngtoft [Horningtoft], and Gatele [Gateley], held of William Pekenham, clk., Thomas Wodhous, Edward Knyvet, and **William Gurnay**, esqs., Thomas Blakeney, Edmund Harberd, clk., William Wayte, and John Pegeon, as of…

## Wider Henry VII feoffee circuit (series 2, vol. 1 pp. 430–451; vol. 2 pp. 223–251)

Beyond the Townshend estate, "William Gurnay" recurs as a feoffee across the adjacent Norfolk/Suffolk inquisitions of the same calendar, sitting with the same bloc of Heydon, Calthorpe, Drury, Knyvet, l'Estrange, Paston and Hobart trustees.

**John Wynter, esq. (IPM; d. 5 March; heir Henry Wynter aged 10) — the "younger" distinction:**

> John Heydon, Roger Straunge, and William Gurnay the younger, esqs., and Thomas Langdon, were seised of the under-mentioned manor of Wynter Bernyngham with the advowson of St. Mary's in fee, to his use.

**Geddyng / Drury indenture, dated 15 December, 6 Henry VII (1490), for the manors of Lakford, Flempton, and Westowe (Suffolk):**

> He subsequently by indenture dated 15 Dec., 6 Hen. VII, upon an agreement between him and Robert Drury, esq., for the erection of houses in Lakford at the latter's expense, enfeoffed John Hevenyngham and Henry Heydon, knts., Roger Drury, Philip Calthorp, Thomas Hevenyngham, William Gurnay, Roger le Straunge, John Garneys, William Stanley, Richard Coote, Robert le Straunge, William Drury, Robert Lee, Thomas Underhyll, Henry Bladwell, Thomas Knyghton, Henry Tornor, and Edmund Lee, esqs., of the said manors…

**Tendale / Townshend feoffment:**

> William Tendale, knt., and Mary his wife, being seised of the under-mentioned manor in fee, enfeoffed him, William Tendryng and William Gurney, esqs., and Edmund Harberd, clk., thereof, to his use and to the use of his heirs and assigns.

**Sir John Fortescu, knight (IPM; d. 28 July; heir John aged 21+) — the Calthorpe dowager's life-trust:**

> …he entered into the same and thereof enfeoffed John Paston, John Wymondham and Robert Clere, knights, Philip Calthorp and Robert Darcy, now knights, then esquires, James Hubbard, the king's attorney, William Gurnay, esquire, Walter Aslak and Thomas Sotherton, who survive… for the term of the life of Dame Elizabeth, who survives, late the wife of Sir William Calthorp, knight, and afterwards wife of the said Sir John Fortescu…

The Fortescue trust is held for the life of **Dame Elizabeth, widow of Sir William Calthorp, knight** — the same Calthorpe household into which William Gurney IV had married (his wife Anne Calthorpe was Sir William's daughter). Across these entries William Gurnay sits repeatedly with **Henry Heydon, Philip Calthorpe, Robert/Roger Drury, Edward Knyvet, John Paston, James Hobart (the king's attorney), and the l'Estranges** — the dominant post-Paston legal-gentry bloc, giving roughly fifteen dated feoffee acts c. 1488–1502.

## What the source establishes

- William Gurnay, esq. (West Barsham family) was a trusted feoffee in Sir Roger Townshend's principal Norfolk estate (the Raynham group of manors and Hadleigh in Suffolk), the trust live at Townshend's death in 1493.
- It places the West Barsham Gurneys inside the Townshend–Heydon–Knyvet–Blakeney–Pekenham trust circle at documentary, primary-record level — the same affinity recorded on the marriage side (Sir Roger Townshend's wife Amy Heydon was sister to Anne Heydon, wife of William Gurney V).

The calendar gives the abstract, not the Latin original; "Gurnay" is the calendar editors' spelling. The entries name Gurnay as a feoffee/trustee, not as a tenant-in-chief, so there is no Gurney inquisition of his own here.

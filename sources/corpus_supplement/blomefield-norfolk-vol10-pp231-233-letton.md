# Blomefield, *History of Norfolk*, vol. 10, pp. 231–233 — Letton

Source: Francis Blomefield, *An Essay Towards a Topographical History of the County of Norfolk*, vol. 10 (London, 1809), "Letton," British History Online, https://www.british-history.ac.uk/topographical-hist-norfolk/vol10/pp231-233. Source ID: `blomefield-norfolk`.

## LETTON

> William Earl Warren had the lordship of this town at the survey... In 1161, Gilbert de Rysing was lord, and a great benefactor to the priory of Lewes... Michael de Poynings and his tenants, had lands held of the Earl of Warren's fee, in the 9th of Edward II. and Robert de Scales, the fourth part of a fee in the 6th Edward III... After this, it was in the Bramptons, and came to John Gurdon, Esq. [Page 232] eldest son of Robert Gurdon, Esq. of Assington in Suffolk, by the marriage of Amy, sole daughter and heir of William Brampton, Esq. of Letton, son of Sir Thomas Brampton, who was son of Sir Peter Brampton. This John was high sheriff of Suffolk, in 1585, and died in 1623, leaving Brampton Gurdon, Esq. his son and heir...

The manorial descent independently places the **Gurdon family of Assington, Suffolk** — the same family behind Brampton Gurdon's 1627 letter to John Winthrop cited in the John Gurney (G13) wardship-network research — as lords of Letton from the late sixteenth century, a coincidence of surname network rather than a documented Gurney/Gurdon kinship.

## Lewes Priory Manor

> Gilbert de Rising, in 1161, gave to this priory in Sussex, by deed... half the soccage of Letton... and the other mediety, together with the advowson, to the priory for ever. At the Dissolution, King Henry VIII. December 22, in his 29th year, granted it to Thomas Duke of Norfolk. **Hugh de Gurnay of Letton, granted them a tenement, &c. sans date.**
>
> ...This came to the Lords Bardolf, and in the 3d of Henry IV. Robert Read, and his parceners, held here, in Shipdam, Yaxham, &c. lands, sometime William Atte Rodes, by the 5th part of a fee, of the honour of Wirmegey...

The "Hugh de Gurnay of Letton" grant (undated) is the sole Gurney/Gurnay appearance in the Letton parish entry — see `sources/corpus_supplement/ancient-deeds-a2972-hugh-gurnay-letton.md` for the corresponding Ancient Deeds A.2972 grant text and the identity discussion (senior-line forename vs. an otherwise-unrecorded local Gurnay). A separate portion of Lewes Priory Manor's tenurial chain independently passed to the **Lords Bardolf** — the same family whose barony the Swathings/Hardingham tenure (CIPM vii.243) is held of, though the Letton and Hardingham/Swathings Bardolf holdings are not shown to be the same fee.

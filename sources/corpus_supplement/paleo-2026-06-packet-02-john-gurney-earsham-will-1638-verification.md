## Bottom line

The parish/place reads `Eursham` at the visible occurrences, supporting modern Earsham. The wife's forename is `Mary`, not Alice. I do not find a separate second parish-name in the lands clause; the relevant clause appears to read that the messuages, lands, and tenements are "situate lying & being in Eursham afores[ai]d," followed by purpose language for bringing up John and paying the sister's legacies. The earlier "[& ye parish of --]" target looks like a misread of the following words, not an unresolved place.

One substantive correction to the working transcription: John the son appears to take/enter the property at age fourteen, when "he may be capable to take his guardianship," not at age twenty-one. The will later uses age twenty-one for payment scheduling of other legacies, so both ages occur in the broader text.

The probate clause is not present in the two staged images; it remains on unstaged image 456 as the existing working transcription notes.

my Sonne all those my messuage lands & tenem[en]ts

that my wife ye sd Mary during ye yearely summe

Some words in this crop are pale and partly overwritten by the page exposure, but `Mary my wife`, `Eursham aforesaid`, and `fourteen yeares` are all more secure than the surrounding connective wording.

## Answers to packet questions

### (a) Verify "Eursham" = Earsham at its three occurrences

The staged images support `Eursham` at the visible occurrences:

- testator style: `John Gurney of Eursham in ye County of Norff`
- lands clause: `situate lying & being in Eursham aforesaid`
- Souter clause: `John Souter late of Eursham deceased`

Modern Earsham remains the natural identification.

### (b) Wife's forename

The wife's name reads `Mary`. The opening clause is `unto Mary my wife`, and the annuity/dower clause repeats `ye sd Mary`. This corrects the earlier working reading of Alice.

### (c) Unresolved second place-name in lands clause

I do not find a second parish-name in the staged images. The lands clause reads `situate lying & being in Eursham aforesaid for & towards...`, then continues into the purposes of the wife's holding during John's minority. The supposed `& ye p[ar]ish of [--]` appears to have been a misread of that purpose language.

### (d) Corrections to the working transcription

- Change wife from `Al[ice?]` to `Mary`.
- Change John's first possession/guardianship threshold from `one & twenty years` to `fourteen yeares` in the opening land clause.
- Keep age twenty-one for the later contingency/legacy-payment clauses; it is present there.
- Remove the unresolved second-place note unless another image supplies it; the staged images show only `Eursham aforesaid` in the relevant lands clause.
- The probate clause is still not staged and should remain a separate pull from image 456.

## Confidence notes

- `Mary`: high confidence. It appears twice and is legible.
- `Eursham`: high confidence at the testator style and Souter clause; moderate-high in the pale lands clause, supported by `aforesaid`.
- No second place-name: moderate confidence. I see no separate parish formula in the staged crops, but the page is pale enough that a future image-level enhancement or image 456 context could still refine wording.
- `fourteen yeares`: moderate-high confidence. The word is pale but the numeral-word shape is much closer to `fourteen` than `one and twenty`, and the phrase "when he may be capable to take his guardianship" fits a younger age threshold.

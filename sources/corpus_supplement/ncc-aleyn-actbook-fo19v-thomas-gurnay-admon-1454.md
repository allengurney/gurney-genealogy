# NCC act book (register-Aleyn period), fo. 19v — administration of Thomas Gurnay of Great Ellingham, 5 August 1454

Administration act (admon = grant of administration of a deceased person's goods, here because he died intestate) for **Thomas Gurnay, late of Great Ellingham (Elyngham Magna), Norfolk**, entered in the act-book section bound with the Norwich Consistory Court register-Aleyn-period probate volumes. NROCAT catalogues the entry as "NCC will register Aleyn 19": Gurnay, Thomas, of Great Ellingham, Norfolk, 1454 — a grant of administration, not a will. The "Aleyn 19" citation rides the act book's own foliation, not the register-text foliation: register-text fo. 19 (probates dated 23/26 May 1449) carries no Gurnay entry on either side, and the staged kalendar pages ("Testamenta," fols. ~92–163) carry no Gurnay row.

## Transcription (expert image read, 2026)

The entry is crossed through with ordinary cancellation strokes (normal act-book completion practice), which obscure the middle of the act:

> Die quinto mensis Augusti Anno domini supradicto commissa fuit administracio bonorum **Thome Gurnay nuper de Elyngham magna** ab intestato decedentis **Alicie relicte dicti defuncti** et Thome A[p]pulton/Appulton de D[…], ad solvendum debita […] in forma iuris […]

— "On the fifth day of the month of August in the year of the Lord aforesaid [1454], administration of the goods of Thomas Gurnay, late of Great Ellingham, dying intestate, was committed to Alice, relict [widow] of the said deceased, and Thomas Appulton of D[…], to pay the debts … in form of law …"

Reading notes:

- `Thome Gurnay nuper de Elyngham magna` is secure; `Alicie relicte dicti defuncti` is secure enough to use — **Alice is the widow-administratrix**.
- The act is dated **5 August 1454**: the entry itself uses `anno domini supradicto`, and the preceding folio run carries explicit 1454 dating continuing through these entries.
- The co-administrator appears to be **Thomas Appulton/Appelton**; the cancellation stroke passes through the surname, so it should be quoted bracketed. His place begins `D…` and is not secure (Deopham and Debenham are both candidate readings; neither is supported enough to choose).
- The marginal docket beside the entry does **not** read as an Elyngham form (it looks more like `Felemyng`/`Fololond`); the parish sits in the entry text itself, and the margin word should be treated as a clerk's docket, not a parish heading.
- The entry gives no status word (gentleman, husbandman, clerk) for the deceased, and no sureties or inventory detail beyond the standard administration/bond formula.
- No other Gurnay/Gournay/Gurney token was found in the swept range, act-book fols. 16v–20r (images 258–261). A `de Matyshale` (Mattishall) entry appears at the foot of fo. 18r; it names no Gurnay.

This Thomas Gurnay, dead by 5 August 1454 leaving a widow Alice, is distinct from Thomas Gurnay of Harpley/West Barsham, armiger (d. 1471, will in register Jekkys fo. 211v–212v — see `ncc-jekkys-211-thomas-gurnay-will-complex-1471.md`), and from the 1434 "Alice, formerly wife of William Gurnay" of Heigham (register Surflete 162) — two different mid-century Gurnay widows named Alice.

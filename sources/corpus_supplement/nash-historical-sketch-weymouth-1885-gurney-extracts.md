# John Gurney extracts from `historicalsketch00nash_0.pdf`

## Full bibliographic citation

Nash, Gilbert. *Historical Sketch of the Town of Weymouth, Massachusetts, from 1622 to 1884*. Published by the Town of Weymouth, under the auspices of the Weymouth Historical Society. Weymouth, Mass., 1885. Printed by Alfred Mudge & Son, 24 Franklin Street, Boston.

**Source file:** `historicalsketch00nash_0.pdf`

## Scope and notes

- This extract focuses on **John Gurney** and spelling variants found in the volume: **John Gurny**, **John Gurnie**, and **John Gurney**.
- Page numbers below are the **printed book page numbers**, since that is how the volume and its index cite the entries.
- The quotations below are reproduced **verbatim as they appear in the PDF text layer** except for the page 282 line, which was **transcribed from the page image** because that page's OCR/text layer was badly garbled.
- The references on pp. 258, 270, and 278 are **not separate biographies**. They are land descriptions under other owners, preserving that a parcel had been **"first granted"** to John Gurny/Gurnie.
- The reference on p. 282 is different: it is a **direct list entry** naming John Gurney among those entitled to lots in 1651-2.
- **Appendix C structure and dating.** Nash's Appendix C is headed *"Property Owners and Description of Property in 1643… First Division, 1636; Second Division, 1651; List of 1663,"* and the text notes *"in 1643 a partial record of the then property owners was made which has been preserved."* The pp. 258, 270, and 278 land descriptions belong to that **1643** record — so the parcels "first granted to John Gurny/Gurnie" were, by 1643, already held by the named owners (Robert Randoll, Nathaniel Addames, Thomas Richardes). The p. 282 great-lot list is the **Second Division of 2 February 1651-2**. John is therefore an original (pre-1643) grantee of the East Field and Mill Field parcels and a direct 1651-2 great-lot grantee.

## 1) Printed pp. 258-259 - **John Gurny**

### Verbatim extract

> The land of Robert Randoll  
> Two acres in the East field     first   granted to John Gurny  
> bounded on the East with the land of Edmond hart the hiewaie  
> on the west the land of Robert Cooke on the north and of  
> Nathaniel Addames on the south

### Summary / explanation

This entry occurs under **"The land of Robert Randoll."** The record is therefore not saying that Robert Randoll and John Gurny are the same person, nor that John is the current owner at the time this list was compiled. Rather, it preserves chain-of-title style information: a **two-acre East Field parcel later associated with Robert Randoll had originally been granted to John Gurny**.

The page break from 258 to 259 does **not** continue John Gurny's entry. His relevant reference is complete on p. 258; p. 259 moves on to other landholders.

## 3) Printed pp. 277-278 - **John Gurnie**

### Verbatim extract

> The land of Thomas Richardes  
> ffive acres of firesh meddow lying at the begininge of the  
> Hingam boundes first giuen to m'' Busley bounded on the north  
> with the Riuer and with Aingell Hollards lot on the south  
> Eighteene acres bounded on the East witli Ilingam boundcs  
> m'' Jeff'eries on the West Aingcll IloUards on the south  
> ffower acres in the mill ffield first giuen to John Gurnie  
> bounded on the East with Nicholas Norton land on the west  
> with Richard Wulinges on the north with John Gilles on the  
> south with William Brandones

### Summary / explanation

This entry occurs under **"The land of Thomas Richardes."** Again, the pattern matches the previous two references: the compiler is listing land under a later owner, but preserving the memory of **who first received the parcel**. Here, the relevant parcel is **four acres in the mill field**, recorded as **first given to John Gurnie**.

Taken together, pp. 258, 270, and 278 suggest that John was remembered in Weymouth's early land records not by a narrative sketch, but by **multiple original grants whose later ownership had changed hands**.

## 4) Printed pp. 281-283 - **John Gurney**

### Verbatim extract

**Context from p. 281:**

> LIST OF 1651.  
> The next list of property owners is found upon the  
> town records, under date of Nov. 26, 1651, of which the  
> following is a copy: -  
> The great lots named in the old town Book and formerly  
> granted to be laid out on the East side of Fresh Pond next to  
> Mrs Richard's mill joining the small lots formerly laid out but-  
> ting on Fresh Pond to run 18 rods towards Hingham line and  
> in case any lots run beyond the Pond they shall be upon the  
> same Line.  
> NAMES OF THOSE TO HAVE LOTS 2 FEBRUARY 1651-2.

**Direct entry from p. 282 (transcribed from image):**

> John Gurney . . . . . . . . . 16

### Summary / explanation

This is the strongest reference in the volume because **John Gurney is named directly**, rather than only as an earlier grantee embedded inside another man's property description. The surrounding context on p. 281 shows that this is part of a **1651-2 list of those entitled to lots**. On p. 282, **John Gurney is listed as number 16** in that sequence.

The reason pp. **281-283** are worth reading together is contextual:

- **p. 281** introduces the 1651 list and explains what kind of record it is.
- **p. 282** contains the actual **John Gurney** entry.
- **p. 283** continues into the 1663 material and does **not** add another John Gurney entry under that spelling.

So, in practical terms, the relevant John material in this page group is **the p. 281 heading/context plus the p. 282 line**.

**Pond identity (geo reconciliation).** The p. 281 heading reads "East side of **Fresh Pond** next to Mrs Richard's mill … towards **Hingham line**"; another transcription of the same Appendix C passage (and the *History of Weymouth* summary of John's grant) reads "**Great Pond**." These name the same body of water: **Whitman's Pond**, which was called **Fresh Pond** when the town bounds were set in 1636 and took the Whitman name after John Whitman Jr. settled on it (the Bates Mill was built on it in 1640). It lies in **East Weymouth** near the Hingham line — matching the grant's "Mrs Richard's mill" and "Hingham line" landmarks. This is **not** the larger **South Weymouth** pond now called "Weymouth Great Pond" (which is well west of the Hingham line and has no early mill cluster). So John Gurney's three early grants — East Field, Mill Field, and great lot no. 16 east of the pond — all cluster in **East/North Weymouth around Whitman's Pond and the Back River**, not at the modern Great Pond reservoir. (Richard² Gurney's separate 1683 grant "west side of Great Pond" post-dates Whitman's renaming and does denote the South Weymouth pond.) These loci are tracked as place records in `research/places/`.

### Summary / explanation

The index is valuable because it shows how the compiler understood these spellings. It explicitly groups **John** under the variant forms **"Gnruy"** and **"Gurnie"** and points to only **four** page references for him: **258, 270, 278, and 282**.

That strongly suggests that, within this volume, those four pages are the intended complete set of John Gurney references.

## Overall interpretation

Within *Historical Sketch of the Town of Weymouth*, John Gurney appears in **two different documentary modes**:

1. **As an earlier grantee remembered inside later land descriptions** (pp. 258, 270, 278).  
   These entries show parcels that were, by the time of the compiled list, associated with other owners, but which the record still notes had been **first granted** to John under variant spellings.

2. **As a directly named person in a 1651-2 lot list** (p. 282).  
   This is the clearest and most straightforward appearance of his name in the book.

The index confirms that the compiler treated **Gurny / Gurnie / Gurney** as the relevant John variants and that the intended references are limited to **pp. 258, 270, 278, and 282**.

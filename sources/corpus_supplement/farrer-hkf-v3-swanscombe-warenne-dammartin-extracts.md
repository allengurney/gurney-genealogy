# Farrer, Honors and Knights' Fees, vol. 3 (1925) — Swanscombe, Lewes/Dammartin, and South Wootton–Kimberley extracts

Source: William Farrer, *Honors and Knights' Fees*, vol. 3 (London, 1925). Full text already held at `sources/corpus_supplement/farrer-honors-knights-fees-v3-fulltext.txt`; existing Gurnay extracts at `sources/corpus_supplement/farrer-honors-knights-fees-v3-gurnay-extracts.txt`. This file adds the passages surfaced 2026-07-14 for the Walter-de-Gournay-as-son-of-Gerard question and adjacent subjects.

## 1. The honour of Talbot of Swanscombe — moieties of 1187 (p. ~7 region of the Munchensy account)

> In 1187 Agnes de Muntchenesy accounted for £15 (for a moiety of the honor of Talbot of Swanscombe) in Kent, of which she paid £10, and £5 was pardoned to Stephen de Glanvill; and Walter de Mayenne accounted for £15 for scutage of his knights (of the same honor). In 1190 the countess of Hereford, late the wife of Walter de Mayenne, accounted for £14 10s. to the scutage of Wales in respect of 14 fees of her moiety of the same honor.

And separately: "Particulars of the honor of Talbot of Swanscombe, part of which was held in the Survey of 1086 by Helto under the bishop of Bayeux, will be found among the extents of the lands of Aymer de Valence taken in 1324."

The honour of Talbot of Swanscombe — the honour in which Addington (the Gurnay-of-Kent seat) and Manasser de Dammartin's three Kent fees lay — was split by 1187 into Mayenne and Munchensy moieties, matching Hasted's statement that Addington was held "of the family of Montchensie, as capital lords... as of his manor of Swanscombe."

## 2. Odo de Donmartin a benefactor of Lewes priory by 1121 (p. 313, honour of Warenne introduction)

Within the confirmation of 1121 of the possessions of Lewes priory (the Warenne family's Cluniac foundation):

> In the bishopric of Winchester [the priory] has the church of Gatton and what Herfred gave there and in the same place ½ virgate given by Odo de Donmartin.

Odo de Dammartin (d. 1131, father of Manasser) was a donor to the Warennes' own family monastery at Gatton, Surrey, by 1121 — placing the English Dammartins inside the Warenne circle a generation before Manasser enfeoffed Walter de Gournay.

## 3. The Warenne tenant-table, p. 297

Farrer's introductory table of Warenne fees includes: no. 34 Wing, Crafton, Bucks — tenant "Talbot ... Warenne"; no. 39 Mapledurham, Oxon — tenant "Gurnay - Bardolf ... Wormegay." In the Wing account (p. ~415) Farrer records that Giles Talebot gave 100s. rent in Wing to **Beaubec abbey in Normandy** — the abbey founded by Hugh de Gournay IV in the pays de Bray. A Talbot tenant of the Warenne honour endowing the Gournays' family abbey is a further strand of the Gournay–Talbot–Warenne interlace.

## 4. The fee of Gurnai (after Bardolf) in South Wootton, Bedingham and Kimberley (pp. 141–143, honour of Arundel section LVIII)

> In the following account some particulars are included which relate to the fee of Gurnai (after Bardolf) in South Wootton, Bedingham and Kimberley, held by the Stutevills. [...]
>
> On 3 January, 1207, these lands were restored to Nicholas de Stutevill, grandson of Hugh de Gurnai, whereof Nicholas de Stutevill his father had been disseised by reason of the disseisin of the Normans. [...]
>
> In 1234-5 a fine of land in Kimberley was levied between Roger Buteaute and Nicholas de Stutevill, to which Hugh de Gurnay put in his claim. [...]
>
> In 1265 John de Bulemer was attached to answer Alice de Balesham for taking her goods in the vill of Wootton; he replied that he went to the manor of John de Gurney in South Wootton and because the said John was in the conflict of Evesham against the king he seised that manor into his own hands as against the king's enemy.

Notes: the 1265 entry is the crown-side counterpart of the plea Daniel Gurney printed at length in his Supplement Note 112 (John de Bolemer v. Alice de Balesham; John de Gurney of the Norfolk junior line in arms against the king at Evesham). Farrer's "grandson of Hugh de Gurnai" for the 1207 restoration reflects the writ's "nephew" (nepos) — see Clay, EYC ix, pp. 45–47, resolving the relationship as son of Eidiva, daughter of Hugh IV. The identity of the Hugh de Gurnay claiming at Kimberley in 1234–5 — after the senior male line's extinction in England — is an open question (possibly of the junior Norfolk line).

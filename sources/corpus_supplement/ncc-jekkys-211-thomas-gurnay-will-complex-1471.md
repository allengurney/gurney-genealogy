# NCC register Jekkys, fo. 211v–212v — will complex of Thomas Gurnay, armiger (testament, ultima voluntas, probate), 1470/71

Register copy of the will complex of Thomas Gurnay of Harpley and West Barsham, armiger (G20 Thomas Gournay II, d. 1471), in the Norwich Consistory Court will register Jekkys: **testament** beginning mid-fo. 211v under the marginal rubric `Testamentum Thome Gurnay Armigeri`, **ultima voluntas** (the "last will" disposing of lands, paired with the testament, which disposes of goods and soul) on fo. 212r under the rubric `Ultima voluntas eiusdem Thome Gurnay`, and **probate clause** at the top of fo. 212v. NROCAT catalogues the entry as "NCC will register Jekkys 211": Gurney (Gurnay), Thomas, senior, armig[er], of Harple[y] St Laurence, 1471.

The transcription below is a paleographically verified clause-level reading (expert image read, 2026), not a final critical Latin edition; uncertain readings are bracketed. A visual sweep of the neighbouring pages (fo. 211r preceding, fo. 213r following) found no other Gurnay/Gournay entry in this run of the register.

## Testament (fo. 211v–212r)

Date: `xviij die mensis Marcij Anno Domini M CCCC lxx` — 18 March, A.D. 1470 (old style; = 18 March 1471 new style), regnal-year clause of Edward IV (numeral not independently settled from the image; Blomefield's printed summary gives 9 Edward IV, i.e. dates the will 18 March 1469/70 — see the witness comparison below). The day reads better as 18 (`xviij`) than 28.

Opening (working read):

> In dei Nomine Amen xviij die mens[is] Marcij Anno d[omi]ni M CCCC lxx° et anno r[egni] R[egis] Edwardi quarti [?] … Ego **Thomas Gurnay armiger** compos mentis et bone memorie condo testamentum meum in hunc modum. In primis lego animam meam deo omnipotenti beate Marie virgini et omnibus sanctis corpusque meum ad sepeliendum **infra cancellum ecclesie [par]och[ialis] S[an]c[t]i Laurencij martiris de Harpele** si me ibidem obire contigerit vel **in ecclesia fratrum minorum Nor[wici]** …

— "I leave my soul to almighty God, the blessed Virgin Mary, and all saints, and my body to be buried within the chancel of the parish church of St Lawrence the Martyr of Harpley if I die there, or in the church of the Friars Minor at Norwich [if I die at Norwich]." The manuscript rubric styles the testator simply `Thomas Gurnay Armiger`; the "senior" styling appears in the register's catalogue description and in Blomefield, not in this visible rubric.

Legacies (verified at clause level; amounts marked `(?)` need a tighter read):

- High altar of Harpley, 13s 4d; **high altar of West Barsham, 6s 8d**.
- **Every householder ("vocati householder") within West Barsham, 12d each**; residents/householders in Harpley a smaller sum (6d? — amount not secure). A paired living-community legacy to both home parishes.
- Repairs of the Friars Minor, Norwich, 40s; the Augustinian, Preacher (Dominican), and Carmelite houses at Norwich, 20s each; Friars Minor of Walsingham, repair/sustenance, 40s(?).
- **His gold ring with a precious stone called `in turkeys` (turquoise) to the chapel of Blessed Mary of Walsingham**, with a condition that it never be given away after his death.
- A legacy to the altar/church of **St Gregory's, Norwich** (the parish of the family's Norwich town house).
- Church of **Depeden** (Depden, Suffolk — the Wauncy-inheritance manor), repairs, 10s.
- Godson: `Thome [Colvyle?] filiolo meo quem de sacro fonte levavi` — "to Thomas [Colvyle?], my godson, whom I raised from the sacred font," 6s 8d (surname not secure).
- Confessor: `Johannem [...] ordinis minorum Norwici confessori meo` — a named Norwich Franciscan as his confessor; the personal name after "John" is not secure in the image. Blomefield's summary names him John Bernard, Friar Minor of Norwich.
- Servants: Thomas Derby 20s; Robert Stonford 13s 4d; William Wolchyrche and his wife 13s 4d(?). (Spellings to be normalized at final edition.)

Family clauses (fo. 211v):

> … si [Willelmus filius meus] bene fideliter et humiliter … gesserit erga dictam **Margaretam matrem suam** et observaverit [instrumenta et cartas] dictorum Thome et Margarete …

Son **William** (`Willelmus filius meus`) takes under an explicit good-conduct condition — if he bears himself well, faithfully, and humbly toward Margaret his mother and observes the instruments/charters made by Thomas and Margaret. Fo. 212v adds security/payment language for William, with visible sums including `xl libras` (£40) and `xx marcas` (20 marks). The clause is unusually guarded — fair evidence of controlled succession or concern about William's conduct — but the manuscript does not state a quarrel.

Wife **Margaret**: jewels (`jocalia`), books, and goods clauses on fo. 212v, including the provision Daniel Gurney extracted — the woollen and linen cloths made by Margaret and her servants are left to her. Residue to the disposition of Margaret and John Jernegan/Jernyngham, armiger, executors. **Supervisor** (overseer): best read **John Heydon** — agreeing with Blomefield's printed summary and the expected Baconsthorpe/Heydon circle; exact line to be rechecked at final edition.

## Ultima voluntas (fo. 212r–212v)

Made at Norwich; the separate day/regnal-year clause is present but not securely resolved from this pass — no precise day should be quoted from the image yet.

- Confirms all grants and annuities made by his charters.
- Charters sealed to `Edmundo filio meo` — son **Edmund** — concerning the **Depeden** property stand confirmed.
- **Prior and convent of Blessed Mary of Walsingham**: construction money so that "me et uxorem meam in specialiori memoria habeant ut fratres et sorores" — that they hold Thomas and his wife in more special memory as brother and sister (a confraternity/beadroll provision for Thomas and Margaret).
- Works/vestments at Harpley church, 40s; books/vestments at West Barsham.
- **Lands/tenements called Swathings in Hardingham**, lately bought (vendor's name not secure in the image; Blomefield: bought of Catherine Sturmer), **to be sold by the executors** for the performance of the will and pious uses for his soul and Margaret's.
- William's payment schedule: annual instalments at a feast; sums including 20 marks to Margaret and a £40 figure.

## Probate (fo. 212v)

> Probat[a] fuerunt presens testamentum una cum ultima voluntate eiusdem … apud Nor[wicum] coram nobis Officiali Curie d[omi]ni [Episcopi] Norwicen[sis] … **xxvij die mensis Julij Anno domini M CCCC lxxj** Et commissa fuit administracio … **Margarete executrici** …

Proved at Norwich before the Official of the court of the Bishop of Norwich, **27 July 1471** (the final numeral reads best as `j`, settling an earlier uncertainty over 26 vs. 27), with administration committed to **Margaret as executrix**. The probate language appears to reserve or allow like commission to John Jernegan/Jernyngham as co-executor rather than recording a full separate grant to him.

## Parallel witnesses

**Blomefield's English summary** (West Barsham parish entry, *Topographical History of Norfolk* vol. vii, pp. 42–47) is the fullest printed account of the same will:

> "Thomas Gurnay, senior, Esq by his will, dated March 18th, in the 9th of Edward IV. appoints his body to be buried in the chancel of St. Laurence the Martyr, of Harple, if he dies there; and if at Norwich, in the Friar-minors church to whom he gives 40s. to the Austin-friars, Friars-preachers, and Carmes there, 20s. each; to the manors of Walsingham 40s.; to the chapel of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin at Walsingham his gold ring, with a precious jewel set in it, called a turkeys; to Margaret his wife all his utensils, and then to William his son, after her death; appoints his wife, John Jerningham, and Edmund Bokenham, Esq. his executors; and John Heydon, supervisor; his sons, John and Edmund, to whom he confirms all grants made to them out of his manor of Depeden; to the prior of Walsingham 10l. towards a new work there, on condition they remember him and his wife in their beadroll, as brother and sister of that priory; all his manor, or tenement, called Swathyns, in Hardingham, which he bought of Catherine Sturmer, and all his tenements in Norwich to be sold to William his son for 80 marks. This house was in St. Gregory's parish at Norwich, and John Bernard, a minor of Norwich, was his confessor. This will was proved July 27, 1471."

**Daniel Gurney's extracts** (*Record of the House of Gournay*, Part I, pp. 280–282) give the three-residence picture (West Barsham, Harpley, Norwich St Gregory's) and the Margaret textile clause ("all the woolen and linen cloths are left to Margaret his wife, being her own work and that of her servants").

**Witness comparison.** The register images, Blomefield, and Daniel Gurney converge on: the Harpley-chancel/Norwich-Greyfriars burial alternative; the Norwich friary legacies (40s/20s); the Walsingham turquoise-ring gift and the Walsingham confraternity provision for Thomas and Margaret; Margaret as surviving executrix; John Jerningham as co-executor; John Heydon as supervisor; sons William and Edmund; the Depeden grants; the Swathings-in-Hardingham purchase; the St Gregory's Norwich house; and probate 27 July 1471. The Swathings-disposal difference (sold by executors vs. sold to William for 80 marks) is a real divergence between the register text as read and Blomefield's summary; the two may reconcile in the unread portions of the ultima voluntas (a directed sale with William as named purchaser), but neither reading should be silently preferred until the final Latin edition.

## Scope

The published index lists Richard Gurney as a haberdasher and points to port-book entries 13, 28, 510, 557, 563, 564, 583, 593, 617, 728 bis, 741, and 754.

The selected extracted entries currently recorded here cover only the entries supplied for the May-July 1568 portion of the book. A full extraction of entries 13, 28, 510, 728 bis, 741, and 754 remains open.

## Research value

Richard Gurney appears as a recurring London haberdasher-importer in the 1567-1568 port book, with repeated Baltic/Danzig trade entries concentrated in flax and related goods, plus Spanish iron and eels in the selected entries. This places a Gurney/Gournay-name London tradesman in a textile-adjacent mercantile setting about thirteen years before Robert Gourney's 1581 Drapers' Company freedom at Old Change.

The record is useful as London civic and mercantile context, especially when read beside Robert Gourney of Old Change and the later Haberdashers' Gournay charity. Alfred P. Beaven's aldermen list later identifies Richard Gourney, Haberdasher, as alderman for Portsoken and Bridge wards, Sheriff, Auditor, and Master of the Haberdashers' Company, with death on 5 March 1597 and PCC will 35 Cobham. The port-book haberdasher and alderman are a strong working correlation, but the port-book record alone does not establish kinship between Richard Gurney, Robert Gourney, the later Sir Richard Gurney civic line, or John Gurney of Braintree.

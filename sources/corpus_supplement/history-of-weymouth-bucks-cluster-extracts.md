# Early-Weymouth Buckinghamshire (Aylesbury-Vale) settlers — extracts from the History of Weymouth

Genealogical entries from the *History of Weymouth, Massachusetts*, 4 vols. (Weymouth Historical Society, 1923), vol. 3, for the settlers whose Weymouth land bordered or contained John Gurney Sr.'s (G13) East Field and Mill Field parcels, together with the wider Wendover/Lee network those neighbours belonged to. Close transcription from the OCR text layer (spacing normalised); the volume's own cross-citations (Massachusetts State Archives, Suffolk Probate, Aspinwall's Notarial Records, English parish registers) are retained as pointers. Source ID: `history-of-weymouth`.

## The Wendover/Lee deposition (Massachusetts State Archives 129:16)

On **2 July 1688** "Jonas Humphrey and Robert Randall, both of Weymouth, deposed that they lived in Wendover for some time, and that they were acquainted with **Enoch Hunt of Titenden, in the parish of Lee, about two miles distant from Wendover**, blacksmith, and his eldest son Ephraim Hunt, and that the said Enoch and his son Ephraim afterwards removed into New England and for some time dwelt in Weymouth." Two of John Gurney's documented Weymouth land-neighbours (Randall) and proprietors (Humphrey's circle) thus testified under oath to a shared origin in **Wendover, Buckinghamshire**, and a shared acquaintance with the Hunt family of the adjacent parish of Lee.

## Robert Randall — Wendover, Buckinghamshire

"ROBERT RANDALL came from **Wendover, county of Bucks, England**, to Weymouth **before 1640**. He was born near 1608 and died at Weymouth, 3 May, 1691. He deposed 2 July, 1688, that he was 'aged Eighty yeares or thereabouts,' and that he was living for some time in Wendover in the county of Bucks … and did well know Enoch Hunt of Titenden in the parish of Lee about two miles distant from Wendover." He married (2) Mary French. "He had **three lots granted to him 'in the East field,'** and seven acres in the First Division, and twenty-one acres in the Second Division … (Weymouth Land Grants, **258**, 283.)" Daughter Mary married **Abraham Staples of Mendon**; daughter Hannah married John Warfield of Mendon. Land Grants **258** is the same page that records John Gurney's East Field two-acre parcel "first granted to John Gurny."

## William Brandon — Ashton Clinton, Buckinghamshire

"WILLIAM BRANDON came from **Ashton Clinton, county of Bucks, England**, to Weymouth. His land in Weymouth was bounded by the lands of John Taylor, John Staples, Simon Whitmarsh and **Thomas Richards**. Land was granted in his name 3 Feb. 1651." His widow Mary (16 Nov. 1647) was "late of Ashton Clinton, in county of Buckingham," and constituted Richard Baldwin of Milford, Conn., her attorney to receive an account from Thomas Branden of Putnam, county of Hertford, yeoman (Aspinwall's Notarial Records, 104). The volume cites Ashton Clinton parish-register marriages (Branden, 1621–1636). William Brandon bounds John Gurney's Mill Field four-acre parcel on the south (Land Grants 278).

## Enoch and Ephraim Hunt — parish of Lee, Buckinghamshire

"ENOCH HUNT came from the **parish of Lee in Buckinghamshire**, Eng., to Weymouth." A blacksmith of Titenden, ~2 miles from Wendover; "he probably came as early as 1639 and died in England some time before 1647." Son **Peter Hunt** "was one of the first purchasers of Rehoboth about 1643." Son **Ephraim Hunt** (b. near 1610, prob. parish of Lee) settled and died at Weymouth (1686/7); "he married (1), near 1645, **Anna Richards, daughter of Thomas and Wealthan (Loring) Richards of Weymouth**." "Ephraim Hunt of Weymouth, blacksmith, appoints Thomas Richards of Weymouth his attorney, to take possession of lands in **Beaconsfield, Buckinghamshire**, formerly in possession of John Hunt of Winchmore Hill, in the parish of Agmondsham (**Amersham**)." The Hunt family thus tied the Lee/Wendover Buckinghamshire group to John Gurney's Mill Field landholder Thomas Richards by marriage and by a Buckinghamshire land-agency.

## Jonas Humphrey — Wendover, Buckinghamshire

"JONAS HUMPHREY, Sr., with his family, came to **Dorchester from Wendover, Buckinghamshire**, Eng., arriving **9 Sept. 1637**." Born near 1587; constable of Wendover 1632; married (1) at Wendover, 11 June 1607, Frances Cooley; freeman 1640. Daughter Elizabeth married (1) William Frye of Weymouth. Son **Jonas Humphrey, Jr.** "removed from Dorchester to Weymouth near 1653," deacon of the First Church, resided on Neck Street in "Old Spain." His 1688 deposition repeats the Wendover/Lee/Hunt acquaintance (Massachusetts Archives 129:16).

## Thomas Richards — Mary and John (1630), Dorchester stream

"THOMAS RICHARDS came probably in the ship **Mary and John**, sailing from Plymouth, 20 Mar. 1630, and arriving at Nantasket, 30 May, 1630." Freeman 1640; born ~1590; died Weymouth 1650-51; married in England **Wealthean Loring** (probably sister of Thomas Loring of Hull). Children included Mary (m. Gov. Thomas Hinckley), Alice (m. Dep.-Gov. William Bradford), and **Anna (m. Col. Ephraim Hunt of Weymouth)**. John Gurney's Mill Field parcel lay within "the land of Thomas Richardes" (Land Grants 278). Richards is a West-Country/Dorchester-stream man who married into the Buckinghamshire Hunt family.

## Nicholas Norton — West Country / Hull-adjacent

"NICHOLAS NORTON appears at Weymouth as early as **1637**." Linked to Richard Standerwick of Broadway, Somerset, clothier, and to Mr. Joseph Hull's New England cattle (1639); "he is not named in the list of Rev. Joseph Hull's Company." His Mill Field lot "had first been given to Richard Adams and Richard Silvester." Among the grantees of 3 Feb. 1651; removed to Martha's Vineyard 1659; deposed 1676 aged 66. Norton bounds John Gurney's Mill Field parcel on the east (Land Grants 278).

## Nathaniel Adams — dish-turner; the 1642–1644 grant window

"NATHANIEL ADAMS, a dish-turner, **had land granted to him between 1642 and 1644**. Four of his five lots were in the 'East field.' He was born near 1601, and his wife's name was Sarah. He was made a freeman … 26 May, 1647. He removed to Boston before 1652." John Gurney's second East Field parcel is recorded under "the land of Nathaniel Addames" (Land Grants 270); Adams' documented 1642–1644 grant window is the basis for dating the East Field division in which John's parcel sits.

## Edmund Hart — Mary and John (1630), Dorchester stream

"EDMUND HART probably came in the **Mary and John**, arriving at Dorchester in 1630." Freeman 1635; granted land at Dorchester 1632; "had 18 acres granted to him in Weymouth in 1636, and 11 acres in the 'East field.'" (Weymouth Land Grants 261, 280, 283.) Hart bounds John Gurney's first East Field parcel on the east (Land Grants 258); a West-Country/Dorchester-stream man.

## Staples — early Weymouth; the Randall–Mendon tie

"GEOFFREY STAPLES, an early settler of Weymouth, died at Weymouth, 1646-47" (Jeffery Staple bounds John Gurney's East Field parcel under Nathaniel Adams, Land Grants 270). "JOHN STAPLES, Sr., was an early settler of Weymouth, living there from 1639." His son **Abraham Staples** "married at Weymouth, 19 Sept. 1660, Mary Randall, daughter of Robert and Mary (French) Randall … They removed to Mendon in 1663." The Randall–Staples line carried into Mendon, where John Gurney's son John also settled.

## Richard Adams — Hull's company (1635), West Country

"RICHARD ADAMS (a servant to William Read who came from Batcombe, county of Somerset) … sailed from **Weymouth in Dorsetshire in Rev. Joseph Hull's Company** on or about 20 Mar. 1634-35, and arrived at Dorchester 7 June, 1635." Freeman 1635; Deputy from Weymouth 1637; "had seven lots granted to him in Weymouth between 1635 and 1644"; removed to Malden before 1649. The first grantee (with Richard Silvester) of the Mill Field lot later held by Nicholas Norton.

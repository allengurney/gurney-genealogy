# Will of Benjamin Gurney (G11), 1738 — Plymouth County Probate, vol. 8, pp. 98–99

Source ID: `ma-wills-probate-records`

Verbatim working transcription of the will of Benjamin Gurney Sen.r of Middleborough (G11; Rigler's Benjamin-3), from the page image supplied via chat (Ancestry, "Massachusetts, U.S., Wills and Probate Records, 1635–1991"; original Plymouth County Probate Court records). The will is dated 4 May 1738 and was proved 2 July 1739. Original spelling and capitalisation kept; bracketed text marks a reading recovered from context or a word lost to the page edge.

---

## Page 98 (the will)

> In the Name of God Amen. The fourth Day of May One Thousand Seven Hundred ~~thirty~~ and eight, I Benj.a Gurney Sen.r of the Town of Middleborough in the County of Plim[outh] in New England, Husbandman, being Very Sick and Weak in Body but of Perfect Mind and Memory, Thanks be given to God therefor; Calling to Mind the Mortality of my Body, [do] make and ordain this my last Will and Testament.
>
> **Imprimis.** I give and bequeath unto my Well Beloved Wife Rebekah Gurney all that my one half of the Homestead that is in Partnership between my self and my Son Benj.a Gurney, with my Dwelling House and Barn, to be at her Disposing as she shall think best.
>
> **Item.** I give and bequeath unto my Son Benj.a Gurney that my Parcel of Land that I bought of [Joseph] Richards, to him his Heirs and Assigns forever.
>
> **Item.** I give and bequeath to my well beloved Daughter Rebeckah Taylor, the Wife of Daniel Taylor, Forty Pounds, to be paid her out of my Estate at her Mother's [decease], and to her and her Heirs forever. Likewise I give to my Son in Law Daniel Taylor abovesaid the Sum of Twenty Shillings, to be p[aid] —

## Page 99 (continuation, signature, and probate)

> — aid to him out of my Estate. And as for any moveable Estate, both Goods and Chattels, after my Just Debts and funeral Charges are paid, I give and bequeath unto my abovesaid Wife Rebekah Gurney, to be at her own Disposing. I likewise Constitute and Appoint my Wife Rebekah Gurney and [Son] Benj.a Gurney to Execute this my last Will & Testament. In Witness whereof I have hereunto Set my Hand and Seal the Day and Year above Written.
>
> Signed Sealed & Declared in Presence of us:
> James Whaley
> Gershom Sa[m]son
> Nehemiah Bennett
>
> [Signature:] Benj.a [Gurney] — *his mark* [signed by mark]
>
> **Probate.** Plim.o ss. July ye 2d 1739. Gershom Sa[m]son and Nehemiah Bennett, two of the Witnesses to this Will, made Oath that they Saw the [said] Benj.a Gurney Sign and Seal, and heard him Declare this Instrument to be his last Will and Testament, and that they at the Same time, in the Presence of the Testator [and] with James Whaley, Subscribed as Witnesses; and that according to the best of their Judgement the Testator was then of Sound [Mind]. Before me, John Cushing, Judge of Probate.

## Notes

- **No further children named.** The will names only son Benj.a Gurney (G10) and daughter Rebeckah Taylor (wife of Daniel Taylor). It reveals no additional surviving children beyond the two the genealogies already carry.
- **Death bracket.** Made 4 May 1738; proved 2 July 1739 — so Benjamin died between those dates.
- **Signed by mark**, consistent with a testator "Very Sick and Weak in Body."
- **Uncertain readings:** "Sen.r" (the hand could read "Son," but "Senior" fits — he is distinguished from his son Benjamin); "Sa[m]son" (witness Gershom; the Plymouth-area Samson/Sampson family). The struck "thirty" in the date is a scribal correction; the year is plainly 1738.
- Confirms the paraphrase already carried from Rigler (Plymouth Probate 8:98) and adds the witnesses (James Whaley, Gershom Sa[m]son, Nehemiah Bennett), the executors (wife Rebekah and son Benjamin), and the probate judge (John Cushing).

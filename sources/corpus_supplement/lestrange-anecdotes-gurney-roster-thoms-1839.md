# Gurneys in Sir Nicholas Lestrange's manuscript: prefatory notice and roster (Thoms, 1839)

William J. Thoms printed J. G. Nichols's "Notices of Sir Nicholas Lestrange, Bart. and His
Family Connexions" as the prefatory notice to *Anecdotes and Traditions, Illustrative of Early
English History and Literature, Derived from MS. Sources* (Camden Society, old series, vol. 5,
1839). The work prints material from Sir Nicholas L'Estrange's manuscript, British Library,
Harley MS 6395. Internet Archive: `anecdotesandtra00thomrich`. Source ID:
`thoms-anecdotes-traditions-1839`.

## Prefatory notice: Gurney and L'Estrange connections

The following is the relevant passage from p. xviii, including Nichols's notes. It is a
nineteenth-century editorial account of relationships and identifications, not a contemporary
sixteenth- or seventeenth-century pedigree proof.

> The Gurneys were Lady Lestrange's maternal cousins; and they were also more distantly related
> to Sir Nicholas himself. Martha, daughter of Sir Edward Lewkenor, of Denham, and aunt of Lady
> Lestrange, was married to Thomas Gurney, Esq. of West Barsham, Norfolk, who died in 1614;
> leaving issue Edward Gurney, Esq. of the same place, who is the "Ned Gurney" of our book. He
> married Frances Hood, and died in 1641. Thomas Gurney was a younger brother of Edward, and was
> living after the Restoration. "My couz. Dol." or Dorothy Gurney, was sister to "Ned," and died
> single. Her will was proved in 1641. Sir Nicholas Lestrange was himself related to the Gurneys,
> through his great-grandmother Elizabeth Gurney, the wife of Richard Stubbe, Esq. (married 25th
> Sept. 1561), and the aunt of Thomas Gurney, Esq. already mentioned.
>
> Francis Gurney (No. 120) was an uncle of Edward, and a merchant in London. In an account-book
> at Hunstanton, apparently written by Alice Lady Lestrange, is frequent mention of Francis
> Gurney the merchant. "Parson Edmund Gurney," whose facetiousness is exhibited in the Anecdotes
> xi. and cv., was brother to Francis. He was presented to the rectory of Edgefield in Norfolk by
> his uncle (by marriage) Richard Stubbe, Esq. in the year 1614, and held it until 1620; in which
> year he was preferred to the rectory of Harpley in the same county, on the presentation of Sir
> William Yelverton; and he seems to have held the latter benefice until 1648.
>
> [Nichols's note p.] Independently of this connexion through the family of Stubbe, the Gurneys
> were related to the Lestranges through the Calthorpes and Heydons: and an old pedigree at
> Hunstanton gives a marriage of Lestrange and Gurney, but I have not met with a confirmation of
> this. The only coat of arms in glass remaining at Hunstanton Hall, previous to the late
> alterations, was Lestrange impaling Gourney; but I think this was intended for Heydon, the arms
> of which family are nearly similar to those of Gurney. — Communication of Daniel Gurney, Esq.
> of North Runcton, Norfolk, F.S.A.

Nichols's note records Thomas Gurney's 1614 death and names the 1605 death of Sir Edward
Lewkenor of Denham as the source of the Lewkenor context; it also directs readers to the 1838
*Evidence on the Camoys Peerage* for associated funeral and will material.

## Roster from the numbered jests

The Gurney members named in Sir Nicholas Lestrange's anecdote manuscript are listed below.

- **Fra. Gurney** (No. 120) — "an uncle of Edward, and a merchant in London" = **Francis
  Gurney G14**, here documented as actively present in the Lestrange / West Barsham orbit
  while a Londoner.
- **Ed. / Ned Gurney** (No. 135) — **Edward Gurney, Esq., of West Barsham (d. 1641)**,
  Francis G14's nephew (grandson-heir of Henry G15).
- **Tho. Gurney** (Nos. 99, 271, 273) — Thomas Gurney, Edward's younger brother.
- **"my couz. Dol. Gurny"** (Nos. 169, 234, 343, 354, 359, 364) — Dorothy Gurney, sister to
  Ned, died single.
- **Ed. Gurney** (No. 30, the mathematician joke) — Edmund Gurney, the rector (Edmund the
  Divine, G14b).

**No "John Gurney" appears anywhere in the anecdotes.**

## Named-person coverage

No John Gurney is named in the numbered anecdotes. The notice identifies Francis as the uncle of
Edward of West Barsham and identifies Edmund as Francis's brother; it does not name Francis's
children or describe their residence, guardianship, schooling, or apprenticeship.

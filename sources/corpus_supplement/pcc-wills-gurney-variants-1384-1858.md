# Prerogative Court of Canterbury wills, Gurney and variant surnames, 1384–1858

A complete surname sweep of the Prerogative Court of Canterbury (PCC) will index for the
Gurney surname and its curated variants, giving every testator, the place and status the
index records, and the `PROB 11` reference needed to order or read the register copy.

Source ID: `ukda-4816-pcc-wills-index`

## What this source is, and what it is not

The index is the deposited electronic edition of the PCC will register index — the same data
behind The National Archives' Discovery entries for series **PROB 11** (registered copy
wills, 1384–1858). It gives, for each will: forename, surname, title, occupation or status,
parish, county, the date, and an identifier joining the entry to its register piece and
quire. It does **not** give the text of the will; the register copy itself must be read
separately.

Two consequences follow. First, the occupation and place fields are index derivations and
can disagree with the register text — three such disagreements are set out below. Second,
the index covers only PCC. The Norfolk Gurneys of the direct line generally proved in the
**Norwich Consistory Court**, so their absence from this list is a property of the court's
jurisdiction, not evidence that no will exists.

## Coverage of the sweep

The sweep matched the surname field only, against forty-seven curated Gurney variant forms
(the Modern, English, and Norman families together, at their broad setting). Matching on the
surname field rather than the whole row separated out twenty-nine further rows in which a
Gurney form appears only as a **place-name** — chiefly "Mapuldurham Gurney, Oxfordshire" —
which are not people and are excluded here.

The sweep returned **212 testators**. By century: one in the fourteen-hundreds, seven in the
fifteen-hundreds, forty-one in the sixteen-hundreds, fifty-five in the seventeen-hundreds,
and one hundred and eight in the eighteen-hundreds.

Surname forms as spelled in the index: Gurney (177), Gourney (15), Gorney (4), Garney (2),
Gournay (2), and single instances of Garney or Gaurney, Girney, Gurny, Gurnay, Garnee or
Gurney, Claverie otherwise De Gournay, Gurney or Gurnee, and five married-name compounds
(Gurney formerly Skyrin, Staines formerly Gurney, Taylor formerly Gurney, Backhouse
heretofore Gurney, Gurney heretofore Hanson).

**No testator in the whole 1384–1858 range matches a direct-line ancestor.** This is the
expected result of the jurisdictional split described above, and it is corroborated
independently: a National Archives search recorded in the repository found no PCC will for
Francis Gurney (G14), and Henry Gurnay (G15) was proved at Norwich in 1623.

## How the repository status column is meant

The final column records, **as of 22 July 2026**, how far each will has been carried in this
repository. A PCC register piece contains many wills, so a piece can be cited here for one
testator while another testator in the same piece is untouched; the column distinguishes
those cases. The four values are:

- **Transcribed in full in this repository** — the register copy has been read and its
  contents are recorded. See `sources/corpus_supplement/tna-pcc-west-barsham-gournay-wills.md`.
- **Examined previously as part of the John Gurney elimination set** — read from supplied
  scans and catalogued in `sources/corpus_supplement/tna-pcc-gurney-elimination-corpus.md`,
  under source `tna-pcc-gurney-elimination-corpus`.
- **Register piece cited for a different will; this will not examined** — the `PROB 11`
  piece appears in the repository, but for another testator in the same volume.
- **Not examined** — no reading or citation of this will exists in the repository.

Of the forty-nine wills before 1700: two are transcribed in full, fourteen were examined in
the elimination set, two sit in pieces cited for other wills, and **thirty-one have not been
examined at all**.

## After 1700

One hundred and sixty-three testators fall after 1700, of which four sit in register pieces
already cited in the repository. Their counties: Middlesex 38, Norfolk 22, Buckinghamshire
21, Surrey 12, Kent 11, Cornwall 8, City of London 7, Hertfordshire 7, Northamptonshire 6,
Bedfordshire 5, Herefordshire 4, with ten giving no county.

Two coherent groups stand out. The Norfolk block is the **Norwich and Earlham banking
family** — Bartlett Gurney, banker, of Norwich (1803); John Gurney, banker, of Earlham
(1809); Joseph Gurney, banker, of Norwich (1831); Joseph John Gurney of Earlham Hall (1847);
Richard Hanbury Gurney of Hethersett (1854); and Anna Gurney of Northrepps Cottage (1857).
The Cornish block is a clerical family: the Reverends Richard Gurney of Tregony (1825),
Samuel Gurney of St Erth (1834), Thomas Peter Gurney of Kenwyn (1848), and Warwick Oben
Gurney of Paul (1849). Also titled: Sir John Gurney (1845) and Dame Maria Gurney of St
George Bloomsbury (1849).

## Three places where this index and the repository's earlier readings disagree

The repository's elimination-set readings were taken from supplied scans of the register
copies; the entries below are the catalogue index. They conflict on the place, and in two
cases on the status, of three wills. Neither witness is automatically authoritative — index
place-strings are themselves derivations, and a scan can be misread — but the disagreements
are recorded here so that whichever is checked first can be reconciled against the register.

| Reference | Reading recorded in the elimination set | Reading in this index |
|---|---|---|
| PROB 11/335 | John Gurney, husbandman, of Albury, Hertfordshire | John Gurney, ironmonger, of Aylesbury, Buckinghamshire |
| PROB 11/241 and PROB 11/242 | John Gurney, shepherd, of East Chilton or East Chiltington, Sussex | John Gurney, shepherd, of East Claydon, Buckinghamshire |
| PROB 11/252 | John Gurney, yeoman, of East Grinstead | John Gurney, gentleman, of East Greenwich, Kent |

The PROB 11/54 entry is a related but different case: the elimination set cites that piece
for Tobias and Edward Gurney, 1577, while this index carries John Gurney, husbandman, of
Berton, Buckinghamshire, 1572, in the same register volume. Both can be true; a piece holds
many wills.

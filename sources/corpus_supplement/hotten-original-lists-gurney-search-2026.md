# Hotten, *Original Lists of Persons of Quality* (1874) — Gurney-surname search and the relevant emigrant extracts

Verbatim extracts from John Camden Hotten, ed., *The Original Lists of Persons of Quality; Emigrants; Religious Exiles … Who Went from Great Britain to the American Plantations, 1600–1700* (London: Chatto and Windus, 1874) — the published transcription of the surviving London port emigration / examination registers (TNA E 157 and allied State-Paper records). Full text: Internet Archive [`originallistsofp00hottuoft`](https://archive.org/details/originallistsofp00hottuoft). Source ID: `hotten-original-lists-1874`.

## Surname coverage
A whole-text search of the 1874 edition across the Gurney spelling set (Gurney, Gurnay, Gourney, Gournay, Gurny, Gurnie, Gurnee, Gurne) and common print-OCR mangle forms (rn→m, G→C, u→n) and the printed index returns no emigrant of that surname in the 1600–1700 lists, apart from the two entries transcribed below. The volume's coverage is partial — passenger and examination lists survive for only some ships and years.

## "America" to Virginia, examined 23 June 1635 (p. 95)
List header, as printed:

> THEIS under-written names are to be transported to Virginea, imbarqued in the *America*, W[illia]m Barker, M[aste]r, p[er] Cert: from the Minister of the Towne of Gravesend, of their conformity to the orders & discipline of the Church of England.

Among the men's names with ages, as printed:

> … JOHN SHAWE 16 · **RICHARD GumY 21** · BARTHOLMEW HOLTON 25 · JOHN WHITE 21 · THOMAS CHAPPELL 33 · HUGH FOX 24 …

Hotten's index renders this passenger **"Gummy, Richard, 95."** The surname as printed and indexed is *Gummy* (the "GumY" letterforms, terminal capital). The standard secondary transcription (Olive Tree Genealogy, 2003) likewise reads "Richard Gummy."

## Oath list before the Mayor of Dartmouth, 20 February 1634 (p. 86)
List header, as printed:

> LIST of the names and surnames of those p[er]sons w[hi]ch are bound for S[ain]t Christophrs … haue taken the oath of Allegeance before Mr WILLIAM GOURNEY Maior: of Dartmouth, they beinge brought befor me the Twentyeth day of February in y[e] Yeare of o[u]r lord god 1634 …

Here *William Gourney* is the Mayor of Dartmouth administering the oath of allegiance — a port official, not an emigrant.

## Source caveats
- A Victorian print transcription, itself once removed from the manuscript registers (TNA E 157/20); letterforms such as "GumY" are Hotten's reading. The original register and Peter Wilson Coldham's independent transcription are the arbiters of the surname.
- The lists record name, age, ship, destination, and (for many) a minister's certificate of conformity; they do not give parentage or English parish of origin.

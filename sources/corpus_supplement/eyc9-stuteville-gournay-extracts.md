# Early Yorkshire Charters, vol. 9 (The Stuteville Fee, Clay 1952) — Gournay extracts

Source: *Early Yorkshire Charters*, vol. 9: *The Stuteville Fee*, ed. Charles Travis Clay (Yorkshire Archaeological Society, Extra Series 7, 1952), pp. 45–47. Full text: `sources/corpus/eyc9-stuteville-clay-1952-fulltext.txt` (archive.org `YASES7`). Extracted 2026-07-14.

## Nicholas de Stuteville's Gournay marriage — Eidiva, not Gunnora (pp. 45–47)

Clay establishes that Nicholas de Stuteville the elder acquired Kimberley and Bedingham, Norfolk, through marriage to a Gournay wife:

> He had a son Nicholas the younger. On 4 Jan. 1206-7 the sheriff of Norfolk was informed that the king had restored to Nicholas de Stuteville, **nephew of Hugh de Gournay**, the land of Bedingham and Kimberley, of which Nicholas his father had been disseised by reason of the disseisin of the Normans [...]
>
> The combined effect of these references shows that the elder Nicholas married **Eidiva sister of Hugh de Gournay V and daughter of Hugh de Gournay IV**; and there can be no doubt that it was through this marriage that he acquired his interest in Kimberley and Bedingham.

Charter evidence: Nicholas the elder's charter to Valmont abbey mentioning "Aidieve his wife"; the 1219 darrein presentment plea over Kimberley church quoting charters of "Nicholas de Stuteville, father of Nicholas, and Eidm' [sic, ? recte Eidiu'] his wife."

Clay's footnote 4 corrects the older tradition:

> It has been supposed that Kimberley and Bedingham were given to Nicholas de Stuteville by Hugh de Gournay in marriage with Gunnora his daughter (Blomefield and Parkin, Norfolk, i, 749; v, 1103; and cf. D. Gurney, Rec. of the House of Gournay, i, 93). But there appears to be no evidence for Gunnora; and her name may be the result of a confusion with Gunnora d'Aubigny, who married a different Nicholas de Stuteville [...] It is likely that Eidiva was so named as a variant of the name of her paternal grandmother Edith daughter of William de Warenne, first earl of Surrey, by his wife Gundreda (E.Y.C., viii, 6); Hugh de Gournay IV's mother occurs as Edwa [recte Ediua] in a charter of king Henry II (Delisle et Berger, Actes de Henri II, i, no. 325).

Clay's footnote 5 adds: the Gournay tenancy-in-chief at Kimberley and Bedingham descended to the Bardolfs; and A. H. Cooke, *The Early History of Mapledurham* (Oxfordshire Record Society vol. 7), pp. 9, 11, shows Hugh de Gournay IV died c. 1180 and Hugh V was alive in 1207.

## Reading notes

1. Clay corrects Blomefield and Daniel Gurney: the Stuteville bride was **Eidiva de Gournay** (daughter of Hugh IV, sister of Hugh V), not "Gunnora." The name Eidiva echoes her grandmother Edith/Ediva de Warenne — the same rare name Richardson (SGM 2003) noted for Gerard's wife.
2. The royal writ of 1206-7 styling Nicholas the younger "nephew of Hugh de Gournay" is direct crown-record confirmation of the Gournay-Stuteville tie in the senior line's last generation.
3. Note the naming echo: Hugh de Gournay IV's mother appears as "Edwa/Ediua" in a Henry II charter — supporting the Aidieve/Ediva form of Gerard's wife's name over the conventional "Edith."

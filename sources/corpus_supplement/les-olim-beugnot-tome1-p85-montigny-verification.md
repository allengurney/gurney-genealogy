# Les Olim, ed. Beugnot, Tome I (1839), p. 85 — direct verification of the Eustace de Montigny entry

Direct read of the printed edition, verifying the text Daniel Gurney printed in *Record of the House of Gournay*, Part II (1848), p. 293. Read July 2026 from the Internet Archive scan of Tome I (identifier `bub_gb_DI5crXZamY4C`; a second Tome I scan, `bub_gb_Y9TfZH8I1WYC`, carries the same text). Beugnot's Tome I covers 1254–1273.

## Session and placement

The entry is no. **XXII** of the inquests headed:

> INQUESTE TERMINATE ET DELIBERATE PARISIUS, IN PARLAMENTO PENTHECOSTES, ANNO DOMINI MCCLIX.

("Inquests concluded and decided at Paris, in the Pentecost parlement, in the year of our Lord 1259.") It is the last entry of that session, printed at p. 85, immediately before the header for the Nativity-of-the-Blessed-Mary parlement of 1259.

## Verbatim text (OCR-corrected against context)

> XXII. Eustachius de Montigniaco, armiger, petebat a domino Rege quod sibi permitteret ut posset vendere libere et quitte, absque tercio et dangerio, nemora sua de feodo de Montigniaco, ubicunque sint, prout antecessores sui semper fecerunt, et tenuerunt de dono domini Guillelmi de Gornaio, qui sibi dedit ita libere et quitte, sicut ipsemet tenebat a domino Rege, de quo sibi fecit cartam quam rex Henricus confirmavit. Verum petit super hoc inquestam patrie utrum antecessores sui ita libere et quitte tenuerunt : Non probat idem Eustachius quod possit vendere nemora sua predicta sine tercio et dangerio; nec vendat.
>
> Hæc fuit terminata in scacario.

(The Internet Archive OCR reads "sihi" and "hbere" for *sibi* and *libere*; the print is regular.)

## What the direct read confirms and adds

1. **Daniel Gurney's transcription (Part II p. 293) is accurate.** The recital — Eustace's ancestors held the woods of the fief of Montigny "of the gift of the lord William de Gournay, who gave it to them as freely and quit as he himself held it of our lord the king, of which he made them a charter, which King Henry confirmed" — and the denial ("Eustace does not prove... let him not sell") both stand as Daniel Gurney printed them.
2. **The closing rubric "Hæc fuit terminata in scacario"** — "this one was concluded in the Exchequer" (the Exchequer of Normandy, the ordinary forum for Norman business) — attaches to this entry; Daniel Gurney's print does not draw attention to it. It marks the case as Norman business handled through the Norman exchequer session, consistent with Montigny's location in the pays de Bray.
3. **A different Montigny appears at p. 79** (Enquêtes no. VI): an inquest whether "villa de Montigniaco" was of the fief of Mello (Melloto), in the Creil orbit (Beauvaisis). It is unrelated to the Bray Montigny of entry XXII; the two should not be conflated when citing "Les Olim i, p. 85" vs "p. 79." Beugnot's index separates them: "Montigniaco (villa de), p. 79, VI" and "Montigniaco (feodum de), p. 85, XXII."

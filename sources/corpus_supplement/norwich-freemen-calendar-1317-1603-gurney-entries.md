# L'Estrange/Rye, Calendar of the Freemen of Norwich, 1317–1603 — Gurney entries

Complete Gurn*-surname harvest from John L'Estrange (comp.), Walter Rye (ed.), *Calendar of the Freemen of Norwich from 1317 to 1603 (Edward II to Elizabeth inclusive)* (London: Elliot Stock, 1888). Full text swept from the Cornell scan (archive.org `cu31924029785528`; the Google scan `freemennorwich1317` was checked and has dirtier column OCR). The calendar is alphabetical, name / trade / regnal year of admission.

| **Gurney, Geoffrey** | "le barbour" (barber) | 20 Ric. II (1396/97) |
| **Gurney, William** | "barker" (tanner) | 9 Hen. IV (1407/08) |
| Gurnell, John | "Cordwr., not app." (cordwainer, not apprenticed) | 5 Edw. VI (1551) |

Adjacent non-family: Richard Garneys (the Garneys gentry family, a distinct surname) also appears in the calendar.

The two admissions of 1396/97 and 1407/08 are the entire Gurney-surname freemen presence at Norwich across the calendar's 286-year span; the calendar records **no Tudor Gurney freeman at all**. John Gurnell (1551) belongs to the distinct Gurnell family of Norwich.

## Editor's caveat

Rye's introduction warns that the printed calendar is L'Estrange's transcript, unchecked against the original roll:

> "I have not had the opportunity of reading or checking the proofs with the original roll, so must distinctly disclaim any responsibility for any errors which may have been made in the transcript, and wish to warn my readers that it by no means follows that what is here printed is all that is contained in the roll, so anyone interested in any entry should look at the original."

The freedom-roll structure also changed in 1450 (mayoralty of Thomas Aleyn), when admissions began to be entered under trade headings and earlier names from 1 Henry IV were posted up "where they could be ascertained" — a further source of possible loss for the earliest period.

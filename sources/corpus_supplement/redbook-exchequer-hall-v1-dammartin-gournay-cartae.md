# Red Book of the Exchequer (Hall ed., 1896), vol. 1 — Dammartin / Gournay / Mayenne cartae of 1166

Source: *The Red Book of the Exchequer* (Liber Rubeus de Scaccario), ed. Hubert Hall, Rolls Series 99, vol. 1 (London, 1896). Full text: `sources/corpus/redbook-exchequer-hall-v1-fulltext.txt` (archive.org `redbookofexchequ9911grea`). Hall's footnotes record variants from the Liber Niger ("L. N."), the manuscript Daniel Gurney used via Hearne's 1774 edition. Extracted 2026-07-14 for the Walter-de-Gournay-as-son-of-Gerard question.

## 1. Carta of Manasser de Danmartin (Suffolk, 1166) — Walter de Gournay's enfeoffment (pp. 409–410)

> Carta Manasseri de Danmartin.
>
> H[enrico] Regi Anglorum, carissimo domino suo, Manasserus de Danmartin salutem et fidele servitium.
>
> Notum vobis facio, quod vobis facio servitium j militis de dominio meo, et tantum fecit pater meus in tota vita sua, et ego, post illius obitum, tantum feci die, qua Rex H[enricus] fuit vivus et mortuus. Et in tempore Gwerrae de illo feodo dedi Waltero de Cornaco [Hall's n. 10: *Gornaco*, L. N.] j quartum militis. Et nunc illam partem tenet Willelmus, filius suus, in auxilium mihi illud servitium faciendo. Et de novo feffamento nichil habeo.

Manasser holds one fee in chief in Suffolk (Mendlesham); his father held the same through his whole life and on the day Henry I died (1 December 1135). "In the time of the war" (the Anarchy, 1135–1154) Manasser gave Walter de Gornaco a quarter fee out of that fee; by 1166 Walter's son William holds that part, "aiding me in performing that service." No new feoffment.

## 2. Carta of Walter de Meduana (Kent, 1166) — the Talbot-of-Swanscombe honour, with a William de Gornaco (pp. 195–196)

> Carta Walteri de Meduana.
>
> Henrico Dei gratia Regi Anglorum, carissimo domino suo, Walterus de Meduana salutem et fidele servitium.
>
> Notum sit vobis quod anno et die in quo Rex Henricus avus vester vivus et mortuus fuit, tenuit Galfridus Tallebot in capite de illo xx milites, quos gratia vestri de vobis modo teneo, scilicet: —
>
> Manasser de Danmartin, iij milites.
> Willelmus de Cloville, iij milites.
> Robertus de Sancto Claro, ij milites. [etc.]
> Robertus de Swanescampe, dimidium militem. [etc.]
>
> De novo feffamento in tempore Regis Stephani: —
>
> Willelmus de Gernaco [Hall's n. 3: *Gornaco*, L. N.], tenet de dominio meo dimidium militem unde nullum servitium habeo. [...]

Geoffrey Talbot held the twenty-knight honour in chief on the day Henry I died; Walter de Mayenne holds it in 1166 by the king's grace. Manasser de Danmartin holds three fees of the old feoffment (i.e., pre-1135). Of new feoffment in Stephen's time, William de Gornaco holds half a fee of the lord's demesne "from which I have no service" — a gratuitous demesne grant, made during the Anarchy, within the same honour where Manasser held. Hall's scutage tables elsewhere record the honour as "Honor Walteri de Meduana ... per Ciciliam" — held through Cecilia (the Talbot heiress).

## 3. Carta of Odo de Danmartino (Suffolk, 1166) — the second Dammartin branch (p. 403)

> Carta Odonis de Danmartino.
>
> H[enrico] Regi Anglorum, ut domino suo ligio, Odo de Danmartin salutem.
>
> Sciatis, quod pater meus tenuit de avo vestro, et de vobis servitium j militis, et tantum de vobis teneo, nec de novo feodo aliquid teneo. Valete.

Odo (son of Odo de Dammartin, d. 1131) holds one fee in chief in Suffolk, as his father held of Henry I.

## 4. Carta of Albric (Aubrey) de Danmartin (Suffolk, 1166) — brother William enfeoffed (pp. 411–412)

> Carta Albrici de Danmartin.
>
> Dilecto domino suo Henrico Regi Anglorum, etc., Albricus de Danmartin, ligius homo ejus, salutem.
>
> Sciatis quod ego, tempore Henrici Regis, tenui de eo feodum j militis, quod de vobis modo teneo, et tempore Henrici Regis feffavi Willelmum de Danmartin, fratrem meum, de me[o] dominio unde facit mihi servitium j militis. Et de novo feffamento nichil habeo.

Aubrey de Danmartin held one fee in chief in Suffolk from Henry I's time and had enfeoffed his brother William de Danmartin of his demesne.

## 5. Earl Warenne's carta (1166) — William de Danmartin a leading Warenne tenant in Surrey (p. 402 region)

Within the earl Warenne's return, under "In Surreia":

> Willelmus de Danmartin, xj milites et dimidium.
> Robertus de Watteville, in Surreia, ix milites. [...]

A William de Danmartin held eleven and a half knights' fees of the honour of Warenne in Surrey in 1166 — the largest single Surrey tenancy listed, ahead of the Watevilles.

## 6. 1210–12 returns — the Mendlesham fee in Warenne hands (p. ~482)

> Willelmus de Warenna, j militem in Mendlesham quod fuit Willelmi de Danmartin.

By 1210–12 a knight's fee in Mendlesham "which was William de Danmartin's" was returned under William de Warenne.

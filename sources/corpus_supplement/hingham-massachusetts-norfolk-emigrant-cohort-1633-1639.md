# Hingham, Massachusetts — the Norfolk emigrant cohort, 1633–1639

Extracts from *History of the Town of Hingham, Massachusetts* (Hingham: published by the town, 1893): vol. 1 (Daniel Cushing's settler list, drawn from the town clerk's manuscript) and vol. 2 (family genealogies giving English parishes of origin). Internet Archive items `historyoftownofh01hing` and `historyoftownofh02hing`. Source ID: `history-of-town-of-hingham-1893`. No Gurney settled at Hingham, Massachusetts; the volumes are context for the dense Norfolk-emigrant community that settled immediately adjacent to Weymouth, on the town line that John Gurney's East Field and Mill Field parcels abutted.

## The settlers came from Hingham and adjacent Norfolk parishes (vol. 1, pp. 201–203)

Quoting the manuscript list preserved by the third town clerk, Daniel Cushing:

> a 'list of the names of such persons as came out of the town of Hingham, and towns adjacent, in the County of Norfolk, in the Kingdom of England, into New England, and settled in Hingham.'

Settlers were present by 1633, "and among them Ralph Smith, Nicholas Jacob with his family, Thomas Lincoln, weaver, Edmund Hobart and his wife, from Hingham, and Thomas Hobart with his family, from Windham [Wymondham], in Norfolk, England."

The 1638 increase, who "took passage in the ship 'Diligent,' of Ipswich, John Martin, master," included:

> Mr. Robert Peck, Joseph Peck, Edward Gilman, John Foulsham, Henry Chamberlain, Stephen Gates, George Knights, Thomas Cooper, Matthew Cushing, John Beal, Jr., Francis James, Philip James, James Buck, Stephen Payne, William Pitts, Edward Michell, John Sutton, Stephen Lincoln, Samuel Parker, Thomas Lincoln, Jeremiah Moore, Mr. Henry Smith, Bozoan Allen, Matthew Hawke, William Ripley.

The totals (vol. 1, p. 203):

> The number of persons who came over in the ship 'Diligent,' of Ipswich, in the year 1638, and settled in Hingham, was one hundred and thirty-three. All that came before were forty-two, making in all one hundred and seventy-five. **The whole number that came out of Norfolk (chiefly from Hingham, and its vicinity) from 1633 to 1639, and settled in this Hingham, was two hundred and six.**

## English parishes of origin (vol. 2 genealogies)

The vol. 2 genealogies record specific Norfolk parishes of origin for the founding families. For example, of the Lincoln/Gates household (Diligent, arrived 10 Aug. 1638): "They were from Windham (Wymondham), County of Norfolk, Eng. Land was granted to him upon his arrival." The Gilman family was "from Hing[ham], Eng."; Edward Gilman's daughter Mary married John Foulsham in England, 4 Oct. 1636, and the Gilmans, Foulshams, and Leavitts afterward removed to Exeter, New Hampshire. The cohort's parishes cluster in south-central Norfolk — Hingham, Wymondham, Attleborough, Great Ellingham, Deopham and their neighbours.

## Note on dispersal (vol. 1, p. 203)

Many Hingham settlers later moved on: "a considerable number had previously obtained lands at Rehoboth," and others scattered "during the militia difficulties which occurred within a few years after the settlement of the town."

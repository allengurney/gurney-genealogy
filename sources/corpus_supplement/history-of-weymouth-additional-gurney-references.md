Gurney occurrences across all four volumes of the *History of Weymouth, Massachusetts* (1923) that fall outside the Vol. 3 "Genealogy of Weymouth families" Gurney section (extracted separately at `history-of-weymouth-vol3-gurney.md`). ## Vol. 2 — "Extinct Weymouth Families: GURNEY" (a narrative family sketch)

A historical sketch of the Weymouth Gurney family, distinct from the Vol. 3 genealogical entries:

> **GURNEY.** The Gurneys were also an ancient family, Richard, of Weymouth, having children born to him, according to the record, from 1656 to 1667. The family also continues its name for four generations, the last survivor being **Jacob, who died in 1793, when the name became extinct in Weymouth**. It is yet green and flourishing in towns to the south, **Abington and Bridgewater, branches from the main trunk that had its root in this town**. Hobart's "History of Abington" says that **John was the original settler, having sons Richard, David and Nathan**, the latter of whom went to that section. Richard settled in Weymouth, but where, it would perhaps be difficult now to determine, **possibly on East Street towards Old Spain, and extending to Back River**. Quite early, however, the family removed southward, for about the year 1700 **Zechariah, the son of Richard**, owned the property afterwards in possession of his son **Jacob**, and located on Front Street… The son Jacob married Elizabeth, widow of James Richards, and great-grandmother of the first president of this society. They had no children… The estate was a large one, embracing some two hundred or more acres.

This is the town history's own statement that the Weymouth Gurney trunk threw the **Abington/Bridgewater branch** to which the direct line (Richard G12 → Benjamin G11 → the Abington/Bridgewater/Middleboro Gurneys) belongs, that the Weymouth name went extinct with Jacob (d. 1793), and that "John was the original settler" of the Abington branch with sons Richard, David, and Nathan — the later John (son of Richard G12) who removed to Abington about 1690, not the emigrant John Gurney Sr. (G13). (Printed in the "Extinct Weymouth Families" chapter, vol. 2, ~pp. 467-468.)

## Vol. 4 — John Pratt's 1714 will names "my Kinsman John Gurney"

In the will of **John Pratt of Weymouth, husbandman** ("being aged and weake in body"), made 12 July 1714 and proved at Boston 19 November 1716:

> To his Kinsman **John Gurney £25 at 21** "if he live with me so long as I live."

The bequest establishes a documented **Gurney–Pratt kinship** in early-eighteenth-century Weymouth: a young John Gurney (a minor in 1714, so born after c. 1693) styled "kinsman" by John Pratt, who died at Weymouth 10 July 1716 aged 82. The exact kinship link is unworked. (Pratt–Gurney ties recur in the family: Mary Gurney, daughter of Zachariah, married John Pratt of Weymouth in 1733/34.)

## Vol. 1 — King Philip's War soldiers list (Peter and Zechariah Gurney)

A list of "the names of the men of Weymouth who served in this war" includes, among Hezekiah King, Isaac King, Jonas Humphrey, Joseph Richards, John Whitmarsh:

> **Zechariah Gurney, Peter Gurney**, John Reed, James Reed, John Ford, John Lovell Sr., …

**Peter Gurney** is the emigrant John Gurney Sr.'s (G13) son Peter, "a soldier, killed in the war in 1676"; **Zechariah Gurney** is Richard G12's son. The list is a primary witness to their King Philip's War service.

## Vol. 1 — the early Weymouth land grants (John Gurny / Gurnie)

Vol. 1 carries the same early land-grant transcription as Nash's Appendix C:

> Two acres in the East field **first granted to John Gurny**, bounded on the East …  
> Two acres in the East ffield **first giuen to John Gurnie**, bounded on the East …  
> ffower acres in the mill ffield **first giuen to John Gurnie**, bounded on the East …  
> **John Gurney … 16** [the 1651-2 great-lot list]

So the 1643 property record and 1651-2 lot list survive in the *History of Weymouth* (vol. 1) as well as in Nash (1885); the two are independent printings of the Weymouth town land records. (See `nash-historical-sketch-weymouth-1885-gurney-extracts.md` for the dating and bounds.)

## Vol. 3 — the Dorchester "Gurnell" family flagged as distinct

A Vol. 3 entry records:

> 1677, **Jane Gurney, widow of John Gurney (Gurnells) of Dorchester**.

The parenthetical "(Gurnells)" is the town history's own signal that the Dorchester John Gurney bore the surname **Gurnell** — corroborating that the Dorchester Gurnell family is distinct from the Weymouth/Braintree Gurneys (relevant to the separate "John Gurnell, freeman 1643" question).

## Later and collateral Gurneys (context only)

The volumes carry numerous eighteenth–nineteenth-century Gurneys descending from Richard G12 (Charlotte, Francis M., Nathan, Perkins, Jacob, Martha, James S., Joseph, Edmund, Chandler, Betsey, Sally, Diantha of Abington, etc.) and the related Abington line, plus the **separately surnamed Garey family of East Weymouth** (Andrew J. Garey, John Hutchins Garey and descendants) — a distinct family, though the town history notes Richard Gurney's surname was itself "found spelled Garey, Garry, Gerry, and Gurny." These are not treated individually here.

# Bois-Gautier, Montigny (Montagny), and Massy — Seine-Maritime toponymy extracts (Dictionnaire topographique via DicoTopo)

Extracts from the *Dictionnaire topographique de la France* database (DicoTopo, CTHS, dicotopo.cths.fr), which digests the departmental topographic dictionaries — for Seine-Maritime, Charles de Robillard de Beaurepaire and Dom Jean Laporte, *Dictionnaire topographique du département de la Seine-Maritime* (2 vols, Paris, 1982–84). Retrieved July 2026. These entries test De la Mairie's conjecture (carried in Daniel Gurney, *Supplement* (1858), Note 104) that the village of "Bois Gautier" near Montigny-sur-Andelle was named for Walter de Gournay, and independently corroborate the Montigny–Massy tenurial pair behind the 1259 *Les Olim* recital.

## 1. Bois-Gauthier — a real hamlet, formerly a parish, in the canton of Argueil

DicoTopo place P27558354:

> **Bois-Gauthier** — Hameau, commune de La Chapelle-Saint-Ouen, anc. paroisse et commune. [dpt 76, canton Argueil; long/lat 1.4244, 49.5192]
> Attested form: "Bois-Gautier," 1629 (Hondius).

- The hamlet sits in the **canton of Argueil** — the very district Thomas Stapleton identified as the portion of the Bray fief severed for Walter — about 5 km north of Montagny (the medieval Montigny, below). It was formerly a parish and commune in its own right.
- Earliest form recorded by the dictionary: **"Bois-Gautier" on Hondius's map of 1629**. No medieval form (e.g. *Boscus Walteri*) is recorded for this place, so the naming-for-Walter conjecture cannot be pushed back to the twelfth century from the dictionary alone — but the place is real, is where De la Mairie put it, and sits inside Walter's conjectured Norman portion.
- Distinguish two other Norman homonyms with recorded medieval forms, both unrelated: Bois-Gautier at Intraville (canton Envermeu), "Boscum Galteri" 1324; and Le Bois-Gautier at Civières (Eure, canton Écos), "Boschus-Galterii" 1241. A further "Bois Gautier" field-name at Beaubec-la-Rosière (canton Forges-les-Eaux; 1754, Arch. S.-M. 2 H 493) lies in the Bray near Beaubec abbey.

## 2. Montagny (Nolléval) — the medieval Montigny, a half-fee extending toward Argueil

DicoTopo place P47177631 (hameau, commune de Nolléval, canton Argueil, anc. paroisse et commune; long/lat 1.4807, 49.4879 — in the upper Andelle valley). Selected attested forms:

| Form | Date | Reference (as given by the dictionary) |
|---|---|---|
| "R. de Montegni" | 12th century | **Gurney, p. 103** |
| "E. de Monteniaco, dimidium feodum ad Monteniacum ; N. de Monteniaco dimidium feodum ad Monteniacum" | c. 1210 | [Norman feodal survey] |
| "Ecc. Sancti Petri de Monteigniaco" | — | H. Fr. XXIII, 639, 315 |
| "W. de Montegni" | 1249 | Arch. Eure, Isle-Dieu H 370 |
| "Montegni" / "Montegny ½ fief" | 1337, 1398, 1399, 1431 | Arch. Nat. P. 303 etc. |
| "½ fief nommé le fief de Montagny, assis en la par. de Montagny et s'estend es parties d'Argueil…" | 1503 | Beaucousin 65, châtellenie de la Ferté-en-Bray |
| "Capella de Montigniaco" | 16th century | Arch. S.-M. G. 4 |
| "Montaigny" | 1757 | Cassini |

- The dictionary's 12th-century form is cited from **"Gurney"** — Daniel Gurney's *Record* itself, which the Seine-Maritime dictionary uses as an authority for this place. The identification Montagny (Nolléval) = the Gournay Montigny is therefore the dictionary's own.
- **"E. de Monteniaco," holding a half-fee at Montigny c. 1210**, is the family of Eustace de Montigny, the 1259 petitioner of *Les Olim* i, p. 85 — independent confirmation that a Montigny family held a fee there in the generation after the Gournay grant.
- The fief remained a **half-fee** through the 14th–16th centuries, held within the **castellany of La Ferté-en-Bray** (a Gournay-family castellany), and in 1503 "extended into the parts of Argueil."

## 3. Massy — held of Montigny, in the castellany of La Ferté-en-Bray

DicoTopo place P31767858 (commune, canton Neufchâtel-en-Bray). Selected forms:

| Form | Date | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| "In villa de Masci ; Apud Mascheium…" | 12th century | **Gurney, 98, 100, 121** |
| "Villam de Maciaco" | 1203 | — |
| "Pro Maciaco ; Pro feodo de Maci" | c. 1210 | — |
| "A Massy en la châtellerie de la Ferté-en-Bray" | 1390 | — |
| "Terre de Macy, ½ fief tenu du ½ fief de **Montigny-en-Bray**" | 1406 | — |
| "Massy, fief noble de pleines armes" | 1450 | — |

- The 1406 entry — Massy a half-fief **held of the half-fief of Montigny-en-Bray** — preserves, two centuries later, exactly the dependency Daniel Gurney reported ("Montigny with its member Massy"); and Massy too sat in the La Ferté-en-Bray castellany. The dictionary again cites "Gurney" for its 12th-century forms.

## Bearing on the Bois Gautier question (L-222)

The toponymy neither proves nor retires De la Mairie's conjecture, but it substantially firms its setting: Bois-Gautier, Montigny (Montagny), and the Argueil district form one tight cluster in the upper Andelle/Bray country; Montigny was a half-fee with a resident *de Monteniaco* family by c. 1210 and remained tied to the Gournay castellany of La Ferté-en-Bray; Massy's tenure "of Montigny" held into the fifteenth century. What is missing is any recorded medieval form of Bois-Gautier at La Chapelle-Saint-Ouen — the earliest is 1629 — so the attribution to Walter personally remains a conjecture, now precisely located.

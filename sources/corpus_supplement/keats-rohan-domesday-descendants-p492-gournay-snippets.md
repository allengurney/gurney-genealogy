(Keats-Rohan's Gournay numbering runs one lower than the numbering used on this site: her Hugh I = this site's Hugh III (G33); her Hugh II = Hugh IV.)

## What the snippets establish

1. **The leading modern prosopography accepts the Walter → William filiation.** An entry on p. 492 reads "Son of Walter de Gournay. Held [a] fee of Manasser de Dammartin in 1166," citing Red Book p. 409 — i.e., Keats-Rohan reads the 1166 Dammartin carta exactly as Daniel Gurney did: William, the 1166 holder, was Walter's son. Caveat: she calls the holding "half a fee"; the carta text is "j quartum militis" (a quarter). Either a slip or a conflation with the Kent half-fee of Red Book pp. 195–96.
2. **She is silent on Walter's own parentage.** The Gerard entry's issue clause, as captured, names only "Hugh II de Gournay and Gundreda" — Walter is not named among Gerard's issue, and no snippet connects Walter upward. On present (partial) evidence Keats-Rohan neither endorses nor contradicts the Note-104 identification of Walter as Gerard's son; she simply carries Walter as the father of the 1166 William.
3. **A genuine modern dissent on Gerard's death.** Keats-Rohan states Gerard "died before Jerusalem was taken in 1099," citing Robert of Torigny's interpolations in William of Jumièges (ed. van Houts) — reading the *Hierosolymam petens in ipso itinere mortuus est* passage as the crusade journey itself, and (per the adjacent snippet) placing the wife's accompaniment on that same journey. This conflicts with the reading followed on this site (Orderic places Gerard at Nicaea after May 1097; the St-Sauveur roll shows him alive in/about 1104; Clay, EYC 8, pp. 6–7: "died not earlier than 1104"). Record as a conflicting-information item for G32, weighted against the 1104 documentary anchor Keats-Rohan's entry does not discuss (on the captured evidence).

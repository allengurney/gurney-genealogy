# Blomefield, *History of Norfolk*, vol. 1, pp. 428–432 — Larling (Lerling)

Source: Francis Blomefield, *An Essay Towards a Topographical History of the County of Norfolk*, vol. 1 (London, 1805), "Hundred of Shropham: Lerling," British History Online, https://www.british-history.ac.uk/topographical-hist-norfolk/vol1/pp428-432. Source ID: `blomefield-norfolk`.

## LERLING

> Lurling, Lirling, and now Lerling, or Lerlingford, is so called from its low situation on the river, upon the ford or biggest passage that any where crosses it... At the time of the Confessor's survey, there were two manors; the least was then held by a freeman, and at the Conqueror's time was given to William Earl Warren, who infeoffed Hugh in it... the capital manor... belonged to Ulketell, and the soke... belonged at that time to Bukenham castle.

## The Manor of Welholme's (or Easthall) — Anthony Gurnay's 1532 feoffment

> Or Easthall, as it is sometimes called, was split out of Gonvile's manor about the beginning of Henry the Third's time... Adam de Methelond held it of **William Wauncy**, who held it of the **Bardolphs**, and they of the Earl Warren, as of his castle at Acre, by the fourth part of a fee, it being part of those eight fees that William de Wauncy and his tenants held of the said castle... in 1439, Oliver Groos of Sloley, Esq. was possessed of two parts of it... In 1453, Oliver Groose, Esq. gave a part to Cecily his daughter; in 1491, Robert Grosse held the moiety of it, and John was his son and heir. **In 1532, Anthony Gurnay, Esq. settled the manor on John Tirrell, Esq. and others, and the same year John Heydon, Knt. and Catherine his wife, and Christopher Heydon, Knt. settled it on Sir Tho. Jermyn, Knt.** and soon after it extinguished, the whole being manumised, and the demeans divided into many parcels, most of which still pay their proportions to the lord of the hundred, for the suit fines which were due from this manor to the hundred.

The manor Anthony Gurney (G17) settled in 1532 was specifically **Welholme's (Easthall)**, a fraction split from Gonville's manor centuries earlier — not the capital Lerling manor. Its tenurial chain runs through **William Wauncy** and the **Bardolphs** — the same two families whose fees and inheritance recur elsewhere in the Gurney story: the Wauncy inheritance brought West Barsham itself into the family in 1372 (Katherine de Wauncy m. Edmund Gournay, G23), and the Bardolfs held the barony of which the junior line's Swathings tenure (Hardingham) was a fragment. Anthony's 1532 Larling holding therefore sits within the same old Wauncy–Bardolph–Warenne tenurial fabric as the family's other Norfolk lands, by 1532 reduced to a small, soon-extinguished fraction transferred to the Heydon/Jermyn circle — consistent with the working Gurney–Heydon alliance documented elsewhere in this generation.

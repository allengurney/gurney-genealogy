# G32 Gerard de Gournay: Drogo, Edith, Pilgrimage, and Death-Date Window

AI-ready research handoff for Gerard de Gournay (G32), focused on the later Holy Land pilgrimage, the 8 May death notice, Edith de Warenne's remarriage to Drogo/Dreux de Mouchy, the Henry I wardship sequence, and whether the children's births strain the chronology.

## Working Conclusion

Gerard de Gournay died on **8 May**, after the First Crusade and after the 1104 St-Sauveur-en-Cotentin evidence that Daniel Gurney treated as proving Gerard still alive. The strongest current bracket is:

> **8 May, after/about 1104 and before 1112; probably 8 May 1104 or 8 May 1105, with 1106-1111 possible but increasingly unattractive unless the second journey became unusually prolonged.**

The bracket turns on one unresolved technical point: whether the St-Sauveur evidence places Gerard alive **before** 8 May 1104, **after** 8 May 1104, or only generally within/around 1104. If Gerard is known alive after 8 May 1104, then 8 May 1104 is excluded and **8 May 1105** becomes the first possible obituary date. If the St-Sauveur record only proves he was alive sometime before 8 May 1104, then **8 May 1104 remains possible**.[^gurney-st-sauveur-1104][^dhi-gerard]

## Chronological Reconstruction

### Before 1091: Gerard and Edith married

Douglas Richardson's SGM pedigree, echoed in later online transcripts, gives Gerard's marriage to Aidieve/Ediva/Edith de Warenne as before 1091 and says her maritagium included Mapledurham, Oxfordshire. This is useful for the child chronology because a marriage before 1091 allows Hugh and Gundred to have been born before the First Crusade or between Gerard's First Crusade return and the later pilgrimage.[^richardson-sgm][^cybergata-gerard]

### 1096-1099: Gerard, Edith, and Drogo I in the First Crusade world

Gerard is firmly attached to Robert Curthose's First Crusade contingent. DHI treats his participation as certain and names Edith as his wife and First Crusader. Drogo I of Mouchy-le-Chatel is also treated as a First Crusader in the same database universe; the France regional index lists him in Picardy/Oise, and DHI's Hugh/William Warenne entries identify him as First Crusader and stepfather/husband in the Gournay-Warenne network.[^dhi-gerard][^dhi-france-drogo][^dhi-hugh][^dhi-warenne]

Orderic and William of Tyre give Drogo independent crusading context: Orderic names Drogo de Monceio among those joining the First Crusade; William of Tyre names Drogo de Monci in the Antioch army order. The William of Tyre evidence is later and should not outweigh Orderic, but it reinforces that Drogo's crusader identity was not invented solely from his later marriage to Edith.[^orderic-v5-drogo][^william-tyre-drogo]

### 1101-1103: Drogo in the Beauvais/Oise orbit

Suger places Drogo in conflict around Montmorency/Beaumont and then describes Louis acting against Drogo for wrongs done especially to the church of Beauvais. A modern military-history treatment repeats this as Louis intervening for the church of Beauvais against Drogo of Mouchy.[^suger-drogo][^medieval-france-drogo]

The Saint-Martin de Pontoise cartulary adds a sharper ecclesiastical link: a 1103 diploma of the chapter of Beauvais names Drogo de Monceio among witnesses. The site was unstable during this pass, but search-index text exposed the relevant line and URL; the source should be rechecked directly when the cartulary is reachable.[^pontoise-1103]

This matters because Gerard's obituary was preserved by a Beauvais church. Drogo's documented local/church presence in 1101-1103 makes the later Edith-Drogo remarriage regionally plausible, not accidental.

### 1104: Gerard alive and/or second pilgrimage begins

Daniel Gurney used the St-Sauveur-en-Cotentin evidence to reject the old 1097 death year and concluded Gerard was still living in 1104. DHI's Gerard entry separately summarizes that Gerard later went on a Holy Land pilgrimage in 1104.[^gurney-st-sauveur-1104][^dhi-gerard]

This is the critical unresolved item. If St-Sauveur's 1104 date is a terminal date in a roll rather than a dated act signed after 8 May, it may not exclude 8 May 1104. If it is a dated act after 8 May 1104, then Gerard cannot have died until 8 May 1105 or later.

### After Gerard's death: Edith returns, marries Drogo

The Jumieges/Gesta tradition gives the sequence: Gerard seeks Jerusalem with Edith, dies on the journey, Edith returns, and she marries Drogo de Monceio, by whom she has a son also named Drogo. The Saint-Leu d'Esserent cartulary note repeats this sequence in French, citing the Jumieges chronicle tradition.[^gurney-second-pilgrimage][^saint-leu-drogo-note][^ediva-fmg-transcript]

This sequence argues **against** Drogo I being Edith's husband or known companion on Gerard's fatal second pilgrimage. Drogo I could have known Gerard and Edith through the First Crusade/regional world, but the surviving relationship evidence makes him the post-return second husband, not the second-pilgrimage companion.

### Before 1112: Gerard dead, Edith remarried, Hugh restored

The strongest practical upper bound is not 1118 but **1112**. Gurney says Hugh was of full age in 1112 when confirming donations to Bec made by his father, grandfather, and grandmother. Gurney's later narrative explains the minority sequence: Hugh was in tender years when Gerard died; Drogo de Monchy had custody/governance of the honor of Bray for a time; Hugh was sent to Henry I's court and raised by the king.[^gurney-hugh-full-age-1112][^gurney-henry-drogo-sequence]

Thus, by the time Hugh was adult/full age in or around 1112, the following events had already occurred:

1. Gerard died on the later Jerusalem journey.
2. Edith returned from the journey.
3. Edith married Drogo I.
4. Drogo governed/administered Hugh's honor during Hugh's minority.
5. Henry I raised Hugh and restored him to his paternal honor.

That is why the date range should not remain a loose "before 1118" unless 1112 is rejected.

### 1118: Henry I relationship visible in Orderic

Orderic's 1118 rebellion narrative confirms the earlier sequence. Hugh son of Gerard de Gournay had been raised by Henry I like a son, armed as an adult knight, restored to his paternal honor, and made prominent among the magnates; Drogo his stepfather had governed the honor for a time.[^orderic-v6-hugh][^remacle-orderic-1118]

This 1118 notice is valuable corroboration, but it is not the upper-bound date. It reports a background already complete before the rebellion.

### 1131 x 1133: Drogo I probably still living or represented in a Henry I context

A Dunstable Priory acta study includes Drogo de Monceio / Drogo de Mouchy in a witness list dated c. 1131 x 1133. The charter itself is treated as false, but the editor notes that the witness list and place-date may have been derived from authentic acts. Use this cautiously: it supports Drogo I's continuing Henry I orbit, but it should not be treated as clean primary proof without the acta context.[^dunstable-drogo]

Richardson's SGM pedigree also states Drogo/Drew de Monceaux was living 1131.[^richardson-sgm]

## Pilgrimage Duration Analysis

The duration evidence argues for an earlier end date within the 1104-1111 bracket.

Saewulf's pilgrimage is the closest western comparator after the First Crusade. His account runs from departure at Monopoli in Apulia on 13 July 1102 to the return voyage from Jaffa on 17 May 1103, with the narrative ending during the return through the eastern Mediterranean. A modern article describes Saewulf's account as covering Monopoli-Jaffa, the Kingdom of Jerusalem visits, and the return from Jaffa to Marmara Ereglisi; the Wikipedia/DNB-style summary gives the same 1102-1103 span. From northern France/Normandy, add land travel to Italy or another embarkation port, but the model is still roughly one year, not seven.[^saewulf-gutenberg][^saewulf-article][^saewulf-summary]

The Russian Abbot Daniel provides a second early-twelfth-century comparator. His Holy Land pilgrimage is dated with some confidence to 1106-1107, with Easter 1107 in Jerusalem. He travelled widely in Palestine, but the named pilgrimage still belongs to a one- to two-year frame, not an open-ended seven-year absence.[^abbot-daniel]

General reference treatments agree that a Holy Land pilgrimage took **months**, and that the early twelfth century saw renewed access to shrines under the Latin Kingdom.[^encyclopedia-pilgrimage][^pringle-pilgrimage]

**Chronological implication:** If Gerard and Edith left in 1104 and Gerard died "on the journey," an 8 May death in 1104 or 1105 is more natural than a death as late as 1111. A long gap to 1111 would require either repeated delay, prolonged residence, capture, severe travel disruption, or a broader use of "pilgrimage" covering years abroad. No source currently supports such an extended second journey for Gerard and Edith.

## Edith Records Before the Drogo Marriage

No direct pre-Drogo charter for Edith's return has yet been located in this pass.

The best records are indirect:

- The Jumieges/Gesta tradition states Edith returned and then married Drogo.[^gurney-second-pilgrimage][^saint-leu-drogo-note]
- Orderic confirms Drogo as Hugh's stepfather, meaning Edith's marriage to Drogo occurred before Orderic's 1118 background narrative and likely well before Hugh's adult restoration.[^orderic-v6-hugh]
- Gurney's reconstruction implies Edith had returned, remarried, and Hugh's guardianship situation had stabilized before Hugh's full-age activity in 1112.[^gurney-hugh-full-age-1112][^gurney-henry-drogo-sequence]
- A later Henry II charter, recited in a 1212 plea roll, refers to all the land that Hugh's mother Ediva had in England. This is valuable evidence for Edith/Ediva's own English tenure but does not date her return before the Drogo marriage.[^gurney-ediva-english-land]

Searches for an Edith-Henry I record did not produce a direct early charter. The likely next pulls are *Early Yorkshire Charters*, vol. 8, pp. 6-7; Oxfordshire Record Society 7 (1925), pp. 7-15; and Delisle and Berger, *Actes de Henri II*, vol. 1, no. 325, which Richardson says mentions "Edwa" corrected to Ediva.[^richardson-sgm]

## Children and Chronological Tension

The children do not break the post-1104 death window, but they constrain how late the death should be allowed to drift.

### Hugh de Gournay

Two birth models coexist:

- Richardson/SGM style: Hugh born c. 1091, with Gerard and Ediva married before 1091.[^richardson-sgm]
- FMG-derived/modern transcript style: Hugh born [1098/1100], partly inferred from his long life to 1180.[^hugh-fmg-transcript]

The c. 1091 model is easiest: Hugh was born before the First Crusade and was still a minor when Gerard died after 1104. Full age by 1112 is possible if "of full age" means approximately 21.

The [1098/1100] model also can work, but only if either Edith accompanied Gerard on the First Crusade or Hugh was born shortly after their return. DHI treats Edith as First Crusader and as Gerard's wife, so the possibility that Hugh was conceived/born during or around the expedition cannot be dismissed.[^dhi-gerard][^dhi-expedition-first]

However, if Hugh were born as late as 1100, full age in 1112 is difficult under a strict age-21 interpretation. That is a reason to prefer either (a) an earlier Hugh birth, or (b) treating "full age in 1112" as Gurney's reconstruction rather than a primary age statement.

### Gundred de Gournay

Gundred married Nigel d'Aubigny in June 1118, in the context of Henry I's handling of Hugh's rebellion. If Gundred was of marriageable age by 1118, she was probably born before the second pilgrimage or soon after the First Crusade return.[^gurney-henry-drogo-sequence]

A Gerard death in 1104/1105 gives an easy window for Gundred to be Gerard and Edith's daughter. A death as late as 1111 also allows it biologically, but it becomes historically less neat because Edith would need to be absent or unsettled for many years before returning, remarrying, bearing Drogo II, and seeing Hugh restored before 1112.

### Drogo II, son of Edith and Drogo I

Drogo II was the son of Edith and Drogo I. He later took the cross and died on the Second Crusade in 1148.[^saint-leu-drogo-note][^royal-holloway-drogo-1148]

If Edith married Drogo I soon after Gerard's death, a birth for Drogo II c. 1106-1111 is plausible. If Gerard died as late as 1111, Drogo II's birth shifts later, but he would still be old enough for the Second Crusade in 1147-1148. Thus Drogo II does not disprove a late death date, but he does not require one.

### Net effect of child chronology

The children favor a shorter sequence:

> Gerard alive in/about 1104 -> second pilgrimage -> death on 8 May 1104 or 1105 -> Edith returns and marries Drogo -> Drogo II born -> Hugh's minority/wardship/restoration completed by 1112.

They do not force this sequence, but it is cleaner than a long 1104-1111 absence.

The Saint-Leu d'Esserent note explicitly distinguishes Dreux II, son of deceased Dreux I, from the earlier Jumieges tradition about Gerard, Edith, and Dreux de Mouchy.[^saint-leu-drogo-note]

## Source Tensions and Cautions

1. **The current G32 fact-sheet phrase "before 1104" appears backwards.** The research trail supports "after/about 1104" or "after 1104" depending on the St-Sauveur date.

2. **1097 should not be used for Gerard's death.** It conflicts with Gerard's attested First Crusade presence after 8 May 1097.[^orderic-v5-gerard][^gurney-beauvais-obit]

3. **A modern Norman-crusading source has an apparent name confusion.** One passage says Edith, sister of William de Warenne, went on crusade with "her husband Drogo de Mouchy." This appears to conflate her later husband Drogo with her First Crusade husband Gerard; the same broader source tradition elsewhere knows Drogo as Hugh's stepfather and Gerard as natural father. Do not adopt "Edith went with Drogo" without checking the author's note and source apparatus.[^normans-possible-error][^hurlock-drogo-network]

4. **DHI's "pilgrimage in 1104" is useful but may be derivative.** Treat it as a modern prosopographical synthesis and a pointer back to Orderic/Gesta/Van Houts/Gurney, not as an independent dated primary record.[^dhi-gerard]

5. **Drogo I on Gerard's second pilgrimage remains unsupported.** Existing evidence places Drogo I as Edith's second husband after her return, not as a known fellow pilgrim on Gerard's fatal journey.[^gurney-second-pilgrimage][^saint-leu-drogo-note]

## Best Current Wording for the Ancestor File

> Gerard de Gournay died on 8 May, after/about 1104 and before 1112, probably during a later pilgrimage toward Jerusalem with his wife Edith/Ediva de Warenne. A tighter working hypothesis is 8 May 1104 or 8 May 1105: 1104 remains possible only if the St-Sauveur-en-Cotentin evidence does not place Gerard alive after 8 May 1104; if it does, 1105 becomes the first plausible obituary year.

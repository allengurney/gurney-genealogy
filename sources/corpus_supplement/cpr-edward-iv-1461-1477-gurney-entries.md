# Calendar of the Patent Rolls, Edward IV, 1461–1477 — Gurney/Gourney/Garney entries

Curated extracts of every Gurn*-family entry in the two printed Patent Roll calendars covering 1461–1477: *Calendar of the Patent Rolls, Edward IV, A.D. 1461–1467* (London: HMSO, 1897; archive.org identifier `calendarpatentr14offigoog`, Google scan) and *Calendar of the Patent Rolls, Edward IV–Henry VI, A.D. 1467–1477* (HMSO, 1900; archive.org `calendarofpatent00grea`). Both volumes were swept at index and full-text level with variant and OCR-mangle families (`G[ou][ou]?rn[ae]`, rn→m confusions, `Garney/Gerney`); the entries below are the complete Gurn*-surname harvest. OCR-mangled forms in the scans (`Gbirney`, `Gumoy`, `GK>umey`) are silently normalized in the headings but noted where they affect the quoted text.

## CPR Edward IV 1461–1467

**p. 188 (2 Edward IV, 1462, membrane 14) — John Gourney, esquire: Barbican messuages regranted:**

> Grant for life to Master James Fryse of a messuage called 'le grete belle' by le Barbican in the parish of St. Botolph in Aldrisshegatestrete in the suburbs of London with 14 other messuages annexed, late of John G[o]urney, esquire, and Nicholas Browne of the grant of Henry VI., to hold by homage and fealty only. By p.s.

**p. 273 (3 Edward IV, 20 June 1463, membrane 5) — John Gurney's Westminster-palace lodge custody regranted** (recital of a commitment of 1 March 2 Edward IV to Richard Felde, converted to a life grant):

> …the custody of the lodges between the round tower within the palace [of Westminster] and the little conduit which John Gu[rn]ey lately held as above [i.e. at farm, of the grant of Henry VI.], the custody of a house within the palace which John Prudde lately held as above… [regranted to Felde for life]. By p.s.

**p. 284 (3 Edward IV pt. II, 28 July 1463, membrane 17) — Thomas Garney of London, yeoman, mainpernor** (mainpernor = a surety who undertakes for another's performance):

> Grant for life to Thomas Dovedall, 'gentilman,' by mainprise of Emoreus Saintlaurence of London, 'gentilman,' and Thomas G[a]rney of London, 'yoman,' of all messuages, lands, rents, services and other hereditaments of the king in Coly in Ireland, rendering yearly their true value at the Exchequer of Ireland. By p.s.

(Body OCR reads "Gbirney"; the volume index gives "Garney, Thomas, 284.")

**p. 297 (3 Edward IV, 5 July 1463, membrane 6) — the brewhouse 'le Lambe,' late of Blakeney and Gourney:**

> Grant for life to Robert Grey, one of the king's minstrels, for his good service to the king's father and the king, of a tenement or brewhouse called 'le Lambe' in Distaflane [Distaff Lane], London, to the value of 40s. yearly, which John Blakeney, esquire, and John Gourney lately had of the grant of Henry VI. by letters patent, having come into his hands by virtue of an act of resumption in Parliament at Westminster, 9 July, 33 Henry VI. and which is now in the king's hands but through its ruinous condition has been unoccupied for a long time, to hold without rendering anything to the king. By p.s.

(A later entry in the same volume, p. 362 area, 4 April [1464×67], regrants the same "tenement or brewhouse commonly called 'le Lambe' in Distaf Lane, London" to Thomas Worley of the king's chapel after the death of Robert Grey.)

**Matthew Gurney, knight (d. 1406), posthumous place/manor references** — index "Gurney, Matthew, 23, 176, 362"; representative entry, p. 23 (20 July 1461):

> The like [grant for life] to John Byconell, from 4 March last, of the office of steward of the king's manors of Stoke under Hamdon, Cori Malet, Stratton super le Vosse, Faryngdon Gurney, Welton alias Welweton, Inglescombe, Melton Faucomberge, Midsomernorton, Wydecomb, Laverton, Shepton Malet and Westharptree, co. Somerset, and Rymo with members, co. Dorset, late of Matthew Gurney, knight, receiving 5l. yearly at the hands of the receiver of the manors, as did Thomas Yonge. By p.s.

These are administrative references to the long-dead Somerset knight's former estates ("late of Matthew Gurney"), not to a living person. The index also carries the place-name rows "Farrington Gournay, Faryngdon Gurney, co. Somerset, 23, 362" and "Gorneworth, co. Devon, 586."

**Index coverage, 1461–67 volume.** The complete Gurn*-family index entries are: "Gourney, John, 188, 297"; "Gurney, John, 273"; "Gurney, Matthew, 23, 176, 362"; "Garney, Thomas, 284"; plus the place rows above. The volume records **no Thomas Gournay/Gurnay/Gurney entry of any kind** apart from the London yeoman mainpernor of p. 284 — in particular, no pardon to any Thomas Gurnay in 1461–67. (A clerk convict — a felon who escaped the gallows by claiming benefit of clergy — was delivered to the bishop's prison for purgation rather than pardoned by the crown, so such an episode would leave its trail in King's Bench indictment files, the supplementary pardon rolls (TNA C 67), or the bishop's registers, none of which the printed CPR calendars.)

## CPR Edward IV–Henry VI 1467–1477

**p. 152 (7/8 Edward IV) — Thomas Gourney of Halton, Buckinghamshire, feoffee:**

> Grant, for 10l. paid to the king, to Richard Fowler of the custody of six messuages, 200 acres of land, 20 acres of meadow, 30 acres of pasture and 20s. of rent in Cherdesle, Warmeston and Queynton, co. Buckingham, in the king's hands during the minority of Nicholas Bryghtwell son and heir of Walter Bryghtwell late of Cherdesle because the said Walter was seised in his demesne as of fee of the said messuages and other premises, held in chief as of the honor of Wallingford by knight service, and enfeoffed John Baldewyn, Thomas Verdour, William Andrewe, John Fryday and Thomas Gourney of Halton of the same by collusion to defraud the king of the custody, as appears by an inquisition taken before Thomas Billyng the younger, escheator in the county of Buckingham, to hold during the minority of the heir with his marriage without disparagement and so from heir to heir. By bill of the treasurer.

**Index coverage, 1467–77 volume.** "Gourney, Thomas, 152" is the only Gurn*-surname index entry in the volume (index and mangle sweeps). The volume records no entry connected with the death (1471) of Thomas Gurnay of Harpley/West Barsham, armiger.

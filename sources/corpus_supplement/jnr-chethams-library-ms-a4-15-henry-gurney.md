## Extracted research value

Modern manuscript-culture article that adds a second modern scholarly context for Henry Gurney's poems beyond May's *Spenser Studies* article.

Key findings to retain:

- Chetham's Library MS A.4.15, also called MC15, is a substantial early modern prose-and-poetry manuscript associated with Inns of Court literary culture but also with Norfolk and East Anglia.
- MC15 contains poetry by Henry Gurney on folios 69r-80r, placed in the main poetry section among otherwise urban/metropolitan literary material.
- Swann identifies Henry Gurney as an amateur poet/farmer, born 1549 and died 1616.
- Gurney wrote poetry in the 1590s after beginning at age 43, from the manor of Great Ellingham, Norfolk, just under 20 miles southwest of Norwich and roughly 100 miles northeast of London.
- He circulated poems and lent books among at least two dozen nearby readers, with the most distant known recipient about 30 miles away in Suffolk.
- Outside MC15, Gurney's poems survive only in his own personal notebook, though other copies were produced and Gurney prepared copies for friends and relatives.
- MC15 preserves thirteen of the seventeen agricultural poems found in Gurney's own anthology, derived from poems scattered across Bodleian MS Tanner 175, folios 49v-232v.
- The MC15 copies are more coherent and organized than their Tanner 175 originals, suggesting an intermediary stage between Gurney's own manuscript and MC15.
- Swann notes that Gurney may have had a London correspondent, offering a plausible transmission path from Great Ellingham to metropolitan manuscript networks.
- The Gurney poems in MC15 were copied in hand D, a major hand in the manuscript rather than an outsider's addition.
- Swann argues that MC15's ownership history may point away from a purely London origin and toward Norfolk book-trade or Norfolk ownership contexts, including possible transmission through Thomas Martin, Richard Farmer, and Chetham's Library.

# Ancient Deeds A.7857 — Henry Heydon enfeoffs William Gurney of the manor of Ormesby (Flegg, Norfolk), 1493

A single deed abstract from the printed *Descriptive Catalogue of Ancient Deeds* (TNA A-series), giving primary-deed confirmation that **Henry Heydon, knight**, enfeoffed **William Gurney** — alongside Philip Calthorp and James Hobart — of the core Heydon estates in Flegg, Norfolk. Abstract level; the original deed has not been examined.

## Extract (verbatim, Descriptive Catalogue of Ancient Deeds, vol. 4, p. 229–230)

> **A. 7857.** Feoffment by Henry Heydon, knight, son and heir of John Heydon to Philip Calthorpp, esquire, James Hobert, Richard Southwell, William Gurney, John Heydon, Robert Southwell, John Barley S.T.D. and Thomas Wymer, clerk, of the manor of Ormesby, the advowson of Wynterton and of the chapel of Est Somerton and of lands &c. in Ormesby, Hemmesby, Scrowby, Wynterton, Maudeby, Filby, Runham, Clepisby, Somerton, Horssey, Yermouth, Martham and Castre in Flegge, co. Norfolk, which the said John Heydon, together with Reginald Rous, Simon Briggegate, clerk, Thomas Arnold, William Bonde and John Marsham, deceased, whom he survived, had by the feoffment of Robert Clere the elder, esquire, since deceased; attorneys to deliver seisin Edward Andreus and William Haye. Ormesby, 25 March, 8 Henry VII. Witnesses. Seal.

## What the source records

- **Date:** 25 March, 8 Henry VII = 25 March 1493 (8 Henry VII ran 22 Aug 1492 – 21 Aug 1493).
- **Grantor:** Henry Heydon, knight, son and heir of John Heydon of Baconsthorpe — the same Henry Heydon who raised men-at-arms for William Gurney IV at Saxthorpe in 1472, and whose daughter Anne Heydon married William Gurney V (G18).
- **Feoffees include William Gurney**, sitting with Philip Calthorp, James Hobart (the king's attorney), Richard and Robert Southwell, and John Heydon — the same Heydon–Calthorpe–Hobart bloc documented in the contemporaneous Calendar of IPM Henry VII feoffee circuit.
- **Property:** the manor of Ormesby with the advowson of Winterton and the chapel of East Somerton, plus lands across the Flegg hundred (Ormesby, Hemsby, Scratby, Winterton, Mautby, Filby, Runham, Clippesby, Somerton, Horsey, Yarmouth, Martham, Caister-on-Sea).

The deed is a primary-record instance of the Gurney–Heydon trust bond that the family's Blomefield-derived material had established at secondary level: Henry Heydon placing a Gurney among the trustees of his own core Flegg estates in the same year (1493) the Gurney–Heydon marriage tie was two decades mature.

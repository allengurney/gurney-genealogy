## Bottom line

The date is not 22 Charles I and not 1646/7. Both visible headings read 14 April 1629, with a regnal phrase for Charles I. Since 14 April 1629 falls in Charles I's fifth regnal year, the unclear regnal abbreviation is best treated as a scribal/regnal note accompanying the clear calendar year, not as `22 Caroli`.

The entry is a court presentment/order about an orchard. It names `John Gurne`, without any descriptor such as senior, junior, son of John, occupation, residence, or deceased predecessor. The substance is that John Gurne had planted about twelve fruit trees in the same orchard and had made or allowed grass there; the following Latin notes that the lord and officers understood the orchard ought to be kept/restored in its proper form. This is not an admission, surrender, heirship recital, or parentage record.

## English summary

John Gurne is recorded in connection with an orchard. He had planted about twelve fruit trees there and had made/allowed the ground to grass. The court then frames the matter as one known to the lord and officers and contrary to the expected form/custom of the manor, leading to a precept/order. The entry is therefore a regulatory presentment about land use in an orchard.

## Answers to packet questions

### (a) Resolve the date

The date is 14 April 1629. The `22 Caroli` reading should be rejected. If a regnal year is supplied, the calendar date places it in 5 Charles I, not 22 Charles I.

### (b) Presentment or encroachment?

Presentment/order, not a conveyance. It concerns orchard use and likely alteration of the orchard's required condition. The line about fruit trees and grass is followed by Latin stating what was known to the lord and officers and by precept/order language.

### (c) Which John?

The entry names only `John Gurne`. No `senior`, `junior`, `son of John`, occupation, residence, wife, predecessor, or heir is visible in this packet.

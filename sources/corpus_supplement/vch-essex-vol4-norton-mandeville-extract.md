## Manor (pp. 151–152)

Two Norton manors appear in Domesday. The relevant one was held in 1086 by Wimund under Hamon *dapifer* (1½ hide and 15 acres, held in 1066 by "Gotil"). Hamon *dapifer* was succeeded by his son Hamon (sheriff of Kent, dead by Michaelmas 1130), then by Robert FitzHamon's line: the overlordship passed with the earldom of Gloucester and ultimately to the Clares.

By the late 12th century the Dammartins held the tenancy. William de Dammartin (d. 1195) succeeded his brother Bartholomew (d. before 1190); they were sons of Manasser de Dammartin. On William's death the king granted the wardship of his daughter and heir Galiena to William Brewer (Briwerre), who married her to his brother John (d. 1210 without issue). She then married Robert de Burgate (d. after Easter 1220), and by 1228 Ernald de Mandeville. In 1254 Galiena conveyed the manor of Norton to her son John de Mandeville, retaining a life interest. The manor later passed through the le Rous family and in 1490 was sold to Merton College, Oxford.

## Church (pp. 152–154)

In 1181 Norton was included for ecclesiastical purposes in the parish of High Ongar, but Norton had its own church within nine years of that date: **Bartholomew de Dammartin (d. before 1190) and Galiena his mother granted the church to the priory of St. Leonard, Stratford-atte-Bow.**

## Reading note

The church grant is the VCH's direct attestation of the elder Galiena (Galiena I, widow of Manasser de Dammartin d. 1178/9) acting with her son Bartholomew c. 1181–90 — distinct from her granddaughter Galiena II (daughter of William de Dammartin), whose three marriages the manor section recounts. Norton Mandeville's overlordship (FitzHamon → Gloucester → Clare) is independent of both the Warenne and the Talbot/Swanscombe honours in which the family's other holdings lay.

# Descriptive Catalogue of Ancient Deeds — A.2972, Hugh de Gurnay of Letton (Norfolk)

Source ID: `descriptive-catalogue-ancient-deeds`

Abstract of deed A.2972, *A Descriptive Catalogue of Ancient Deeds in the Public
Record Office*, vol. 2, pp. 132–144 (Norfolk deeds section). British History Online,
<https://www.british-history.ac.uk/descriptive-cat-ancient-deeds/vol2/pp132-144>.
Undated (12th–13th century).

## The abstract (verbatim)

> [Norf.] **A. 2972.** Grant by **Hugh de Gurnay of Letton**, to the church of
> St. Pancras and the monks of Lewes in frank almoin, of the tenement that Matilda
> daughter of Aliz held of him in Lettune, viz. 18d. yearly issuing from land, part
> upon **Wrongelond**, part in **Sudfeld**, part in **Humelescroft**, and part on
> **Cranewrdefeld**. Witnesses:—Alexander de Alenzun, Roger de Risinges, William de
> Kayli, and others (named).

## Blomefield corroboration (Letton)

Blomefield's Letton entry (*Topographical History of Norfolk*, vol. 10, pp. 231–233,
"Lewes Priory Manor") records the same grant among the priory's Letton benefactions:
"Gilbert de Rysing was lord" of Letton in 1161 and "a great benefactor" to Lewes;
and, sans date, "**Hugh de Gurnay of Letton, granted them a tenement, &c. sans
date.**" The deed's witness **Roger de Risinges** matches the Rysing lordship of
Letton, situating the grant in the local Letton/Lewes-priory frame.

## Neutral note on the record

Letton adjoins Cranworth and Hardingham in Mitford hundred — the field name
"Cranewrdefeld" (Cranworth field) locates the tenement on that boundary. St Pancras,
Lewes, was the Warenne family's Cluniac priory. The abstract is at catalogue
(descriptive) level, not the original charter; it names a Hugh de Gurnay styled "of
Letton" but records nothing of his descent or wider landholding.

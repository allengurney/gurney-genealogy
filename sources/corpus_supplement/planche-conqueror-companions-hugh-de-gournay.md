## Scope searched

Searched the user-supplied 1066.co.nz markdown for `Gurney`, `Gurnay`, and `Gournay`. Relevant material appears in Planches entries for Giffard, Mortimer, Grandmesnil, Albini, Aubigny, Stuteville, Monceaux, and especially the Hugh de Gournay article.

## Extracted findings

- Planché repeats the Beauvaisis-left-bank acquisition label from De la Marie: certain parishes on the left bank of the Epte annexed by Hugh de Gournay in the twelfth century were called the "Conquets Hue de Gournay." This belongs with the aggregate Beauvaisis frontier acquisitions place file.
- Walter Giffard first appears, in Planches account, as a companion of Hugh de Gournay in the 1035 attempt by Edward, son of Ethelred, to recover England. This reinforces Hugh II's direct engagement in English succession politics before the Conquest.
- In the Mortemer account, Hugh de Gournay is listed with Roger de Mortemer, Robert of Eu, Hugh de Montfort, William Crispin, and Walter Giffard among the Norman commanders sent against Count Eudes/Odo in 1054.
- In the Evreux and Conches conflict, William Rufus ordered Stephen of Aumale and Gerard de Gournay, with other commanders in Normandy, to support Raoul de Toeni and provision castles.
- In a later Evreux/Bertrade settlement, Ecouchi was excepted because it was held by Gerard de Gournay. Planché treats Gerard as part of the same broad Evreux/Tosny kin context.
- The Albini entry says Gundred, daughter of Gerard de Gournay, married Nigel de Albini; their son Roger assumed the Mowbray name, while Henri became ancestor of the Albini of Cainho.
- The Sigi priory passage says Hugh de Gournay founded or confirmed a priory before 1035. Its witness cluster includes Rodulf Taisson, Neel the viscount, Geoffrey the viscount, William the Count son of Duke Robert, and William the count's master.
- The Hugh de Gournay article identifies Wace's "le viel Hue de Gournai" and preserves the three-Gournay-at-Hastings problem: Hue de Gournay, the Sire de Brai, and the Seigneur de Gournay.
- Planché treats Hugh II as the senior pre-Conquest lord and Hugh III as his son, the husband of Basilia Flaitel, daughter of Gerard Flaitel, sister of Walter Giffard's wife, and widow of Raoul de Gacé.
- Planché says the elder Hugh came to Hastings with strong forces from the Pays de Bray and that they did substantial damage among the English. This is derivative and must be balanced against DG/Hannay's chronological caution.
- Planché repeats the origin tradition: the first Gournay was presumed to have followed Rollo and received part of Le Brai; principal places included Gournay, La Ferté, Lions, Charleval, and Fleury.
- For Hugh I, Planché says Hugh son of Eudes first made Gournay a place of strength; ancient family records credited him with a citadel, triple wall and fosse, and the tower called "La Tour Hue," still standing at the beginning of the seventeenth century.
- Planché says Renaud is the first family member found in a charter; his wife was Alberada; his sons were Hugh and Gautier; Gautier held La Ferté and founded the priory around 990 with family consent for the souls of Renaud and Alberada.
- Planché records Daniel Gurney's suggestion that Calvelville may be modern Conteville, but treats it as unproved unless supporting facts can be found.
- Planché says the Sire de la Ferté named by Wace at Hastings was not a Gournay of the old La Ferté branch because that branch had died out before the invasion.
- On the Cardiff death tradition, Planché rejects the 1074 French chronicle version as garbled but treats the Welsh/Powell form under 1094 as not fully destroyed: Hugh was said to have been wounded between Cardiff and Brecknock and to have died later in Normandy. He stresses Hugh was not Earl of Gournay, but says the title error does not by itself disprove the wounded-Hugh tradition.
- Planché records Roger de Alneto as possibly related to Gundred de Gournay, wife of Nigel de Albini.
- Planché repeats La Roques derivation of the lords of Gournay, Milly, and others from Bernard the Dane stock. This is origin-claim context only.
- Planché says Nicholas d'Estouteville married Gunnora, daughter of Hugh IV de Gournay and widow of Robert de Gant; he links Stuteville/Estouteville to La Ferté-en-Bray and suggests Robert d'Estouteville may have followed Hugh II de Gournay to England.
- Planché repeats that Drogo/Dreux de Monceaux was Edith de Warenne's second husband after Gerard de Gournay; either Drogo or his son witnessed Henry I's Dunstable foundation charter.

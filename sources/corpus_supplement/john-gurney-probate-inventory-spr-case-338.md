## NEHGR extract — Widow Wilson section

The same p. 53 page includes a separate but relevant Gurney reference under “Widow Wilson.” This appears to concern property or money belonging to the widow Wilson estate, including £4 in John Gurney’s hands.

```text
WIDOW WILSON. An Inventory of that estate wee William Alis and
Francis Eliot did find in Brantrey to belonge to the widow Wilson late of
Brantrey.

Money in hands of David Walsbe, put out to John Woord for 4 years;
£4 in hands of John Gurney. Francis Eliot, of Brantre, deposed 30 April
1663.

The Court accepted the Care and Indeauours of Francis Eliot & Wm Ellise
& order that ye Estate in the hands of said Eliot be pd Equally to the
Children of said widow Wilson, [charges deducted and a due proportion of
the £4 allowed them with other creditors to ye late John Gurney’s estate.]
```

### Notes

This passage is independently useful because it shows that, at the time of John Gurney’s death or probate processing, another estate had a claim involving **£4 in John Gurney’s hands**. The court language appears to place the Wilson heirs or Wilson estate claimants alongside **other creditors to the late John Gurney’s estate**.

This also strengthens the **Francis Eliot** connection. Eliot appears both in the Widow Wilson matter and in the John Gurney debt context. That does not make Eliot kin, but it does place him in John’s immediate Braintree estate-accounting orbit.

### Land-interest note

The land line is best read as:

```text

Because NEHGR gives the full inventory total as **£55 14s 6d**, do not yet assert whether the Quinapaug interest was included in, excluded from, or only noted alongside the appraised total until the original image is checked more closely.

Gregory Belcher &c., wch they have added a true Inventory of that

```

### Notes

This supersedes an earlier working reading that treated the bottom block as an oath by Grizell/Grisell Gurney. The widow is still present in the abstract, but only generically as **“the widow or friends of sd Gurney.”** The printed abstract does not name her.

The “Sam Broadstreet” form may represent **Samuel Bradstreet / Broadstreet** or a printed reading of a manuscript abbreviation. A later source check should compare the manuscript directly to Bradstreet/Broadstreet forms. Richard Wharton should be treated as a legal/probate deponent, not necessarily an ordinary estate creditor.

An independent re-read of the manuscript image (June 2026) reached the same headline figures — goods/estate **£55 14s 6d**, debts **£37 12s** — and the same key negative: **no kin is named in the case papers.** The widow appears only as a creditor (“to the widow for goods impaired out of her estate,” ≈£12 18s 4d), never by name, and neither Grizell nor any child or relation is named anywhere in the inventory or the bottom note. That re-read tentatively rendered the bottom note as an administration grant **to Richard Wharton**; the NEHGR abstract's clearer “deposed to the Inventory” wording is preferred, so Wharton (with Bradstreet) is best read as a **deponent verifying the inventory**, not as the named administrator. The image confirms the document carries no relationship statement — the estate file does not, on its own, connect John Gurney Senr of Braintree to a named wife or heirs.

## Normalized abstract

Braintree, 16 March 1663. An inventory was taken of the goods and estate of **John Gurney Senr**, deceased. The inventory was appraised by **Gregory Belcher, Edmund Quincy, and Thomas Faxon** and totaled **£55 14s 6d** according to the NEHGR abstract. The manuscript inventory includes clothing, boots and hat, bedding, blankets, pillows and cases, pewter, pincers, brass skillet and kettle, cupboard, musket, plow irons, coulter, axes, mattock, chisel, chair or frame goods, barrels, trays, wheat, Indian corn, grain boxes, hoops, old tubs, a horse, a yoke of oxen, sows and pigs, young cattle, an old barrow, wheels, and axletree. The manuscript also notes a land interest described as **“land at Quinapaug wch we know not.”**

The debts due from the estate include a substantial line for the widow’s impaired goods, plus debts or claims involving **Peter Brackett, Joseph Adams, Francis Nucomb/Newcomb, John Dassit Senr, Goodman King of Waymuth/Weymouth, Goodman Baly, John Mills, John Cleverly, Smith, Collins at Boston**, and others. Separate manuscript lines also appear to include **Francis Eliot**, **charges at Boston and funeral**, **Mr Alcocke**, **the Church of Braintree**, and **Mr Wharton**. A related Widow Wilson entry on the same NEHGR page states that **£4 was in the hands of John Gurney** and that the Wilson estate or children were to be allowed a due proportion of that £4 with other creditors to the late John Gurney’s estate.

## Research interpretation

### Probate confirms a broader creditor network

The probate file is not just an estate valuation. It preserves a working creditor and obligation network across Braintree, Weymouth, Boston, and the frontier land orbit.

### Braintree cluster

Braintree names include **Gregory Belcher, Edmund Quincy, Thomas Faxon, Francis Eliot, Francis Nucomb/Newcomb**, and the **Church of Braintree**. This strengthens the view that John’s final residence and probate community were firmly Braintree-centered.

### Weymouth bridge

The debtor list includes **Goodman King of Waymuth/Weymouth**. This is relevant to the question of why John appears in Weymouth land contexts while later living in Braintree. It suggests the Weymouth connection remained financially active or at least socially connected at death.

### Mendon / Quinapaug / Nipmug land thread

The manuscript land line at **Quinapaug** is the strongest new land finding. It should be correlated with the already-known Mendon proprietary record stream in which John Gurny and Grisel Gurney appear as twenty-acre lot holders. Peter Brackett’s presence in the debt list is especially important because Brackett is tied to the Braintree-led acquisition of the Mendon/Nipmug tract.

### Boston-related names

The Boston thread should now be framed more conservatively. The confirmed printed abstract reads **“Smith, Collins at Boston”**, not “Goodman Sweet of Boston.” The manuscript also appears to include **charges at Boston & funerall**. The bottom notation names **Mr Sam Broadstreet** and **Mr Richard Wharton** as deponents to the inventory. These references show Boston-facing legal, administrative, or commercial connections, but they do not by themselves identify John Gurney of Braintree with the 1636 John Newgate apprentice. Boston was the county seat and administrative/probate center for Suffolk County, so Boston charges and deponents are expected in a Braintree probate file.

### Widow Wilson cross-reference

The Widow Wilson entry is genealogically valuable because it places John Gurney in another estate-accounting matter: **£4 in the hands of John Gurney** was to be allowed with other creditors against the late John Gurney’s estate. This may explain part of the estate’s creditor context and adds another Braintree connection through Francis Eliot.

## Recommended research note

John Gurney’s 1663 probate inventory materially expands the Braintree, Weymouth, Boston, and Mendon/Quinapaug context around his estate. The inventory, taken at Braintree on 16 March 1663 by Gregory Belcher, Edmund Quincy, and Thomas Faxon, was abstracted in NEHGR as totaling £55 14s 6d. The manuscript page includes ordinary household goods, livestock, agricultural equipment, grain, and a land interest described as “land at Quinapaug wch we know not.” The debt list names Peter Brackett, Joseph Adams, Francis Nucomb/Newcomb, John Dassit Senr, Goodman King of Waymouth, Goodman Baly, John Mills, John Cleverly, Smith, Collins at Boston, and others. A related Widow Wilson entry on the same NEHGR page reports £4 in the hands of John Gurney and directs a proportional allowance with other creditors to the late John Gurney’s estate. The combined probate evidence strengthens the Braintree–Weymouth–Mendon land trail and shows Boston-facing probate or commercial obligations, but it does not by itself overcome the chronology problem that separates John Gurney of Braintree from the 1636 John Newgate apprentice.

> In the name of God Amen. The tenth day of August in ye fourteen[th] yeare of ye raigne of our Soveraign Lord [Charles] by ye grace of God of England, Scotland, ffrance & Ireland King, Defendor of ye ffaith &c. [1638] **I John Gurney of Eursham [Earsham] in ye County of Norff: yeoman**, being in [sickness?] of Body but p[er]fect and sound remembrance (thanks be given to God) doe ordaine & make this my last will & testament in manner & form following. ffirst I comend my Soule into ye m[er]cifull hands of God, steadfastly beleeving to have full pardon of all my sinnes [through] ye merritts of Jesus Christ my Sweet Saviour & redeemer; my Body [I] will shall be Buried at ye Discretion of my Executors hereafter in this my will to be named.
>
> Item I give & bequeath unto **Mary my wife** short[ly?] during ye minority of John Gurney my Sonne all those my messuage[s] lands & tenem[en]ts, as well ffreeholds as Copyholds, situate lying & being in Eursham aforesaid, for & towards ye bringing up of ye s[ai]d John [and] for & towards ye paying of my Sisters legacies & ye sufficient repairing [of the] messuage of my houses; And after ye s[ai]d John shall accomplish his age of **fourteen yeares, when he may be capable to take his guardianship,** then [I] give & bequeath all ye s[ai]d messuage lands & tenem[en]ts to him ye s[ai]d John Gurney his heirs & assignes forever, provided always that [he pay unto] my wife ye s[ai]d Mary ye yearely summe of four pounds of lawfull English money at ye two usuall feasts of ye Annunciation of our blessed lady St Mary ye Virgine & St Michael tharchangell by even [&] equall portions, for & during ye term of ye naturall life of her ye s[ai]d Mary, for in lieu & full satisfaction of all mann[er] of dowry or estate w[hi]ch she ye s[ai]d Mary may or can demand out of ye same.

## Reading notes

- **Parish.** The testator's parish reads "Eursham" at each of its three occurrences (testator style; lands clause; "John Sou[t]er late of Eursham"). Earsham, on the Waveney by Bungay in south-east Norfolk, is the natural fit, and the brother-in-law executor "John Youngman of St Andrews" — most plausibly Ilketshall St Andrew, one of the "Saints" villages immediately across the Waveney from Earsham — corroborates the south-east Norfolk localization. Confidence in the Earsham reading: high (~85–90%).
- **Wife.** The forename reads **Mary** (verified at expert-read level 2026-06-11; it appears twice — "unto Mary my wife" and "ye s[ai]d Mary" in the annuity clause — superseding an earlier "Al[ice]" working reading).
- **Son's taking age.** John takes the property at **fourteen**, "when he may be capable to take his guardianship"; the age twenty-one appears only in the later contingency clause (if John dies before 21, remainder to brother Lyon) and the legacy-payment scheduling.
- **No second parish.** The supposed second place-name in the lands clause was a misreading of the purpose language ("for & towards ye bringing up…"); all the lands lie in Earsham. (Expert verification, 2026-06-11 — see `sources/corpus_supplement/paleo-2026-06-packet-02-john-gurney-earsham-will-1638-verification.md`.)
- **Regnal date.** 10 August 14 Charles I = 10 August 1638.
- **The register.** The film bundles Cambridgeshire, Suffolk, and Norfolk wills 1638–1639 — a Norwich Consistory Court register volume (NCC registered copy wills vol. 131 per the FamilySearch catalog film list; NCC jurisdiction spanned Norfolk, Suffolk, and part of Cambridgeshire). The probate clause confirms the court: proved at Norwich, 26 December 1639, before the chancellor's surrogate — sixteen months after the will's making, so the testator died between August 1638 and late 1639.
- One further place-name in the lands clause ("& ye p[ar]ish of [—]") was not recovered at this read level.

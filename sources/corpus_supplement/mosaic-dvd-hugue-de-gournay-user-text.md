## Extracted research value

Derivative summary of the senior Norman Gournay line and the later Somerset and Norfolk younger branches. It substantially overlaps with Cleveland's *Battle Abbey Roll* entry but is useful as a separate page-level lead because it directly states that the name appears on the Battle Abbey Roll.

Key findings to retain:

- Hugue/Hugo de Gournay is described as lord of Gournay in Normandy with arms "pure sable."
- The page places him among the barons commanding at Mortimer/Mortemar against the French in 1054.
- It says he came to England with Duke William, participated at Hastings, and received manorial grants in Essex held at Domesday.
- It identifies his son Gerard/Gerard de Gournay as baron of Gournay in Normandy and baron of Yarmouth in England.
- It says Gerard married Editha, daughter of William de Warren, Earl of Surrey, and granddaughter of the Conqueror.
- The stated issue are one son and two daughters: Gundred, wife of Nigel de Albini; an unnamed daughter who married Richard de Talbot; and Hugo de Gournay.
- The page says Hugo was great-grandfather of Julia de Gournay, bride of William Rudolph, baron of Wormegay, ending the chief male line.
- It identifies two younger branches: a Somersetshire branch at Barrow Gurney and Englishcombe, and a Norfolk branch holding manors as mesne lords under the barons of Gournay.
- The Norfolk branch is said to produce the Gurneys of Harpley and West Barsham, with a younger son leading to the Norfolk Gurneys represented by Hudson Gurney of Keswick.

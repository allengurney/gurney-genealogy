**GURNEY OF KESWICK.**

Gurney, Hudson, Esq. of Keswick, co. Norfolk, F.R. and A.S., b. 19 Jan. 1775; m. in 1809, Margaret, dau. of the late Robert Barclay, Esq. of Ury, Kincardineshire, M.P. for that county, by Sarah, his wife, dau. of James Allardice, of Allardice, and heiress of line to the Earls of Airth and Menteith. Mr. Gurney was elected M.P. for Shaftesbury in 1812, and for Newtown, Hants, in 1816, and six successive parliaments, and was high sheriff of Norfolk in 1835. Mrs. Gurney d. 16 Dec. 1855.

**Lineage** [OCR: "HiltCaiJC"] â€” The name of Gurney, or Gournay, is derived from the town of Gournay, in Normandy.

When Normandy was ceded to Rollo by Charles the Simple, in 912, it is stated by the local historians that the town of Gournay, and adjacent territory of Le Brai, was given to Eudes, one of his followers, from whom descended the ancient lords of Gournay. The first of these, authenticated by deed, was Reginald [OCR: Rcoinald], Lord of Gournay, whose son, Walter, founded the monastery of La FertÃ© [OCR: Ferte], about the year 996, with the consent of Hugh, his older brother. Another Hugh, called Hugo II, was one of the Norman generals at the battle of Mortemer, in 1054; he and Hugo III were at the battle of Hastings. This latter m. Basilia Flaitel, and from him the descents of this family are clear; she was dau. of Gerard Flaitel, and sister (with Agnes, wife of William Gifford [OCR: Oitford], Earl of Buckingham) of William, Bishop of Evreux. Hugh de Gournay III held manors in Essex at the time of the survey, and d. at the Abbey of Bec, in Normandy, where he was shorn a monk before the year 1093.

**Gerard de Gournay** was his son; he m. Editha, dau. of William, 1st Earl Warren, by Gundreda, who, according to the Warren charters, was a dau. of William the Conqueror. With her he received in frank marriage various manors. He held in Norfolk, Caistor-by-the-Sea, Cantley, Hardingham, Losingham, Kimberley, etc., from the forfeiture of Ralph Guader. Caistor was the caput baroniae of the Gournays in England. Gerard de Gournay had several children. **Hugo de Gournay IV** was eldest son; Walter de Gournay, a younger son; and his daus. were Gundrida, who m. Nigel de Albini, in 1118; and a 2nd dau. m. Richard de Talbot, ancestor of the Earl of Shrewsbury. Gerard de Gournay d. about the year 1104, in the Holy Land, and Editha, his widow, re-married Dreux de Monceaux.

**Hugo de Gournay IV** was eldest son of Gerard; he m. twice, 1st, Beatrice, dau. of Hugh, Count of Vermandois; and 2ndly, Millicent de Maria, dau. of Thomas, Lord of Coucy; he d. in 1180, at a great age, leaving by his 2nd wife, Hugh de Gournay V, and a dau. Gunnora, who m. Nicholas de Stuteville, who had with her Kimberley and Bedingham, in Norfolk.

**Hugh de Gournay V**, vacillated between King John and Philip Augustus, and eventually lost all his Norman possessions in 1204, and took refuge in England, where he d. in 1214, and was buried at Langley Abbey, Norfolk. He m. Julia de Dampmartin, sister of Reginald, Count of Boulogne, and had a son, Hugh VI, and a dau., Millicent, m. 1st, to Almeric, Count of Evreux, and 2ndly, to William de Cantilupe.

**Hugh de Gournay VI** d. in 1235, having m. Matilda â€”, and left an only dau., Julia, who carried the English inheritance of this elder line of the Gournays to the Bardolfs, she having m. William, Lord Bardolf, of Wormegay [OCR: Wcroegiy], in Norfolk, and d. in 1295. These Lords of Gournay are said to have borne for arms a shield, *pure sable*; they are now represented, in the female line, by Lord Beaumont [OCR: BeaunMot], through the families of Bardolf, Phelip, Beaumont, Lovell [OCR: Lvni], and Stapleton.

Two younger branches of this family continued to exist from this period; the one which was the most distinguished was seated at Barew-Gurney [OCR: Barew-Guraey] and Inglishcombe [OCR: lnglishcombe], in Somersetshire, as early as the Survey, and retaining the name of Gournay, through two female descents, added to their territory the estates of the Harpetrees [OCR: H.ui>etroee] and other considerable families, and became powerful barons in the west of England. The most generally known of this family of the Gournays was Sir Thomas de Gournay, one of the murderers of Edward II, and his youngest son, Sir Matthew de Gournay, frequently mentioned by Froissart, who d. in 1406, at the advanced age of 96, after having assisted at all the great battles of Edward III and the Black Prince. This fief of Montigny was held by the tenure called parage, in capite of the Duke of Normandy, it having been severed in their favour on the death of Gerard de Gournay.

### 2. Maldon, Essex Branch
The 1862 edition explicitly names:
- **Francis Gurney** (b. 18 Sep 1581 at West Barsham, d. 1650): 6th son of Henry Gurnay I and Ellen Blennerhassett. Admitted to the Merchant Taylor's Company in London, 1606. Resided at St. Benet Fink, London, and occasionally Norwich and Lynn. Married **Ann, dau. of William Browning, of Norwich and of Maldon in Essex**. Children included Thomas Gurney (Quaker, companion of George Fox, ancestor of Sir John Gurney the judge).
- **Francis Gurnay of Maldon, Essex** (b. 1623): 2nd son of the above Francis and Ann Browning. Merchant at Maldon; one of the bailiffs of the borough in 1664. Married **Ann, dau. of Jeremy Browning, of Maldon**.
- **John Gournay/Gurney** (b. 1655): eldest son of Francis of Maldon. Quaker; established as a merchant in Norwich. Married 1687, Elizabeth Swanton; d. 1721.

**Note on John Gurney b. 1655**: This is the Norwich Quaker wool merchant, grandfather of the banking Gurneys. Burke states he was born 1655 (not c.1609/10). He is the son of Francis of Maldon (b. 1623) who in turn is the son of Francis Gurney (b. 1581) of St. Benet Fink. A John Gurney who emigrated to New England c.1630sâ€“40s would be of a different generation â€” possibly a sibling or cousin of Francis of Maldon (b. 1623), or a child of Francis the elder (b. 1581) through his first marriage to Margaret Ryvett (children John b. c.1610 and Anne b. c.1610 per Pease's Gurney descendants genealogy).

### 3. De Gournay Descent Through Norfolk Places
Burke asserts the following descent for the Norfolk Gurneys of Keswick (both 1850 and 1862 editions):

The Norfolk Gurneys descend from **Walter de Gournay**, a younger son of Gerard de Gournay (who married Editha de Warenne). Walter's line held, as mesne lords under the senior Gournay barons:
- **Runhall** (Norfolk)
- **Swathings-in-Hardingham** (Norfolk)
- **Hingham-Gurneys** (Norfolk)

The 1850 edition names "Sir William de Gournay, Knt., Lord of the manor of Runhall, in Norfolk, temp. Henry II, and of Swathings, in Hardingham, and Hingham" as the first named holder. The 1862 edition names Walter de Gournay (younger son of Gerard) as the ancestor, then William I and Matthew as next generations, with the manor of Harpley acquired by Matthew through his marriage to Rose de Burnham (given by Hameline Plantagenet, Earl Warren, c.1180).

The line then runs through John de Gurney I (Holy Land, 1270), William III, John III, John IV, Edmund de Gurnay (the recorder, married Katherine de Wauncy of West Barsham, d. 1385), Sir John V (d. 1408), Thomas I, Thomas II, William IV, William V, Anthony, Francis (b. 1521, d. in lifetime of father), Henry I (b. 1548, d. 1616), and then **Francis the 6th son** (b. 1581), whose son Francis of Maldon (b. 1623), whose son John of Norwich (b. 1655).

**Burke cites no specific authority** for this descent. He references:
- *Liber Sive Scaccarii*, by Hearne, Vol. I., p. 298 (for Walter de Gournay's period).
- Burke's own *Extinct Peerage* and *Heraldic Illustrations* (self-references).
- No specific primary source is cited for the connection of the Maldon line to the Norfolk/West Barsham senior line.

### 5. Link Between Maldon Gurneys and West Barsham/Norfolk
**Burke explicitly connects them.** The 1862 text traces the Maldon branch as a **cadet line** descending from Henry Gurnay I (b. 1548) of Great Ellingham and West Barsham â€” the same senior Norfolk line that held Runhall, Hardingham, Hingham, Swathing. Francis (b. 1581) is identified as the 6th son of Henry, who moved to London; his son Francis (b. 1623) established himself at Maldon, Essex; that Francis's son John (b. 1655) moved to Norwich.

**Burke does not explicitly link the Maldon branch back to the West Barsham line with a primary source citation.** It is an asserted descent within his own narrative. The claimed authority is the genealogical narrative structure itself, not a named external source for this specific link.

## Cautions on Burke as a Source
- Burke is a Victorian secondary compiler with a reputation for accepting family claims without rigorous source verification.
- No specific primary authority is cited for the crucial 16thâ€“17th century links (especially the Francis b. 1581 â†’ Francis of Maldon b. 1623 â†’ John b. 1655 chain).
- The "1541" OCR date for Francis's birth appears to be an OCR error for "1581" (consistent with Henry I being born 1548 and having a 6th son).
- The description "6th son" of Henry is consistent with other genealogical sources examined (Pease's Descendants of Anthony Gurney; Geni profiles).
- Burke's Hingham-Gurneys claim should be compared with the *Topographical History of Norfolk* (Blomefield) for primary corroboration.

## Record jurisdiction and county scope

<!-- Routed here from the 2026-07-01 massbay-campaign dump §0 "Scope map" per an [AG][DIRECTIVE]; a colonial-records orientation note for searchers, kept in the place layer rather than a person topic. -->

Which record series to search for a Weymouth resident depends on the county the town sat in, and that changed repeatedly:

- **Suffolk County (created 1643)** held Boston, Braintree, Weymouth, Hingham, Dorchester, Roxbury, and Dedham for the whole of John Gurney's colonial life. Every county-level court, deed, and probate record for his towns from 1643 to his death in 1662/3 is in the **Suffolk** series (Suffolk Deeds, Suffolk Probate, Suffolk County Court files) — the colonial spine already worked in the Gurney research. There is no separate 1640s–60s "Weymouth deeds" registry to hunt for.
- **"Old" Norfolk County (1643–1679)** was Salisbury, Haverhill, Hampton, and the towns north of the Merrimack — nothing to do with Weymouth or Braintree. Colonial "Norfolk County" hits for this family are a **false-friend trap**.
- **Modern Norfolk County (created 1793)** is the one that contains Weymouth, Braintree, Quincy, and Randolph today, so it is relevant only for post-1793 series — nineteenth-century cemetery and vital-record transcriptions, and the "Norfolk County" labels on sites such as Find a Grave.
- **Plymouth County / Plymouth Colony.** Hingham moved into Plymouth County only in 1803; in John's era Hingham was Suffolk. Plymouth **Colony** (a separate seventeenth-century jurisdiction) is where to look for Scituate matters and for the colony-boundary line just south of Weymouth and Hingham.
- **Before 1643** — John's first record is 1641 — there were no counties at all: the roll-up is the colony itself, the Massachusetts Bay General Court and Court of Assistants records and the Boston-centred notarial, church, and land books. That is why colony-level and Boston-level sweeps are the correct widening for the pre-1641 arrival window.

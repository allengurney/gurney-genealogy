## Research notes

**What we know.** John Gurney appears as **great lot no. 16** in the 2–3 February 1651–52 division of great lots, listed in Gilbert Nash's *Historical Sketch of Weymouth*, Appendix C (p. 282). The order describes the lots as "ordered to be laid out on the East side of [the] Pond next to Mrs Richards mill … which great lots are to but on Hingham line on the East and on the great lots on the west, with a sufficient drift-way between the two divisions." So John's lot lay in a strip running from the east side of the pond eastward to the Hingham town line, next to a mill.

**Which pond.** The pond is **Whitman's Pond**, which was called **Fresh Pond** when the town bounds were set in 1636 and took the Whitman name after John Whitman Jr. settled on it; the Bates Mill was built on it in 1640. It sits in East Weymouth near the Hingham line — the geography the grant describes. Nash's main text (p. 281) reads the pond as "Fresh Pond"; the *History of Weymouth* summary calls the Gurney grant "east side of Great Pond," conflating it with the larger, separately-named **South Weymouth** Great Pond reservoir (which is well west of the Hingham line and has no mill cluster). The Fresh-Pond / Whitman's-Pond reading is preferred here on the geography.<!-- lead L-182; source conflict: Nash p.281 "Fresh Pond" vs History of Weymouth "Great Pond" -->

**What we don't.** John's specific lot no. 16 within the strip is not individually surveyed, so the coordinate is a representative point east of the pond toward the Hingham line (medium precision).

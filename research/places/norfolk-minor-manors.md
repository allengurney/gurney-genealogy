# Minor Norfolk manors and landholdings

A bundled place file for Norfolk locations tied to the Gurney/Gournay family by a single deed, a settlement, or a monument — too thin on their own to carry the full weight of a dedicated place file, but real datapoints worth a place-centred summary and a fixed home in the place registry. Each entry below is deliberately short: the full genealogical argument for each finding already lives in a person or topic companion, linked from each section, and this file does not duplicate that argument or its citation trail — it gives the place-side context (location, tenurial history, why the place matters) that the person/topic files do not carry.

**Map-pin status.** None of the five places below has a confirmed manor-site pin. The coordinates registered in `data/places.json` are approximate parish/village centroids, not surveyed manor-site locations, and are flagged `coordinatePrecision: "low"` for that reason. Treat any map marker for these places as a general locality indicator only, not a specific-site claim.

## Letton

Letton is a small Norfolk parish adjoining Cranworth and Hardingham — the same neighbourhood as the Swathings manor above. Blomefield's Letton entry records one Gurney/Gurnay appearance: **"Hugh de Gurnay of Letton, granted them [Lewes Priory] a tenement, &c. sans date,"** in the Lewes Priory Manor portion of the parish.[^letton-blomefield] The same grant survives independently in the Ancient Deeds catalogue: deed **A.2972**, by which "Hugh de Gurnay of Letton" granted the church of St Pancras and the monks of Lewes, in frank almoin, the tenement Matilda daughter of Aliz held of him in Letton — 18d. yearly from land part on Wrongelond, part in Sudfeld, part in Humelescroft, and part on Cranworth field.[^letton-a2972]

**Why it matters.** The grant puts a senior-line forename ("Hugh") into exactly the Mitford-hundred corner where the junior Norfolk line held Swathings — it may be a senior-line Hugh holding this parcel directly, or an otherwise-unrecorded local Gurnay; the identity is carried as an open cadet-enfeoffment datapoint, not a resolved descent.[^letton-topic] A separate portion of Lewes Priory Manor's Letton holding passed, centuries later, to the **Gurdon family of Assington, Suffolk** — the same family behind Brampton Gurdon's 1627 letter to John Winthrop in the John Gurney (G13) wardship-network research — a coincidence of surname network, not a documented Gurney/Gurdon kinship, and not evidence of any Letton-to-Massachusetts connection.[^letton-gurdon]

Full analytical treatment: [`research/topics/anderson-yvery-harpetre-gournay-collateral.md`](../topics/anderson-yvery-harpetre-gournay-collateral.md) ("The Hugh de Gurnay of Letton datapoint"). Cross-linked from the G25–G28 junior-line companions.

## Larling

Larling (Lerling, Lurling, Lirling), in the hundred of Shropham, lies a few miles south-west of Great Ellingham on the old post road from London to Norwich.[^larling-blomefield] Anthony Gurney (G17) held a fraction of the parish — specifically **the manor of Welholme's, also called Easthall**, a division split from Gonville's manor centuries earlier — and settled it by feoffment in 1532: **"In 1532, Anthony Gurnay, Esq. settled the manor on John Tirrell, Esq. and others, and the same year John Heydon, Knt. and Catherine his wife, and Christopher Heydon, Knt. settled it on Sir Tho. Jermyn, Knt."**[^larling-welholme]

**Why it matters.** Welholme's tenurial chain runs through **William Wauncy** and the **Bardolphs**, holding of the Earl Warren — the same two families whose fees recur elsewhere in the Gurney story: the Wauncy inheritance brought West Barsham itself to the family in 1372, and the Bardolfs held the barony of which Swathings (above) was a fragment. Anthony's 1532 Larling holding therefore sits inside the same old Wauncy–Bardolph–Warenne tenurial fabric, by then reduced to a small fraction soon transferred out to the Heydon/Jermyn circle — consistent with the working Gurney–Heydon alliance of this generation. The feoffment names **John Tirrell/Turrell**, the same associate who served as Anthony's co-executor on Henry Spilman's 1524 Great Ellingham will.

Full treatment, including the Tirrell connection and Anthony's broader Shropham-hundred dealings: [`research/people/g17-anthony-gurney-fact-sheet.research.md`](../people/g17-anthony-gurney-fact-sheet.research.md) ("Anthony's manor of Larling, settled by feoffment in 1532").

## Booton

Booton is a small parish immediately south of Cawston (below). Ancient Deed **B.991** records a "Grant by Robert Mallard [and others], citizens of Norwich, to Robert Skynner of Causton, **William Gurnay of Boton** [Booton], and Thomas Halys of Causton, of a messuage at Kyrkgate … in Causton," dated 21 Edward IV (1481) — placing a Gurnay of enough standing to act as a Norwich-citizen grantee's transferee in the Cawston–Booton parishes a full century before the copyhold family documented in the seventeenth-century manorial courts.[^booton-b991]

Full treatment, including the open question of whether this 1481 Gurnay is the same cadet line as the 1578 Cawston monument below: [`research/people/gurney-family-cawston-manorial.md`](../people/gurney-family-cawston-manorial.md).

## Cawston

Cawston, 11 miles north of Norwich, is where an armigerous Gurney was buried in 1578: **"Here lieth the Body of William Gurney, Gent. March 10 1578, and Anne his Wife 19 January 1596, they had 1 Son and 3 Daughters,"** with arms **"Gurnay, arg. a cross ingrailed gul. with a crescent, impaling Wayte."**[^cawston-blomefield] The crescent is the heraldic difference for a second son, and *argent, a cross engrailed gules* is the West Barsham Gurney coat — identifying this William as an armigerous **cadet of the West Barsham line**, not an unconnected same-name townsman. A sustained but separate copyhold Gurney family (William & Ann, Emme, Agnes, and others) appears in the Cawston manorial courts from the 1640s into the 1750s; whether it descends from the 1578 cadet, or from the 1481 Booton Gurnay above, is an open question, not a proven line.

Full treatment, including the manorial-court entries and the cadet-to-copyhold open question: [`research/people/gurney-family-cawston-manorial.md`](../people/gurney-family-cawston-manorial.md). The monument is also cited from the G19 companion as evidence for the Walter-of-Cley cadet branch: [`research/people/g19-william-gurney-iv-fact-sheet.research.md`](../people/g19-william-gurney-iv-fact-sheet.research.md) ("Armstrong 1781 — 1500 Thuxton advowson presentation and the Cawston cadet-branch monument").

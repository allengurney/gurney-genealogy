# Harpley, Norfolk

Village in north-west Norfolk, England. Coordinates: **52.8088, 0.6486**.

The **ancestral Norfolk seat of the junior Gournay line** from c. 1183 (Matthew's marriage to Rose de Burnham) through the mid-15th century. Held continuously across approximately 250 years of Gurney lordship.

## Primary-source hooks

- **Manorial court roll 1354** — BL Add. MSS. 8841, fol. 112 (Harpley manor, John IV first court). Specific, citable British Library reference — worth pulling.
- **1294 conveyance deed** (William III to Rector John) — the seal on this deed is the earliest surviving physical impression of the Gournay engrailed cross. Described by DG but no archive reference given; may survive in NRO or a private collection. [DG-I-339–341]
- **1331 deed of Rector John** mentioning John III — likely in the same BL Additional Manuscripts 8841 collection.
- **1332 advowson presentation** — Blomefield, *History of Norfolk*, vol. viii, p. 455. Was it John III or John IV who presented? Worth resolving.
- **1357 settlement of 100 marks p.a.** from West Barsham and Denver on Edmund and Katherine by Sir William de Wauncy (31 Edw. III).

## St. Lawrence Church

Surviving parish church on the site the Gurneys patronized for generations. Notable medieval bench-end carvings survive (per Pevsner). The engrailed-cross arms appear carved in stone. John, Rector of Harpley, was buried in the chancel (d. 1332 per pedigree, though DG-I p. 279 gives 1333 — minor internal DG discrepancy).

The church is the natural hero-image anchor for G31–G22. Current structure postdates the earliest Gurney tenure (the present fabric is significantly 14th-century, some earlier elements) but stands on the site.

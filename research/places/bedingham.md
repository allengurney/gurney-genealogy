# Bedingham, Norfolk

Bedingham is a senior Gournay/Stuteville place rather than a direct junior Norfolk branch seat, but Blomefield makes it useful for the broader Gournay place network. He says Hugh de Gournay was lord there in Henry II's reign, and that in the beginning of King John's reign Hugh de Gournay, a Norman baron, gave it with Gunnora his daughter in marriage to Nicholas de Stuteville. Nicholas was later disseised as part of the lands of the Normans after the 1205 rebellion, and King John presented to the church during the seizure before the patronage was restored.[^blomefield-bedingham]

Blomefield's later descent says Nicholas de Castello and Christiana his wife settled the lordship on Nicholas their son and Margaret his wife, held of Lord Bardolf as heir to the Gournays.[^blomefield-bedingham]

Bedingham should therefore be treated as collateral senior-line place context: useful for Hugh de Gournay's Norfolk reach and for the post-senior-line Bardolf inheritance pattern, but not as a direct-line junior-branch manor unless later evidence ties it to Walter/William/Matthew's line.

[^blomefield-bedingham]: Francis Blomefield, "Loddon Hundred: Bedingham," in *An Essay Towards A Topographical History of the County of Norfolk*, vol. 10 (London, 1809), pp. 100-105, British History Online, https://www.british-history.ac.uk/topographical-hist-norfolk/vol10/pp100-105.

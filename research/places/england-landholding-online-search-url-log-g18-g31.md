## Purpose

This file records the online URLs and search approaches attempted during the G18–G31 England landholding-discovery work. The goal is **not** to restate the evidence narrative, but to help future research focus quickly on:

1. high-value source families and URLs;
2. promising leads that still need direct verification;
3. sources that were useful only as search indexes or hints;
4. searches/URLs that were inaccessible, inconclusive, or low-value.

## Caveat

This log reconstructs the online research trail from the chat thread, the created research files, and the URLs preserved in footnotes. It may not include every transient search-result URL returned by search engines. When no durable URL was retained, the item is recorded as a **search approach** rather than as a URL.

## Search strategies that worked best

### 1. Search by official list-entry source plus exact church/site name

Best pattern:

```text
Historic England "Church of St Giles" "Houghton St Giles"
Historic England "St Mary" Hellesdon "Richard de Heylesdon"
Historic England "Former Church of St James" Whitefriars Norwich
Historic England "All Saints" "East Barsham"
```

Why it worked: official sources often use formal building names and civil-parish names rather than the genealogical/manorial phrasing.

### 2. Use church names as anchors where manor names fail

Examples:

```text
"St James the Less" Pockthorpe archaeology
"St Mary" Hellesdon Heylesdon brass
"St Giles" "Houghton in the Dale"
"All Saints" "East Barsham"
"St Margaret" Drayton Norfolk church medieval
"St Mary" Denver Norfolk Historic England
```

Why it worked: churches survived and are better indexed than manor-house sites.

### 3. Use settlement-archaeology terms for lost places

Examples:

```text
"North Barsham" "deserted medieval village"
Pockthorpe "deserted medieval settlement"
Thuxton "deserted medieval village" "East Anglian Archaeology"
Letton "deserted medieval village"
```

Why it worked: some sites are indexed as DMVs or lost settlements rather than as manors.

### 4. Use formal record systems after a lead is found

Best escalation path:

1. broad web search / locality page identifies a lead;
2. Historic England / Heritage Gateway gives list entry, Hob UID, NGR, or record number;
3. HER / specialist report confirms archaeology/site interpretation;
4. repo source establishes Gurney/Gournay relationship.

### 5. Avoid using modern standing houses as proxies without continuity evidence

This matters most for:

- East Barsham Manor vs. Waldgraves;
- any modern hall near South Wootton;
- any modern Pockthorpe building vs. The Lathes / Hassets’ Hall;
- Denver later heritage features vs. medieval Wauncy/Gurney manor.

## Bottom-line guidance

For this project, the highest-value online sources are:

1. **Historic England / NHLE** — best for official listing names, grades, list-entry numbers, and NGRs.
2. **Heritage Gateway / HE Research Records** — best when archaeological watching briefs or Hob UIDs exist.
3. **Norfolk Heritage Explorer / NHER and Suffolk HER** — likely best next step for DMVs, settlement archaeology, and manor-site questions, though not successfully extracted in this thread.
4. **Open Domesday** — valuable for deep-place context and disambiguation, especially Houghton and Barsham cluster work.
5. **Specialist church sites** — likely high-value for narrative, architectural survival, images, and parish continuity.
6. **Wikipedia/locality pages** — useful as discovery indexes only; not final authority for catalog fields.

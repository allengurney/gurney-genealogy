## Research notes

**What we know.** The firm record is the 1683 town-meeting vote granting Richard six acres of the town common "on the west side of the Pond" (Great Pond), "to build a house & fence to dwell thereon with his family … in an absolute state of Inheritance" (*History of Weymouth*, p. 251, quoted in Rigler's Richard-2 entry). The 1691 death inventory — "A Dwelling house & barne with about six Acres of land adjoyning" — is almost certainly that same six-acre lot built up into a homestead, which fixes the place as a real, occupied house site.

**Which pond.** By 1683 "Great Pond" denotes the large **South Weymouth** pond of that modern name — distinct from John Sr.'s earlier (1642–51) grants, which clustered around **Whitman's Pond / Fresh Pond** in East Weymouth (the East Field, Mill Field, and the great lot east of Whitman's Pond). So Richard's homestead is a separate, southern locus from his father's holdings.

**What we don't.** The grant locates the lot to the *west side of [the South Weymouth] Great Pond* but not to a surveyed parcel, so the coordinate is a point on that shore (medium precision). Rigler adds a competing tradition that Richard settled "possibly on East Street towards Old Spain, and extending to Back River" — which is in **North** Weymouth, a third part of town. The readings are not reconcilable as a single spot; the west-side-of-Great-Pond grant is the firmer evidence and is used here, with the Old Spain alternative noted. Resolving which is correct is a research lead (Weymouth proprietors' land-grant records; abuttals in the 1683 grant and adjacent deeds). <!-- lead L-183 -->

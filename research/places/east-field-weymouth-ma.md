## Research notes

**What we know.** John Gurney Sr. held a two-acre grant "in the East field," recorded in Nash's **1643** property list under "the land of Robert Randoll" as a parcel "first granted to John Gurny" — so an original Gurney grant already passed to Randoll by 1643 — bounded east by the land of Edmond Hart and the highway, west by Robert Cooke, north by Nathaniel Adams (Nash, *Historical Sketch of Weymouth*, Appendix C, p. 258; a second East Field reference under Nathaniel Adams' land, p. 270, bounds another two-acre Gurney parcel by Jeffery Staple, Robert Randoll, a cedar swamp, and the highway). The East Field was one of early Weymouth's communal open-field arable divisions in the original North/East Weymouth settlement: neighboring lots are bounded "with the sea," the Back River, and "a greate pond" (Whitman's Pond), so the field lay east of the first settlement toward the water. Several of these neighbours were Buckinghamshire (Aylesbury-Vale) emigrants — Robert Randall of Wendover, and (in the adjacent Mill Field) William Brandon of Ashton Clinton — part of a documented Wendover/Lee → Dorchester → Weymouth cluster; see the cohort analysis in `research/topics/g13-john-gurney-immigration-by-association.md`.

**What we don't.** The bounds of the 17th-century East Field are not established from sources to hand, and the grant was "subsequently granted to others," leaving no later Gurney occupation to anchor it. The coordinate is a low-precision placement in the North/East Weymouth settlement area near the Back River. Locating it would require the Weymouth proprietors' land-grant book entries.

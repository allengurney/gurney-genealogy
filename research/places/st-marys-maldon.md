# St Mary's parish, Maldon, Essex, England

Ancient parish and church in the lower part of the town of Maldon, Essex, on the north bank of the River Blackwater where it widens into the Blackwater estuary. St Mary's is the riverside parish of Maldon; the church stands near the Hythe (the old quay) and serves the mercantile and maritime quarter of the borough, distinct from the larger civic parish of All Saints, Maldon (St Mary's, All Saints, and St Peter's together form the three historic Maldon parishes).

Approximate coordinate for structured display: **51.7307, 0.6820**.

## Why this parish matters to this project

St Mary's Maldon is a **documented cluster of Gurney activity in the mid-to-late seventeenth century**, with at least three distinct Gurney heads of household present there within a thirty-year window. Its relevance spans both the direct-line John Gurney-1 problem (`research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v4.md`) and the collateral Maldon branch descended from Francis G14.

Three Gurney/Gurney-adjacent houses of substantial size (nine hearths for the bachelor John, a separately-recorded Browning house, plus Francis Jr.'s rented merchant house on the Blackwater) were present at St Mary's Maldon across 1663/4 and 1674 — a concentration that merits a named place file in its own right.

### Research value for the direct line

Although no direct-line Gurney ancestor was based at St Mary's Maldon, the cluster matters for the G13 John Gurney-1 (Candidate B) case in several ways:

1. **It is a documented Gurney-surname cluster in the right social register and the right decade.** Any surviving Maldon document referring generically to "Mr Gurney" or "Mr Gournay" in the 1650s–1680s is a candidate for disambiguation across three known Gurney households. That discipline is worth carrying into any future Maldon-area record pull.
2. **It attaches a Browning/Gurney in-law network to a specific Essex town.** Anne Browning (Francis G14's second wife, per Bernau the daughter of William Browning "of Norwich, merchant, and later of Maldon") places a Browning mercantile household at Maldon by the 1620s–1630s. One generation down, the Browning intermarriages continue at Maldon through Francis Jr.'s 1654 marriage to Anne daughter of Jeremiah Browning, and possibly through the Dr. Thomas Browning / Anne Gurney hypothesis.[^browning-network] The Browning thread is the same surname that recurs in Coleman Street emigrant contexts in London, discussed in `research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v4.md` §10.4.
3. **It is the specific Maldon parish to search first** if evidence ever surfaces suggesting the direct-line John Gurney-1 had Maldon connections — e.g., if the Essex social-network pattern catalogued in `research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v4.md` §10.3 (son-in-law Daniel Shed of Finchingfield; landlord William Tyng of Stanford Rivers; Braintree MA named for Braintree Essex) turns out to trace specifically to Maldon rather than generically to Essex. The 1628 Francis Jr.'s settlement at St Mary's was after G13's likely 1638–1641 emigration, but if William Browning's branch of the family was at Maldon from the 1620s, a pre-emigration Browning-in-law visit to Maldon by the older brother John would be entirely plausible.

[^browning-network]: `research/people/g14-francis-gurney-fact-sheet.research.md` ("The Browning in-law network" subsection); `research/people/francis-gurney-of-maldon.md`, "Thomas Browning, D.D." section.

### Historical / topographical context

Per Bernau, Francis Jr.'s St Mary's house "was rented of the Corporation of that borough, in which he was a merchant" — it was a riverside tenement on the banks of the Blackwater, with direct access to the quay trade that his estate schedule documents (salt from Goldhanger salt-coats, coal by the hundred bushels, faggots, livestock, imported Purbeck fuller's earth and Portland stone).[^bernau-house] The position of the house on the river is the specific detail Bernau uses to argue against the suicide ruling: if Francis had intended self-destruction by drowning, the Blackwater flowed past his door; the fact that he died in the domestic well argues for accident or mental illness rather than deliberate self-destruction.[^bernau-house-argument]

[^bernau-house]: *Bernau 1913*, "His Death" section.
[^bernau-house-argument]: *Bernau 1913*, "His Death" section, paragraph on the position of the house.

## Primary register material

- **St Mary's Maldon parish register** — baptisms, marriages, and burials 1558 onward. The ten children of Francis Jr. of Maldon (1655–1667) were all baptized here; Francis Jr.'s own burial presumably entered here 12 August 1677 (Bernau does not print the burial line).
- **St Mary's communicants list** of 1664 — the source Bernau cites for Francis Jr.'s tenure as Bailiff of Maldon in 1664.

## Archive repositories

- Essex Record Office, Wharf Road, Chelmsford, CM2 6YT — holder of the Maldon parish registers and the Maldon Borough records (Corporation deeds including the 1662 Alderman appointment printed by Daniel Gurney at *Record* p. 539).
- The National Archives, Kew — Lay Subsidy 246/12 (1663/4 Hearth Tax) and 246/22 (1674 Hearth Tax) for Essex, both of which Bernau cites by reference number.

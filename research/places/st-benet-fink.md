# St Benet Fink, City of London, England

Parish in the Broad Street Ward, City of London. The parish church (St Benet Fink, also Finck) stood on the south side of Threadneedle Street at the corner of Finch Lane; it was destroyed in the Great Fire of 1666, rebuilt by Sir Christopher Wren 1670–1681, and demolished in 1844 to make way for the expansion of the Royal Exchange and the east wing of the Bank of England. The historic churchyard site now lies within the modern Bank of England complex.[^wikipedia-sbf]

Approximate historical coordinate for structured display: **51.5139, -0.0861**.

## Why this parish matters

St Benet Fink is **the primary London parish of Francis Gurney G14** during the documented residential years 1619–1637. The surviving parish register (London Metropolitan Archives, P69/BEN1/A/001 and P69/BEN1/A/002) contains the bulk of his children's baptisms. The 1633 or 1634 Heralds' Visitation of London (see the 1634-vs-1633 conflict documented in `research/people/g14-francis-gurney-fact-sheet.research.md`) captured the family at this parish.

Two independent extractions of the St Benet Fink children-list have been made so far and they disagree:

- The current **`fact-sheets/g14-francis-gurney-fact-sheet.md`** children table lists Dorothy 1619, Roger 1621, Anne c. 1624, Francis 13 Dec 1625, Anne (second) 14 Sept 1628, Margaret 10 Sept 1630, Deborah 21 Aug 1632, Elizabeth 10 Feb 1634/5, and Mary 19 Dec 1637.
- **Bernau, *The British Archivist* (1913)** lists Dorothy 1619, Roger 1621, Frances 18 Jan 1626 (female, buried 30 Sept 1626), Francis 13 Nov 1628 (male, of whom hereafter — the future Francis of Maldon), Lucretia 28 Oct 1630, Thomas 19 Apr 1636, Margaret 28 July 1637, plus a conjecturally-eldest Anne living in 1634, and a John born after 1634 who died a bachelor at Maldon in 1681.[^bernau-kids]

The two lists agree on Dorothy 1619 and Roger 1621 — both are firmly anchored by the 1633/34 Visitation — but from 1625 onward they diverge on names, dates, and sexes. This divergence cannot be resolved from derivative sources; it requires primary examination of the register.

[^bernau-kids]: Charles A. Bernau, "Unrecorded Biographies: Francis Gournay (or Gurney), of Maldon, Essex," *The British Archivist* vol. I, no. 7 (September 1913), pp. 49 ff., "His Parents' Children" section. Corpus extract at `sources/corpus_supplement/The_British_Archivist-Unrecorded-Biographies-Francis-Gurney.md`.

## Parish context in the emigration period

The parish sat in the Broad Street Ward. Immediately west lay the Coleman Street Ward, **London's most active Puritan emigration hub in the 1630s** — John Davenport preached at St Stephen Coleman Street until 1633, and the 1637 Hector voyage to New Haven was organized from that same ward. A William Gurney (identity unknown; possibly the William of PROB 11/252/152) appears in the 1641–42 Protestation Returns at St Stephen Coleman Street. The proximity of Francis G14's parish to this emigration infrastructure is a standing piece of context evidence in `research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v5.md` §10.4.[^colemans-st-context]

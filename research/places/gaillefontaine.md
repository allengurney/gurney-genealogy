# Gaillefontaine castle / domain

Gaillefontaine is one of the strongest high-priority additions because it is both a landholding / castle place and part of a named Gournay frontier triad.

## Why this place matters

Orderic Vitalis records that Gerard de Gournay delivered Gournay, La Ferté, and Gaillefontaine to William II Rufus in 1089/90. That makes Gaillefontaine a documented castle-place in Gerard's political and military world, not just a later title.

## Frontier geography

The heritage record for the Domaine de Gaillefontaine identifies the motte castrale as evidence of the site's importance in the Pays de Bray, on the old frontier between Normandy and the northern royal domain. This fits the broader Gournay pattern: fortified places guarding the eastern frontier and headwaters / valleys leading toward the royal sphere.

## What remains from the medieval frontier site

The strongest survival element is not the present château, but the motte castrale. The POP / Mérimée notice states that this motte testifies to the site's importance in the Pays de Bray, on the old frontier between Normandy and the northern royal domain.

The present château and domain should be treated separately in the narrative. POP describes the major visible building campaigns as 19th and early 20th century: park and outbuildings from 1867–1877, house and immediate surroundings from 1881–1899, and a funerary chapel completed in 1903.

## Gournay interpretation

For the Gournay library, Gaillefontaine matters because Orderic places it in the same defensive and political triad as Gournay and La Ferté. Gerard de Gournay delivered Gournay, La Ferté, and Gaillefontaine to William Rufus. The motte confirms that the site itself had a medieval fortified significance consistent with that record.

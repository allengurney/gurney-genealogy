## Passage 1 — original quadripartite estate division

### Explanation

This is the more important of the two references. The deed is not primarily *about* John Gurney; rather, it is a legal division of the estate of **William Ting** among his four daughters (and their husbands or marital interests). In that division, **Bethia Ting** and **Mercy Ting** are allotted **two Braintree messuages or tenements**, and the deed specifies their then-current occupants. One was in the hands of **Gregory Belcher**; the other was in the hands of **John Gurney**.

So this passage establishes that, at the time of this recorded indenture, **John Gurney was occupying, by lease, one of the two Braintree properties assigned to Bethia and Mercy Ting**. The wording strongly implies a tenant or leaseholder relationship, not ownership.

## Passage 2 — later agreement regulating the same Braintree farms  
**PDF pages 120–123; printed pages [89]-[90a]-[91]**

### Verbatim extract

> This Indenture made the Sixteenth day of December, in  
> the fowerteenth yeare of the reigne of Soveraigne Lord  
> Charles the Second &c Betweene Richard Wharton of Boston  
> in New England Gentleman and Bethia his wife one of the  
> Daughtᵉʳ of Wm Ting Late of Boston aforesaid Merchant de-  
> ceased on the one part And Samuell Bradstreete of Boston  
> aforesaid Gentleman & Mercy his wife another of the Daugh-  
> tᵉʳ of the Said Wm Ting on the other part Wittnesseth, that  
> Whereas by an Indenture Quadrupartite bearing date the  
> Eight and twentieth day of March One thousand Sixe hun-  
> dred Sixtie & one Thomas Brattle of Boston aforesaid  
> merchant & Elizabeth his Wife Eldest Daughter of the said  
> Wm Ting on the first pt and Thomas Shepard of Charles-  
> towne Clark & Ann his wife Second daughter to the said Wm  
> Ting on the Second part, & Bethia Ting now wife to the  
> Said Richard Wharton, & third Daughter of the Said William  
> Ting on the third part & Mercy Ting now wife to the said  
> Samuell Bradstreete & youngest Daughter of the said  
> Wm Ting on the fowerth part, did mutually Covenant &  
> agree vpon a devision of the Estate of the Said William Ting,  
> ... And that they the said Bethia  
> and Mercy, should to them theire heyres & assignes, foreuer  
> haue & hold two messuages or tenements in Braintree aforesaid  
> together with all houses outhouses Edifices Buildings Barnes  
> Stables yards, Lands Meadowes Pastures feedings Commons  
> Commodities profits priviledges & appurtenances whatsoeuer to the  
> said Messuages or tenements **(which by Lease are in yᵉ posses-  
> sion of Gregory Belcher and John Gurney)** doth or may by  
> any right belong or appertaine, wᶜʰ the said Bethja & Mercy  
> accepted as theire part & portions of the Estate devided by  
> & expressed in the afore recited Indenture vpon the Con-  
> sideration therein expressed And whereas the two Last men-  
> tioned tenements or farmes granted to the said Bethja and Mercy  
> are yet vndevided, neither is any Legall or formall act deede  
> or acknowledgeᵐᵗ made past or done, whereby to Evidence  
> how farr the pticular Interests of the said Bethja and Mercy,  
> doe in the premisses Extend, And whereas a present devision  
> would be very Injurious to the Improvement thereof, Now it  
> is Covenanted, Concluded & Mutually agreed by all the  
> parties to these present Indentures That they the said Richard  
> Wharton & Bethja his wife theire heyres executors Admᵣs &  
> assignes for euer shall & May to theire proper vse and be-  
> hoofe Have hold occupy possesse & enjoy as an Estate in fee  
> simple one halfe of all the Estate, Granted to the said Bethja  
> and Mercy By the afore recited deed ...  
>  
> ... And it is likewise  
> Covenanted Concluded & agreed, by all the parties herevnto,  
> that each of them will vpon the request of the other, or any  
> of theire heyres Executors Admᵣs or Assignes proceed to the  
> devision of the premisses, & at theire Joynt & equall Charge  
> devide allott, and Lay out the said two farmes & premisses  
> into two Parts of equall value ...  
>  
> ... In wittnesse whereof, the parties herevnto have Inter-  
> changeably set theire hands & scales, the day & yeare first  
> above written being Anno Domini One thousand sixe hundred  
> sixtie and two  
> Samuell Bradstreet & a scale  
> Mercy Bradstreete & a scale

### Explanation

This second reference is a **follow-up deed or agreement** that explicitly points back to the earlier quadripartite division. Its purpose is not to create the original division, but to regulate the ownership relationship between **Richard Wharton and Bethia** on one side and **Samuel Bradstreet and Mercy** on the other.

The key point is that it again identifies the same **two Braintree tenements/farms** as being **“by Lease” in the possession of Gregory Belcher and John Gurney**. The document then explains why the parties are making this new agreement: although Bethia and Mercy had previously accepted those two Braintree farms as their collective portion of the Ting estate, the properties had **not yet been formally partitioned between them**. Because an immediate physical division would be “very Injurious to the Improvement thereof,” they agree on rules for possession, leasing, alienation, and eventual partition.

This makes the second passage especially useful, because it confirms that **John Gurney’s leased occupation was not an incidental one-off mention**; it was still relevant when the co-heirs later formalized how the two Braintree farms were to be held and eventually divided.

## Overall interpretation

Taken together, the two passages indicate:

- **John Gurney was in Braintree** at the relevant time.
- He was **holding one of two Braintree Ting-estate properties by lease**, not as owner of record in these particular instruments.
- The first passage shows him as the current occupant/lessee when the estate was first divided among the Ting daughters.
- The second passage shows that the same leased occupancy remained part of the legal description when the co-heirs later entered into a further agreement about partition, possession, and future alienation of those properties.

In genealogical terms, these are **context-rich tenancy references** rather than direct family-relationship statements. They are still important, because they place **John Gurney in Braintree, connected to a specific estate and specific leased premises, in the early 1660s**.

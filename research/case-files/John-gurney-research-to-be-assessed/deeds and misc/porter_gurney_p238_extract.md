## Explanation / analysis

This is not a standalone biographical sketch of John Gurney. Instead, it is an incidental but useful notice embedded in Joseph W. Porter’s account of **John Porter of Hingham and Salem**.

The key evidentiary value of the passage is this:

1. **It places John Gurney in Weymouth by 2 June 1641.** The author expressly glosses the quoted record with: **“Ludden and Gurney were of Weymouth.”**
2. **It connects John Gurney to a formal colonial enforcement matter involving required military readiness or equipment.** The fine was incurred “for want of gunpowder,” then later remitted by the General Court.
3. **It shows Gurney appearing in company with James Ludden and John Porter**, but the narrative focus remains on Porter. So the passage supports John Gurney’s presence and civic/military-regulatory exposure in the period, but does **not** on its own establish broader details such as age, family, landholding, or office.

In practical genealogy terms, this is a **small but solid contextual datapoint**: it confirms an early **John Gurney of Weymouth** in 1641, cited within a secondary genealogy that appears to be drawing on town and court material concerning John Porter.

## Caution on interpretation

Because the passage is a compiled secondary-source narrative rather than a full transcription of the underlying court record, it is best used as:

- a **locational and date clue** for John Gurney,
- a pointer to a likely **General Court / Hingham-related original record**, and
- supporting context rather than a sole proof for any larger identity conclusion.

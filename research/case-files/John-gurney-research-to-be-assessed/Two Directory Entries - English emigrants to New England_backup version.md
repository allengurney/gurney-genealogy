## EXTERNAL ASSESSMENTS: WHAT PUBLISHED COMPILERS SAY

Two major compilations of English emigrants to New England address John
Gurney-1. They reach different conclusions, and the contrast is informative.

### Charles Edward Banks / Elijah Ellsworth Brownell (1937)

Banks's *Topographical Dictionary of 2885 English Emigrants to New England,
1620-1650*, edited posthumously by Brownell and published in March 1937,
assigns John Gurney to **Bury St. Edmunds, Suffolk**, with destination
Braintree, Massachusetts. The source given is "Banks Mss." — Banks's own
unpublished working notes. No ship, parish register, will, or
apprenticeship indenture is cited.

The attribution appears on p. 151 within a dense cluster of Bury St. Edmunds
emigrants that includes John Newgate, Clement Chaplin, William Knapp, John
Goodrich, Henry Bright, William Townsend, Theodore Atkinson, Peyton Cooke,
and Samuel Wright — a genuine Bury-area migration group, most of them well
documented from primary sources. Banks was perceiving a real neighborhood
pattern and placed Gurney in it.

**Assessment:** A lead rather than a proof. An entry sourced only to "Banks
Mss." carries no primary citation and cannot be weighed against registered
records. However, Banks's cluster judgment is worth taking seriously, and
the attribution is most easily explained by an apprenticeship record of a
"John Gurney/Girney" at Bury St. Edmunds in the 1620s or 1630s — consistent
with, rather than contradictory to, Candidate B. A Norwich-born son of
Francis Gurney who served out a Bury St. Edmunds apprenticeship would be
exactly the person Banks's note would describe.

This attribution weakens Candidate A (Stewkley, Bucks) more than it weakens
Candidate B: Banks points firmly into East Anglia, not into Buckinghamshire.

*Citation:* Charles Edward Banks, *Topographical Dictionary of 2885 English
Emigrants to New England, 1620-1650*, ed. Elijah Ellsworth Brownell
(Philadelphia: Bertram Press, 1937), p. 151 (Suffolk — Bury St. Edmunds).

### Robert Charles Anderson, *The Great Migration Directory* (2015)

The modern standard compilation lists the entry as:

> **Gurney, John:** Unknown; 1636; Boston, Braintree [WJ 2:422; MBCR 1:331;
> NEHGR 62:94; SPR Case #338; Weymouth Hist 3:251; TAG 10:70-73].

Three features of this entry matter.

First, Anderson gives the English origin as **"Unknown."** Anderson worked
with full access to Banks and routinely cites Banks-supported origins when
the underlying evidence holds up. His "Unknown" is an implicit judgment
that Banks's Bury St. Edmunds attribution is insufficient for publication
under modern Great Migration standards. It is not a rejection — Anderson
is cautious with negatives — but it is a clear signal that no primary
record has surfaced to prove any specific parish.

Second, Anderson gives the arrival year as **1636**, earlier than the
June 1641 Weymouth record that has been treated as John's first Massachusetts
appearance in this case file. Reconciling this discrepancy is now a
priority. The most likely source of the 1636 date is TAG 10:70-73 (Clarence
Almon Torrey or a successor in *The American Genealogist*); it should be
pulled and evaluated.

Third, Anderson's listed settlements are **Boston and Braintree only** —
Weymouth is not credited as a settlement. This may reflect a judgment that
the 1641 Weymouth record is transient, or it may reflect an unexamined
conflict between two John Gurneys. Either way, it warrants checking.

*Citation:* Robert Charles Anderson, *The Great Migration Directory:
Immigrants to New England, 1620-1640, A Concise Compendium* (Boston: New
England Historic Genealogical Society, 2015), p. 158.

Both compilers examined the problem, neither produced primary evidence for

context (Anderson's listed sources are all East Anglian and Massachusetts
Bay records) — into the East Anglian rather than the Buckinghamshire

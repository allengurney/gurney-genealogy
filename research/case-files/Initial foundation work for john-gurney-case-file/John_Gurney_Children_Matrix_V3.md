### Critical Observation: The Absence of "Francis"
John-1 named no child Francis. In this period, naming eldest son after paternal grandfather was near-universal. If Francis of London was John-1's father, the absence of "Francis" among the children (Richard, John Jr., Peter) is the strongest single naming-pattern argument against Candidate B.

### ★ The Peter Hypothesis
Peter is foreign to every documented Gurney family. It almost certainly comes from **the wife Mary's family** — a maternal grandfather, uncle, or godfather named Peter. Identifying Mary's maiden name and finding a "Peter" in her family is potentially the key to identifying John-1's English origin. The name "Peter" is the strongest forensic fingerprint we have.

## Summary (V3)

**Berkhamsted (Candidate C): 2/5 matches** (Richard 1626, Sarah 1634). No disqualifying evidence. No post-1640 children found. Wife unknown. ~30 miles from London. PRIORITY: search marriage registers and remaining baptisms. Currently ~3-5% but could rise dramatically with additional matches.

**Buckinghamshire (Candidate A): ~8-10%.** ★ 1642 tax list places John Gurney at Edlesborough and Isaac Gurney at Cheddington. Isaac Sr. (1596-1644) fathered John (1634) and Isaac II (1638). VCH confirms John Gurney held Stewkley manor by 1687 with son Isaac, sold 1701. Father/son ambiguity prevents elimination, but deep roots in Bucks argue against emigration.

**Candidate B (Francis of London): ~38-42%.** ★ Naming pattern analysis weakens the case. No child named Francis. Sarah and Peter are Puritan/biblical, not Norfolk gentry names. Peter is completely absent from all Gurney families. The occupational and geographic circumstantial case remains strong but the children's names don't support it.

**★ Eythorne (Kent): ELIMINATED.** Full pedigree confirmed.

**★ Key Forensic Insight:** The name PETER is the single most distinctive clue. It doesn't match any Gurney family. It likely comes from wife Mary's family. Finding Mary's maiden name — specifically a family with a "Peter" — is the most promising path to identification.

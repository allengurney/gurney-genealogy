The GenGuide reference to "City of London Rolls held in the City of London Sessions records" at LMA is the probable source.

## Critical Finding 1: No John Gurney in Buckinghamshire

The Stewkley parish returns are well-represented, with five Gurneys documented there: Anthony, Robert (x2 — one as Hundred official), Thomas, and Walter. Additionally, Gurneys appear at Linslade (Ezekiel, Thomas x2), Ivinghoe (Thomas), Soulbury (Thomas), Marsh Gibbon (Richard), Chedington (Isaac), and Mentmore (Thomas).

**But NO JOHN appears anywhere in Buckinghamshire.**

This is consistent with our prior evidence:
- TNA E 115/180/113 (1641): Certificate of residence for John Gurney moving from Aylesbury half-hundred to Northamptonshire
- 1650: John Gurney tenant at Walgrave, Northamptonshire
- Jonathan Gurney baptized Aylesbury 22 Nov 1647 — a John still fathering children, but at Aylesbury not Stewkley

The Buckinghamshire John(s) had left Stewkley by early 1642. Either both relocated to Northamptonshire, or one emigrated.

**Robert Gurney is still alive at Stewkley in 1642.** This weakens the "adoption hypothesis" that John Jr. adopted Robert's son Richard before emigrating — Robert was alive and present.

**Impact on Candidate A:** The absence does NOT help Candidate A — it's equally consistent with John moving to Northants (which the 1641 certificate and 1650 tenancy confirm) as with emigration. Combined with the Jonathan 1647 baptism counter-evidence, Candidate A remains weak at ~10-12%.

## Critical Finding 2: William Gurney at St Stephen Coleman Street

**Location:** Coleman Street Ward, City of London — immediately adjacent to Broad Street Ward where St Benet Fink (Francis Gurney's parish) is located. The two parishes are separated by a few hundred yards.

**Significance of the parish:**
St Stephen Coleman Street was one of the most intensely Puritan congregations in London:
- Vicar **John Davenport** (to 1633) — later founded New Haven Colony, Connecticut
- Successor **John Goodwin** (from 1633) — prominent Puritan preacher, ejected 1645
- Parishioner **Sir Isaac Penington** — Puritan Lord Mayor 1642-43 (replaced the Royalist Sir Richard Gurney, who was from Kendall, Westmorland, NOT the Norfolk Gurneys — per Daniel Gurney App. LXXXIX)
- The Five Members of Parliament hid at Coleman Street in January 1642 after Charles I attempted their arrest
- During Commonwealth, communion restricted to committee-approved parishioners; two committee members had signed Charles I's death warrant

**William Gurney's identity is unknown.** He could be:
- A son of Francis Gurney (unrecorded — Francis had "several children" per Daniel Gurney, but Burke names only Roger, Francis of Maldon, Thomas)
- A nephew or other relative within the London Gurney merchant network
- An unrelated Gurney from a different line

**Research action:** Search St Stephen Coleman Street parish registers (survive from 1538, held at London Metropolitan Archives) for any Gurney entries — baptisms, marriages, burials. The registers are available via Ancestry (London, England, Baptisms, Marriages, and Burials, 1538-1812).

## Critical Finding 3: Abel Gurney at St Pancras Soper Lane

**Location:** Cheap Ward, City of London — the heart of Cheapside, about 300 meters south of Coleman Street Ward.

**Parish context:** St Pancras Soper Lane was a small but comfortably wealthy parish in the commercial center of London. Parish register records survive. Parish assessments 1628-1642 are available online through the School of Advanced Study, University of London.

**Abel is an unusual name** for a Gurney. It does not appear in any branch of the Norfolk Gurney family documented by Daniel Gurney. This may indicate he is from a different Gurney line entirely, or it may reflect Puritan naming practice (Abel being a biblical name favored by nonconformists).

**Research action:** Check the published registers — *The Registers of St. Mary Le Bowe, Cheapside, All Hallows, Honey Lane, and of St. Pancras, Soper Lane, London* (Harleian Society Vol. 45, 1915) — for Abel Gurney entries. Also check the parish assessments 1628-1642 for Abel Gurney's taxable property.

## Wider Observations

### The Buckinghamshire Gurney Cluster
The returns reveal a substantial Gurney population across multiple Buckinghamshire parishes, all in Cotteslow Hundred or nearby:
- **Stewkley:** Anthony, Robert (x2), Thomas, Walter — 5 adults
- **Linslade:** Ezekiel, Thomas (x2 — one as official) — 3 adults
- **Ivinghoe:** Thomas
- **Soulbury:** Thomas
- **Marsh Gibbon:** Richard
- **Chedington:** Isaac
- **Mentmore:** Thomas (Gurny)
- **Marsworth:** Richard Gurne (x2)

This is at least 15 adult male Gurneys in Buckinghamshire in 1642 — a deeply rooted local family. The frequency of "Thomas" (7 entries!) suggests multiple branches.

### No Gurney in Norfolk, Suffolk, or Essex
This reflects survival, not absence. Norfolk and Suffolk returns don't exist. Essex coverage is negligible. We know from other records that Francis of Maldon was alive in Essex and the laceweaver Francis was in Norwich — their absence tells us nothing.

### Sir Richard Gurney, Lord Mayor — Not Related
Daniel Gurney's Appendix LXXXIX confirms: "Sir Richard Gurney was not related to the Norfolk family." The 1634 Heralds' Visitation records him as "Richard Gurny, alias Gurnard, descended from the Gurneys of Kendall" — a Westmorland family using a different surname (Gurnard). His son died under age; his daughter Elizabeth married Sir John Pettus; daughter Ann married Lord Richardson. The line ended. This Gurney is irrelevant to our research.

## Impact on Candidate B Probability

The Protestation Returns add several threads to the Candidate B case:

**Strengthening factors:**
1. William Gurney at St Stephen Coleman Street places a Gurney in a radical Puritan parish adjacent to Francis's St Benet Fink — exactly the religious-geographic milieu that produced Massachusetts emigrants
2. John Davenport preached at St Stephen's until 1633, then organized his New England venture — this parish was a hub for emigration planning
3. The absence of Francis from the returns is explained by the non-survival of City of London returns, not by Francis's absence from London
4. No John Gurney in Buckinghamshire confirms that Stewkley John had left by 1642

**Neutral factors:**
1. William Gurney's identity and relationship to Francis (if any) is entirely unknown
2. Abel Gurney's presence doesn't clearly connect to any known Gurney line

**Revised probability distribution:**
- Unknown origin: ~44%
- Candidate B (Francis of London's son): ~40-42% *(slight increase — Coleman Street Puritan connection)*
- Candidate A (Stewkley, Bucks): ~10-12% *(unchanged — absence from returns is ambiguous)*
- Candidate C (Berkhamsted, Herts): ~3-5% *(unchanged)*

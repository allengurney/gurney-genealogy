### 1.2 NEW — The Coleman Street Neighborhood Network

Francis Gurney's parish of St. Benet Fink was in Broad Street Ward, City of London. The adjacent Coleman Street Ward contained St Stephen Coleman Street — one of London's most intensely Puritan congregations. These parishes were separated by a few hundred yards.

**Key figures in the immediate neighborhood:**

| Person | Location | Significance |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| **Theophilus Eaton** | "Between Coleman Street and Lothbury Street" — adjacent to St Benet Fink | Co-founder of New Haven Colony; wealthy London merchant; organized 1637 emigration on ship Hector |
| **Rev. John Davenport** | Vicar, St Stephen Coleman Street (1624-1633) | Co-founder of New Haven Colony; took parishioners to New England 1637 |
| **Rev. John Goodwin** | Vicar, St Stephen Coleman Street (from 1633) | Prominent Puritan preacher |
| **Sir Isaac Penington** | Parishioner, St Stephen Coleman Street | Puritan Lord Mayor 1642; replaced Royalist Sir Richard Gurney (NOT related to Norfolk Gurneys — per DG App. LXXXIX, descended from Gurneys of Kendall) |
| **William Gurney** | Parishioner, St Stephen Coleman Street | **Protestation Returns 1641-42. Identity unknown. See §1.3 below.** |

**St Stephen Coleman Street emigrants to New Haven (1637):**
Named parishioners who sailed with Davenport: Nathaniel Rowe, William Andrews, **Henry Browning**, James Clark, Jasper Crane, Jeremy Dixon, Nicholas Elsey, Francis Hall, Robert Hill, William Ives, George Smith, George Ward, Lawrence Ward. Plus ~20 more "from the neighborhood."

**The Henry Browning connection:** Francis Gurney's wife was Anne Browning, daughter of William Browning of Norwich. A "Henry Browning" appears as a St Stephen Coleman Street parishioner who emigrated with Davenport to New Haven, where he was assigned to Square 3 of the town plan. Whether this Henry is related to Anne's family is unknown but merits investigation.

**Significance:** Francis Gurney's household was immersed in exactly the London Puritan merchant networks that organized and executed emigration to New England. His neighbors were leaving for America. His wife's surname appears among the emigrants. The same infrastructure that sent 250 people to New Haven on the Hector in 1637 was available to any Gurney son contemplating passage to Massachusetts.

### 1.4 The Francis-to-John Gap — THE CRITICAL UNSOLVED PROBLEM

The circumstantial case for Candidate B has grown steadily stronger across three research sessions: occupational match, geographic corridor, Puritan family, 1634 land sale, Essex social network, and now the Coleman Street emigrant neighborhood. **But the single biggest weakness remains unchanged: there is no documented link between Francis Gurney of London and John Gurney-1 of Braintree.**

**What would constitute proof or near-proof:**
- A baptism: "John son of Francis Gurney" at any parish, c.1600-1610
- A marriage record: Francis Gurney marrying anyone before Anne Browning
- An apprenticeship record: "John Gurney, son of Francis Gurney, Merchant Taylor"
- A will: Francis naming a son John, or John naming his father Francis
- A deposition or legal record linking the two by name
- An emigration record: "John Gurney, tailor, of London" on any ship passenger list

**What has been searched without result:**
- FamilySearch "England Births and Christenings" index — no John son of Francis Gurney at any London parish, 1600-1618
- St. Benet Fink register — starts 1619; John born c.1603 predates it
- No ship passenger list found bearing John Gurney (Banks Mss. notes "no ship")
- PCC will indexes — not yet systematically searched for Francis Gurnay

**What has NOT yet been searched:**
1. **Norwich parish registers 1606-1618** — Francis was "occasionally resident at Norwich." If he married a first wife there and had children, the record would be in Norwich, not London. ~30 parishes to check. Ancestry "Norfolk, England, Church of England Baptism, Marriages, and Burials, 1535-1812" covers most.
2. **London Apprenticeship Abstracts** — Findmypast. If John was apprenticed to his father or another Merchant Taylor, the binding record would name his father.
3. **PCC will indexes 1637-1660** — Francis's will (if proved at PCC rather than diocesan court) might name all children including a son John.
4. **Archdeaconry of Norwich wills** — if Francis died in Norfolk/Norwich, his will would be here.
5. **St Peter Le Poor, London** — Daniel Gurney notes "a John Gurney living in 1696" at this neighboring parish to St Benet Fink. Could be a grandson of Francis.
6. **St Stephen Coleman Street registers** — the unidentified William Gurney of 1642 may yield clues.
7. **Great Migration Directory 2nd Ed. (2025)** — Robert Charles Anderson's entry for John Gurney may state a position on origin.

### 2.2 Buckinghamshire — No John Gurney

At least 15 adult male Gurneys appear in Buckinghamshire: Stewkley (Anthony, Robert x2, Thomas, Walter), Linslade (Ezekiel, Thomas x2), Ivinghoe (Thomas), Soulbury (Thomas), Marsh Gibbon (Richard), Chedington (Isaac), Mentmore (Thomas), Marsworth (Richard Gurne x2). **But NO JOHN anywhere.**

Combined with:
- TNA E 115/180/113 (1641): John Gurney moving from Aylesbury half-hundred to Northamptonshire
- Jonathan Gurney baptized Aylesbury 22 Nov 1647 (father John — a John still fathering children in Bucks 6 years after Braintree John's first MA record)
- 1650: John Gurney tenant at Walgrave, Northamptonshire

**Conclusion:** The Stewkley John left Buckinghamshire by 1642, almost certainly relocating to Northamptonshire. The Protestation Returns confirm but do not further resolve whether the Braintree John came from Stewkley or elsewhere. Candidate A remains at ~10-12%.

**Robert Gurney alive at Stewkley 1642:** This weakens the "adoption hypothesis" that John Jr. adopted Robert's son Richard before emigrating — Robert was alive and present when the oath was administered.

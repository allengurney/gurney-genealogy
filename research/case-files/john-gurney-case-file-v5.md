<h2 id="s1">1. PROBLEM STATEMENT: WHO WAS JOHN GURNEY BEFORE ARRIVING IN MASSACHUSETTS</h2>

John Gurney was an English emigrant who settled in Massachusetts Bay Colony circa 1640. His English origin has never been definitively established. No known published authority including <a href="https://www.americanancestors.org/robert-charles-anderson-fasg-0">Robert Charles Anderson</a>'s *Great Migration Directory* (2025), which lists his origin as "Unknown", has identified his parents or home parish.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n1" id="ref-1">1</a></sup>

**Key criteria for matching:** Any English candidate must plausibly account for a tailor named John Gurney, married to a woman named Mary, with children including Sarah, Mary, Richard, John, and Peter, born in England c.1600–1610, who disappeared from English records by c.1641.

### The Central Question

<div class="discovery">Who was John Gurney before he appeared in Massachusetts in 1641? Where in England was he born, who were his parents, and why did he emigrate?

<h2 id="s2">2. THE CANDIDATE: JOHN GURNEY, SON OF FRANCIS GURNEY AND MARGARET RYBETT</h2>

This case file argues that John Gurney was the son of **Francis Gurney**, a Merchant Taylor of Norfolk and London, by his first wife Margaret Rybett. The case rests on two primary source discoveries and a web of other evidence:

| Evidence Type | What It Shows |
|---|---|
| **★ Margaret Rybett marriage** (NRO PD 12/1) | **New Discovery** -- Francis Gurney had a first wife, married 1611 — children from this marriage would be born c.1609–1617, the right generation for the emigrant |
| **★ John Gurney baptism** (NRO PD 86/41) | **New Discovery** -- A baptism record being read as "John son of ffrancis Gurnie" at East Dereham, c.1609/10 — re-read from an entry previously indexed as "Nicholas Gorne". |
| **Occupational match** | John the immigrant was a tailor. No English record shows the East Dereham John's occupation, but his father Francis was a Merchant Taylor, and in this era sons frequently carried forward their father's trade — though not, in John's case, through any formal London or Norwich admission that survives (see §10.1). Of the other named candidates, only Candidate D shares a textile-trade link, and Candidate D stays in London (see §8.4) so is not a match. |
| **Birth/age** | Born c.1607–12 — six to eight years younger than a literal reading of the 1653 "aged about 50" deposition (c.1602/3). The early edge of the baptism window (c.1607) narrows the gap to four or five years and is also the reading most consistent with John's c.1627 marriage (see §10.1). |
| **Emigration corridor** | Francis lived in precisely the Norfolk → London geography that produced the Great Migration |
| **Financial motive** | John's father Francis sold ALL his lands in 1634, leaving elder sons with no land to expect |
| **Puritan connections** | John Gurney's uncle Edmund (Francis's brother) living in the area was a militant Puritan clergy; Francis's London parish adjoined the Coleman Street emigration hub |
| **Process of elimination** | Every other identified English-born John Gurney of the right era has been eliminated or reduced to low probability as the emigrant — by continuing English residence, a non-matching wife or children, or a death in England — leaving no competing candidate who fits the colonial signature (see §8).<sup class="fn"><a href="#n60" id="ref-60h">60</a></sup> |

No other identified candidate matches more than one or two of the criteria. Candidate B matches more of them than any other identified candidate — trade lineage, corridor, motive, and network — with the 1653 age statement the principal unresolved tension.

### 2.1 Who is Francis Gurney of West Barsham, Norwich, and London

Francis Gurney (b. 13 September 1581, West Barsham Hall, Norfolk) was the sixth son of Henry Gurnay, Esq., of Great Ellingham and West Barsham, by Ellen Blennerhassett his wife.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n11" id="ref-11">11</a></sup> Henry had twelve children; the family was ancient Norfolk gentry, but by the late sixteenth century the younger sons had limited prospects.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n12" id="ref-12">12</a></sup>

Francis was bound apprentice in London on 14 May 1599, aged about seventeen, to Henry Tryme of the Merchant Taylors' Company "Near Ludgate," for a seven-year term beginning at Whitsun 1599. On 3 February 1605 the Company Court ordered him assigned over to William Smooth, Merchant Taylor of Lothbury, ahead of a documented multi-month journey into the north — the earliest concrete trace of his Norfolk re-engagement, six years before his September 1611 Norwich marriage to Margaret Rybett.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n13" id="ref-13">13</a></sup> The Merchant Taylors' Company binding-book records his admission to the freedom of the Company on 30 June 1606.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n14" id="ref-14">14</a></sup>

Genealogist <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Gurney">Daniel Gurney</a> noted that Francis's "commercial life began at Norwich."<sup class="fn"><a href="#n15" id="ref-15">15</a></sup> From an ancient account-book at Hunstanton Hall, Francis served as "a sort of agent, or banker, for the Lestranges," with payments documented from January 1612 through May 1636.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n16" id="ref-16">16</a></sup>

### 2.2 The First Marriage: Margaret Rybett (★ Primary Source Discovery)

<div class="discovery">
    ★ <strong>PRIMARY SOURCE DISCOVERY:</strong> On 23 September 1611, "Franc Gurny & Margaret Rybett" were married at St Martin at Palace, Norwich, Norfolk.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n17" id="ref-17">17</a></sup>


This marriage record, discovered in March 2026 in the Norfolk Record Office parish register PD 12/1, is one of the most important primary source finding of this research project. Genealogist Daniel Gurney, despite decades of family research in the 1800's, never found this record. The discovery confirms:

1. Francis had a first wife before Anne Browning — resolving the unexplained thirteen-year gap between his freedom (1606) and the first recorded child at St. Benet Fink, London (1619).
2. The marriage was at Norwich, in the geographic corridor of Francis Gurney's family and strong alignment with genealogist Daniel Gurney's statement about Francis's Norwich commercial origins.
3. The Pease genealogy's claim of a "Margaret Ryvett" first marriage, transmitted from an American researcher (Philis Wainford) through Sir Joseph Gurney Pease, Bt., is now validated by primary source evidence.

</div>
<sup class="fn"><a href="#n18" id="ref-18">18</a></sup>

**The Rybett/Ryvett family** were established Norfolk and Suffolk gentry, documented at Fritton (Norfolk), Rishangles, Rattlesden, Stowmarket, and Bildeston (all Suffolk).<sup class="fn"><a href="#n19" id="ref-19">19</a></sup> The Heydons of Baconsthorpe were directly connected to the Gurneys through Anne Heydon's marriage to William Gurney V; a separately reported marriage of Myrable Ryvett to Sir John Heydon is not borne out by the primary Visitation of Suffolk (Metcalfe, p. 63), which records her husband as William Burd, Customer of London.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n20" id="ref-20">20</a></sup>

**The St Martin at Palace connection.** St Martin at Palace was the Rybett family's parish church, with Rivett entries spanning 1539–1603.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n21" id="ref-21">21</a></sup> Margaret almost certainly married at her own family's church, following standard practice.

**The Rivett geographic cluster around East Dereham.** FreeREG searches reveal a Rivett/Ryvett family presence near East Dereham: Richard Ryvett at Gressenhall (3 miles) and Rivett entries at Garveston (2 miles).<sup class="fn"><a href="#n22" id="ref-22">22</a></sup>

### 2.3 Financial Collapse and the London Years

Francis's London career was marked by expanding financial difficulty. A failed King's Lynn textile manufacturing venture (c.1622–1625) required Sir Hamon Lestrange to pay his £100 bond.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n23" id="ref-23">23</a></sup> On 11 July 1634, Francis sold ALL his Norfolk and Suffolk lands for £1,000 — a forced liquidation through the Court of Wards.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n25" id="ref-25">25</a></sup> By 1638 he had left St Benet Fink.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n26" id="ref-26">26</a></sup> He was buried at St Botolph Bishopsgate, London, on 9 January 1646/7.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n28" id="ref-28">28</a></sup>

Francis's financial loss was substantial — plausibly the equivalent of $7+ million US dollars in today's value— his entire net worth over a few decades. This financial strain falls during John's late-teens-to-twenties, and any father-son dynamics around emigration would have been shaped by that backdrop of no land to expect.<sup class="fn"><a href="#nA1" id="ref-A1">A1</a></sup>

### 2.4 Room for an Older Son: What Daniel Gurney Didn't Know

For John to be Francis's son, he must be older than Roger Gurney whom the 1633 Heralds' Visitation called Francis's "eldest sonne."<sup class="fn"><a href="#n24" id="ref-24">24</a></sup> But this may not be a conflict as the visitation recorded only the children Francis *presented*, not necessarily all who existed. [Heralds' visitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldic_visitation) were periodic tours that registered and regulated the coats of arms of nobility, gentry, and boroughs, and recorded their pedigrees. Three considerations suggest the record was incomplete:

1. **Daniel Gurney's limited source base.** Daniel framed Dorothy as the eldest child "mentioned in" the St Benet Fink register and judged Roger, the eldest son in that London sequence, to have "probably died young."<sup class="fn"><a href="#n29" id="ref-29">29</a></sup>
2. **Francis's child list was not closed.** Daniel Gurney's account added a possible son John of Maldon and a probable son George beyond the St Benet Fink list, and Bernau later warned that the known record of Francis's children by Anne Browning was incomplete and fragmentary.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n30" id="ref-30">30</a></sup>
3. **The first marriage was unknown.** Neither Daniel Gurney nor Bernau ever recorded the Margaret Rybett marriage, so any children born c.1609–1617 were invisible to both. A grown son from that marriage, living independently or estranged, may also have been absent from a London visitation.

Roger (baptized St Benet Fink in December 1621 — Daniel Gurney's transcription reads the 27th, the modern index the 20th) was the eldest son of the *second* marriage. John, if born c.1609/10, would have been twelve years older.


*Where and how John spent his childhood — the competing household scenarios, the collapse of his father's credit, and a reconstruction — is treated separately in [`research/people/g13-john-gurney/topics/identity/53-refactor-childhood-hypotheses.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/people/g13-john-gurney/topics/identity/53-refactor-childhood-hypotheses.md). The supporting work behind Sections 6 to 11 — the East Dereham register limits, the Francis first-marriage sweep and death record, the Buckinghamshire elimination pass, and the open-action list — is organised in the same directory as the `50-` to `59-` refactor units.*

John, Edward, Agnes, and the later Marye (Anne Browning's first child) are children of Francis Gurney at East Dereham. The earlier Marye at Entry B is likely a niece — the relationship word in the register does not read as "daughter." Entry F (Francis, 8 November 1633) is a probable son: Francis Gurney was resident at East Dereham, no other documented Francis Gurney of the period fits the date. Entry E — John, c.1609/10 — is the central paleographic finding and is examined in §5.

### 4.3 Unknown - Before or After the Marriage?

Entry E's estimated date (c.1609/10) may predate the Margaret Rybett marriage of 23 September 1611 by one to two years. Two possibilities: the date estimate is off by one to two years which is within the ±2 year register margin (no original year listed on page of parish register), or John was born before the marriage. Pre-marital conception was reasonably common in this period so either scenario is plausible.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n45" id="ref-45">45</a></sup>

### 4.4 Two Baptisms 9 Month Apart

Dorothy Gurney's baptism at St Benet Fink (London) on 2 March 1619 is only nine months after Marye's 25 May 1618 baptism at East Dereham (Norfolk) — a tight spacing for one mother, possible only if Anne conceived again within weeks of Marye's birth. Marye is nevertheless almost certainly Anne's child rather than Margaret's: Margaret died c.1616–17, and a May 1618 baptism implies conception around August 1617, after Francis's remarriage. The record shows this specific Francis moving between East Dereham and London in exactly this period. The alternative — that Margaret lived into late 1617 and Marye was her last child — cannot be fully excluded while Margaret's burial remains unfound, which is why the timeline marks Marye's mother as probable rather than certain.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n42" id="ref-42">42</a></sup>

<h2 id="s5">5. THE PROBABLE BAPTISM OF JOHN GURNEY-1</h2>

<div class="discovery">
    ★ <strong>PRIMARY SOURCE DISCOVERY:</strong> Structured paleographic analysis of a degraded entry in the East Dereham parish register (NRO PD 86/41) has identified the baptismal record of John Gurney-1. The entry, on image 00715, was indexed by Findmypast/FreeREG as "John the sonne of Nicholas Gorne." Comparative analysis against four confirmed "ffrancis Gurnie/Gurny" entries in the same register and hand indicates the entry more likely reads <strong>"John the sonne of ffrancis Gurnie."</strong>



This is the likely the record that connects the Massachusetts emigrant to the Norfolk Gurney gentry line. Combined with the Margaret Rybett marriage discovery, it establishes John as the eldest known child of Francis Gurney, born at East Dereham before the family relocated to London. In modern genealogy research, it was previously not commonly known due to the mis-transcription and Francis' first marriage was not known in old historical genealogy research.

</div>

### 5.1 Letterform Evidence: ffrancis over Nicholas

Four independent letterform tests favor "ffrancis" over "Nicholas": initial stroke cluster, mid-body structure, word segmentation, and terminal formation. No test favors "Nicholas." The Findmypast/FreeREG index reads "Nicholas Gorne" because the entry sits on degraded microfilm transcribed without targeted attention to the Gurney surname universe; magnified examination against confirmed Gurney entries in the same register and hand resolves to "ffrancis Gurnie."

> A separate [AI Assistant Procedure for Parish Record Analysis](/key-research/east-dereham-ai-assistant-procedure.html) details the analysis; the full paleographic crops and comparator sweeps are catalogued in the Francis Gurney research companion at `research/people/g14-francis-gurney-fact-sheet.research.md`.

### 5.2 Limitations and Next Steps

This is an AI-assisted paleographic analysis, not a professional examination of the physical register. Professional paleographic examination of the original register at the Norfolk Record Office, The Archive Centre, Martineau Lane, Norwich NR1 2DQ, is the canonical path for confirming Entry E.

<h2 id="s6">6. JOHN'S FAMILY AND THE ENGLISH PARISH RECORD SEARCH</h2>

### 6.1 The Children Search Matrix

**No indexed English parish-register cluster matches the colonial John's full family signature.** A John Gurney + Mary household producing the colonial children - Sarah, Mary, Richard, John Jr, and Peter - in the right age window does not surface in any covered parish 1620-1640, despite over twenty Gurney baptisms reviewed across FamilySearch, Findmypast, and Ancestry indexed collections.

**That negative is a floor, not a bound, and the reason is mostly how the searches were built.** Re-run across all four major indexes - Findmypast, FamilySearch, Ancestry and FreeREG - the July 2026 per-child sweep proves to have been narrowed in three ways that each return fewer rows rather than an error. It constrained the **surname** to the stem `gurn*`, which does not reach *Gourny* or *Gourney*. It constrained the **child's forename** to the exact form *Mary*, when seventeenth-century clerks write *Marie*, *Marye* and *Maria* - forms this file's own records already use. And, as the Berkhamsted burials showed, a place keyword must match the target set's own spelling of the parish. Re-run on the same instrument and window with the forename wildcarded, the Mary count rises from seventeen to fifty-four rows.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n124" id="ref-124">124</a></sup>

Two baptisms recovered this way illustrate the cost: **Marie Gurney, Epping, Essex, 1625** and **Mary Gourny, St Vedast Foster-Lane, London, 1624**, with a sister **Anne Gourney, 1626**. Both were in Findmypast all along and neither had ever been seen. Both name a father who is not John - Robert, with a mother Sarra, at Epping; Richard at St Vedast - so neither becomes a candidate, and the Epping household mattered enough to check immediately because Essex is where the colonial associations run.

**Provider coverage compounds the problem rather than causing it.** No one index holds everything: FreeREG carries no record of the Hitcham 1631 baptism or the Berkhamsted 1626 one, and Ancestry's IGI-derived set carries neither Hitcham nor Berkhamsted nor the Aylesbury 1638 baptism, while FamilySearch and Findmypast hold all three. Four partial instruments that each drop something different do not add up to a complete one. **The correct reading of every "England-wide" child negative in this file is that it states a minimum number of Gurney baptisms, not a maximum.**<sup class="fn"><a href="#n124" id="ref-124">124</a></sup>

The marriage of John and Mary was in England before 1628. Mary Gurney, John's daughter, married Daniel Shed at Braintree in 1647; Daniel was baptized 25 June 1620 at Finchingfield, Essex, and the Braintree Book of Records preserves seven births to Daniel and Mary 1647-1658. Even at a minimum marriage age of 16, Mary Gurney was born by 1631; standard derivative tradition places her at c.1628.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n97" id="ref-97a">97</a></sup> No John Gurney + Mary marriage 1620-1635 surfaces in eastern-England parish-marriage indexes outside the eliminated Eythorne, Kent / Mary Marsh event. The negative now holds at wildcard level: searches for any G*rn* groom — with and without the forename John, so immune to forename mis-indexing — marrying a Mary (with variants) 1620-1634 return no East Anglian John Gurney + Mary event; the only East Anglian Gurney-surname hit is a Robert Gvrney + Mary at Norwich in 1622.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n113" id="ref-113">113</a></sup>

**That negative is now settled on five independent instruments, and it is sharper than an absence.** Ancestry's England-wide marriage set, which indexes both parties, records only two John Gurney × Mary marriages in the whole country before 1660 — Eythorne 1632 and Ackworth 1636. The Hertfordshire county set records no John Gurney marriage of any kind between 1600 and 1654. The Buckinghamshire county set, run from the groom side and the bride side, records five John Gurney marriages inside the emigration window and not one bride is a Mary — Alice 1628, Susan 1629, Elizabeth 1630, Avis 1638, Rebecca 1640 — with the earliest Mary to marry a Buckinghamshire John Gurney being Mary Kidgell at Edlesborough in **1663**, two years after the colonial Mary had died at Braintree. And FreeREG, whose records name both parties, yields ninety-seven Gurney marriages across England 1615-1645 in which **every** John Gurney groom's bride is identified and **not one is a Mary**.

**What makes this more than an absence is that the name was plainly available.** In those same FreeREG holdings nine Gurney men married Marys — Robert × Mary Lame at Norwich in 1622, Edward × Mary Bedward at Stepney in 1641, Walter × Mary Hammond at Westminster in 1642, and six more to 1660. It is specifically the Johns who have none.

**The register has also now been asked who the mother was**, which had never been done despite a wife named Mary being among the firmest facts about the household. Across Great Britain 1620-1640, exactly one household whose surname sits securely in the Gurney family is recorded with a father John **and** a mother Mary: Eythorne, Kent — the same eliminated household. **A second appears once the surname is pushed to the outer edge of the variant scale:** John and Mary **Gurley** of Westminster, whose children run Eliz 1627, Ellynor 1628, Alexander 1629, **Mary 1631** and Sara 1633 before the record stops. It satisfies the father, the mother and Mary's primary birth bound, and fails on both sons — there is no Richard and no John, the son being Alexander.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n129" id="ref-129">129</a></sup> The absence of an English marriage is therefore better read as a coverage problem in classes not yet searched — above all the south-Midlands archdeaconry licence series, where a couple marrying away from home would surface — than as a parish-register gap.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n127" id="ref-127c">127</a></sup>

**The trade evidence does not point exclusively to Norfolk and London.** The colonial John was a tailor. Two separate bodies of evidence bear on where a Gurney tailor could come from, and they pull in opposite directions. The Merchant Taylors' Company dataset returns **no John Gurney son of Francis at any binding 1583-1800, and no Gurney patrimony freedom anywhere in the 3,391-row patrimony sheet** - so if the colonial John was Francis's son, his tailoring trade was not transmitted through his father's company by either route.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n113b" id="ref-113b">113b</a></sup> Buckinghamshire, by contrast, holds a documented Gurney tailoring trade across four parishes and eighty-five years: William Gurneye of Aylesbury, tailor (1571); Luke Gurneye of Aylesbury, tailor (1591); Thomas Gurney of Rowsham in Wingrave, tailor (1628); Richard Gurny(e) of Mursley, tailor (1631); and Thomas Gurney of Weston Turville, tailor (1633) - four of the five inside the emigrant's own working life.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n114" id="ref-114">114</a></sup> This does not identify any individual, and no Buckinghamshire tailor is a John. It establishes that a tailoring trade is not distinctive to the Norfolk-and-London reading.

### 6.2 The Peter Naming Gap

Peter is a rare first name across the wider Gurney record set under research. Of twelve indexed Peter-Gurney-variant baptisms 1632–1640 across Findmypast UK Parish Baptisms, none was fathered by a John Gurney. The name therefore did not enter the colonial son's branch through any indexed Gurney parallel; the most likely source is Mary's maiden family. Recovering Mary's surname and identifying a Peter in her kin would independently confirm John's origin regardless of his father's identity.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n55" id="ref-55">55</a></sup>

### 6.3 The Absence of "Francis"

John named no child Francis, the strongest naming-pattern argument against Candidate B. Under the standard English naming custom of the period the eldest son takes the paternal grandfather's name; John's sons in order are **Richard** (c.1630), John Jr. (c.1633), and Peter (c.1635-40). The objection is therefore sharper than a simple absence: the one slot the convention most strongly governs is occupied by another name.

It gets sharper still under Candidate B's own reconstruction of the maternal line, which identifies Margaret as most likely the Garveston Margaret Rivet baptised 1586, **daughter of Francis Rivet**. On that reading John's paternal grandfather and his maternal grandfather were both named Francis, and he named no son Francis at all. The explanation that Richard honours Richard Ryvett of Gressenhall does not resolve it: Richard Ryvett is Margaret's brother or first cousin - John's uncle or first cousin once removed - and not the person the eldest-son slot conventionally names.

This is not decisive. Naming customs were followed loosely, and estrangement after the 1634 financial collapse is a real explanation, one which could equally account for John and his siblings' absence from the 1633 Heralds' Visitation. Other possibilities are negative associations with the name after the ruin, or an earlier child named Francis who died. But the argument runs the same direction as the age evidence, and it costs nothing to weigh.

### 6.3a The convention read forwards — what name it does supply

The same custom that produces the objection above also carries positive information, and reading it forwards produced a candidate.

**Sarah is the weak link in the colonial child list.** She rests on compiled tradition, and no primary record of her has been produced. Set her aside and the pattern resolves: **Mary** is the eldest daughter, named for her mother Mary; **Richard** is therefore the **eldest son**, and so occupies the paternal-grandfather slot; **John Jr.** is a later son carrying the father's own name, as the custom allows once the grandfather slots are filled; **Peter** remains unexplained in the Gurney lines and most plausibly comes from the mother's kin.

**If the custom is being followed at all, John Gurney's father was named Richard.** This also disposes of the explanation offered above for Richard: a maternal Ryvett belongs in the *second*-son slot, and there is no elder son to occupy the first — the Ryvett reading needs Sarah's brother to exist, whereas the paternal reading needs only that Sarah does not.

Tested against the Buckinghamshire church records, that prediction returns one candidate: **Johannes Gurney, baptised Cheddington 5 August 1608, son of Richard Gurney and Jane Founteine**, who married there on 10 August 1606 and whose probate follows in 1626. He does not appear in the Cheddington record again. He is treated with the other unaccounted-for men in §8.7 and weighed in §11; the full assessment, including the argument against him, is in the identity refactor unit.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n119" id="ref-119">119</a></sup>

A second naming signal points the same way, though it is weaker and should be stated carefully. The colonial John's youngest son was **Isaac** — a name absent from every Norfolk Gurney household surveyed, which run to Edward, Marye, Agnes and Francis. Buckinghamshire Gurneys do use it, and one instance is contemporary: an **Isaac Gurney and Martha were baptising children at Cheddington itself in 1636 and 1641**, so Isaac was in that parish's Gurney name-pool in the emigrant's own generation. A John Gurney and Mary also baptised a son Isaac at Cublington in 1664, but that is a generation later than the colonial naming and a different, already-eliminated household, so it shows only that the combination recurs in the county — it cannot show the name was available to the emigrant. The Cheddington instance carries the argument; the Cublington one does not.




<h2 id="s7">7. THE ANN GURNEY / JOHN GILMAN CONNECTION</h2>

If Ann Gurney was John's sister, her marriage at Hingham and her family's New England connections place a second sibling on the Norfolk-to-New-England corridor, strengthening the corridor reading of Candidate B. Ann Gurney married John Gilman, a worsted weaver, at Hingham, Norfolk, on 1 October 1626.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n33" id="ref-33">33</a></sup> Their children were baptized at West Dereham and Hingham.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n34" id="ref-34">34</a></sup> Ann was buried at Hingham on 23 November 1651.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n35" id="ref-35">35</a></sup>

This connection is significant on multiple levels. **Hingham was Gurney family territory** — the manor of "Hingham Gurneys" was an ancient Gurney holding.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n36" id="ref-36">36</a></sup> **The Gilmans were a Norfolk textile family.**<sup class="fn"><a href="#n37" id="ref-37">37</a></sup> **The West Dereham entries** — Ann's first two children were baptized at West Dereham, in western Norfolk near Downham Market. Despite the similar name, West Dereham lies some twenty miles from East Dereham, where Francis's children were baptized, so these entries place the Gilmans in a different part of the county rather than in Francis's home parish; a separately recorded Ann Gurney also married at West Dereham in 1618/19 and may be a different woman (see note 33). **The New England connection** — Ann and John Gilman's son John Jr. emigrated to Exeter, New Hampshire.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n38" id="ref-38">38</a></sup>

The Pease genealogy identifies Ann as Francis's daughter from his first marriage.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n39" id="ref-39">39</a></sup> If Ann was John-1's sister, the Gilman emigration would represent a second sibling who crossed the Atlantic.

**The Pease attribution carries no citation.** In *The Descendants of Anthony Gurney*, the entry giving Francis a first wife Margaret Ryvett and children John and Anne, both "c.1610", carries **no source superscript at all**, while entries on the same page do carry them. The two children are given the same year, which reads as a compiler's placeholder rather than two researched dates. Nor does the attribution derive from Philis Wainford, the American researcher named in the genealogy's source list: her contribution is a modern family website documenting the nineteenth-century Wainford branch - the illegitimate line of Richard Hanbury Gurney by Susan Wainford - and every citation to it in the genealogy sits on that branch.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n115" id="ref-115">115</a></sup> Pease's identification of Francis's son John with the Braintree emigrant is explicit - it gives him "Grizell Fletcher", the colonial John's second wife, married at Braintree in 1661 - but it is exactly the claim in dispute, and it rests on nothing the compiler names.

The Gilman marriage itself is not corroborated in the Norfolk marriage index for a daughter of Francis: a search of Gurney-variant brides against Gilman-variant grooms returns four rows, none an Ann Gurney and John Gilman, and none before 1667.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n115b" id="ref-115b">115b</a></sup> The 1 October 1626 Hingham marriage stands on its own record; what is unsupported is the link from that bride to Francis's household.

### 8.1 Candidate A — Aylesbury hundred Buckinghamshire and Walgrave Northamptonshire

**Candidate A is two men, and both are eliminated.** The records once read as one continuous household split at 1641 on their own evidence, and separating them eliminates two John Gurneys rather than one.

- **A(i), the certificate man.** Married Alice Oliffe at Bierton with Broughton on 24 April 1628; taxed in Northamptonshire and no longer in the Aylesbury half-hundred from 1641; tenant at Walgrave, Northamptonshire, in 1650.
- **A(ii), the Saint Mary Aylesbury father.** Fathered five children baptised at Saint Mary, Aylesbury, between 1638 and 1653.

**Why they are two.** The 1641 certificate of residence is a government record that a specific John Gurney had ceased to be liable in the Aylesbury half-hundred and had become liable in Northamptonshire. That is difficult to reconcile with the same man fathering children at Saint Mary, Aylesbury, in 1645, 1647, and 1653. Aylesbury demonstrably held more than one John Gurney: a marriage to Anne Cowheard on 25 October 1638, a further marriage in 1644, and a third in 1620. And the Saint Mary baptisms for the seventeenth century are indexed with a father and no mother, so no index can assign the children to a household - the wife Alice Oliffe cannot be read onto them.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n116" id="ref-116">116</a></sup>

The chain of indexed records behind both men: 

- **Baptism, 21 February 1603, Stewkley, Buckinghamshire.** Jhon Gurney, son of Jhon Gurney (i.e., John Gurney Sr.).<sup class="fn"><a href="#n85" id="ref-85">85</a></sup>
- **Marriage, 24 April 1628, Bierton with Broughton, Buckinghamshire.** John Gurney and Alice Oliffe. The bride's surname is Oliffe, not Collindridge; the older "Alice Collindridge" reading is unsupported.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n85" id="ref-85b">85</a></sup>
- **Family group, Saint Mary, Aylesbury, 1638-1653.** Five children of John Gurney baptized at Saint Mary: John 16 December 1638; Sarah 22 August 1639; Daniell 26 December 1645; Jonathan 22 November 1647; Hannah 12 November 1653.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n85" id="ref-85c">85</a></sup> <sup class="fn"><a href="#n88" id="ref-88a">88</a></sup>
- **Certificate of residence, 1641.** The National Archives, Kew, **E 115/180/113**, Exchequer: King's Remembrancer: Certificates of Residence, described in the catalogue as "Certificate of residence showing **John Gurney to be liable for taxation in Northamptonshire, and not in the half-hundred of Aylesbury, Buckinghamshire**, the previous area". This is a government record of a specific man's move within England in the same year the colonial John first appears in America, and it is the individuating document on which the elimination of A(i) rests.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n83" id="ref-83">83</a></sup>
- **Tenancy, 1650.** John Gurney listed as tenant at Walgrave, Northamptonshire.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n83" id="ref-83b">83</a></sup>

**Why neither is the Massachusetts emigrant.** The colonial John is documented at Weymouth, Massachusetts by June 1641 (General Court fine), at Braintree by 1653 (Wilson v. Faxon deposition, aged about 50), and dies at Braintree in 1662/3. A(i) is documented in England in 1641 and 1650; A(ii) is fathering children in England through 1653. Neither can be the same person. **Both are eliminated, and A(i) is eliminated by an individuating government record rather than by continuity of the surname in a register** - the distinction that separates a sound elimination from a weak one in a county this dense.

**The 1603 Stewkley baptism is not eliminated by either.** Jhon Gurney, son of Jhon Gurney, was baptised at Stewkley on 21 February 1602/3. The chain above never reached him: the merge into the Bierton household was always an inference from shared hundred and plausible age, and with A now split in two, that inference has one fewer anchor rather than more. The 1603 baptism is treated separately in Section 8.7, where it belongs - it is the closest age match to the 1653 deposition in the entire corpus.

### 8.2 Candidate C — Berkhamsted, Hertfordshire

**Candidate C is two men, and only the elder is eliminated on age.** Searched by parent rather than by child, the Great Berkhamsted baptisms to a father John Gurney run from **1597 to 1637** — forty years, which is not one man. The natural break is the nine-year gap between Sara 1615 and Jhon 1624, and a **Jhon Gourney was buried in the parish in 1620**, between the two.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n122" id="ref-122b">122</a></sup>

- **C(i), the elder John** — Hugh 1597, Anne 1599, Amye 1602, Bridgett 1606, Henrye 1610, Sara 1615. Fathering from 1597 puts his birth about 1570-75, so he is roughly 25 to 30 years older than the colonial John (born about 1603 per the 1653 Wilson v. Faxon deposition, "aged about 50 years"). Eliminated on age.
- **C(ii), the younger John** — Jhon 1624, Richard 1626, Elizabeth 1629, Michaell 1631, Sarah 1634, Francis 1636/7. Fathering from 1624 puts his birth about 1598-1602, so he would be about **51 in 1653**. He was never assessed, because this file eliminated his father and stopped. He is weighed in §11 at ~4%.

**The reason C(ii) does not rise higher is that the household's disappearance is mortality, not departure.** Great Berkhamsted's burial register survives and holds sixteen Gurney-variant burials between 1579 and 1643. Five fall between November 1637 and March 1638/9 — Joane (7 November 1637), **Richard (28 June 1638)**, William (1 October 1638), Elizabeth (21 February 1638/9) and Francis (9 March 1638/9) — and the Elizabeth and Francis burials match, to the day, the death dates independently recorded for two of C(ii)'s own children. The baptism run ends because the children were dying. Two further constraints run against him: **no Mary Gurney is baptized in the parish at any date under any spelling or any father**, and **no John Gurney-variant marriage occurs anywhere in Hertfordshire between 1600 and 1654**. What remains open is only that the head himself has no recorded burial after 1620 — and the record set states that many Hertfordshire registers are defective for 1643-1660, so a death in that window would be invisible.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n122" id="ref-122c">122</a></sup>

Note also that the parish held **four** contemporaneous Gurney households, with fathers John, Thomas, Edward and William, so the Richard buried in June 1638 is either C(ii)'s son baptized in 1626 or the Richarde baptized in 1635 to the Thomas household; the index cannot discriminate and the register image is the next step.

Additionally, the children between the candidate and colonial John Gurney do not align. The Berkhamsted parish register records an eight-child Gurney family fathered by John Gurney between 1610 and 1636: Henry 1610, Sara 1615 (died young, replaced by Sarah 1634), Jhon 1624, Richard 1626, Elizabeth 1629, Michael 1631, Sarah 1634, and Francis 1636. The children compare to the colonial John's set as follows:

- **Children in the Berkhamsted family that the colonial John does not have:** Henry, Michael, and Francis.
- **Children of the colonial John that are missing from Berkhamsted:** Mary and Peter
- **Shared names whose dates do not line up:** Berkhamsted John Jr. 1624 vs the colonial John Jr. about 1633; Berkhamsted Richard 1626 vs the colonial Richard about 1630; Berkhamsted Sarah 1634 vs the colonial Sarah about 1628.

The Hertfordshire burial index records no John Gurney burial at Berkhamsted between 1640 and 1700 — but that window sits inside the county's documented Civil War register defect and is no longer treated as meaningful either way. **C(i) is eliminated on his own age; C(ii) is not eliminated, and is carried as a weak live candidate in §11.**<sup class="fn"><a href="#n86" id="ref-86a">86</a></sup> <sup class="fn"><a href="#n122" id="ref-122d">122</a></sup>

### 8.4 Candidate D - London Drapers' / Old Change

**Candidate D is eliminated.** John Gurney, son and executor of Robert Gurney, citizen and draper of Old Change, London, is documented continuing in London at the same St Augustine Watling Street parish across at least twenty-four years: a 1638 T.C. Dale return entry at GBP10 rent and a 1662 hearth-tax entry at 1 hearth assessed "poore" in the same St Augustine precinct. The 1662 entry falls in the same calendar year the colonial John of Braintree was dying at Boston about March 1662/3; Candidate D and the colonial John cannot be the same person.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n92a" id="ref-92b">92</a></sup> <sup class="fn"><a href="#n110" id="ref-110g">110</a></sup>

He was admitted to the Drapers' Company by redemption on 11 February 1623/4 and proved his father Robert's will on 23 September 1625. John's father Robert was a Drapers' freeman from 16 December 1581 and described as a tailor at Old Change from his admission; Robert married Anne Morris by licence at St Magnus the Martyr on 4 April 1611, after an earlier wife produced three children at St Augustine in the 1590s.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n92a" id="ref-92c">92</a></sup>

John was admitted to the Drapers' by redemption rather than patrimony, despite Robert's long-standing Drapers' freedom. The cleanest explanation is that John served apprenticeship in a different company before taking up the family business - a 1613 Stationers' record places a John Gurney apprentice to master James Boler with no later Stationers' freedom, and if this is the same John, the 1623/4 Drapers' redemption is the natural consequence.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n92b" id="ref-92d">92b</a></sup>

On 3 November 1630 John bound Henry Smith of Kilton, Suffolk as a Drapers' apprentice for seven years; Smith does not surface as a freed Drapers' Smith 1635-1645, and no Drapers' turnover for any Gurney is recorded 1620-1670. The 1638 St Augustine return placing John at GBP10 rent (alongside Joseph Hunscott at GBP12 in the same parish - the Stationer overseer of Robert's 1625 will, the Robert Gurney will-network still in the same parish thirteen years after Robert's death) and the 1662 hearth-tax entry placing John at 1 hearth "poore" together describe a draper whose fortunes contracted sharply between 1638 and 1662.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n92b" id="ref-92e">92b</a></sup> <sup class="fn"><a href="#n92c" id="ref-92f">92c</a></sup> <sup class="fn"><a href="#n110" id="ref-110h">110</a></sup>

Robert's will preamble uses Reformed vocabulary ("elect children of God") consistent with a godly-Protestant milieu but too weak to prove nonconformity. No Puritan minister, lecturer, Coleman-Street-network associate, or Massachusetts bridge appears in the Old Change record set. No London-parish marriage of John Gurney to a wife named Mary, and no baptisms of Sarah, Mary, Richard, John, or Peter to a John Gurney + Mary household 1620-1641, have been located; the closest John Gurney + Mary marriage in window (Eythorne, Kent, 6 November 1632 to Mary Marsh) belongs to a Kent couple who stayed in Kent.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n92c" id="ref-92g">92c</a></sup>

### 8.5 The 1636 Newgate apprentice — a distinct second John, not the Braintree man

**The 1636 apprentice and the Braintree man are not the same person.** Winthrop/Savage's Addenda records that on 21 July 1636 John Newgate brought his apprentice John Gurney before the Boston governor; the court ordered service until age 24, three years from the following 29 September.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n75" id="ref-75b">75</a></sup> That sets the apprentice's birth at c.29 September 1615 and his service term to 29 September 1639. Two independent tests separate him from the Braintree man. First, family status: the Braintree John had married Mary by c.1627 and had three or four English-born children by the late 1630s, while the apprentice was a servant under indenture until September 1639 — apprentices were customarily unmarried, and a court extending a married father of four's service to age 24 is not a credible reading of the order. Second, chronology: a 1615 birth sits twelve to thirteen years from the 1653 deposition ("aged 50 or thereabouts," implying c.1602/3) and six to eight years even from Candidate B's c.1607–12 window — outside any reasonable rounding of "about 50," whose edge Entry E itself already tests.

John Newgate himself was from Horningsheath, Suffolk, three miles from Bury St Edmunds, before emigrating in 1633. The apprentice was most plausibly drawn from Newgate's Suffolk network, which cleanly explains Banks's later Bury St Edmunds attribution: Banks's manuscript memo likely tracked the apprentice rather than the older Braintree John, and nineteenth-century compilers then merged the two Johns into a single biographical sketch.

The recurring American family-memory tradition of a 29 September 1615 birth and Southwark origin — repeated in compiled biographies and online memorials — fits this apprentice, not the colonial John, and should not be carried as a controlling chronology for Candidate B. The apprentice's own post-1639 Massachusetts trail does not surface in indexed records; likeliest readings are early-Boston mortality, return to England, or absorption into a non-Gurney surname via marriage.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n99" id="ref-99b">99</a></sup>

### 8.6 The Buckinghamshire probate tier

Most Buckinghamshire husbandmen and shepherds proved in the Archdeaconry of Buckingham court, not in Canterbury, so a Prerogative Court negative leaves the county substantially unsearched. That tier has been read. The printed **British Record Society vol. 114, *Index to Buckinghamshire Probate Records 1483-1660***, carries **forty-two Gurney-variant testators**, of whom **nine are named John**: 1523, 1552, 1554, 1556, 1589, 1592, 1623, 1630 and 1636.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n114" id="ref-114b">114</a></sup>

Three of those are individuating English deaths for men no register work had resolved, and each eliminates a household outright:

- **John Gurney of Monks Risborough, 1623** (Pec 79/5/3)
- **John Gurney of Hulcott, yeoman, 1630** (Wf 28 134) — eliminating the Hulcott household of John Gourney and Alice, whose daughter Joyce was baptised there in 1603
- **John Gurney of Southcott in Linslade, 1636** (We 31 129, Wf 31 67) — a death at the very start of the emigration window, so the Linslade records stopping in 1636 is explained by a death rather than a departure

The index also corrects a reading of the Stewkley household. The Buckinghamshire parish collections carry no Stewkley Gurney record of any kind after 1614, which had been read as the family going silent; the probate index shows **Robert Gurnie, husbandman, 1618; Robert Gorny/Gerny, husbandman, 1631; and Robert Gurney, yeoman, 1651**, all of Stewkley, the last rising in status. The family continued there for another forty years and the register silence was a coverage artefact. The John of Stewkley who proved in 1592 is a generation earlier than the household active 1602-1613.

**Where the index is silent** is equally load-bearing, because the index runs to 1660: **no John Gurney probate at all for Haddenham, Weston Turville, Hitcham, Amersham, Ivinghoe, or Bierton after 1559.** Those men left no Buckinghamshire archdeaconry probate either, so the elimination test cannot reach them from this direction.

**Coverage limits.** Hertfordshire is absent from the published-index dataset entirely — a control search on a common surname also returns zero — so Candidate C's county probate tier is not reachable this way. Northamptonshire is represented only by administrations indexes from 1677, so the death window of A(i) is not covered.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n114" id="ref-114c">114</a></sup>

**Why they are unaccounted for rather than live.** The colonial John was fathering children in England from about 1628 to about 1635. A household whose record stops in 1603, 1622, 1627, or 1631 is not baptising children anywhere these searches can see during exactly the years he demonstrably was. Sustaining any of them as a candidate requires positing an undocumented move to an unindexed parish, a marriage there, four baptisms there, and then emigration — the same absence-explaining structure that weakens Candidate B, applied to men with far less circumstantial support. In a county holding five to seven simultaneous John Gurneys, being unaccounted for is the ordinary state of a record, not evidence of departure.

Two of them nevertheless warrant named rows rather than group clearance, because they fit the deposition age better than several candidates that already carry rows: **Weston Turville** and **Hitcham**.

<h2 id="s9">9. SEPARATING THE TWO FRANCIS GURNEYS</h2>

| | Francis Gurney <br />(aka Francis-A "Our Francis") | Francis Gurney<br />(aka Francis-B "The Laceweaver") |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | West Barsham, Norfolk gentry | Unknown — Norwich plebeian |
| **Trade** | Merchant Taylor (freed 1606) | Laceweaver |
| **London parish** | St Benet Fink (1619–1637) | St Giles Cripplegate (1638–1640) |
| **Norfolk parish** | East Dereham (c.1608–1618) | Norwich, St Peter Mountergate |
| **First wife** | Margaret Rybett (m. 1611) | Unknown |
| **Second wife** | Anne Browning | Mary |
| **Costessey property** | NOT this Francis | Almost certainly THIS Francis |
| **Death** | 9 Jan 1646/7, St Botolph Bishopsgate | Unknown |

This distinction, first identified by genealogist Walter Rye, is essential for avoiding false attributions.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n53" id="ref-53">53</a></sup>

The Norwich corporate registers add a primary-level anchor on the Norwich side of the separation. James Gurney, son of Francis of St Peter Parmentergate — the laceweaver's documented Norwich parish — was bound apprentice at Michaelmas 1627 for seven years and admitted a freeman of Norwich under Henry Bond on 8 January 1648. The entry documents a Francis Gurney family resident at St Peter Parmentergate in 1627, while Francis the Merchant Taylor was simultaneously baptizing children at St Benet Fink, London: the two households are now separated by simultaneous independent records, not by trade and later parishes alone. The same registers contain no John Gurney admission or enrolled apprenticeship in the emigrant window.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n112" id="ref-112a">112</a></sup>

<p>A 1640 St Giles Cripplegate burial entry adds a likely third Francis to the same parish neighborhood. The entry "John sonne of ffrancis Garney Joyner - 15" buries a London child of a Francis Garney whose trade is joiner, not laceweaver. The Garney spelling sits inside the Gurney/Gurny/Gourney/Garney cluster, and the trade difference means this Francis is not the laceweaver Francis B already eliminated above. Keep him visible as a separate Cripplegate-area Francis Gurney/Garney when searching London same-name households.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n71" id="ref-71">71</a></sup></p>

#### Evidence Against Candidate B

| # | Evidence | Weight | Explanation |
|---|----------|--------|--------|
| 1 | **There is no record of a John connected to this family** | Strong negative | Candidate B rests on a marriage that establishes possibility, a family tradition, and a circumstantial web. No surviving document names a John Gurney as Francis's son. The identification cannot be checked against any record of the man it is about. |
| 1b | **Francis's apprenticeship bounds the birth into a narrow sliver, and it is still pre-marital** | Strong negative | Francis was bound apprentice in London on 14 May 1599 for seven years and admitted to the freedom of the Merchant Taylors on 30 June 1606. London livery companies barred apprentices from marrying and enforced it, so he could not have married, or ordinarily fathered a legitimate child, before mid-1606. The 1653 deposition reads c.1602/3 strictly and c.1603-1608 with age-heaping allowed. Candidate B can only occupy the **1606-1608 overlap** — and every year in it is still three to five years before the September 1611 marriage, so the hypothesis continues to require a pre-marital birth, from a father who was in London and not yet free at the start of the window. A sweep for an earlier Francis Gurney marriage 1606-1611 across the Norfolk parish registers, the England-wide IGI, the Bishop of London's marriage allegations, and the Norfolk marriage-licence-bond index returns nothing.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n117" id="ref-117">117</a></sup> |

| 4 | Roger called "eldest sonne" in 1633 Visitation | Moderate negative | Visitation recorded only children Francis presented - but the record stands as written. |
| 5 | Common-name density and parish-coverage gaps | Moderate negative (cumulative) | Over forty distinct John Gurney heads-of-household across England 1600-1670; parish-register coverage gaps imply additional unidentified Johns. ### 10.2 Narrative Recap

The argument for John as Francis's son rests on four connected lines: trade, corridor, motive, and network.

Francis was a Merchant Taylor; the colonial John was a tailor. Trades in this period commonly passed from father to son, though John appears in the formal admission registers of neither the Merchant Taylors' Company nor the city of Norwich — if Candidate B holds, his training ran through a country master or the household itself. Of the named candidates only Candidate D shares any textile-trade link, and Candidate D's continuing London residence keeps him in London through at least 1638 and probably 1661 (see §8.4); the remaining eliminated candidates were landholders, yeomen, or shepherds.

Francis's commercial life sat inside the East Anglia → London corridor that produced roughly sixty percent of Massachusetts Bay emigrants in the 1630s and under ten percent from London proper. His parish of St Benet Fink adjoined Coleman Street Ward, where John Davenport preached until 1633; a William Gurney is recorded at St Stephen Coleman Street in the 1641-42 Protestation Returns, and a Henry Browning — sharing the surname of Francis's second wife Anne Browning — appears among the Coleman Street emigrants of the same parish.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n62" id="ref-62">62</a></sup> Francis's brother Edmund was a Cambridge-educated militant Puritan rector. The Diligent of Ipswich (Norfolk-Hingham passengers, April-August 1638, settling at Hingham, Massachusetts under ten miles from Braintree-Weymouth) is the 1638 corridor event nearest in time and place to John's June 1641 Weymouth appearance.

The motive is documented. Francis's 1634 forced sale through the Court of Wards liquidated every acre in Norfolk and Suffolk for £1,000. A son trained in the textile trade but with no land to expect had a textbook reason to seek opportunity in New England. The colonial network is real but it establishes reception rather than origin: John's son-in-law Daniel Shed was from Finchingfield, Essex; the Braintree property John occupied "by lease" sat inside the William Tyng estate of Stanford Rivers, Essex (Suffolk Deeds Liber IV); Braintree, Massachusetts itself took its name from Braintree, Essex; and Francis's second wife Anne Browning was from the same Essex-and-Maldon network.

**Essex has now been searched from the English end for the first time, and the result is mixed.** The two parishes the colonial evidence names directly are empty: the county register set holds **one** Gurney-variant baptism at Finchingfield across its whole run, and **none at Stanford Rivers**. But the country four miles from the Tyng estate is not empty. A cluster of Gurney-plausible households sits in the **Epping – North Weald Bassett – Fyfield** triangle through the emigrant's own window, and one of its records — **Richard, baptised at Epping in 1616 to a father John** — names a John. Whether that family wrote itself Gurney, Gurnett or Gurnard cannot be settled from index rows; it needs the registers, and that is now the specific next step rather than more searching.

Essex's one unambiguous Gurney-surname baptism before 1650 is an Anthony Gourney at Aveley in 1615. The county's substantial Gurney presence is at **Maldon**, beginning in **1655** — a generation after the emigration window — where it is Francis's own descendants: the register names a Francis Gourney and Ann with children including a **John (1655)** and a **Thomas (1660)**, matching the bachelor John of Maldon and the brother Thomas who took out his administration in 1681, a family composition that had rested on compiled authorities alone.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n125" id="ref-125">125</a></sup> A trailing thread of the same colonial merchant world: the 1699 will of John Lewis of Nevis names "Mary Gurney the daughter of John Gurney" among legatees of a New England-and-Bristol circle — possibly a granddaughter of the Braintree John through John Jr., an unproven but live network trace.<sup class="fn"><a href="#nA2" id="ref-A2">A2</a></sup>

Banks's Bury St Edmunds attribution is consistent with Candidate B rather than refuting it. Banks placed the colonial John inside a documented Bury emigrant cluster but sourced the attribution only to "Banks Mss." A Norwich-born son of Francis who trained at Bury would fit Banks's note precisely. The continuing 1653-1656 Bury Gurney household (see §8 and §8.5) means Banks's memo most plausibly tracked an earlier-departing apprentice from that Bury household — the §8.5 reading of the 1636 Newgate apprentice — rather than its head.

These are subjective credences over one question — who was the John Gurney who died at Braintree in 1662/3 — not frequencies. The bands are wide on purpose. They are stated so they can be attacked.

**Every row names a person or a specific state of the record, not a feeling.** The categories are mutually exclusive: a candidate who is *documented but never assembled into a person* is not the same as one for whom *no record survives*, and the two call for different work — re-querying records already in hand, versus new discovery.

**How Candidate B is weighed against candidates who demonstrably existed.** There is an asymmetry in this table that has to be stated rather than buried. Candidate B is a *hypothesised* person: no document names a John Gurney as Francis's son. The Buckinghamshire survivors are *documented* people — each has a baptism naming him — for whom no record of emigration exists.

**The earlier ranking rested on a claim that is not supportable: that B "carries an evidenced link to emigration that the others entirely lack."** It does not. The honest statement is a symmetry, and each side is missing the same half of its chain.

- **Candidate B has an English-side corridor with no Gurney at the colonial end.** Francis's Norfolk-and-London geography, the 1634 liquidation as motive, the Puritan uncle, the Coleman Street proximity — all of it describes a departure environment, and none of it puts a Gurney on a ship or in a New England town record.
- **The Buckinghamshire class has a colonial-side reception channel with no Gurney at the English end.** Two of John Gurney's own Weymouth land-neighbours came out of Wendover and Ashton Clinton, three to five miles from parishes where Gurneys demonstrably lived; Weymouth had a documented Aylesbury-Vale stream and no Norfolk stream at all. That is an arrival environment with no identified Gurney entering it.

Neither side has the middle link. The subordination of the Weymouth observation was decided when Candidate B still rested on the East Dereham baptism, which no longer exists, and it has never been re-run.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n121" id="ref-121">121</a></sup>

Two further considerations both run against B. First, **the English-side link is generic**: tens of thousands of East Anglians shared the corridor, and a ruined gentry family is not a rare circumstance — the Bishopstone Gurneys of Buckinghamshire sold their whole estate for £1,700 in 1648/9, so even the liquidation motive is not Norfolk-specific.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n120" id="ref-120">120</a></sup> Second, **B requires a longer chain of unevidenced steps than any rival**: that Francis had a son John who left no record; that he was born in the narrow 1606–1608 sliver; that he was born either pre-maritally or of a marriage nobody can find; and that he emigrated. A documented Buckinghamshire John requires exactly one unevidenced step — that he emigrated. A four-step conjunction should not outrank a one-step conjunction by the margin the earlier estimates gave it.

Running the other way, the Buckinghamshire class has acquired a genuine argument against it. Where the county's records are rich enough to individuate a man — land, office, probate, trade — **Buckinghamshire Johns are visibly settled**, and the deed evidence has not produced a single John who disappears.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n120" id="ref-120">120</a></sup> The men who remain open are open because the record is thin, not because it shows them leaving.

**And a third consideration now outranks both, because it has been demonstrated twice.** The dense-cluster masking effect predicts that where a surname is common, records get merged into too few men — and every time this file has tested a household for that defect, it has found it. Candidate A resolved into two men; the Stewkley "register silence" proved to be a search artefact; Candidate C resolved into a father and a son, and then the parish turned out to hold four Gurney households rather than one. **Three of the four leading rows in the table below are not people at all but statements about the state of the record**, and the leading row is the one that says the man is already in the evidence, unassembled.

**Reading of the table.** No option exceeds twenty percent, and **the leading rows are not people.** Three of them describe the state of the record rather than a man — *no record survives* at 19, *documented but never assembled* at 17, and *documented but in a source not yet searched* at 9 — and together they hold forty-five percent. **The honest reading is that the identification is open**, and that no named candidate is close to a documentary identification. Candidate B remains the leading *named* candidate, because the circumstantial case is real and the rivals are independently eliminated — but it is the fourth row overall and has fallen by a factor of six from the ~65% this file once published. External calibration agrees: Anderson's *Great Migration Directory* (2nd ed., 2025) gives John's origin as "Unknown".

**Each of the four leading rows implies different work**, which is the practical value of separating them. The **unassembled** row is tested by re-sorting records already in hand — repeatedly productive through 2026, and now largely spent, which is why it has fallen from thirty. The **not-yet-searched** row is tested by opening named sources that exist and have never been consulted: the south-Midlands archdeaconry licence series, the Suffolk gap, the Norwich licence bonds, the Buckinghamshire archdeaconry wills. The **no-record** row is moved only by new discovery or by Y-DNA, which bypasses records entirely. And **Candidate B** can now only be advanced from outside East Dereham.

**Why the unassembled row fell rather than grew.** It had been raised each time a merge defect was found — Candidate A resolving into two men, Stewkley into two households, Berkhamsted into a father and a son and then into a parish of four, Denton's father proving to be Josias, Toddington's Thomas, Linslade's Ezechiell, Kelham's William. But **every one of those defects was found and then resolved**, and each resolution drains the row rather than filling it. The row had been fed by the very process that was emptying it. With the father axis now enumerated to completion for 1615-1649, the mother axis swept for the first time, and the marriage question settled on five independent instruments, the searched corpus contains exactly **two** unaccounted-for households in the whole window — Hitcham on the child axis and Ackworth on the marriage axis — and both already carry named rows. There is no third thing left for an unassembled residue to be.

**What the closing of that funnel does to the two survivors.** Ackworth rises to 9 and Hitcham to 5 not because new supporting evidence appeared for either, but because when everything around them resolves they carry more of the weight. Ackworth holds the **only** unaccounted-for John Gurney × Mary marriage in England before 1660, and the marriage is the hardest requirement in the colonial signature; Hitcham holds the **only** unaccounted-for colonial-name child to a father John in Britain, on two axes independently. Both remain single-record candidates with real blocks, and neither should be read as approaching identification.

**Falsifiability note.** A probability table is exactly the kind of artefact that looks like rigour while resting on judgement. Two guards: each row names the evidence it turns on, and the numbers are stated so they can be moved. If a Norwich Consistory marriage licence bond turns up a Francis Gurney first wife 1606-1611, B should rise sharply. If the Buckinghamshire archdeaconry wills of 1630 or 1636 name a son John who then disappears, the Buckinghamshire rows should rise. If the East Dereham household proves to be a different Francis, B should collapse toward the unassembled and no-record rows. **And if the 1626 will of Richard Gurney of Cheddington names a son John living at that date, that row should rise sharply** — a will naming children is the one document that can turn a predicted household into a documented one. **Falsifiers for the two record-state rows, since they now lead the table.** The *not-yet-searched* row falls if the south-Midlands archdeaconry licence series, the Norwich 1606-11 licence bonds and the Buckinghamshire archdeaconry wills are opened and return nothing - those are named, extant sources and the row is largely made of them. The *unassembled* row falls further if a complete county-set pass (rather than the cross-collection pool) still returns no unaccounted-for John-and-Mary household; it would rise sharply if any single county set produced a household the pool had hidden, as Lamport and Earsham already show it can. **The C(ii) row has a cheaper falsifier still:** the Berkhamsted burial register entry for 28 June 1638 will, in the ordinary form of the period, name whose son Richard was. If it reads "son of John", C(ii) should fall to near zero; if it reads "son of Thomas", C(ii) recovers part of what it lost today.

**A previously stated colonial-side check is withdrawn.** This section formerly held that the naming inference in §6.3a "depends entirely on Sarah, the supposed eldest child, not existing." That is a logical error and it should not be relied on: the naming custom counts son-slots and daughter-slots separately, so **a daughter cannot displace Richard from the eldest-son position** whether or not she existed. The Cheddington row's rationale is more robust than the earlier text allowed. Sarah remains doubtful on her own account — she is the only member of the colonial roster with no record attesting she lived, and Torrey's compendium of New England marriages before 1700 carries no Sarah Gurney — but she discriminates between nothing, because what her existence would imply (a maternal grandmother named Mary) matches neither Margaret Rybett nor Jane Founteine.<sup class="fn"><a href="#n123" id="ref-123">123</a></sup>

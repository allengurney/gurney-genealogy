# Case file: Walter de Gournay as son of Gerard de Gournay (G31/G32 junction)

**The question**: was Walter de Gournay (G31, fl. c. 1108–1154, holder of Suffolk lands under the Dampmartins in Stephen's reign) a son of Gerard de Gournay (G32) and Edith de Warenne, or not?

**Why this matters**: the repo's direct line from Allen Gurney back through the entire Norfolk junior branch (G31 → G15) and ultimately to the senior Norman barons (G32 → G37) hinges on this junction. If Walter was Gerard's son, the repo's chain holds. If he was unrelated to the senior Gournay line, the chain back to Eudes via this branch dissolves.

**Status**: the repo follows position 1 (DG view: Walter is Gerard's youngest son). This is a **conscious editorial choice**, not a settled scholarly fact.

---

## 1. The three documented positions

### Position 1 — Walter is **son** of Gerard

Five voices in the Norman / English antiquarian tradition.

**(a) Daniel Gurney, *Record of the House of Gournay* (1845), p. 70, verbatim**:

> "Walter de Gournai was, I think, undoubtedly another son of Gerard; he held lands in Suffolk under the Dampmartins in the reign of Stephen; and was ancestor of the Gournays of West Barsham in Norfolk. It appears a portion of the great fief of Bray was severed, probably at the death of Gerard, in favour of his son Walter, and his descendants, to be held by the tenure called paragium, which I have before noticed. I shall give the details of this Norman fief in the Second Part of this Record, which treats of the descendants of Walter de Gournay."

DG's confidence wording is qualified: *"I think, undoubtedly."* He combines (i) a documentary observation (Walter held Suffolk lands of the Dampmartins in Stephen's reign) and (ii) a feudal-mechanism inference (paragium tenure of a younger-son's portion of Bray) to make the case.

**(b) DG-Supp Note 104 (1858), pp. 776–777** — generational arithmetic:

> "Hugh de Gournay IV, eldest son of Gerard, was of full age in 1112; he was therefore born about 1090; and the younger sons of Gerard de Gournay were therefore born between 1090 and 1104 — the date of Gerard's death."

DG-Supp also offers a naming logic: Walter "might probably have been named by his father Gerard, after his near relation Walter Giffard, Earl of Buckingham" (Gerard's mother Basilea Flaitel's sister married Walter Giffard) "or after Walter de la Ferté."

**(c) T. J. Pettigrew, "On the House of Gournay," *Collectanea Archaeologica* vol. 2 (London 1871), pp. 185–186**:

Pettigrew's section on Gerard names one son Hugh and two daughters (one married Richard de Talbot, the other married Neil d'Albini), then adds: *"another son Renaud has been ascribed to him and that DG thought Walter de Gournai was another."* Pettigrew here transmits DG's view without independently endorsing it; his summary preserves DG's claim as the available historiography.

**(d) N.-R. P. de la Mairie, *Recherches Historiques, Archéologiques et Biographiques sur les Possessions des Sires Normands de Gournay* Tome I (Gournay-en-Bray 1852), p. 80** — explicit endorsement:

> "Girard la laissa, avec ses autres biens, à Hugues IV de Gournay, à l'exception des fiefs dont hérita son fils puîné, **Gautier, tige de la branche des Gournay de Norfolk**."

Translation: *"Girard left it [the seigneury of la Ferté], with his other goods, to Hugues IV de Gournay, with the exception of the fiefs that his younger son **Gautier, root [tige] of the branch of the Gournays of Norfolk**, inherited."*

NRP-I 1852 (uncle of Daniel Gurney's likely informant; same Gournay-local antiquarian tradition) **explicitly endorses** Walter as Girard's son and progenitor of the Norfolk branch.

**(e) Geni curators (R Green, R.B. Stewart, Mellcene Smith)**:

Multiple Geni-side curators list Walter among Gerard's children, citing variously DG, paragium-tenure logic, and family-tradition passes. The Geni profile for Gerard de Gournay (https://www.geni.com/people/Gerard-de-Gournay-Baron-of-Gournay/6000000006582994318) reproduces the DG passage verbatim and integrates it into Gerard's children list.

R.B. Stewart's "Five children" list (Geni source): "*Hugues III, Gautier, Anseau, Gonnor and Gundred*" — Stewart names a son **Gautier** (= Walter), supporting DG.

### Position 2 — Walter is **grandson** of Gerard (Pattou)

**Etienne Pattou, *Seigneurs de Gournay (-en-Bray) & Gurney*, *Racines Histoire* (last update 2025-08-11), p. 5**:

Pattou's chart describes Walter as *"possible petit-fils de Girard de Gournay et d'Edive de Warenne"* — *"possible **grandson** of Girard de Gournay and Edive de Warenne."*

This is a single source, hedged with "possible." Pattou's framing splits the difference: the Walter / senior-Gournay link is real, but generational placement is shifted to the next generation down (i.e., Walter would be a grandson of Gerard via an unnamed son or daughter). Pattou's chart-genealogy hedges this notation with characteristic `?` markers throughout the Norfolk junior branch (Pattou pp. 5–11 chart the entire Norfolk line down through John III de Gournay m. Jeanne de Lexham and the post-15th-century Antoine / François / Henry chain to Hudson Gurney of Keswick).

Pattou's "possible petit-fils" is **not** an active rejection of the senior-line connection; it is a chart-typesetting artifact of his hyper-cautious notation. But it is a different generational placement.

### Position 3 — Walter is **unrelated** to the senior Gournay line (Richardson)

**Douglas Richardson, soc.genealogy.medieval (SGM) post 11 Sep 2002** (preserved in the FS export at PDF pp. 19–21 of the LBGV-H99 family group record):

> "Gurney Rec. of the House of Gournay (): 286-289, 292-293, 776-777 [author alleges that Gerard de Gournay and his wife, Ediva, had a younger son, Walter de Gournay (living 1150), ancestor of the Gurnay family of Norfolk. Such an individual existed but he had no association in the records with the senior branch of the Gournay family. Rather, it appears that Walter was closely related to a Galiena de Gournay, born say 1120, living c. 1170, wife of Manasser de Dammartin (died 1178/9), of Mendlesham and Cotton, Suffolk, Norton Mandeville, Essex, etc. Surviving records show that Manasser de Dammartin enfeoffed Walter de Gumay with a 1/4 knight's fee in Suffolk during the Civil War between King Stephen and Empress Maud. In 1150 Walter de Gournay witnessed a charter to Missenden Abbey for Manasser and Galiena. Charter evidence proves that Galiena was the granddaughter of an unplaced William de Gumay, of Addington, Kent, whose gift to the see of Rochester she confirmed by her own undated charter. Given these facts, it is doubtful that Walter de Gournay was the son of Gerard de Gournay as alleged."

Richardson's bibliography (the most extensive modern apparatus on the question):

Richardson's structural argument:
- Walter de Gumay (Suffolk) is documented in 1150 as a witness to a charter for Manasser de Dammartin and his wife Galiena.
- Galiena de Gournay was granddaughter of an "unplaced" **William de Gumay of Addington, Kent** — not of the senior Norman line.
- The relationship between Walter and Galiena is **uncle-niece or similar**, both descending from the unplaced William de Gumay rather than from Gerard.

Richardson's evidence is **structurally independent** of the French local-tradition cluster: it draws on English-side feudal and charter material (Suffolk, Essex, Kent) that DG, Pettigrew, and NRP-I did not engage with. Richardson is the modern voice arguing the Walter-Norfolk-line documentation does not require descent from Gerard.

## 2. Evidence cluster analysis

The Position 1 cluster (DG, DG-Supp, Pettigrew, NRP-I, Geni curators) all derive from the **same local Gournay antiquarian tradition**:

```
Cordier MS Histoire de Gournay (c. 1710-1738)
   ↓
Langloys notes (avocat, late 17th c.)
   ↓
René Potin notes (date unknown)
   ↓
Pierre Potin de la Mairie 1842 (Tome I Recherches sur la ville)
   ↓
N.-R. P. de la Mairie 1852 (Recherches sur les Possessions, T. I, p. 80)
   ↓
Daniel Gurney 1845 / 1848 / 1858 Supplement
   ↓
Pettigrew 1871 (Collectanea Archaeologica)
   ↓
Geni curators (R Green, R.B. Stewart, Mellcene Smith)
```

Position 3 (Richardson 2002) is **structurally independent** of this entire chain. Richardson draws on:
- English-side feudal records (Hasted, Copinger).
- English-side charter publications (Loyd & Stenton, Jenkins, Gervers).
- 20th-century local histories (*VCH Essex*, *Genealogist*, Tanner).
- 21st-century scholarship (Power *Norman Frontier*).

The two evidence bodies do not directly contradict — they describe different documentary corpora. **Both could be right**: there could have been a Walter who was Gerard's son (per the French tradition) and *another* Walter who was Galiena's relative (per the English documents). DG himself acknowledges (1845 p. 70) that "there is some confusion" in the available material.

But the documentary evidence Richardson cites for Galiena and her grandfather William de Gumay of Addington is *concrete* (specific charters, specific holdings), while the French local-tradition evidence is structurally diffuse (no specific charter naming Walter as son of Gerard; only DG's combination of paragium-tenure logic + generational arithmetic + Suffolk documentation).

## 3. The paragium-tenure mechanism

DG's positive case for Walter = son of Gerard relies on the **paragium tenure**: a medieval feudal arrangement where a younger son holds a portion of his father's fief under his elder brother as feudal superior. DG-I p. 70 says: *"a portion of the great fief of Bray was severed, probably at the death of Gerard, in favour of his son Walter."*

This is a **plausible feudal mechanism**: paragium tenure was indeed used for younger-son inheritance arrangements in 11th–12th-century Normandy. But the evidence for *specifically Walter* being the recipient of *specifically a paragium portion* of *specifically the Bray fief* is DG's editorial inference from the structural pattern of feudal tenure, not a direct charter attestation.

---

## 4. Generational arithmetic

DG-Supp Note 104 makes the chronological case:
- Hugh de Gournay IV (Gerard's eldest son): of full age 1112 → born c. 1090.
- Younger sons (including Walter) born c. 1090–1104 (1104 = the year of Gerard's death).
- Walter active c. 1108–1154 (DG's range; Richardson's "living 1150" is consistent).
- Walter's grandson Matthew first appears c. 1160 — consistent with two intervening generations (Walter → William → Matthew).

The arithmetic works, but doesn't *require* Walter to be Gerard's son specifically — only that a Walter active 1108–1154 is consistent with being a son of someone born c. 1050–1080.

Richardson's reading (Walter as nephew or cognate of Galiena, who was granddaughter of an unplaced William de Gumay of Addington Kent) is also chronologically consistent: William de Gumay born c. 1080–1090, Walter as son or nephew of William, born c. 1110, active 1150, dying after 1170.

---

## 5. The repo's editorial choice

The repo follows **Position 1** (DG view) for the following reasons:

1. **Multi-witness depth in the Norman tradition**: five voices in the same local-Gournay antiquarian transmission cluster. The names DG, Potin de la Mairie, NRP de la Mairie, and the Cordier MS chain all converge on Walter as Gerard's son.

2. **The paragium-tenure mechanism is plausible**: even if not directly attested for Walter specifically, the feudal pattern of younger-son inheritance via paragium fits the documentary evidence (Walter holding Suffolk lands of the Dampmartins, who were a Norman frontier family aligned with the Gournays).

3. **DG's generational arithmetic stands**: Hugh IV "of full age 1112" → c. 1090 → younger sons born c. 1090–1104 → consistent with Walter active 1108–1154.

4. **The naming pattern**: DG-Supp's observation that Walter could have been named after Walter Giffard (Gerard's mother's brother-in-law) or Walter de la Ferté (a Gournay collateral) is structurally supportive.

5. **The Norfolk-line continuity**: the West Barsham Gurneys, the Keswick Gurneys, the banking Gurneys — all the descendants of Walter — preserved a strong tradition of descent from the senior Norman line. This is itself a piece of evidence (later than the documentary base, but structurally significant): the family's own tradition consistently held that Walter was Gerard's son. While later-tradition is weaker than primary attestation, it is consistent with Position 1 and not with Position 3.

The repo's adoption of Position 1 is a **conscious editorial choice**. The case is not closed in modern scholarship; Richardson's argument is real and the bibliography supporting it is extensive. **Future work would benefit from**:

- Direct examination of the Hasted, Copinger, Loyd & Stenton, *VCH Essex*, *Genealogist*, Jenkins, Gervers, Power, and Tanner sources Richardson cites.
- An attempt to reconcile the two evidence bodies — could the senior-line Walter and the Addington-Kent Walter be the same person, with the Addington connection being a marriage or feudal acquisition rather than descent? This would let both traditions coexist.
- A primary-document search for the 1150 Missenden charter, to see whether Walter de Gumay is named in a way that ties him explicitly to either the senior Norman line or the Addington-Kent line.

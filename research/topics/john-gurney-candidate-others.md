## Same-name eliminators and comparators (from the G13 origin analysis)

### John Gurnell, freeman 1643 — a distinct surname, not John Gurney-1

A separate "John Gurnell" admitted freeman in Massachusetts on **10 May 1643** is **not** the emigrant John Gurney-1. Because surviving 1600s Massachusetts Gurnell records are sparse, the spelling invites a Gurnell → Gurney reading; that reading does not hold. A **30 July 1675 Dorchester death entry, examined directly, reads "Gurnell"** — confirming Gurnell as a real, distinct surname rather than a clerk's variant of Gurney. The 1643 freeman therefore belongs to that family, not to John Gurney-1, who first appears at Weymouth in June 1641 and is consistently recorded under Gurney/Gurny/Garny variants, never Gurnell.[^paige-freemen-g13]

### English same-name elimination - St Mary Harrow 1668/9 and St Giles Cripplegate 1640

Two London-area parish-register burial entries reinforce that several English John Gurneys with overlapping wife names continued to live, marry, and bury children in England well after the 1641 Massachusetts emigrant record. Each is a small but real elimination check rather than a candidate for the emigrant.

St Mary, Harrow on the Hill (LMA DRO/003/A/01/005) records two burials in early 1669 (Old Style 1668) under the heading "Buryed 1668": "Janry 30 - Ann/Anne daughter of John & Mary Gurney of Okington" and "Feby 8 - Isaac son of John & Mary Gurney of Okington." A John Gurney with a wife named Mary, still parenting and burying children in England in early 1669, cannot be John Gurney-1, who died at Braintree in 1662/3. The "Okington/Oakington" residence is most likely Oakington, Cambridgeshire, but the second-syllable letters are partly obscured. Keep this John & Mary Gurney as a separate English household for any future Mary-named-wife John Gurney work.[^lma-harrow-1669]

St Giles Cripplegate, City of London (LMA P69/GIS/A/002/MS06419/003) records a burial in December 1640: "John sonne of ffrancis Garney Joyner - 15," indexed as 16 December 1640 with "Mother: Francis Garney." The index field is almost certainly the father's name, and "Joyner/Joiner" is the father's occupation, not part of the surname. This is a London child burial of a John, son of Francis Garney, joiner, in the same St Giles Cripplegate parish where the case file already records a separate Francis B "the laceweaver" Cripplegate cluster active 1638-1640. The trade difference (joiner vs laceweaver) means this Francis Garney is almost certainly a third Cripplegate-area Gurney/Garney, not the same Francis B already eliminated in the case file. Garney sits inside the Gurney/Gurny/Gourney/Garney spelling cluster and should be retained when searching same-name London households.[^lma-cripplegate-1640]

The Toddington Beds John Gurney + Elizabeth Moreton household has positive English-side death evidence: John Gurney was buried at Toddington in September 1641, three months after the Massachusetts emigrant's June 1641 Weymouth fine date and twenty-one years before the Massachusetts John's 1662/3 Braintree death. The Toddington John cannot also be in continuous Massachusetts residence through 1662/3, so the Toddington row is firmly eliminated on death-evidence grounds rather than on the wife-name alone.[^toddington-burial-2026-05-11]

## Wider Norfolk and same-name Gurney records (comparators)

Same-name Gurney households and Norfolk records that surfaced during the origin campaign. Each is a comparator or elimination aid for the John Gurney problem, not the emigrant himself; full treatment of those with their own subjects lives on the linked subject files.

**A Gurney probate inventory at Norwich, c. 1613–1619 — a Gurney decedent inside the origin window.** A probate inventory indexed under the extracted name "Septeph Gurney" — almost certainly **Stephen Gurney** — survives for Norwich, dated 1613–1619.[^stephen-gurney-inventory-2026-06] The transcript is a heavily garbled room-by-room household inventory (buttery, pantry, cupboards, beds, "at the Grange," a long table and forms), so the parish, the exact year, and whether the named Gurney is the decedent or an appraiser are not legible and require an image read. If it is a Gurney decedent's own inventory it is the first probate of a Gurney *of the emigrant's father/grandfather generation* found inside Norfolk, and it nuances the "no Gurney testator in the window" bounding result below — which rests on the Norwich Consistory *registered-copy will* registers and would not capture an inventory or an Archdeaconry-court estate. Most plausibly a Norwich-city Gurney (cf. the St Peter Parmentergate plebeian line), not yet tied to West Barsham or to Francis G14's East Dereham household.

The Norwich Consistory Court registered-copy will registers for **1624–1637** (vols 119–130) — not previously mapped to FamilySearch image-group numbers — were resolved from the catalogue (record `koha:278818`) and swept for Gurn*/Gourn*/Gorn*, and the 1638–1651 volumes re-checked. Across the whole window the Consistory series carries **no Gurney testator of its own** beyond the already-known John Gurney of Earsham (will 1638, vol. 131–132 = DGS 008076858) — a useful bounding result: a humble Gurney testator in the emigrant's father's window is not in the Consistory registers, which redirects the search to the **Archdeaconry of Norwich** court (the lesser-estate jurisdiction; see leads). Three in-window Gurney *sightings* surfaced, each named inside another person's will and each a machine-transcript read with the full-resolution image staged for expert transcription:

- **Clement Gurney, Gent** — a testator's will (NCC regd. wills 1631–32, vol. 124–125, DGS 008076514, [ark 3:1:3Q9M-CSN8-YRB3](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSN8-YRB3)) bequeaths a bond "bearing date the one and twentieth day of November Anno Dm 1622 … wherein [Clement] Gurne[y] Gent [was bound] … in the penal sum of forty pounds." A *gentleman* Gurney active c. 1622–31; the forename Clement is rare and recurs nowhere else in FamilySearch full-text before the 19th century, so this register page is its sole witness. An image read confirms **"Clement Gurney gent"** (high confidence) and the bond — penal sum £40 securing £20, dated 21 November 1622 — which the testator bequeaths to "Clement my sonne." The testator's name and parish lie on the will's *opening*, not on this continuation page; the full will was traced across three register images and staged as **packet 20** (still pending) to recover them, the open crux being whether "Clement my sonne" *is* "Clement Gurney gent" (so the testator himself a Gurney) or two distinct Clements (L-138).[^paleo-ncc-sightings-2026-06]

- **John Gurney's house, Langley** — a 1634 will (NCC regd. wills vol. 127, DGS 008076861, [ark 3:1:3Q9M-CSND-T9KS-K](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSND-T9KS-K)) devising meadow "lying in Langley … next unto John Gurney's house." **East Dereham — a Gurney household predates Francis G14's residence: Margery Gurney, widow, dead by 1602–03, her Ellingfield copyhold remembered in the courts for decades.** The Norfolk probate master index carries a **will of Margery Gurney (indexed "Gurnye"), widow, of East Dereham, proved 1602–03** in the **Archdeaconry of Norfolk (ANF)** court — register Liber 35 (Offwood), folio 191 (NRO microfilm MF 197; FamilySearch film 008045772). Her widowhood puts a Gurney husband dead at East Dereham **before 1602** — roughly seven years before John G13's documented baptism there (c. 1609/10) and before Francis G14's documented residence years — and her landholding is independently visible in the manor court: at the court of **3 April 1627** (folio 25), Christopher Fuller was admitted to about fourteen acres "apud Ellingfeild in East Dereham" abutting west on *"terram nuper Margerie Gurney vid[ue]"* — land **formerly** of Margery Gurney, widow — and the court of 10 October 1636 repeats the parcel memory as *"terram quondam Margarete Gurney vid[ue]"* (the same court as the Hugh Browne surrender below; the fuller abuttal context names the late Francis Elwyn, clerk, Osborne, and Roger Daynes as neighbours). The operative *nuper/quondam* ("late/formerly") shows the Gurney tenancy at Ellingfield was already a past reference by 1627, consistent with the 1602–03 will. An expert walk of the **entire surviving 1623–1635 court book** found the 1627 abuttal to be the **only** secure Gurney occurrence — no Gurney admission, surrender, presentment, or homage appearance, and no Rivett or Podmer — so the family's connection to the manor in John's own boyhood was a remembered parcel, not an active tenancy.[^margery-anf-2026-07][^dereham-abuttals-2026-07] Taken together this is the strongest available answer to the open **"why East Dereham?"** question: an established Gurney copyhold household existed in the parish a generation before Francis G14 baptised his children there (c. 1609/10–1633), making a pre-existing kin presence a plausible reason he settled his young family five miles from the Great Ellingham seat. Whether Margery's household connects to Francis, to the later Matthew/Richard Gurney tenants, or to neither is untested — her will is the test, and **its image is gated**: the ANF register film is affiliate-library-restricted on FamilySearch (all leaves padlocked; the download route returns HTTP 403), FindMyPast and Ancestry carry the index only (each linking out to the NRO to order a copy), and the NRO's own digitised will images begin at 1800 — so the will must be read at a FamilySearch affiliate library, at the NRO, or by paid copy order. ("Ellingfield" is a field-name locus *within* East Dereham parish — the echo of Great Ellingham is a name coincidence, not a tenurial link.)[^margery-anf-2026-07]

**East Dereham — the widow's tenure begins *before* October 1636, and the 1697 tenant is Matthew, not Michael.** The 1636 card resolves to a court of **10 October 1636 (12 Charles I)** at which Hugh Browne, gentleman, surrendered a holding described as "…inter alia ten[ementa] **quondam Margarete Gurner vid[uae]**" — *formerly* of Margaret Gurner, widow. No husband, no "nuper uxor," and no male Gurney predecessor is visible. Two consequences, both now sharpened by the 1602–03 will and the 1627 abuttal above: (1) the widow herself was dead by 1602–03, so **the male Gurney whose death made her a widow died before 1602** — a generation *before* Francis G14's documented East Dereham years, not within them; (2) the Edward-free-bench hypothesis (Edward b. c.1610/11 as the deceased husband) is **eliminated for this widow** — Edward was not yet born when she was already a widow. The 1697 page resolves to **13 May 1697**, with a recital of a customary messuage, meadow, and ~5 acres in several pieces "prout nup' in tenura **Mathei Gurney**" — **Matthew**, not the machine-read "Michael," and his tenure had already ended by that date; the page's Margaret is Dame Margaret **Castleton**, not Gurney. The 18th-century "Michael Gurney" recitals in the later English-language entries therefore need re-checking against "Matthew." The 1662/1665 page could not be securely read (Loveland/Rogers parties visible; the Gurney target is present per the card but visually buried) — unresolved, not negative.[^paleo-dereham-2026-06] A dedicated full-text re-hunt of the 1623–1689 book (DGS 004389483) for any *earlier male* Gurney before the October 1636 court returned **no new candidate** — the male whose death widowed Margaret is not surfaced by full-text and remains to be found by a folio-level read of the earlier courts.[^paleo-dereham23-2026-06]

A continuing **East Dereham copyhold Gurney family** runs through the volume: land "late of Margery Gurney" in abuttal recitals (her tenure dating to the 1680s entries in the earlier roll); parcels "in tenura Michaelis [Michael] Gurney" 1697–1758, recited as "late in the Tenure of Michael Gurney" into c.1800; and Richard Gurney from 1703, recited to 1837 — meadow and pasture parcels (typically three roods) near Moorgate Street, East Dereham. Separately, the Norwich banking Gurneys appear as transaction parties: John Gurney of Norwich, merchant, 1756; Joseph Gurney, Richard Hanbury Gurney, and Joseph John Gurney as mortgage parties, 1818–19. **Case relevance:** a local Gurney copyhold family persisted at East Dereham from at least the 1680s. Whether their holding walks back through the 1623–1689 roll into Francis G14's residence years — most temptingly through **Edward** (b. c.1610/11, John's full brother, fate unknown; see Open Questions) — is now a precise, answerable question, because manorial recitals chain each tenant to the previous one. Alternatively an unrelated East Dereham Gurney family would itself be a material comparator discovery.[^dereham-court-sweep-2026-06]

**L-5 — the Spelman Gurney pedigree is a dispersed manuscript, not online.** Daniel Gurney saw the pedigree "amongst the Spelman manuscripts, collected by Dr. Macro, in the possession of Hudson Gurney."

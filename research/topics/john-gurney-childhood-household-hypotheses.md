# John Gurney G13 — childhood household and formation hypotheses

This is a prioritization aid, not a finding that John was fostered.  It tests the possibility that, if Margaret Rybett died before or near Francis Gurney's remarriage, John and likely Edward spent time in another household or learned a trade outside Francis's formal company route.  Neither Margaret's death, John's parentage, nor any placement is proved.

**Priority is comparative, not a probability of custody.** Several rows can be true together: John might have lived with a relative, trained under another master, and later used a patronage or migration network. “Network” rows are leads to test, not claims of residence.

**Related working files:** [Rivett family of Garveston](../people/rivett-family-of-garveston.md), [Francis Gurney G14](../people/g14-francis-gurney-fact-sheet.research.md), [Francis Gurney and the L'Estrange household network](francis-gurney-lestrange-household-network.md), and [G13 research library](../people/g13-john-gurney/topics/00-research-library.md). ## Best first sequence

1. **Whinburgh-with-Members, 1604–1626.** This can resolve Margaret's parentage and may directly name an heir, guardian, or household.
2. **L'Estrange account text and originals.** Reconstruct Francis's role around Roger, then search the earlier 1612–26 run for John, Edward, dependents, and addresses.
3. **Henry G15's Great Ellingham archive.** Extract the named circle first, then test households in Great Ellingham, Hingham, Attleborough, and Deopham.
4. **Hingham / West Dereham.** Resolve Ann Gurney before treating the Gilman household as a sibling or migration route.
5. **Gressenhall and Garveston.** Test the two maternal-household models against named dependents and trade paths.

## Source and access findings

- **Hunstanton offers a pre-remarriage Browning connection, but no £30 entry amount.** Database entry 5249, dated 21 November 1615 in `LEST/P7` section 027, names Mr Browning of Norwich, Francis Gurney, and Lock; its entered currency fields are blank. The April 1616 evidence is two adjacent rows: 5459 records Francis’s payment through Lock the tailor, margin “Lock 20,” and 5460 records books at Norwich, margin “20,” without a Francis people link. These details strengthen Browning as a business-network lead before Anne's marriage, but prove neither kinship nor a child's residence.[^hunstanton-account-detail][^lestrange-ukda]
- **The L'Estrange database is valuable but does not contain the 1634 answer.** UK Data Archive study 5726 indexes and transcribes a selected 1606–1626 sample from `LEST/P6` through `LEST/P11`; its coverage of 1630–54 is a document summary, not entry transcription.[^lestrange-accounts-catalog] It can still establish Francis's earlier role and aliases, but the Roger/Eaton sequence needs the relevant post-1626 NRO account-book image or microfilm, not a database search.
- **The 1634 source pull can now be specified more tightly.** The original account books are NRO `LEST/P6–P11`; all were microfilmed for the project, and the guide says copies can be ordered. Ask first for the 1634 disbursement-account section containing the Roger, `Eaton`, trunks, Francis Gurney, and Cambridge entries, with its immediately preceding and following account pages. That is the only way to restore dates, headings, amounts, and sequence lost in Daniel Gurney's disrupted extract.[^lestrange-accounts-catalog]
- **Great Ellingham is not one manorial search.** The parish had Ellingham Hall, Ellingham Rectory, and Bury Hall, while some local holdings answered to additional outsoken manors.[^ellingham-manors] An early Gurney household can therefore be missed by a single-manor search.
- **Great Yarmouth offers a defined fostering/apprenticeship test.** Rutledge's 1563–1665 enrolled-indenture calendar has an index, so Rivett and Ryvett variants can be checked without first reading every borough-court entry.[^yarmouth-apprentices] No relevant entry was located in the public catalogue material.
- **Henry G15's manuscript is an earlier network map, not a crisis-year household record.** Its dated contents run only to about 1608.[^tanner-dating] It remains valuable for identifying people and estates to trace forward into 1615–1623.
- **Hingham now has a defined core court series.** Eight Hingham Manor rolls survive for 1583–1619 (`KIM 1/6/26–34`), followed by a 1620–21 rental and 1628–39 bailiffs' accounts.[^hingham-manor-series] These are the right sequence for testing a Gurney or Gilman tenant, dependent, or household connection.
- **Hingham needs the register spelling as well as the modern surname.** The known 1626 marriage reads Ann *Girnye*, so an exact `Gurney` index search can miss the very household that makes Hingham relevant.[^gilman]
- **The Spelman lead is metropolitan as well as Norfolk.** Sir Henry Spelman moved permanently to London in 1612, four years before his son Francis's binding to Francis G14.[^spelman-lucas] Its published 1612–1618 letter conspectus names no Gurney, so the binding remains the sole link and does not support an assumed Middleton/Congham residence for John.
- **The 1636 Newgate indenture is a control, not a placement lead.** Its John Gurney was still an apprentice until 1639 and implies a birth about 1615, too young to be the colonial John.[^newgate-control] Do not use Newgate's Suffolk connection to explain this John's childhood.
- **King's Lynn is a bounded training context, not a presumed placement.** The printed **11 October 1622** deed is a formal civic contract, not an ordinary private apprentice indenture. King’s Lynn advanced £200 to Francis Gurney, Ambrose Tompson, and Martyne Hill for three years. In return, the three men were to live in the borough until Michaelmas 1625, provide wool or other material to poor borough residents seeking work, and teach children whom the mayor and aldermen sent to them. The contract says those children had to live in the borough or its liberties, and it allowed the firm the benefit of each child’s work for the first four months without an allowance.[^lynn-residence] In plain terms, the town financed a local textile-work scheme and required the men to relocate, employ residents, and train selected local children.

- **What the Lynn agreement proves—and what it does not.** It creates a strong reason to test whether Francis actually lived in King’s Lynn from October 1622 to Michaelmas 1625, and whether borough records name trainees or a household. It does not prove that he complied personally, that John or Edward lived there, or that either boy was one of the locally resident children the town could send. The HMC calendar nevertheless describes a **17 October** agreement; the original Hall Book and sealed deed must determine whether these are two instruments, a calendar error, or a transcription issue.[^lynn]

- **The Rivett witness is a strong relationship lead, not a proved relationship.** The full printed deed ends `Teste me—Tho Rivett coi clico`. Francis’s prior marriage to Margaret Rybett makes a Rivett/Rybett kin or affinity connection the project’s strong working presumption; the approximate “100:1” weighting is a research prior, not a measured source result. The printed form does not identify Thomas, say that he acted for Francis, or state his degree of kinship, so an original or an independently identified man is still required before naming the relationship.[^lynn-rivett-witness]

- **The HMC calendar now has its full printed wording in the repository, but it remains narrower than the deed.** [`bho-hmc-kings-lynn-francis-gurney-1622.md`](../../sources/corpus_supplement/bho-hmc-kings-lynn-francis-gurney-1622.md) preserves the HMC's 17 October calendar of an agreement to teach poor Lynn children worsted spinning, provide wool, employ the poor, and pay non-learners fit wages. It does not give the 11 October deed’s residence clause, bond, witness line, arbitration clause, or four-month learner term. The two printed sources therefore corroborate the venture while preserving a date/instrument discrepancy that only the original material can resolve.[^lynn]

- **Do not silently normalize “Francz Guybon” to Francis Gurnay.** A 30 June 1626 Lynn entry prints a new £100 obligation by “Francz Guybon”; Daniel Gurney’s editorial note says that this “seems wrongly written for Francis Gurnay.” A 1613 official catalogue entry independently places the established Guybon surname in the King’s Lynn area. Until the manuscript is read, the 1626 name is genuinely ambiguous and cannot be used as proof that Francis Gurney incurred that later liability.[^lynn-guybon]

- **Maldon offers a defined Browning-record test, not a proven household.** The same printed appendix records a 1583 All Saints, Maldon marriage of William Browning and Dorothy Vernon, and a 1636 borough-account payment by “Mr Browning” for coal dues; its editor could not identify the relevant Browning branch. These entries make Maldon merchant and parish records worth a focused household search, but they do not identify Anne’s father or place John or Edward there.[^browning-maldon]

## Record-level results: Francis's household and residence

- **Francis's house handled a named young man's transition.** The later account extracts preserve a more coherent cluster than the isolated quotations suggest: "To Roger at Eaton by Frank Gurney" precedes charges for bringing Roger's trunks and bedding from Eaton to Francis's house, Francis's journey "for Roger to Eaton," shipping the bedding and trunks, and porters taking a Mr. L'Estrange trunk from his lodging to Francis's and "thence to Cambridge." A separate 1631 entry says that Francis's bill for Roger included 20s. "for his master."[^lestrange-hosting][^lestrange-roger-sequence] Together these show Francis disbursing for, travelling for, housing baggage for, and forwarding a young L'Estrange in an education/transition context.

- **Roger L'Estrange and Eton are now the best working identification, but still not a proved reading of the account.** Venn's Roger, born 1616, is the right age and is independently said to have attended Sedgeford, Westminster, and Eton before entering Sidney Sussex on 6 November 1634.[^roger-cambridge] Thus `Eaton` is no longer a bare geographical guess: Eton is a strong contextual reading of the printed string. But only the original account can prove that its Roger is Sir Hamon's third son, that `Eaton` is Eton, the location of Francis's house, and whether all the quoted payments belong to one 1634 journey.

- **The analogy has a defined limit.** The account cluster makes Francis a documented adult intermediary in another family's son's schooling and Cambridge move; it does not name John or Edward, establish a Gurney child's residence, or show that Francis used the same arrangement for his own children.

- **The likely Roger has an independently documented Cambridge timetable.** Roger L'Estrange, third son of Sir Hamon, was born at Hunstanton in 1616, attended Sedgeford, Westminster, and Eton, and entered Sidney Sussex College as a fellow-commoner on 6 November 1634.[^roger-cambridge] The school and date make the account cluster a strong lead for reading the original L'Estrange book, but the printed extraction's sequence is too broken to claim a fully proved journey.

- **The 1606–26 database reconstructs Francis’s earlier business role, but not the 1634 event.** Its people and place indexes and original/modern spelling fields yield 31 Francis-linked entries from 1612–26, including Lock and Lawrence Michael relationships, East India Company and apparel payments, deliveries to “Mr Gurney’s,” and 1625–26 Lynn obligations. It separately records John Spratt’s 1616 apprenticeship to Michael and 1618 apprenticeship activity, but names no John or Edward Gurney. The full ledger and limits are in the Francis–L’Estrange topic; none of this permits a database reconstruction of Roger’s 1634 route.[^lestrange-accounts-catalog][^lestrange-ukda]

## Proposed Francis Gurney residence timeline — working model, not evidence

The 1622 deed changes the best reconstruction of Francis's movements. Its condition was not a loose business connection: it required each of the three partners to **“inhabit and dwell” in King’s Lynn until Michaelmas 1625** (29 September 1625). The competing HMC calendar is dated 17 October, rather than the deed's 11 October, but neither printed source changes the stated three-year residence condition.[^lynn-residence] The table treats parish events as evidence that a household used a place, not as automatic proof that Francis personally slept there on every date.

## Record-level results: Whinburgh and Hingham

- **The target Whinburgh leaf is dated by the manuscript: 6 September 1639.** It is [ARK 3:1:S3HT-6X4K-X5](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:S3HT-6X4K-X5), not the separate 1648 comparator. The upper-right entry contains the Henry/Allen passage, but its struck-through relationship wording remains unresolved. It supplies neither an image-supported Gurney relationship nor evidence of a placement.[^whinburgh-1639-paleo]

- **The 1648 comparator is a different leaf.** [ARK 3:1:S3HT-6X45-XZ](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:S3HT-6X45-XZ) visibly reads 26 October 1648 at upper right (with April 1648 on the left). FamilySearch full-text's “7 of Sept 1879” is a transcript error, never a source date, and should not be used to discredit the 1639 leaf.[^whinburgh-1648-paleo]

- **The variant strings remain unconfirmed search leads.** Full-text returned `Gurnee`, `Gurnell`, `Gurnip`, and `Gurnd`; no one is presently an image-read Gurney surname. Do not use them for John, Edward, or a fostering claim without a page-level reading.[^whinburgh-fts]

- **A direct-line spelling variant has usable content.** A 22 December 1602 petition from Corpus Christi fellows to Sir Robert Cecil is signed “Edmund Gurnay, prœlector top.” It confirms this uncle's `Gurnay` form and Cambridge teaching role, but not a nephew's residence or schooling.[^edmund-1602]

- **A direct-line spelling control has real content.** Henry's 1614 will calls him Henry *Gurnay* and says, “Concerning my children, forasmuch as most of them have had theyr portions delivered them, as Thomas, Henry, Edmund, Anthony, Francis, Leonard, Elizabeth, and Anne.” Francis had therefore received his portion by 1614; this says nothing about where Francis's children lived after Margaret's likely death.[^henry-1614]

- **Hingham's manor history narrows, rather than proves, the connection.** Blomefield writes that Gurney's Manor “continued always in the family of that name residing at Barsham and Great-Elingham,” then: “Henry Gurney was lord in 1572; how it passed afterwards I do not find.” A later source places John Haynes at the house or manor around his c.1616 Hingham marriage to Mary Thornton. No source yet connects Haynes to Francis, John, Edward, or a Gurney tenancy. The defined original sequence is eight KIM court rolls for 1583–1619, then the 1620–21 rental and 1628–39 bailiffs' accounts; each needs a variant-aware read for `Gurney`/`Gurnay`/`Gourney`/`Girney`/`Girnye`, Gilman, Haynes, Thornton, and household-status terms.[^hingham-manor-status][^haynes-hingham][^hingham-manor-series]

- **Great Ellingham is a potential paternal-household test with a specific succession crisis.** By 23 March 1615 Thomas Gurnay had died. His widow Martha held his evidences, while their son and heir Edward was about five and required an office concerning his estate. The 1615–16 inventory must therefore be sought with the administration, guardianship or wardship, and manorial material—not because any record yet places John there, but because that bundle may name the widow, minor heir, servants, or collateral Gurney children in the years after Margaret’s likely death.[^thomas][^thomas-1615]

## Explicit working inferences — not evidence

The following are search models, not findings. The percentages are subjective triage estimates, not statistical measurements. A model becomes evidence only if a record supplies the predicted identity, relationship, residence, or child.

- **The 1622 witness is almost certainly acting as a borough officer, not as kin — the earlier ~100:1 kinship weighting is withdrawn.** The subscription reads `Teste me—Tho Rivett coi clico`, and the abbreviation expands most naturally as *communi clerico* — **common clerk**, the town clerk of King's Lynn. That reading turns the attestation into exactly what one expects at the foot of a borough instrument: the corporation's own clerk attesting a deed the corporation is party to. The document is a civic contract with the mayor and aldermen, not a private indenture, so an officer's teste is the default, not the anomaly.

  The Lynn record then supplies a coherent civic career for the same man rather than a kinsman: a Thomas Rivett styled "gentleman of Lynne" in 1629, and **Alderman** Thomas Rivett ordered to send for Matthew Hopkins in 1646.[^rivett-lynn-1629][^rivett-lynn-1646] A town clerk in 1622 who is a gentleman by 1629 and an alderman by 1646 is an ordinary progression through borough office.

  Francis's marriage to a Margaret Rybett remains a real fact, and a Rivett/Rybett kin or affinity link to the Lynn family remains possible — Rivett is well attested at King's Lynn St Margaret's in the 1620s. But **the indenture subscription is no longer evidence for it.** Treating a probable office-holder's formal attestation as a kinship signal was reasoning from surname coincidence in a town where the surname is common and the office explains the signature completely. The prior should be roughly even, and the burden sits on finding a record that joins the Lynn Rivetts to Margaret's Garveston family — not on the teste line.[^marriage][^lynn-rivett-witness][^rivett-lynn-1629][^rivett-lynn-1646]

- **The civic Lynn Thomas is a promising way to identify the presumed relation, not an alternative to it.** A Thomas Rivett described as a “gentleman of Lynne” in a reported 11 October 1629 incident and Alderman Thomas Rivett in a 1646 town order form a coherent same-name, same-town sequence with the 1622 witness.[^rivett-lynn-1629][^rivett-lynn-1646] They may be one adult, but no wife, age, office, estate, or signature yet joins either later record to the subscription.

- **The 1640 father is only a weak extension of the Lynn/Rivett model (~15–25%).** The supplied Findmypast transcript names Thomas and Margaret Rivet as parents of Thomas, baptized 21 March 1640, but its `PD 669/1` reference puts them at Ellingham near Bungay. A mature Lynn man could in principle have had a late child baptized in a different parish, but a same-name local household is equally possible. The record cannot be used to place a Rivett household at Great Ellingham or to connect it to Martin Hill.[^rivett-ellingham-1640]

- **The Lynn scheme remains a possible setting for John and Edward only at the lowest evidentiary level (~5–10%).** Their likely ages in 1622 were compatible with textile instruction, but the contract limited the town-selected learners to children living in the borough or liberties. A placement hypothesis would need two additional facts now missing: that either boy lived in Lynn and that borough authorities sent him to the firm. The contract alone supplies neither.[^lynn-residence]

- **The fastest test is now office-first, not relationship-first.** Read the attestation clause on any other King's Lynn borough instrument of 1620–1625: if the same `Tho Rivett coi clico` subscribes them too, the office reading is settled and the kinship line closes. That is a cheaper and more decisive test than pursuing Thomas as a relative, and it should be run before any further Rivett-affinity work on the Lynn side. An Ellingham-near-Bungay register sweep for Thomas and Margaret's marriage, other children, and burials remains a separate identity question of low value. Even a documented Rivett connection would not by itself place John or Edward in Lynn.[^lynn-rivett-witness][^rivett-ellingham-1640]

## Campaign boundary after this pass

- **No reviewed record supports a fostering, residence, apprenticeship, or mentorship placement for John or Edward.** The L'Estrange accounts now demonstrate an analogous adult role by Francis for Roger L'Estrange, not a placement of a Gurney child. The Whinburgh leaves, full-text variants, Newgate control, and published Spelman correspondence do not change that result.
- **Do not reopen generic Gurney-variant full-text searching without an image-readable target.** `Gurnee`, `Gurnell`, `Gurnip`, and `Gurnd` are retained only as search cues, not people.
- **The material open gates are now specific:** the unfilmed Whinburgh 1604–1626 rolls/surrenders; the **post-1626** L'Estrange account pages (not the database sample); the exact King's Lynn Hall Book/indenture; Hingham KIM rolls/rental; and Great Ellingham probate, wardship, and manor records. These can produce the named household evidence the hypothesis requires.[^whinburgh][^lestrange-accounts-catalog][^lynn-residence][^hingham-manor-series][^thomas]

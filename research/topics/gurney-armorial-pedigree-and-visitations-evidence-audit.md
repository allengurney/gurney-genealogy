# Gurney armorial pedigree and visitations evidence audit

## Purpose

This note corrects and extends the earlier broad heraldry note. The main target is the armorial evidence that may corroborate the medieval and Tudor direct line, especially the multi-part shield and the visitation-style pedigree evidence.

Two separate eight-part evidence sets must be kept distinct:

1. **Walsingham armorial pedigree, Shield No. 19** — an eight-quarter West Barsham Gurney shield preserved in stained glass remnants and described by Daniel Gurney.
2. **West Barsham church arms list** — an eight-family list recorded by Armstrong in 1781, mostly impalements with Gurney.

The Walsingham shield is probably the item being recalled as the subdivided arms. It is more directly useful as a compact proof-map for earlier marriages and inheritances.

## 1. Walsingham armorial pedigree — Shield No. 19

Daniel Gurney says that Walsingham Abbey preserved remnants of an armorial pedigree of the Gurneys. He thought it probably came originally from West Barsham Hall. He says eight out of nineteen shields remained. Shield No. 19 contained all the quarterings of the West Barsham Gurneys:

| Quarter | Family / coat named by DG | Direct-line meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Gurney | Base paternal line: Norfolk Gournays / Gurneys. |
| 2 | De Burnham, or Warren, with a difference | Rose de Burnham, wife of Matthew de Gournay, bringing Harpley into the line. This reaches back to G29. |
| 3 | Baconsthorpe | Likely tied to the disputed Katherine Baconsthorpe / Ingoldesthorpe problem around G28/G26; needs careful handling. |
| 4 | Wauncy | Katherine de Wauncy, wife of Edmund Gournay G23, bringing West Barsham / Denver / Depden context. |
| 5 | Lovell | Margaret Lovell, wife of Anthony Gurney G17. |
| 6 | Conyers | Ela Conyers, mother of Margaret Lovell; carries the Conyers coheir line. |
| 7 | FitzRalph | Intermediate inheritance line behind Mortimer of Attleborough. |
| 8 | Mortimer of Attleborough | Barony-of-Mortimer inheritance represented through the Lovell / Conyers descent. |

### Research value

This shield compresses several generations of the pedigree into one heraldic object. It is not just decorative. It says the West Barsham Gurneys claimed quarterings from:

- the original Norfolk / Gurney line;
- the Harpley acquisition through Burnham/Warren;
- the West Barsham acquisition through Wauncy;
- the Great Ellingham / Mortimer-of-Attleborough inheritance through Lovell, Conyers, FitzRalph, and Mortimer.

The shield therefore supports a large part of the line from roughly G29 through G17, though each quarter still needs to be matched to conventional genealogical evidence.

### Cautions

- The **Baconsthorpe** quarter is the least immediately resolved. It may relate to Daniel Gurney's earlier “probably a Baconsthorpe” reading for Katherine or to a related Baconsthorpe-side inheritance, but the repo now treats the Katherine identification as unresolved.
- The shield is a later armorial memory of descent and inheritance. It is good corroborative evidence, not a substitute for deeds, fines, wills, or visitations.
- Because Daniel thought the glass may have come from West Barsham Hall, its original installation context needs further investigation.

## 2. Armstrong 1781 — West Barsham church arms list

Armstrong gives another eight-family heraldic list at West Barsham church. This is not the same as the Walsingham eight-quarter shield. Armstrong records Gurney arms and impalements / associated arms for:

| Family | Likely project meaning |
|---|---|
| Wauci / Wauncy | Katherine de Wauncy, wife of Edmund Gournay G23. |
| Calthorpe | Anne Calthorpe, wife of William Gurney IV G19. |
| Lovell | Margaret Lovell, wife of Anthony Gurney G17. |
| Holdich | Helen / Ellen Holdich, wife of Francis Gurney G16. |
| Blennerhasset | Ellen Blennerhasset, wife of Henry Gurney G15. |
| Lewknor | Martha Lewknor, wife of Thomas Gurney, Henry G15's heir. |
| Jernegan / Jerningham | Margaret Jerningham, wife of Thomas Gournay II G20. |
| Probable Wentworth-like coat | Open lead: no secure Gurney-Wentworth marriage currently identified. |

### Research value

This source is strongest for the later West Barsham marriage chain from G23 through G15 and the immediate post-G15 heir line. Seven of the eight families match existing marriages or known direct-line / heir-line alliances. The probable Wentworth-like coat remains unresolved and should stay as an open lead.

## 3. Visitations and visitation-like sources already in the repo

### Rye / Harleian Society Norfolk Visitations

Rye's Norfolk visitation pages give a formal heralds' pedigree layer for the Tudor line:

- p. 132: West Barsham / Cley / Cawston / Aylsham cadet branch with a crescent for difference.
- pp. 140–141: direct Anthony Gurney → Francis Gurney + Ellen Holdich → Henry Gurney + Ellen Blennerhasset → Henry's children, including Francis of London.
- p. 40: Constance Gorney / Gurney, daughter of William Gorney of Barsham, married Rafe Blundevill.

This is the most direct formal visitation support for the G17–G14 corridor.

### Dashwood / Harvey 1563 Norfolk visitation

Dashwood p. 58 gives the Kerville pedigree and independently supports the Catherine Kerville marriage to Thomas Gournay I G21. It sharpens the Kerville context by identifying Robert Kerville of Watlington, his will date, and Catherine's siblings.

### Spelman pedigree from family papers

Sir Henry Spelman's pedigree is not a visitation, but it functions like one for this project because it was copied from family papers given by Francis Gurnay in 1639. Daniel Gurney says those original family papers were later lost. The Spelman material is especially important for the 1200–1400 period because it preserves seal descriptions and deed-based pedigree claims:

- Matthew de Gournay + Rose de Burnham and the Harpley acquisition.
- John de Gournay's advowson recovery from Lewes Priory by combat.
- William de Gurnay's seal with a man-at-arms before the shield-arms evidence.
- William de Gurnay's 1294 seal with the cross engrailed on a shield.
- Edmund Gournay's lease at West Barsham with a cross-engrailed seal.
- Later matches through Lovell, Holdich, Blennerhasset, and Lewknor.

This source may be the best “visitation-like” bridge for the medieval generations because it is a 1639 transcript of family evidences rather than a later narrative alone.

## 4. Medieval substitute evidence for 1200–1400

True heralds' visitations are early-modern, not medieval. For 1200–1400, the closest substitutes in the repo are:

| Evidence type | Repo example | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Roll of arms | DG's ancient roll reference for John de Gournay G27 | Earliest named arms-bearing ancestor. |
| Seal descriptions | Spelman pedigree / DG: 1294 William de Gurnay cross-engrailed seal; Edmund Gurnay cross-engrailed seal | Direct heraldic use, not merely later family tradition. |
| Feet of fines / final concords | DG appendices around Matthew, William II, William III, Harpley, Swathings | Property and relationship proof where parish records do not exist. |
| Chartulary material | Lewes Priory charters; Hardingham / Letton / Swathings grants | Contemporary or near-contemporary landholding and kinship context. |
| Inquisitions post mortem | DG Supplement notes and project fact sheets for Sir John V / William IV | Heirship and estate succession proof. |
| Church / glass / tomb heraldry | Walsingham glass; Harpley arms; West Barsham church arms; Norwich cloister arms | Later survival of medieval family identity and alliance claims. |
| Local topographical histories using lost records | Armstrong, Blomefield, Farrer | Secondary but often preserves now-lost local evidence and monument descriptions. |

## 5. Current interpretation

The most useful evidentiary chain is:

1. **G29 Matthew + Rose de Burnham** — Harpley acquisition; reflected in Burnham/Warren quartering.
2. **G27 John de Gournay** — ancient roll arms, cross engrailed.
3. **G26 William de Gournay III** — 1294 cross-engrailed seal.
4. **G23 Edmund Gournay + Katherine Wauncy** — West Barsham acquisition; Wauncy quarter / impalement.
5. **G21 Thomas Gournay I + Catherine Kerville** — supported by DG plus Dashwood Kerville visitation pedigree.
6. **G20 Thomas Gournay II + Margaret Jerningham** — appears in the Armstrong West Barsham arms list.
7. **G19 William Gurney IV + Anne Calthorpe** — appears in West Barsham arms list and DG pedigree; cadet children corroborated by Rye.
8. **G17 Anthony Gurney + Margaret Lovell** — Lovell / Conyers / FitzRalph / Mortimer quarterings; Rye direct pedigree begins here.
9. **G16 Francis Gurney + Ellen Holdich** — Holdich impalement and Rye direct pedigree.
10. **G15 Henry Gurney + Ellen Blennerhasset** — Blennerhasset impalement and Rye direct pedigree.

## 6. Recommended next steps

1. Add a formal `armorialEvidence` register or Markdown table keyed by generation, source, shield type, family represented, and evidentiary strength.
2. Treat the Walsingham Shield No. 19 as the central armorial object, separate from Armstrong's West Barsham church list.
3. Create a focused research lead for the Baconsthorpe quarter because it may help resolve or at least better frame the Katherine Baconsthorpe / Ingoldesthorpe issue.
4. Create a focused research lead for the probable Wentworth-like coat in Armstrong's West Barsham church list.
5. For 1200–1400, prioritize seal / charter / fine / IPM evidence over later heraldic compilations, but use the heraldry to identify which records to chase.

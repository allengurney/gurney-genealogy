# Gurney armorial pedigree and visitations evidence audit

## Purpose

This note maps armorial, visitation, and visitation-like evidence that may corroborate the medieval and Tudor Norfolk Gurney line, especially the multi-part shield recalled in prior research and the formal visitation trees.

The main conclusion after the second pass is that the evidence is not one object. It is a small cluster of related heraldic witnesses that should be kept separate and then compared generation-by-generation.

## 1. The three main armorial clusters

### A. Walsingham armorial pedigree — Shield No. 19

Daniel Gurney says that Walsingham Abbey preserved remnants of an armorial pedigree of the Gurneys. He thought it probably came originally from West Barsham Hall. He says eight out of nineteen shields remained, and that the Walsingham glass coincided almost entirely with the Spelman MSS pedigree.[^dg-walsingham-context]

Shield No. 19 contained all the quarterings of the West Barsham Gurneys:[^dg-walsingham-shield]

| Quarter | Family / coat named by DG | Direct-line meaning | Status |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gurney | Base paternal Norfolk Gournay / Gurney line. | Strong. |
| 2 | De Burnham, or Warren, with a difference | Rose de Burnham, wife of Matthew de Gournay; Harpley acquisition. | Strong, but confirm exact Burnham/Warren heraldic path. |
| 3 | Baconsthorpe | The most problematic quarter. It should **not** be used to make G28 Katherine a Baconsthorpe. | Open lead. |
| 4 | Wauncy | Katherine de Wauncy, wife of Edmund Gournay; West Barsham / Denver / Depden context. | Strong. |
| 5 | Lovell | Margaret Lovell, wife of Anthony Gurney G17. | Strong. |
| 6 | Conyers | Ela Conyers, mother of Margaret Lovell; carries the Conyers coheir line. | Strong. |
| 7 | FitzRalph | Intermediate inheritance line behind Mortimer of Attleborough. | Strong as inheritance-quartering, not as a direct Gurney marriage. |
| 8 | Mortimer of Attleborough | Barony-of-Mortimer inheritance represented through Lovell / Conyers descent. | Strong as inheritance-quartering. |

#### Interpretation

This shield compresses several generations of the pedigree into one heraldic object. It is not merely decorative: it says the West Barsham Gurneys claimed quarterings from the Harpley acquisition, the West Barsham acquisition, and the Great Ellingham / Mortimer-of-Attleborough inheritance path.

The strongest reading is:

- Gurney + Burnham/Warren = the early Harpley foundation story.
- Wauncy = the West Barsham inheritance.
- Lovell + Conyers + FitzRalph + Mortimer = Anthony Gurney's marriage to Margaret Lovell and the Great Ellingham / Attleborough inheritance package.
- Baconsthorpe = unresolved and potentially important, but not yet safely placed.

### B. Armstrong 1781 — West Barsham church arms list

Armstrong gives a separate eight-family heraldic list at West Barsham church. This is not the same object as the Walsingham eight-quarter shield. Armstrong records the Gurney arms and the following impalements or associated arms at West Barsham church:[^armstrong-west-barsham]

| Family | Likely project meaning | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Wauci / Wauncy | Katherine de Wauncy, wife of Edmund Gournay G23. | Strong. |
| Calthorpe | Anne Calthorpe, wife of William Gurney IV G19. | Strong. |
| Lovell | Margaret Lovell, wife of Anthony Gurney G17. | Strong. |
| Holdich | Ellen Holdich, wife of Francis Gurney G16. | Strong. |
| Blennerhasset | Ellen Blennerhasset, wife of Henry Gurney G15. | Strong. |
| Lewknor | Martha Lewknor, wife of Thomas Gurney, Henry G15's heir. | Strong, heir-line rather than Allen direct line. |
| Jernegan / Jerningham | Margaret Jerningham, wife of Thomas Gournay II G20. | Strong. |
| Probable Wentworth-like coat | Unidentified: sable, a chevron between three leopards' heads, probably Wentworth. | Open lead. |

This source is strongest for the later West Barsham marriage chain from G23 through G15 and the immediate post-G15 heir line. Seven of the eight families match existing marriages or known heir-line alliances. The probable Wentworth-like coat remains unresolved and should stay as a separate research lead.[^wentworth-lead]

### C. Berryhall / John Tuthill's house escutcheons

Daniel Gurney gives a third armorial cluster for escutcheons formerly in John Tuthill's house called Berryhall. These are not the same as either Walsingham Shield No. 19 or Armstrong's West Barsham list.[^dg-berryhall]

DG enumerates:

| Berryhall armorial item | Likely project meaning | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Gurnay side-by-side with Mortimer | Mortimer of Attleborough inheritance through Lovell / Conyers. | Strong. |
| Gurnay side-by-side with Lovell | Margaret Lovell, wife of Anthony Gurney G17. | Strong. |
| Gurnay side-by-side with Tyrrell | Elizabeth Tyrrell, Anthony Gurney's second wife. | Strong for Anthony's second marriage, but not direct-line descent. |
| Gurnay side-by-side with Heydon | Anne Heydon, wife of William Gurney V G18; Anthony's mother. | Strong. |
| Gurnay side-by-side with Calthorpe | Anne Calthorpe, wife of William Gurney IV G19. | Strong. |
| Gurnay side-by-side with Jerningham | Margaret Jerningham, wife of Thomas Gournay II G20. | Strong. |
| Gurnay side-by-side with Wauncy | Katherine de Wauncy, wife of Edmund Gournay G23. | Strong. |

#### Interpretation

Berryhall is useful because it adds **Heydon** and **Tyrrell** to the visual evidence map. Heydon connects to G18 / G17 maternal ancestry and the Boleyn cousinage; Tyrrell documents Anthony Gurney's second marriage. These should not be folded into Walsingham Shield No. 19, because they are a separate house/glass/escutcheon context.

## 2. Visitations and visitation-like sources

### Rye / Harleian Society Norfolk Visitations

Rye's Harleian Society volume gives the most direct formal heralds' pedigree layer for the Tudor line. The Internet Archive full text confirms the volume is an 1891 edition of the Norfolk visitations of 1563, 1589, and 1613, edited by Walter Rye. Rye says the three visitations were combined by R. Mundy about 1620 and formed Harl. MS 1552; the printed text came from a careful J. Eedes transcript, with obvious errors corrected from the Le Neve, Martin, Norris, and L'Estrange collections.[^rye-preface]

Key Gurney items:

| Visitation item | Evidence value |
|---|---|
| p. 132, cadet branch | Arms: Argent, a cross engrailed gules, a crescent for difference. Captures West Barsham / Cley / Cawston / Aylsham branch.[^rye-p132] |
| pp. 140–141, direct line | Arms: Argent, a cross engrailed gules, in dexter chief point a cinquefoil azure. Captures Anthony → Francis + Ellen Holdich → Henry + Ellen Blennerhasset → Henry's children, including Francis of London.[^rye-p140] |
| Calthrop pedigree | Records Anne married William Gorney, corroborating the Calthorpe match from the Calthrop side.[^rye-calthrop] |
| D'Engayne pedigree | Records Alice, daughter of William Gorney, married Henry D'Engayne of Brunsted; useful collateral sibling evidence.[^rye-dengayne] |
| Stubbs pedigree | Records Richard Stubbey of Ellingham married Elizabeth, daughter of Anthony Gorney of Ellingham.[^rye-stubbs] |
| Wayte pedigree | Records Ann married William Gorney of Cawston, supporting the cadet branch.[^rye-wayte] |

The online check adds a point of method: do not search only the Gurney heading. Allied-family pages in the same visitation volume preserve additional collateral corroboration.

### Index Rerum / Norfolk Pedigrees source-map leads

The newly surfaced `Index Rerum to Norfolk Antiquities` and `Index to Norfolk Pedigrees` source-list material should be treated as a map of manuscript pedigree witnesses, not as genealogical evidence in itself. It adds several arms/pedigree leads that sit beside Rye and Dashwood:

| Source-list item | Why it matters | Current status |
|---|---|---|
| Harl. MS 1552 p. 48, printed Harleian Society p. 132 | This is the Rye printed visitations source already used for the cadet / differentiated-arms Gurney heading. | Covered by `rye-visitacion-norffolk-1891`. |
| Visitation of 1563 by Harvey, Harl. MS 4755, fo. 81 | Likely a manuscript control or variant of the Harvey 1563 visitation separate from the printed Dashwood page already used for Kerville. | New lead. |
| MSS of the late John H. Gurney of Keswick, HMC 12th Report, App. IX (1891), pp. 116, **161** | **Read at description level (2026-06).** The collection (Cox Macro → Hudson Gurney → Keswick; fourteen volumes tied to Sir Henry Spelman) includes **MS 122/16, pp. 1–124 — "Numerous pedigrees, with arms … Bardolf, Calthrop, Gurney, and especially Spelman; Spelman wills 1485–1641"** — the surviving Spelman-circle Gurney pedigree, which corroborates DG's Spelman source-chain. | **Located; the pedigree's generations still need the NRO Keswick MS itself (lead L-85).**[^hmc-keswick-spelman-2026] |
| Norris' MS folio pedigrees, pp. 553-556; Morant's MS folio pedigrees, p. 175 | Manuscript pedigree controls that may preserve variant Gurney arms/matches outside Rye. | New lead. |
| Commonplace Book of Henry G., Tanner MSS, Bodleian Library, fo. 175 | Likely the same source-family as the existing Tanner 175/Henry Gurney lead; update that lead rather than duplicate it. | Existing lead refined. |
| Dawson Turner's illustrated Blomefield, vol. xv, fo. 182 | Could preserve visual/pedigree material absent from printed Blomefield. | New lead. |
| Ashmole MS 848, fo. 5, and Ashmole MS 792, vol. ii | Bodleian manuscript heraldic/pedigree leads. | New lead. |
| King's College, Oxford, lxxix / clxvii, p. 21 | College manuscript pedigree lead needing catalogue identification. | New lead. |

The value here is in triangulation. If any of these manuscript witnesses independently reproduce the Walsingham / West Barsham / Berryhall quarterings, they can either confirm DG's source chain or expose where a later reconstruction entered the tradition. Until inspected, they should stay in the leads CSV rather than in the evidence register.

### Dashwood / Harvey 1563 Norfolk visitation

Dashwood p. 58 gives the Kerville pedigree and independently supports the Catherine Kerville marriage to Thomas Gournay I G21. The repo's v68 research already reframed that page as a third independent witness rather than a new lead: Robert Kerville of Watlington's will was dated 19 November 1434, Catherine was a legatee, and the page says she married Thomas Gurney.[^dashwood]

This is important because it fills a 1400s gap that Rye's Gurney heading does not reach.

### Spelman 1639 family-paper pedigree

Sir Henry Spelman's pedigree is not a formal visitation, but for this project it functions like a medieval bridge source. DG says it was transcribed by Spelman from one given to him by Francis Gurnay in 1639; it was compiled from original deeds then in the family possession, now lost; DG calls it valuable though imperfect.[^dg-spelman-context] The Spelman pedigree itself is now **located and confirmed extant**: it survives in the J. H. Gurney of Keswick deposit as **MS 122/16** (HMC 12th Report, App. IX, 1891, p. 161 — pedigrees with arms of Bardolf, Calthrop, Gurney, and especially Spelman, plus copies of Spelman wills 1485–1641). So the "now-lost original deeds" sit behind a pedigree compilation that does survive; reading its Gurney generations requires the NRO Keswick manuscript (lead L-85).[^hmc-keswick-spelman-2026]

Extract: [`sources/corpus_supplement/gurney-of-keswick-spelman-pedigree-hmc-1891.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/sources/corpus_supplement/gurney-of-keswick-spelman-pedigree-hmc-1891.md). Source ID: `hmc-12th-report-appendix-ix-1891`.

It preserves medieval evidence otherwise hard to reconstruct:

- Matthew de Gournay + Rose de Burnham and the Harpley acquisition.[^dg-spelman-matthew]
- John de Gournay's advowson recovery from Lewes Priory by combat.[^dg-spelman-combat]
- William de Gurnay's 1294 grant to Rector John with a cross-engrailed seal plainly extant.[^dg-spelman-seal]
- Edmund Gournay's Wauncy marriage and West Barsham / Denver rents; Edmund's own cross-engrailed seal on a West Barsham lease.[^dg-spelman-wauncy]
- Anthony, Francis, Henry, and Thomas matches; DG notes that all except the last were visible in colors in Sir Henry Gawdy's hall, formerly the Gurnay house.[^dg-spelman-tudor]

## 3. Medieval substitute evidence for 1200–1400

True heralds' visitations are early-modern, not medieval. For 1200–1400, the closest substitutes in the repo are:

| Evidence type | Repo example | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Roll of arms | DG's ancient roll reference for John de Gournay G27 | Earliest named arms-bearing ancestor. |
| Seal descriptions | 1294 William de Gurnay cross-engrailed seal; Edmund Gurnay cross-engrailed seal | Direct heraldic use, not merely later family tradition. |
| Feet of fines / final concords | DG appendices around Matthew, William II, William III, Harpley, Swathings | Property and relationship proof where parish records do not exist. |
| Chartulary material | Lewes Priory charters; Hardingham / Letton / Swathings grants | Contemporary or near-contemporary landholding and kinship context. |
| Inquisitions post mortem | DG Supplement notes and fact sheets for Sir John V / William IV | Heirship and estate succession proof. |
| Church / glass / tomb heraldry | Walsingham glass; Harpley arms; West Barsham church arms; Norwich cloister arms | Later survival of medieval family identity and alliance claims. |
| Local topographical histories using lost records | Armstrong, Blomefield, Farrer | Secondary but often preserves now-lost local evidence and monument descriptions. |

Armstrong supplies useful non-visitation corroboration for this medieval window: Harpley church windows showed Gourney arms; John de Gournay the rector's chancel gravestone had two shield positions though the shields were already torn off by Armstrong's time; West Barsham church carried the later arms list; Norwich Cathedral cloister windows displayed Gournay arms among major Norfolk families in a glazing program finished in 1430.[^armstrong-harpley][^armstrong-norwich]

## 5. Focused open leads

### Baconsthorpe quarter

The Baconsthorpe quarter is now the highest-value unresolved item. The G28 companion argues convincingly that William II's wife was almost certainly Ingoldesthorpe, based on the 1243 fine's `heredibus ipsius Katarine` wording and DG's own narrative; the earlier “probably Baconsthorpe” label appears to be an outlier.[^g28-ingoldesthorpe]

Therefore the Walsingham Baconsthorpe quarter should be tested against:

1. G26 William III's wife, named in the DG pedigree as Katherine, daughter of Edmund Baconsthorpe.
2. A later Baconsthorpe / Heydon / Warren heraldic memory that entered the glass through Anne Heydon or the Baconsthorpe Castle orbit.
3. A mislabel or reconstruction error in DG's reading of the Walsingham shield.

Do **not** use the Baconsthorpe quarter as proof that G28 Katherine was Baconsthorpe.

### Walsingham glass provenance

DG thought the Walsingham armorial pedigree probably came from West Barsham Hall. The next step is to determine whether any of the glass still survives, whether Lee Warner / Walsingham Abbey papers describe it, and whether DG's image extraction includes a high-quality crop of the entire armorial pedigree rather than only Shield No. 19.

### Gurney's Place / Keswick glass

DG says the bay-window arms from Gurney's Place in St Julian, Norwich, were preserved at Keswick. This cluster includes Gurney impaling Calthorpe, Heydon, Kerville, and Wauncy, plus a broken quarterly shield with Warren / Swathing-or-Scrope / Gurney / Dampmartin-or-Cailey. This is a potentially important bridge between early medieval claims and Tudor family memory.[^dg-gurneys-place]

### Wentworth-like coat

The probable Wentworth coat in Armstrong's West Barsham list remains unresolved. A separate repo lead already records that no Gurney-Wentworth marriage is known in the published West Barsham line and recommends Suffolk visitation / Wentworth genealogy follow-up.[^wentworth-lead]

### Allied-family visitation sweep

The Rye volume contains Gurney references outside the Gurney headings. The next useful sweep is not just `Gurney/Gorney/Gourney`, but the allied names: Calthrop, Wayte, D'Engayne, Stubbs, Holdich, Blennerhasset, Lewknor, Jerningham/Jernegan, Lovell, Conyers, Kerville/Carville, Baconsthorpe, Heydon, Tyrrell.

## 6. Working conclusion

The armorial evidence does meaningfully strengthen the direct-line structure, especially from G29 through G15, but its evidentiary value varies by generation:

- **Very strong:** Wauncy, Lovell/Conyers/FitzRalph/Mortimer, Calthorpe, Holdich, Blennerhasset, Lewknor, Jerningham.
- **Strong but nuanced:** Burnham/Warren and Kerville.
- **Strong for arms use, not marriage proof:** G27/G26 cross-engrailed arms and seals.
- **Open / potentially high-value:** Baconsthorpe quarter and Wentworth-like coat.

The best next research move is not a broader narrative. It is a tight evidence table linking each shield or visitation entry to a specific marriage, inheritance, generation, and confidence rating, with unresolved heraldic items isolated rather than forced into the tree.

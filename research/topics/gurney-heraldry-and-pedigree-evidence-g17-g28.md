# Gurney heraldry and pedigree evidence — G17–G28

## Finding

The G17–G28 corridor has enough heraldic and pedigree evidence to support a formal research layer, not just occasional narrative color. The recurring Norfolk Gurney shield is **Argent, a cross engrailed gules**. Daniel Gurney places the earliest mention in 1270, reports William de Gurnay's 1294 seal with the same coat, and rejects the Somersetshire Gournay paly shield as a Norfolk-family misattribution.[^dg-core]

Rye's published Norfolk Visitation extracts add useful differentiated forms: p. 132 gives the West Barsham / Cley / Cawston / Aylsham cadet pedigree with a crescent for difference; pp. 140–141 give the direct Anthony → Francis → Henry pedigree with a cinquefoil azure in dexter chief.[^rye]

The evidence does not prove the John Gurney-1 emigrant hypothesis. It does strengthen the English-side context above G14 Francis by showing that the West Barsham family was repeatedly recorded through shields, seals, pedigrees, church glass, and marital impalements.

## Evidence layers to preserve

1. **Base coat:** Argent, a cross engrailed gules.
2. **Cadet form:** base coat with crescent for difference in Rye p. 132.
3. **Direct visitation form:** base coat with cinquefoil azure in dexter chief in Rye pp. 140–141.
4. **Crests/devices:** gurnard fish crest, later cap of maintenance, and wrestling collar device / second crest should remain separate from the shield blazon.[^dg-core]
5. **Place evidence:** West Barsham, Harpley, and Norwich each preserve church, glass, or topographical heraldry references that connect the shield to actual family geography.[^west-barsham][^harpley][^norwich]
6. **Image evidence:** the Daniel Gurney image extraction catalog already contains the key visual assets for pp. 279, 283, 284, 319, 321, 415, 418, 446, and 448.[^images]

## Recommendations

- Build an arms-and-pedigree evidence register keyed by generation, evidence type, source, date, and image asset.
- Prioritize G27/G26 as the earliest proof pair: roll evidence plus seal evidence.
- Use G23 as the West Barsham hinge because Edmund's Gurney/Wauncy impalement encodes the acquisition of the seat.
- Use G19/G17 as the visitation bridge because Rye provides both cadet and direct differentiated forms.
- Flag the West Barsham p. 408 slab image as Wauncy, not Gurney, wherever it is reused.
- Keep American or colonial-use questions separate from the English heraldic evidence.

## Conclusion

The heraldic material is strong enough to become a supporting architecture for G17–G28. It should not replace vital, probate, deed, or visitation evidence, but it can help correlate pedigree, marriage, place, and family-identity evidence across the medieval-to-Tudor Norfolk line.

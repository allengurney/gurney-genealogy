# Baconsthorpe quarter — scenario analysis

## Purpose

This note isolates the unresolved **Baconsthorpe** quarter in the Walsingham armorial pedigree and searches it from the Bacon / Baconsthorpe side rather than only from the Gurney side.

The working problem: Daniel Gurney's Walsingham Shield No. 19 lists eight West Barsham Gurney quarterings, including **Baconsthorpe**. The repo's G28 research now strongly favors **Katherine Ingoldesthorpe** as wife of William de Gournay II, based on the 1243 North Wootton fine. Therefore the Baconsthorpe quarter should not be retro-used to make G28 Katherine a Baconsthorpe.

## Source-side anchors

### Gurney-side anchor

The current G26 fact sheet records William de Gournay III's wife as **Katherine, daughter of Edmund Baconsthorpe**, and treats this as a documented Baconsthorpe marriage. The same fact sheet notes that Blomefield independently records the 9 Edward II fine in which John III is identified as “son of Catherine,” supporting the existence of a Katherine in the G26/G25 succession context, though not by itself proving the Baconsthorpe surname.

### Ingoldesthorpe-side corrective

The G28 companion argues that William de Gournay II's wife was almost certainly of the Ingoldesthorpe family. Its key point is the 1243 fine's phrasing: William and Katherine quitclaimed for themselves and for **Katherine's heirs**, which makes the land look like Katherine-side land. This points toward Thomas de Ingoldesthorpe rather than a Baconsthorpe identification for G28.

### Bacon / Baconsthorpe-family-side findings

Online family-side searching points to two separate Baconsthorpe contexts:

1. **A medieval Bacon family of the Baconsthorpe / Gresham area.** Public summaries identify Sir Edmund Bacon of Baconsthorpe/Gresham as holding Gresham and dying in 1336/7, with inheritance disputes involving daughters or female heirs such as Margery, Margaret, and probably Maud. Gresham Castle is also tied to Edmund Bacon's 1318 licence to crenellate.
2. **A later Heydon-led Baconsthorpe Castle context.** English Heritage says the Baconsthorpe Castle site was acquired from the Bacon family in the early 15th century by William Baxter; his son John, later Heydon, built up the castle and family status. This is later than the likely G26 marriage window and should not be confused with a 13th- or early-14th-century Bacon/Gurney marriage.

## Scenario ranking

| Scenario | Description | Fit | Problems | Current rank |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| A | **G26 wife scenario:** Katherine, wife of William de Gournay III, was daughter of Edmund Bacon / Baconsthorpe, and the Walsingham quarter preserves this marriage. | Fits G26 fact sheet and the timing: Edmund Bacon of Gresham/Baconsthorpe is active around the right late-13th / early-14th-century window. | Need first-hand proof that Edmund had a daughter Katherine who married William de Gournay III. Current public Bacon-side summaries name other daughters/heirs, not Katherine. | 1 |
| B | **Place/manor label scenario:** “Baconsthorpe” in the Walsingham quarter may be a place-name shorthand for the Bacon family of Baconsthorpe/Gresham, not necessarily a stable surname as printed. | Explains why searches for “Edmund Baconsthorpe” are thinner than searches for Sir Edmund Bacon of Gresham/Baconsthorpe. | Requires checking DG's image/plate and any original glass notes to see whether DG read a name, a coat, or a place label. | 2 |
| C | **Later Heydon/Baconsthorpe memory scenario:** The quarter entered the Walsingham/West Barsham heraldic memory via the Heydon/Baconsthorpe Castle orbit, especially because Anne Heydon is a Gurney maternal ancestor. | Explains why Baconsthorpe appears in a later West Barsham heraldic object and why the Heydon-Baconsthorpe story is visually prominent. | Chronologically weaker for the Walsingham Shield No. 19 quarter: the shield's eight quarters already have Heydon absent but Lovell/Conyers/FitzRalph/Mortimer present. Heydon appears instead in Berryhall / Gurney's Place glass. | 3 |
| D | **G28 retro-label scenario:** The Baconsthorpe quarter means G28 Katherine was Baconsthorpe. | Matches an older DG pedigree-cell label. | Conflicts with the stronger G28 Ingoldesthorpe fine analysis and should not be used unless new evidence overturns that reading. | 4 |
| E | **Misread / misassigned heraldic quarter:** DG or a later cataloger may have mislabeled the shield's third quarter. | Always possible with old glass, damaged quarterings, and antiquarian reconstructions. | Requires image-level heraldic review; not currently evidenced. | 5 |

## Best current hypothesis

The best working hypothesis is **Scenario A with Scenario B as a modifier**:

> The Walsingham “Baconsthorpe” quarter most likely preserves the G26 marriage of William de Gournay III to Katherine, daughter of Edmund Bacon / Baconsthorpe, but the label may be a place/family shorthand rather than the exact medieval surname form.

This is compatible with the G28 Ingoldesthorpe correction. It also fits the chronology better than a later Heydon/Baconsthorpe Castle explanation: Sir Edmund Bacon of Gresham/Baconsthorpe belongs to the early-14th-century world, while Baconsthorpe Castle as a Heydon monument belongs mainly to the 15th and 16th centuries.

## What would confirm or falsify it

### Confirmation targets

1. **Bacon family pedigree / IPM target:** Find a first-hand or high-quality secondary source listing Sir Edmund Bacon's children and confirming a daughter Katherine married William de Gournay / Gurney.
2. **Arms target:** Identify the actual Bacon / Baconsthorpe arms and compare them with DG's Walsingham third quarter. This is crucial: if the coat differs from known Bacon arms, “Baconsthorpe” may be a misread or a place shorthand.
3. **DG image target:** Inspect the original Walsingham armorial pedigree image, not only the OCR text, to see whether the third quarter has a visible coat that can be independently blazoned.
4. **Blomefield / Parkin target:** Pull the exact Blomefield text for Gresham, Baconsthorpe, and the Harpley fine. The repo already preserves the Harpley line through the v54 patchset; the Bacon-side entries need a comparable capture.
5. **NRO / TNA target:** Search for IPMs, fines, or charter references for Edmund Bacon / Baconsthorpe / Gresham in the 1310–1340 period and for any Gurney transaction involving Bacon lands.

### Falsification signs

- Sir Edmund Bacon's documented daughters are complete and none is Katherine.
- The Bacon/Baconsthorpe coat does not match the Walsingham third quarter.
- The Walsingham third quarter is visually identifiable as a different family.
- The G26 Katherine Baconsthorpe claim traces only to DG's unsupported pedigree table and not to any deed, fine, visitation, or monument.

## Research implications

- Keep G28 as **Katherine Ingoldesthorpe** unless new primary evidence says otherwise.
- Treat G26 as **Katherine Bacon/Baconsthorpe — plausible but source-chain needs strengthening**.
- Do not conflate **Bacon family of Baconsthorpe/Gresham** with the later **Heydon family of Baconsthorpe Castle**.
- Add a targeted lead for Sir Edmund Bacon of Gresham/Baconsthorpe and the possible Katherine Bacon/Baconsthorpe marriage to William de Gournay III.

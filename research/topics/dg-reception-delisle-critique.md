# Daniel Gurney's *Record* and its 19th–21st century reception — the Delisle critique

The *Record of the House of Gournay* by Daniel Gurney (1845, with Supplement 1858) is the single most-cited source in the repo. This topic file collects the documented scholarly reception of DG's work — particularly **Léopold Delisle**'s 19th-century critique — and frames the strengths and limits of DG as primary-derivative source.

---

## 1. DG's project and its evidentiary base

### 1.1 Informal nickname: "The Apocryphal Book of Dan"

A new reception lead explains the alternative title found in Norfolk source lists. Walter Rye's `Index Rerum to Norfolk Antiquities` indexes "Apocryphal Book of Dan" to the Gurney pedigree entry, and the Gurney entry glosses Daniel Gurney's `Record of the House of Gournay` as "otherwise known as the Apocryphal Book of Dan."[^rye-index-apocryphal-dan]

The stronger social-context note comes from the Somerset Archaeological and Natural History Society excursion report. In a footnote on Daniel Gurney's mistaken identification of a house as Sir Matthew Gournay's residence, the report says the book was generally known among his friends as the "Apocryphal Book of Dan," citing `The Bibliographer`, I, 59.[^sanhs-apocryphal-dan]

Treat this as an informal or joke-name among family/friends and antiquarian readers, not as evidence for a separate edition, missing version, or variant text of Daniel Gurney's work. It does, however, nicely reflects the mixed reception captured elsewhere in this topic file: Daniel Gurney's work was foundational, ambitious, and beloved inside the family circle, while its early legendary strata were already treated with affectionate skepticism.

Daniel Gurney (1791–1880), banker and antiquarian, published the four-volume *Record of the House of Gournay* between 1845 and c. 1858. The work draws on:

- Norman archives at Évreux, Rouen, and elsewhere (DG describes archival visits in Volume I introduction).
- The *MS. Histoire de Gournay* tradition (Cordier MS c. 1710–1738; Langloys late-17th-century notes; René Potin notes; M. de Gondeville's MS).
- Pierre Potin de la Mairie's *Recherches historiques sur la ville de Gournay-en-Bray* Tome I (1842) — DG knew this work and cites it.
- Standard medieval Latin sources (Orderic Vitalis, Wace's *Roman de Rou*, Domesday Book, Bayeux *Liber niger*, etc.).
- English-side feudal and ecclesiastical records (cartularies of Bec, Saint-Wandrille, La Trinité de Rouen, etc.).

**The core strength of DG**: he produced the only comprehensive English-language genealogy of the Norman Gournays + Norfolk junior line + Somerset cadet branch + Continental cadets (Lorraine), with substantial primary-source extracts and citation apparatus.

**The core weakness of DG**: the early generations (G37 Eudes / G36 Hugh I / G35 Renaud) rest on local-tradition transmission via the *MS. Histoire de Gournay* chain rather than on contemporary documents. **DG himself acknowledged this**: of Eudes specifically (DG-I p. 24), he wrote *"the existence of Eudes…is a matter of tradition"* — a phrase preserved in FMG MedLands citation [881].

## 2. The Delisle critique — primary attestations

**Léopold Delisle** (1826–1910) was the most prominent French archivist and medievalist of the 19th century, director of the Bibliothèque nationale (1874–1905), and author of foundational charter editions including *Recueil des actes de Henri II roi d'Angleterre et duc de Normandie* (with E. Berger, 1909–1927).

### 2.1 The la Ferté charter — Delisle's specific finding

DG-Supp Note 8 (p. 731) records a critical verdict:

> "M. Léopold Delisle has also examined in detail the archives at Évreux, and is quite certain this charter is not there."

The charter in question is the la Ferté-en-Bray priory foundation charter [989/96] — the only primary attestation of Renaud (G35). Delisle's archival examination found that the original was not preserved in the Évreux archives where it would most logically reside. What survives is DG's transcription via the Gondeville MS (FMG MedLands [884]/[885] explicitly: "Gurney (1845), pp. 26 and 31, citing M. de Gondeville's MS Histoire de Gournay, but adding that the original charter no longer exists.").

### 2.2 Pattou's transmission of the Delisle critique

Pattou *Racines Histoire* (2025-08-11) p. 2 (sidebar narrative):

> *"Selon la légende familiale, Rollon aurait confié le territoire de Gournay-en-Bray à un certain Eudes, chevalier à l'écu noir, point de départ de la lignée. Ceci n'est pas ou peu attesté. **Au milieu du XIXe siècle, Daniel Gurney a établi une généalogie souvent reprise dans les ouvrages d'histoire sur la région mais ses travaux ont vite été critiqués par des érudits normands comme Léopold Delisle**. Daniel Gurney suggère un lien familial entre les maisons de La Ferté et de Gournay. Il fait d'Hugues de Gournay un des descendants de Renaud et Aubrée (Alberade), parents du fondateur de la collégiale de La Ferté."*

Translation: *"According to family legend, Rollon would have entrusted the territory of Gournay-en-Bray to a certain Eudes, the knight with the black shield, starting point of the lineage. This is not or barely attested. **In the middle of the 19th century, Daniel Gurney established a genealogy often reprinted in regional history works, but his work was quickly criticized by Norman scholars such as Léopold Delisle**. Daniel Gurney suggests a family link between the houses of La Ferté and Gournay. He makes Hugues de Gournay one of the descendants of Renaud and Aubrée (Alberade), parents of the founder of the collegiate church of La Ferté."*

### 2.3 French Wikipedia's contemporary endorsement of Delisle

The French Wikipedia article *Famille de Gournay* (consulted in 2026) likewise frames DG's work as *"vite été critiquée par des érudits normands comme Léopold Delisle"*, and uses caveats throughout the early generations:
- Eudes: *"existence contestée"* (existence contested)
- Hugues (?): explicit `?` marker
- Renaud: *"douteux"* (doubtful)
- Hugues 1er de Gournay: a more confident statement *"vivant en 984, décédé après 989 (peut-être en 1046), épouse Bathilde de Gerberoy (morte en 1059)"*

The asymmetry is informative: French Wikipedia attaches caveats to the generations preceding Hugues 1er and gives a more confident statement at Hugues 1er level — implying that Delisle's critique applies most strongly to G37/G36 (the truly traditional generations) rather than to G35 (where the la Ferté charter, even if preserved only in transcription, is at least a primary-source attestation) or G34 (where multiple chronicle and charter attestations exist independently of DG).

## 3. The reach of the critique — generation by generation

Mapping the Delisle critique to the repo's generational structure:

| Generation | Repo classification | Strength of evidence | Delisle critique applies how |
|---|---|---|---|
| G37 Eudes | Tradition | No contemporary document. Rests on *MS. Histoire de Gournay* chain (Cordier → Potin → DG). | Strongest — Pattou: *"Ceci n'est pas ou peu attesté."* The repo's "Tradition" classification is appropriate. |
| G36 Hugh I | Limited Historical Record | No contemporary document in his own name. La Tour Hue eponym + William Brito *Philippide* (c. 1224) + local tradition. | Strong — bracketed in FMG: *"…Hugues de Gournay…is a matter of tradition."* The repo's classification is appropriate. |
| G35 Renaud | Confirmed (with transcription caveat) | The la Ferté charter [989/96] is the only primary source; original lost; survives via Gondeville MS / DG transcription. | Significant — Pattou marks Renaud `?`; French Wikipedia uses *"douteux."* The repo retains Confirmed *with the explicit transcription caveat that the original is lost*. |
| G34 Hugh II | Confirmed | Multiple chronicle + charter attestations (Wace, Dumoulin, *Chronique Manuscrite de Normandie*, Vaudreuil 1067 charter, Bayeux *Liber niger* No. 5, the Cardiff/Norfolk tradition). | Modest — DG's framing of Hugh II's life (especially the Cardiff death narrative) is criticized by FMG ("the historical basis of the account is uncertain"). But the structural facts (Mortemer, Hastings, charter witness) are independently corroborated. |
| G33 Hugh III | Confirmed | Multiple primary sources (Domesday, 1067 Saint-Benoît charter, 1073 Marmoutier, 1076 Bec charter, Anselm letters, Chronicon Beccensis). | Limited — Hugh III's career is well-documented in independent sources beyond DG. Delisle's critique applies less here. |
| G32 Gerard | Confirmed | Heavy primary-source coverage (Orderic, Albert of Aix, Baudry, Guillaume de Jumièges, the St-Sauveur cartulary 1104). | Limited — Gerard's career is the best-documented of any Gournay before Hugues IV. Delisle's critique would apply minimally. |

The Delisle critique therefore applies most heavily to **G37 + G36**, with a secondary impact on **G35**. The repo's classification scheme already reflects this — Tradition / Limited Historical Record / Confirmed with transcription caveat — and the published fact sheets use appropriate caveats throughout the early generations.

## 4. Modern scholarship's continued use of DG

Despite Delisle's critique, modern scholarship continues to cite DG as a primary-derivative source:

- **FMG MedLands** (Charles Cawley) cites DG repeatedly for the Norman Gournay lineage, with footnote chains preserving DG's source attributions (e.g., FMG [875], [876], [879], [880], [881], [882], [884], [885] all derive directly from DG 1845).
- **Douglas Richardson, *Royal Ancestry*** (2013) draws on DG for English-side feudal genealogy, while disagreeing with DG on specific points (Walter-as-son-of-Gerard; Hugh III death year of 1110 rather than c. 1093).
- **Lewis Loyd, *Origins of Some Anglo-Norman Families*** (1951) p. 47 cites DG for the Gournay descent.
- **William Farrer, *Honors and Knights' Fees*** vol. 3 (1923–25) cites DG's English-tenure descriptions.
- **K.S.B. Keates-Rohan, *Domesday People*** (1999) cites DG for the Gournay Domesday holdings.

DG remains the standard reference; modern scholars supplement DG with independent primary-source verification rather than displacing it.

## 5. The broader transmission chain — from Cordier to FMG

The local Gournay antiquarian tradition's transmission can be diagrammed:

```
[Original 10th-c. la Ferté charter — LOST]
   ↓ (transcribed in)
[M. de Gondeville's MS Histoire de Gournay — survival status unknown]
   ↓ (transcribed in / conflated with)
Nicolas Cordier MS Histoire de Gournay (c. 1710-1738) — survival unknown
   ↓
Langloys notes (avocat, late 17th c.) — survival unknown
   ↓
René Potin notes (date unknown) — survival unknown
   ↓
Pierre Potin de la Mairie 1842 (Recherches sur la ville)
   ↓
N.-R. P. de la Mairie 1852 (Recherches sur les Possessions Tome I)
   ↓
Daniel Gurney 1845 / 1848 / 1858 Supplement
   ↓
T. J. Pettigrew 1871 (Collectanea Archaeologica vol. 2)
   ↓
J. R. Planché 1874 (The Conqueror and His Companions)
   ↓
James Hannay 1867 (Three Hundred Years of a Norman House)
   ↓
FMG MedLands (Charles Cawley)
   ↓
Pattou Racines Histoire (Etienne Pattou, 2025)
```

Each of these stages **preserves and extends** the previous, with occasional corrections (Pettigrew adds Sigy material; NRP-I adds the la Ferté reversion; Pattou adds the Norfolk and Somerset cadet-line apparatus). But the deep evidentiary substrate — particularly for Renaud, Hugh I, and Eudes — is the lost MS chain transmitted via DG.

**This is exactly what Delisle's critique addressed**: the substrate isn't independently verifiable. Modern scholars accept the chain as the best available evidence (with Delisle's caveat) rather than rejecting it altogether.

## 6. Implications for the repo

The repo's editorial position:

1. **Classify generations honestly** — Tradition / Limited Historical Record / Confirmed (with transcription caveat) / Confirmed — reflecting where the Delisle critique applies most heavily.

2. **Cite the deep transmission chain** — at G35 level, the la Ferté charter is properly cited as "preserved only in transcription via the Gondeville MS; original lost (Delisle)" rather than as a bare primary source.

3. **Retain DG as the standard reference** — DG is cited throughout the repo's research and fact-sheet apparatus because it remains the standard secondary synthesis. Independent primary-source verifications are added where possible (Orderic, Wace, FMG, Pattou).

4. **Surface the critique explicitly** — the repo's research-tier prose acknowledges Delisle's critique rather than smoothing it over. Readers can see for themselves where the evidence is thin and where it is robust.

This topic file is the central place in the repo where the critique is documented and its implications for the repo's editorial choices are laid out.

## 7. Open questions

1. **The Gondeville MS *Histoire de Gournay***: location and survival status unknown. The most likely repositories (in descending order of probability): Bibliothèque nationale Paris, Archives départementales de Seine-Maritime (Rouen), Archives départementales de l'Eure (Évreux), Bibliothèque municipale de Gournay-en-Bray. A future research pass could attempt to locate it directly.

2. **The Cordier MS** (c. 1710–1738): same as above. May be the same MS as the Gondeville reference, or a separate parallel transmission. Potin 1842 names Nicolas Cordier as one of his three principal manuscript sources.

3. **Delisle's specific publications on the Gournay genealogy**: Pattou and French Wikipedia cite Delisle's critique without naming the specific publication. Likely candidates: an article in *Bibliothèque de l'École des chartes*; a footnote in his *Recueil des actes de Henri II*; or a comment in an unrelated charter edition. **Open task**: locate Delisle's specific written critique.

4. **The Évreux archive collection**: Delisle examined it for the la Ferté charter and found it absent. Future research pass could verify by examining the modern catalogue of the archives départementales de l'Eure.

5. **Pierre Bauduin's modern scholarship**: cited briefly by French Wikipedia. Bauduin is a contemporary scholar of early Norman aristocracy at Caen; his publications would be the most current scholarly engagement with the Gournay lineage. **Open task**: identify and consult Bauduin's specific works on the early Gournays.

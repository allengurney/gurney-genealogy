# Francis Gurney and the L'Estrange household network

Francis Gurney was not a one-off supplier to the L'Estranges. The transcribed account sample contains 31 entries linked to him between 1612 and 1626, documenting him as a supplier, bill payee, payment intermediary, recipient of goods, and debtor in the L'Estrange and King’s Lynn financial network. It does not name John or Edward Gurney, establish Francis’s continuous residence in any one place, or record a Gurney child in another household.[^ukda]

## Earlier family context

Nichols’s prefatory notice to Thoms’s 1839 edition identifies Francis as a London merchant and an uncle of Edward Gurney of West Barsham; it says that Alice Lady L’Estrange’s Hunstanton account book mentioned him frequently. The same notice describes a broader Gurney–L’Estrange cousinage through Lewkenor and Stubbe, while specifically declining to confirm an old L’Estrange–Gurney pedigree and heraldic reading. That makes the kinship context useful, but not a substitute for a documented relationship in any individual account entry.[^thoms]

Daniel Gurney likewise described Francis as a London merchant and reported a 1623 Court of Wards bond involving Francis and Sir Owin Smith. The later database entry naming Smith in the Lynn accounts is therefore consistent with a real Francis–Smith financial connection, but neither source proves that every obligation was the same transaction.[^dg-court-wards]

## Direct account result, 1612–1626

The database’s Francis record is `people ref` 718: “Mr Francis Gurney,” male, first appearance 1613, annotated “of London, distant relative. A merchant (merry jests).” It has no formal person-to-place link. Entry 3442 is dated 3 January 1612, so the stated first appearance is not chronological; the study guide cautions that this metadata was not fully corrected after deduplication. The 31 linked account entries nevertheless provide a dated, source-mapped record through `LEST/P6` and `LEST/P7`.[^ukda]

| Function supported by the entries | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier or bill payee | Hops (1612), bills (1613), sweet meats (1616), and payments directly to Francis. | Does not identify a shop, home, or a Gurney household member. |
| Payment intermediary | Francis paid or was to pay Lock, Blanckes, Rust, Lawrence Michael, Hans Weller, the Bishop of Ely, and the East India Company. | A payment route is not proof that he acted as a household servant, resident agent, or guardian. |
| Recipient of goods | Oyster barrels and Lady Hobart’s portrait case were carried to “Mr Gurney’s” on 16 May 1618. | The account does not give the address or identify other occupants. |
| Creditor/debtor in a continuing financial relationship | The 1624 Lock interest entry, followed by 1625–26 Lynn obligations, records credit exposure involving Francis. | The entries do not identify why the debt originated or where Francis lived. |

### Lock and Lawrence Michael

The two strongest named commercial associates are both indexed as London-based tailors and moneylenders. Mr Lock [335] appears with Francis in eight linked entries: 1614–16 payments, the 1615 Browning/Norwich instruction, and two 1624 records. Entry 11898 explicitly charges interest (“use”) on £100 due to Lock from Francis for two years.[^ukda]

Lawrence Michael [894] appears with Francis in seven entries from 1618 to 1622. The entries cover liveries and clothes, apparel for Nicholas L’Estrange, East India Company payments, and settlements through Francis. This is a repeated trade-and-credit connection, not evidence that Michael housed or trained a Gurney child.[^ukda]

The 21 November 1615 Browning entry is precisely bounded: entry 5249, `LEST/P7` disbursement section 027 (fols. 1–85b), names Browning of Norwich, Francis, and Lock but has blank entered currency fields. The database does not support a £30 amount. April 1616 has two adjacent rows: 5459 records a payment through Francis to Lock the tailor, with margin “Lock 20”; 5460 records books at Norwich, with margin “20,” but is not linked to Francis in the people table.[^ukda]

## King’s Lynn obligations

The later entries make King’s Lynn a concrete financial location. Entry 12543, in `LEST/P7` section 043 (1625–26, fols. 142B–147B), concerns £100 due to the town and says that Sir Hamon Le Strange stood bound to the town for Francis. Entry 12818, section 045 (fols. 150–152B), records a payment to the town for Francis after £103 paid by Sir Owin Smith and an obligation by Mr Drury. The precise relationship of these entries to the 1622 textile contract requires the original account sequence; the entries prove obligations, not residence or compliance with that contract.[^ukda]

The separate 1622 Lynn indenture names Francis, Ambrose Tompson, and Martyne Hill in a borough-supported work-and-training arrangement. The transcribed account database contains no Tompson/Thompson or Martin Hill entry and no Great Ellingham text occurrence, so it does not provide an account-based overlap with those partners.[^lynn-indentures][^ukda]

## Rivett and apprenticeship context

The database’s Rivett record [170] is male, has a formal King’s Lynn place link, and is attached to a 1607 interest payment on Roger L’Estrange’s debt and a 1610 payment to “Rivett of Linne” for making a bond. Its stated first appearance of 1610 conflicts with the 1607 linked entry, a known limitation of the post-deduplication person metadata. Neither entry links to Francis, so it cannot identify the man as Margaret Rybett’s relative or as the 1622 Thomas Rivett witness.[^ukda]

The accounts do, however, document a real apprenticeship mechanism around Lawrence Michael. On 25 September 1616, Sir Hamon gave John Spratt money to bind him apprentice with Michael. Entries in June and July 1618 record Sir Hamon going to bind out apprentices and an expense at Docking when unnamed children were bound apprentice. None names John or Edward Gurney. The Le Strange boys’ own teaching and Eton expenses further show schooling arrangements, but not a Gurney placement.[^ukda]

## Bounded results

The search found no John Gurney, Edward Gurney, Anne Gurney, or Margaret Rybett/Rivett in the transcribed account or people tables, and no entry placing a Gurney child as a ward, apprentice, pupil, servant, or household member. The word `ward` appears only in Court of Wards legal context or as the surname Ward. Gressenhall is well represented in estate and household transactions, including servants and unnamed boys, but none names Francis, John, or Edward. These are findings about this transcription’s coverage and indexing, not proof of absence from untranscribed records.[^ukda]

## Record targets

The database narrows later original work to `LEST/P7` section 027 (fols. 1–85b) for the Francis–Lock–Michael and apprenticeship sequence, section 043 (fols. 142B–147B) for the 1625 Lynn obligation, and section 045 (fols. 150–152B) for the 1626 Smith–Drury payment. The 1634 Roger/Eaton material remains outside the transcription sample and must be read in the original post-1626 account books.[^ukda]

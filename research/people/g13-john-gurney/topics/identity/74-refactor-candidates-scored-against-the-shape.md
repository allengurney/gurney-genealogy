# Every candidate scored against the shape — and what the matrix reveals

## The shape as specified

Fifteen attributes, each a scale rather than a binary, none mandatory:

**0** surname Gurney or variant · **1** a household with Mary, Richard and/or John Jr, in that order ·
**2** a Mary born c.1628 · **3** a Richard born c.1630 · **4** a John born c.1633 · **5** a father named
John · **6** a mother named Mary · **7** the father born c.1603 · **8** a John × Mary marriage c.1622–28
· **9** a John × Mary marriage c.1628–40 *(second-marriage hypothesis)* · **10** the father's father
named Richard · **11** a region known for Great Migration participation · **12** a region connected to
Weymouth, Massachusetts or the colonial John's cohorts · **13** the father a tailor · **14** an event
c.1636–39 whose context could support emigration.

*Attribute 14 arrived truncated — "whose context could support advocacy of" — and is read throughout as
**advocacy of emigration**, i.e. a documented push event. If that is wrong the scoring of 14 needs
redoing.*

The three record-state rows — *no record survives* (21→23), *documented but never assembled* (17),
*documented but in a source not yet searched* (9→5) — are not people and cannot be scored. Together
they hold 45%.

**The two rows added 29 July 2026** both come from unit 75 and both illustrate the two-family split
below rather than breaking it. **Gurley of Westminster** is family one taken to its limit: father John,
mother Mary, a daughter Mary baptised 1631 satisfying the primary bound exactly, and the household's
records stopping in 1633 — then no Richard, no John, three surplus daughters, and a surname (`Gurley`,
requiring `n`→`l`) that sits at the bottom of the plausibility scale and was reached only by
FindMyPast's own variant expansion. **Epping/North Weald** is the first candidate that is mostly `~`
rather than mostly `✗`: its household attributes are *untested*, not absent, because the coverage
negative that would have closed them is void (unit 75 §7). It scores `✓✓` on attribute 10 for a reason
no other candidate can match — the parish's Gurn- line **alternates John and Richard across three
generations**, which is the naming behaviour attribute 10 was derived from.

## What the matrix reveals: the candidates split into two disjoint families

This is the most useful thing the exercise produces, and it was not visible before the attributes were
laid out side by side.

**Family one — documented households with the wrong shape.** C(ii), Hitcham, Ackworth and the
eliminated Eythorne all have a *household*: a father, sometimes a mother, children with dates. What
they lack is the right children. C(ii) has a John and a Richard in the right order but **no Mary in the
parish at any date**. Hitcham has a Mary satisfying the hard bound and **nothing else at all**.
Ackworth has the father and mother right and the children wrong. Eythorne has almost everything and is
eliminated by two burials.

**Family two — documented men with no household at all.** Candidate B, Cheddington 1608, A(1603),
Candidate D and Weston Turville are, respectively, the best trade-and-corridor fit, the only candidate
reached by prediction from the naming custom, the exact age match, the best trade match, and the best
Weymouth-proximity fit. **Every one of them scores zero on attributes 1 through 4.** They are men
without wives or children on record.

**No candidate is in both families.** The emigrant needs a man of the right age *and* his household,
and every candidate supplies one half. That is the actual structure of this problem, and it is a
stronger argument for the record-state rows than anything the file has previously articulated: **the
man's own baptism and his household's baptisms are probably not in the same searchable place** — one
side is lost, unindexed, or in a class not yet opened. That is exactly what the 9% *not-yet-searched*
row and the 19% *no-record* row describe.

## Per-candidate notes where the score needs explaining

**Candidate B (11%) is contradicted by attribute 10.** Its whole thesis is that John's father was
**Francis**; the shape predicts **Richard**. B is strong on the contextual attributes — corridor,
trade lineage, and the 1634 total land sale as a push event squarely in the 14 window — and empty on
every attribute that describes an actual household, because no document names a John of this family,
a wife, or a child. B is a context without a household.

**Ackworth (9%) is the only candidate scoring attributes 5 and 6 together**, and attribute 9 is its
strongest card. The shape's explicit allowance for a second-marriage hypothesis is precisely what the
file's central block on Ackworth does not accommodate: it rules the household out because Mary Shed's
1647 Braintree marriage puts her parents' marriage by about 1630, which a June 1636 marriage cannot
produce — **decisive only if 1636 was John's first marriage**, and the record gives no marital status.
Against it: the 1637 baptism names **no parents**, and "John Thomas Gurnoe" may be *"John son of Thomas
Gurnoe"*, an artefact already caught at Toddington.

**C(ii) (4%) is the best household in the file and the worst on attribute 2.** John 1624 and Richard
1626 give two of three colonial sons in the right order; the father's own age, c.1598–1602, is the best
documented fit to "aged 50 or thereabouts"; and its attribute-14 case is the strongest of any
candidate — **two children buried within three weeks in February and March 1638/9**, a documented
family catastrophe inside the exact window. But **no Mary Gurney is baptised at Great Berkhamsted at
any date under any spelling or any father, 1544–1888**, and no John Gurney marries anywhere in
Hertfordshire between 1600 and 1654. Attribute 10 is contradicted: C(ii)'s father was C(i), a John.

**Hitcham falls 5% → 3% on new evidence this round.** Its case is attribute 2 — a Mary baptised 22
January 1630/1, satisfying the hard bound — plus attribute 5. **Attribute 6 has now been damaged.**
Hitcham is a true singleton: one Gurney baptism in the entire Buckinghamshire index, with **no mother
recorded**.[^hitcham] Five miles away, **John Gurney married Susan at Farnham Royal on 30 March 1629**,
and that couple has **no children recorded anywhere in the seventeenth century** — a national parent
search for a father John with a mother Susan returns nothing before 1770.[^susan] Farnham Royal itself
records no John Gurney children. So there is a named, documented, childless-on-record John Gurney
couple within five miles and twenty-two months of the Hitcham baptism, whose wife is **Susan**. That
does not eliminate Hitcham, but the mother of its one child is unknown and now has a plausible
alternative who is not a Mary.

**Weston Turville (3%) is quietly the best on the contextual attributes** and nobody has said so:
attribute 7 (fathering 1627, so about 51 in 1653), attribute 12 (Ashton Clinton, a documented Weymouth
settler parish, is two miles away), and attribute 13 (**a Gurney tailor, Thomas, died in the parish in
1633**). It fails on the entire child core.

**Candidate D (4%) holds the best direct trade match** — his father Robert was *described as a tailor
at Old Change* — and an excellent attribute 7 from his 1623/4 Drapers' admission by redemption. Empty
household, and attribute 10 contradicted.

**The Newgate apprentice (3%) fails attribute 7 decisively** — birth c.29 September 1615, twelve years
off — and cannot satisfy 1 through 6 at all, being an indentured servant. Its only strength is
attribute 12, which it holds absolutely: he was physically in Massachusetts in 1636.

## Feedback on the shape

Eight points, in descending order of how much they would change a score.

**1. ~~Margaret is not a variant of Mary~~ — RETRACTED 29 July 2026. Margaret belongs on the mother
axis as a substantial soft hit.** The original text of this point argued that Margaret was "a different
name — Margareta — and clerks did not swap them," and used that to set the Herefordshire
John-and-Margaret household aside. **That was an unevidenced claim about scribal behaviour, made
without paleographic work — the same error the surname-variant retraction in unit 69 was supposed to
have cured, repeated one field to the left.**

The defeating mechanism is ordinary and specific: in seventeenth-century hands **`g` and `y` carry
near-identical looping descenders**, and *Margaret* was routinely contracted by clerks to **`Marg.`**
or **`Mgt.`** to save paper and ink — so the one letter distinguishing the written token from *Mary* is
exactly the letter most likely to be ambiguous. `Marg.` with a looping `g` reads as *Mary* to a later
clerk, a nineteenth-century transcriber or a modern indexer, and a *Mary* is equally extendable to
*Margaret*. **The corruption is introduced by the transcription chain and runs in both directions.**
This is the sliding scale applied to a forename, and the corpus supplies a worked example: Tittleshall
1620 indexes the same child twice with the mother read **Anne** in one transcript and **Mary** in the
other.

The corrected weighting, and the search that followed it, are in unit 75 §1–2. **The candidate scores
below do not change** — but for a defensible reason rather than the bad rule: the Margaret axis was
then run across FreeREG (585 rows), eight FindMyPast county sets (705 rows) and the national
cross-collection parent search with a passing Eythorne positive control, and **no Gurney household
anywhere has a father in the John family and a mother in the Margaret family.** The Herefordshire
household is eliminated on its date — Stoke Lacy, **1687** — not on its wife's name. *Molly* and
*Polly* remain excluded as eighteenth-century hypocorisms that cannot appear in a 1620s register.

**2. Attribute 2 has a primary bound and attributes 3 and 4 do not — they should not carry equal
weight.** Mary Gurney married Daniel Shed at Braintree in 1647, so at a minimum marriage age of sixteen
she was **born by 1631**. That is a primary constraint. The "c.1628" is compiled tradition from Sprague
(2001) and the *History of Weymouth* (1923). Richard c.1630 and John c.1633 rest **entirely** on those
same compiled estimates with no primary record behind them. **Recommend: score attribute 2 against
"born by 1631" and treat c.1628 as soft; mark 3 and 4 explicitly as compiled-estimate attributes worth
less than 2.** This matters directly — Hitcham's Mary of January 1630/1 satisfies the primary bound
exactly while looking three years off the traditional date.

**3. Attributes 8 and 9 are mutually exclusive**, so a candidate should score one or the other. Scoring
both double-counts the marriage.

**4. Attribute 10 is a derived prediction, not an independent observation.** It comes from reading the
naming custom forwards — eldest son takes the paternal grandfather's name — and it is conditional on
Richard being the eldest son. **It also directly contradicts Candidate B**, whose father is Francis,
and C(ii) and D, whose fathers are John and Robert. Worth being explicit that a hit on 10 is a hit on a
hypothesis, and that a miss is weak evidence because naming customs were followed loosely.

**5. I would add an attribute: the household's records STOP between 1635 and 1641 with no burial or
probate for the head, in a parish whose burials are demonstrably covered.** This is the departure
signature, and it is separable from attribute 14 — a push event explains *why* he might go, this
records *that* the household stops. It needs the coverage control attached or it is worthless: this
project has produced two false departure gaps by omitting it. On a properly controlled test **only
Ackworth currently passes** — 170 Smith burials in the parish and zero Gurney burials.

**6. I would add an attribute penalising children with no colonial counterpart.** A household with the
right three children and no others fits better than one with the right two and four extras. C(ii)
carries Michaell and Elizabeth; the St Clement Danes Gurley household carries Elizabeth, Ellynor and
Alexander. As specified the shape can only reward matches, never penalise surplus.

**7. Attribute 0 should be the weighted scale this file worked out**, not a single ✓: Gurney / Gurny /
Gourney / Gurnie at full weight; Gurne / Gurn as near-certain contractions; **Gurnard / Gurnat at
moderate-to-high, because the Heralds' Visitation of London of 1634 records "Richard Gurny, alias
Gurnard"** and the family's own crest is a canting gurnard; Gorne / Garne / Gernne as ordinary vowel
drift; Gurnett / Gurnel / Gurner lower; and *Garne* in Gloucestershire and *Gurley* in Westminster
probably distinct surnames with their own lineages.

**8. Peter and Isaac should be explicitly excluded from scoring, not treated as missing children.** No
Peter Gurney-variant baptism exists anywhere in Great Britain 1627–47 and no Isaac 1632–52; both men
first appear in colonial records. Their absence in England is **expected** and must not count against a
candidate. The same applies to Sarah, who has no primary record at all and is absent from Torrey's
compendium of New England marriages before 1700.

## Consequence for the table

One row moves: **Hitcham 5% → 3%**, on the Farnham Royal alternative. The two points go to **no record
survives, 19% → 21%** — because Hitcham was the last unaccounted-for record on the child axis, and
weakening it pushes toward the man not being in the searched corpus at all.

Everything else holds. The table remains sixteen rows summing to 100, and the three record-state rows
now hold **47%**.

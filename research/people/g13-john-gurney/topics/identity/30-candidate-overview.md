Every documented same-name English John Gurney household the record sweeps have surfaced is tested against one shared matching-criteria profile, drawn from the colonial baseline and set out below; each household that fails it is eliminated or reduced to a residual. Of the 51 named households, 45 are eliminated outright and five more are held to a residual 1–3% likelihood; only Candidate B — the working hypothesis that John was the son of Francis Gurney of Norfolk and London — survives the test.[^sweep-basis] Candidate B's own probability assessment, including its unknown-corridor residuals, is the argument-specific synthesis of the [John Gurney identity case file](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v4.md) §11; it stays there rather than being re-derived in this graph.[^probability-xref] This unit carries the shared test and its outcome, not the surviving candidate's own case.

## The matching-criteria profile

Any English candidate for the Braintree emigrant must plausibly account for a tailor named John Gurney — a trade already anchored in the colonial record[^occupation] — married to a wife named Mary who bore children Sarah, Mary, Richard, John, and Peter,[^wife-children] born in England within a c.1602–1610 window read off his own 1652/3 "aged 50 or thereabouts" deposition,[^birth] and gone from the English record by the time he first appears in Massachusetts in June 1641.[^arrival-window] A settled Puritan household is a soft fifth criterion, consistent with — though not distinctive of — most Great Migration emigrants of the period.[^religion] It is this whole profile, not a bare name match, that every candidate is tested against.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000073 -->

## How candidates are tested and eliminated

Against that profile, a household is eliminated on structural rather than circumstantial grounds: continuing documented English residence past June 1641, most visibly Candidate A's Saint Mary, Aylesbury baptisms running to 1653 across a continuous chain from a 1603 Stewkley baptism, and repeated again and again across the same-name clearance sweep (Chesham, East Claydon, Denton, and dozens more);[^structural-grounds] a wife whose name is not Mary — the ground that clears the majority of the sweep (Alice Oliffe, Elizabeth, Anne, Rose, Jane, and others);[^structural-grounds] a child set that does not match, or that collides in date, as in Candidate C's eight-child Berkhamsted family, whose 1624 John Jr. and 1634 Sarah do not line up with the colonial John Jr.'s c.1633 or Sarah's c.1628, and which has no Mary or Peter at all;[^candidate-c-grounds] or a documented trade, death, or generation that places the household in the wrong period, as in Candidate D's 1662 London hearth-tax entry — dated the same calendar year the colonial John was dying at Boston.[^candidate-d-grounds] A household attested by only a single indexed event, with no continuing record behind it, is held at a low residual (1–3%) rather than eliminated outright, since one record cannot itself rule out an unrecorded emigration.[^single-attestation] This is the same family-status-and-chronology standard already applied once inside this graph, where it separates the Braintree emigrant himself from a same-named man he is not: the 1636 Newgate apprentice, cleared on the identical combination of a mismatched family status and a chronology that will not bend to fit.[^newgate-precedent]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000074 -->

## The candidate landscape

| Candidate | Standing | Elimination ground |
|---|---|---|
| A — Aylesbury / Bierton / Northamptonshire | Eliminated | Continuous English residence 1603–1653; wife Alice Oliffe |
| **B — son of Francis Gurney & Margaret Rybett** | **Probable (~65%; case file §11)** | Sole survivor of the shared test |
| C — Berkhamsted, Hertfordshire | Eliminated | Eight-child family 1610–1636; wrong age; no Mary or Peter |
| D — London draper, Old Change | Eliminated | Continuing London residence through 1662 |
| Five other named households | Unlikely, ~8% combined | Single-attestation only, capped by chronology or a naming collision |
| Same-name clearance sweep (45 further households) | Eliminated | Died in England, wrong wife, wrong trade, or wrong generation |

The 45 further households of the clearance sweep are not restated one by one here. Their household-by-household enumeration, each with its single strongest elimination ground, is published as the case file's §8.6 table, which this unit cross-references as the master list.[^clearance-sweep-master] Full working detail for each principal candidate sits in its own topic: the Aylesbury/Buckinghamshire, Ackworth, London-draper, and other-eliminations units, with Berkhamsted (Candidate C) carried in the assessment unit.[^candidate-files] Candidate B's own trade/corridor/motive/network argument and its East Dereham baptism reading are in the [Candidate B unit](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/people/g13-john-gurney/topics/identity/32-norfolk-parentage.md), and what the whole elimination amounts to is the [assessment unit](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/people/g13-john-gurney/topics/identity/37-identity-assessment.md). This unit tests and clears the field; it does not argue the surviving candidate's case.[^candidate-b-xref]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000075 -->

# Identity — candidate overview

Every documented same-name English John Gurney household the record sweeps have surfaced is tested against one shared specification of what the emigrant and his household must look like. This unit carries the test, how it is applied, and what the field looks like after it. It does not argue any candidate's own case.

**The test does not produce a single survivor.** Five named men stand in the field alongside Candidate B — the Ackworth household, the Cheddington 1608 John, the younger Berkhamsted John, the Hitcham head and the Epping line — and the leading rows in the assessment are not people at all.

## The specification a candidate is tested against

Any English candidate must plausibly account for a **tailor named John Gurney**, a trade anchored in the colonial record;[^occupation] married to a wife named **Mary**;[^wife-children] with children including **Mary, Richard and John**, born in England, and a daughter Mary **born by 1631** on the primary bound of her own 1647 Braintree marriage;[^mary-bound] the father himself born in England within a c.1602–1610 window read off his 1652/3 "aged 50 or thereabouts" deposition;[^birth] and gone from the English record by the time he first appears in Massachusetts in June 1641.[^arrival-window] A settled Puritan household is a soft further criterion, consistent with — though not distinctive of — most Great Migration emigrants of the period.[^religion]

**Two children are excluded from the test and one is set aside.** No Peter Gurney-variant baptism exists anywhere in Great Britain 1627–1647 and no Isaac 1632–1652; both men first appear in colonial records, so their absence from the English record is *expected* and must not count against any candidate. Sarah is set aside for a different reason — she has no primary record at all and is absent from Torrey's compendium of New England marriages before 1700.[^peter-isaac] The English child set is Mary, Richard, John and possibly Sarah.

The full specification is fifteen weighted attributes, each a scale rather than a binary and none mandatory, running from the surname through the household, the parents, the father's age, two alternative marriage windows, a predicted paternal grandfather, region, trade and a push event. It is set out, with the matrix scoring every non-zero candidate against it, in its own unit.[^shape]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000073 -->

## How candidates are tested, and where the testing went wrong

A household is eliminated on structural grounds rather than circumstantial ones: **continuing documented English residence** past June 1641; **a wife whose name is not Mary**, which clears the majority of the sweep; **a child set that does not match or collides in date**; or **a documented trade, death or generation** that places the household in the wrong period. A household attested by only a single indexed event, with no continuing record behind it, is held at a low residual rather than eliminated outright, since one record cannot itself rule out an unrecorded emigration.[^structural-grounds]

Three corrections to that procedure came out of the July 2026 work, and each of them changed results.

**Counting households is not counting men.** The older enumeration — "of the 51 named households, 45 are eliminated outright" — counts households *as reconstructed*, which repeatedly proves to be too few men. Candidate A was one household and is two. Candidate C was one and is two. Great Berkhamsted alone holds **four** contemporaneous Gurney households rather than the one it was carried as. Stewkley holds two, and Longdon in Worcestershire is a father and a son. **Every household tested for this defect has split.** Any elimination that rests on a household's shape must state the shape hypothesis explicitly and be marked conditional on it.[^merges]

**Requiring the whole child set is a filter built to return nothing.** Against seventeenth-century registers, demanding Mary *and* Richard *and* John in one household is not a test; it is a guarantee of a null result. **One child of the right name in the right window is the threshold; two is a strong hit.** Applied properly, the England-wide sweep returns exactly one unaccounted-for record — Mary Gurny, Hitcham, 1631 — where the stricter form returned none.[^threshold]

**A negative is a statement about an instrument until it is controlled.** Every sweep must return a record already known to exist and in scope before its zero means anything. Two false England-wide negatives were manufactured inside eight days by uncontrolled queries, and both were caught by that control: the "Stewkley register silence after 1614," which was true of one dataset and false of the parish, and the "no Gurney burial at Great Berkhamsted at any date," where the parish in fact holds sixteen. **No index is complete and the gaps between them do not coincide** — tested cell by cell across four providers.[^controls]

**And a whole class of negatives is void.** In a single-dataset query on the principal provider, a year bound and a place keyword **cannot be combined**: the pair fails closed. Proven directly — a set holding a 1616 Epping baptism returns 349 Smith baptisms at Epping with no year bound, and **zero** for the same parish bounded to 1616. Any negative anywhere in this research resting on both parameters at once is void and must be re-run without the year. The related parameter `sortby` is accepted and silently ignored, so a set's earliest coverage year cannot be established by sorting either.[^void]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000074 -->

The remaining probability — **forty-four percent, and the largest share in the model** — sits on three rows that describe the state of the record rather than a man: *no record survives* at 22%, *documented but never assembled* at 17%, and *documented but in a source not yet searched* at 5%. Their basis and the reasoning behind each number are in the assessment unit.[^assessment]

The cleared households of the clearance sweep are not restated one by one here. Their household-by-household enumeration, each with its single strongest elimination ground, is published as the case file's §8.8 table, which this unit cross-references as the master list.[^clearance-sweep-master] Full working detail for each principal candidate sits in its own topic: [Candidate A and the Buckinghamshire cluster](31-candidate-a-aylesbury.md), [Candidate B](32-norfolk-parentage.md), [Berkhamsted](33-berkhamsted-candidates.md), [Candidate D](34-london-draper.md), [Ackworth](35-candidate-ackworth.md) and [other eliminations](36-other-eliminations.md).[^candidate-files]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000075 -->

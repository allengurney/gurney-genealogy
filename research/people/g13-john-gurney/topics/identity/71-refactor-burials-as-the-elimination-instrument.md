# Burials, not probate — testing the rows that carry probability

## The reframing

§8.7 keeps five men "unaccounted for rather than viable", and the phrase it uses for four of them is
**"no probate in either tier."** That is the wrong instrument to lean on. **Probate exists only for men
with enough property to be worth proving.** A husbandman, a labourer or a country tailor commonly left
none at all, so a probate blank says very little about whether such a man died in England.

The record that individuates an ordinary man is his **burial**. And unit 66 established that this
project's burial searching has been defective — wrong surname stem, wrong place token, both failing
closed. So the burial arm was worth re-running properly against every row that currently carries
probability, with a **common-surname control in each parish**, because burial coverage turns out to
vary enormously parish by parish.

Five parishes tested on burials, and the county marriage index run from both sides. **Four rows move.**

## Stewkley: the burial the file said did not exist

The case file states of the man baptised at Stewkley on 21 February 1602/3: *"No marriage, no burial,
no Canterbury will, no archdeaconry probate."*[^casefile] **The burial clause is false.**

> **John Gurney, buried Stewkley, 21 September 1615.** Buckinghamshire Burial Index, bishop's
> transcripts `D/A/T/160`, register range 1575–1835. **No age and no relationship recorded.**[^stewkley]

It sits inside a cluster — **Elizabeth Gurney 1614, John Gurney 21 September 1615, Martha Gurney
1615, Robert Gurney 1618** — which reads like a household being hit by illness rather than a single
death.

**Two readings, and the index cannot choose between them:**

- **(a) It is the boy baptised in 1602/3**, dying at twelve. **A(1603) is eliminated outright.**
- **(b) It is his father, John Gurney senior**, who was fathering at Stewkley to 1613 (Damaris) and
  could well die in 1615. A(1603) survives.

Neither Elizabeth nor Martha is among the recorded children of John senior — his are John 1602/3,
Simeon 1604, Maria 1609, Damaris 1613 — so the cluster cannot be resolved by matching siblings.

**The discriminating evidence is the register entry itself**, which for this period normally reads
"John Gurney the elder", "the younger", or "son of". The bishop's transcript reference is known
(`D/A/T/160`), so this is a specific, cheap image pull.

**Why the row still falls even unresolved.** A(1603)'s 4% rested on two things: the best age match in
the corpus to "aged 50 or thereabouts", and being *unaccounted for on every axis*. The second support
is now damaged whichever way the record resolves — there is a plausible death record for him at
twelve, in his own parish, in an index the file had already searched. **4% → 2%.**

## Hitcham: a hollow support

Hitcham's 5% rested on a single indexed event — Mary Gurny, 22 January 1631, father John — a good age
fit, and the observation that the head is unaccounted for with "no probate in either tier."

**Hitcham has effectively no seventeenth-century burial coverage in the Buckinghamshire Burial
Index.** A Smith control across the whole index returns **one** burial for the parish, dated
**1827**.[^hitcham] So the absence of a Hitcham Gurney burial is not evidence of anything.

This does not make Hitcham a better candidate; it makes one of its supports vanish. Being
"unaccounted for" is only informative where the records would have accounted for him. **5% → 4%**, and
the row's remaining strength is what it always really was — the single Mary baptism to a father John,
which across the whole England-wide per-child sweep is still the only such record in the country.

## Ackworth: the departure gap is real, and the household is thinner than stated

**The gap is now controlled, and it is the first one in this project that is.** Ackworth's burials are
well indexed — 170 Smith burials in the parish — and there is **no Gurney-variant burial there at
all**. The parish's entire Gurney presence is **two records**: the marriage of 6 June 1636 and a
baptism of 19 January 1637. Nothing before, nothing after.[^ackworth]

That is what emigration looks like, in a parish where death would have been recorded.

**But the household is a weaker construction than the file states**, and both records had to be opened
to see it. Both are IGI-derived index rows, and:

1. **The 1637 baptism names no parents at all.** "John Thomas Gurnoe, baptism 19 January 1637,
   Ackworth" — *Father's first name(s): –. Mother's first name(s): –.* The attachment of this child to
   the 1636 couple is an **inference from parish and date**, not something the record states.
2. **The 1636 marriage gives no marital status**, no ages, no parentage — John Gurnoe and Mary Burton,
   and nothing else.
3. **"John Thomas" may not be a double forename.** With the father field blank, "John Thomas Gurnoe"
   is exactly the shape an entry reading *"John, son of Thomas Gurnoe"* takes when an indexer collapses
   it. **This project has already caught that precise artefact** in Bedfordshire, where "John Gernne Or
   Gurney, 1630, Toddington" was read as father John and proves on the county set to be father
   **Thomas**. If that reading holds here, the 1637 baptism belongs to a Thomas Gurnoe and the
   Ackworth "household" is one marriage standing alone.

**And one observation that cuts the other way — labelled speculation.** The file blocks Ackworth
principally because daughter Mary's 1647 Braintree marriage puts her parents' marriage by about 1630,
which a June 1636 marriage cannot produce. **That is decisive only if the 1636 marriage was John's
first.** The record does not say. If it were a second marriage, Mary Burton could be the colonial wife
Mary who died at Braintree in 1661, with Mary, Richard and John Jr the children of an unfound earlier
wife — and the block dissolves. There is no evidence for this; it is a hole in the block rather than a
reason to believe. It should be stated because the file presents the bound as conclusive and it is
conditional.

**Net: Ackworth holds at 7%.** The controlled departure gap strengthens it by as much as the hollowed
household weakens it.

## The Buckinghamshire marriage, tested from both sides

The missing step for **every** Buckinghamshire survivor is the same one: a marriage to a Mary in the
later 1620s. Tested properly on the county marriage index, from the groom side and the bride side:[^bucksmarr]

**Every John Gurney marriage recorded in Buckinghamshire, 1572–1704:**

> 1572 Alice, Denham · 1573 Elizabeth, Bierton with Broughton · **1628 Alice, Bierton with Broughton**
> · **1629 Susan, Farnham Royal** · **1630 Elizabeth, East Claydon** · **1638 Avis, Amersham** ·
> **1640 Rebecca, Ivinghoe** · 1663 **Mary**, Edlesborough · 1691 Susannah, Dinton · 1699 and 1700
> Anne, Chesham · 1704 Elizabeth, Chalfont St Giles

**Five marriages fall in the emigration window, and not one bride is a Mary** — Alice, Susan,
Elizabeth, Avis, Rebecca. Run from the bride side the answer is identical: **the earliest Mary who
marries a John Gurney in Buckinghamshire is Mary Kidgell at Edlesborough in 1663**, and the next is
1710.

The colonial John's wife Mary was married to him by about 1627–30 and died at Braintree in 1661, two
years before that Edlesborough marriage. **No Buckinghamshire John Gurney married a Mary in time to
be him.**

**This is now a three-instrument negative.** Ancestry's England-wide marriage set records only two
John × Mary marriages in the whole country before 1660 — Eythorne 1632 and Ackworth 1636. The
Hertfordshire county set records no John Gurney marriage of any kind between 1600 and 1654. And the
Buckinghamshire county set records five, none to a Mary. Three independent transcriptions agree.

**It does not eliminate the Buckinghamshire rows**, because grooms commonly married in the bride's
parish and the belt's men could have married in Bedfordshire, Northamptonshire or Oxfordshire — which
is exactly what R-00c and R-33 exist to test, and the archdeaconry licence series remain unsearched.
But it does tighten them, and it sharpens the asymmetry with Ackworth, which is the one surviving row
that actually possesses a John × Mary marriage.

**Consequent moves: Cheddington 7% → 6%, the Bucks/Herts/Beds group 6% → 5%.**

## What could not be tested

- **Cheddington (7%).** The burial index's coverage for the parish in the target window is unproven —
  one Smith burial at 1557, then nothing until 1766. **R-00b is not answerable from this instrument**
  and needs the parish register or FreeREG, which also lacks it.
- **Candidate D (4%).** The deviation audit's open action is a St Augustine Watling Street burial
  search 1640–1670. The parish is **not reachable by keyword** in FindMyPast's parish-burial corpus —
  `keywords=augustine` returns Norwich St Augustine and Northbourne St Augustine, Kent, and nothing in
  the City of London. It needs Boyd's London Burials scoped by parish, or the LMA registers. **Still
  unrun**, and the reason is now recorded so it is not re-attempted the same way.

## Actions arising

1. **Pull the Stewkley bishop's transcript `D/A/T/160` for 21 September 1615** and read whether the
   entry distinguishes elder from younger, or names a father. It resolves A(1603) either to
   elimination or to survival, and it is one image.
2. **Read the Ackworth register for 19 January 1637** — does the entry name a father, and is the child
   "John Thomas" or "John son of Thomas"? One image, and it decides whether Ackworth is a household or
   two unconnected rows.
3. **Route the Cheddington burial question** to the parish register or Hertfordshire/Bucks archive
   holdings rather than the county index.
4. **Re-route the Candidate D burial search** to Boyd's London Burials or LMA.

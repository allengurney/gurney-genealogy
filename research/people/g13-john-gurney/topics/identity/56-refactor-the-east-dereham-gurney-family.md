# East Dereham had its own Gurney family, and it was not a humble one

The identification has always turned on a question nobody could answer: **why East Dereham?** Why
should a sixth son of the West Barsham gentry baptise his children in a market town five miles from
the family seat, in a parish no earlier account connected to the Gurneys?

The standing answer is a widow. A Margery Gurnye, widow of East Dereham, proved a will in 1602–03,
and the manor court remembered her Ellingfield copyhold for decades afterwards; the repo reads that
as a pre-existing kin presence, a reason for Francis to settle his young family there.[^margery]

That answer is now much larger than a widow, and it points in the opposite direction from the one
it has been used to support.

## The family, reconstructed

Three records that have never been read against each other, one of them sitting in the repo's own
corpus since the project began:

**1543 — Michael Gurney, of East Dereham, married Marion Waggestar, widow of East Dereham, at St
Stephen's, Norwich.** Walter Rye's chronological list of Gurneys appearing at Norwich carries the
entry verbatim.[^rye-michael]

**Before 1603 — *Gourney v Gourney* in Chancery.** Arthur Gourney, plaintiff, sued **Margery
Gourney, widow of Michael Gourney deceased**. Father and son had been **jointly seised of certain
tenements in East Dereham**; the son consented to his father alienating them, and the father in
consideration bequeathed him a legacy of **£100**.[^c2]

**1591 — Arthur Gurney, gentleman, of Yaxham**, bought a house in Pottergate Street, St Gregory's,
Norwich; Rye adds that he was "probably the Arthur G. of Great Dunham who married Katherine
Walpoole of the same, widow, by licence 12 April 1584."[^rye-arthur]

**Yaxham adjoins East Dereham — the parishes are two miles apart.** Great Dunham is about eight miles
north-west, in the same mid-Norfolk country. Michael is an uncommon forename; the parish, surname,
and period line up on every side. The reconstruction that follows is an identification by name,
place and period, not a proof, and two joints in it should be named: the 1543 bridegroom and the
Chancery defendant's late husband could in principle be father and son of the same name, and Rye's
own Yaxham-to-Great-Dunham link carries his hedge, not mine.

With that stated, the household reads:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Michael Gurney of East Dereham** | m. Marion Waggestar, widow, at St Stephen's, Norwich, 1543; later married **Margery**; held freehold tenements in East Dereham jointly with his son; made a will leaving Arthur £100; **dead before 1603, and probably before 1585** |
| **Margery Gurney, his widow** | of East Dereham; sued by her stepson or son in Chancery; **will proved 1602–03** in the Archdeaconry of Norfolk; her Ellingfield copyhold recited in the manor court in 1627 and again in 1636 |
| **Arthur Gurney, gentleman** | of Yaxham, and probably of Great Dunham; m. Katherine Walpoole, widow, **by licence, 12 April 1584**; bought a house in Pottergate Street, St Gregory's, Norwich, 1591; plaintiff in Chancery for his £100 |

The "probably before 1585" for Michael's death is a coverage inference and worth stating as one: the
Norfolk probate master index has been enumerated as a **closed census for 1585–1650**, and it
contains **no Michael Gurney and no Arthur Gurney** in any Norwich court.[^census] Michael made a
will — the Chancery suit is a claim under it — so if he died after 1585 his probate should be in
that census, and it is not. The same negative leaves Arthur unaccounted for at death: a gentleman
with freehold, a £100 legacy and a Norwich town house who left no Norfolk probate between 1585 and
1650 either died before 1585, which his 1584 marriage and 1591 purchase make impossible, or proved
elsewhere, or left the county.

## This is not the humble household the file has assumed

The repo's reading of the court choice has been that Margery proved in the Archdeaconry of Norfolk,
"the lesser-estate court," so future humble-Gurney finds should be expected in the archdeaconry
series rather than the Consistory.[^census] That reading is sound for Margery's own goods and wrong
about the family.

**Joint seisin is freehold language.** A £100 legacy in the 1580s or 1590s is not a cottager's
bequest. Arthur is styled **gentleman**, married **by licence** rather than by banns, and bought a
house in a Norwich city parish. Set beside the manorial evidence — a copyhold at Ellingfield large
enough that its boundary was still being recited forty years after the tenancy ended, and a parcel
in the later rolls actually named **"Gurneys Croft"**[^croft] — the household reads as a
substantial mid-Norfolk freeholding family with a foot in the county town, seated at East Dereham
from at least the 1540s.

**That removes the main objection to the alternative reading.** The deviation audit raised, and never
resolved, whether the "ffrancis Gurney" fathering children at East Dereham in 1610 and 1618 is
Francis-A of West Barsham at all — noting that nothing positively places Francis-A in the parish
except those children, which is the proposition at issue.[^d10] The instinctive answer has been that
a gentleman's son would not sit among a parish's copyholders. But there was a Gurney family at East
Dereham that was *not* a copyholding family, whose head was styled gentleman in 1591 and whose
freehold the Court of Chancery adjudicated.

Stated plainly, and without overstating it: **the parish held a Gurney family of the right standing,
in the right generation, to have produced a Francis.** Nothing here names one. What has changed is
only that the local reading is no longer socially implausible.

### What this does *not* do — a correction

An earlier draft of this unit said the local reading, if it held, would collapse Candidate B
outright. That overstates it, and the overstatement is worth correcting explicitly because it
confuses two different Francises.

**Francis Gurney G14's own identity and geography are not in question.** He was born at West Barsham
Hall on 13 September 1581, twin with Anthony, sixth son of Henry Gurnay, Esq., and Ellen
Blennerhassett; apprenticed in London 1599; freed a Merchant Taylor in 1606; commercially at Norwich
from about 1606; married Margaret Rybett at St Martin at Palace, Norwich, on 23 September 1611; and
in London at St Benet Fink from 1619.[^francis-chronology] None of that depends on East Dereham.

**Nor are the East Dereham children in doubt as records.** Edward, son of ffrancis Gurney, baptised
27 May 1610, is D-class, fixed by the in-parchment "East Dereham 1610" heading; Marye, daughter of
ffrancis Gurnoe, baptised 25 May 1618, sits in the 1618-19 return. Both survived the deviation audit
intact.[^siblings] What vanished in July 2026 was **John's** baptism, not his siblings'.

So the live question is narrow: **is the "ffrancis Gurney" of those two baptisms Francis G14, or the
local family's man?** If it is the local family's, what Candidate B loses is its *Norfolk residence
anchor* — the "documented Norfolk family in the right window" that sits as row 2 of the supporting
evidence — leaving the 1611 marriage, the Pease tradition and the circumstantial web. That is a
serious reduction. It is not a collapse, and this unit should not be read as claiming one.

**And losing John's East Dereham baptism has relieved a chronological strain rather than created
one.** While that baptism stood, John's birth had to be squeezed into c.1609/10 at East Dereham —
before his father's September 1611 marriage, from a father who had been a bound London apprentice
until 1606, and awkwardly late against a deposed age of about fifty in 1653. Removing it lets the
Francis geo-chronology run cleanly: Norwich from about 1606, married 1611, an East Dereham household
1610–1618, London from 1618-19. The friction now sits entirely on the question of **where a son John
born about 1603–1608 came from** — which is why an earlier, unfound marriage keeps being proposed,
and why the honest reading of the fact pattern is that **another John origin may simply be more
viable, if one can be found that survives elimination**.

## A speculation, labelled

Michael Gurney of East Dereham married at **St Stephen's, Norwich**, in 1543. Francis Gurney married
Margaret Rybett at **St Martin at Palace, Norwich**, in 1611. The childhood unit treats the 1611
parish as an open puzzle — "a parish in which neither family is otherwise documented" — and reaches
for status disparity or a pre-existing child as the reason a couple would ride a day to marry away
from local eyes.[^marriage-parish]

**If the East Dereham Gurneys habitually married at Norwich city churches, the 1611 marriage is a
local-family signature rather than a gentry-discretion one.** Two instances seventy years apart are
not a habit, and I am not treating this as evidence. It is a cheap thing to test — the Norwich
marriage registers and the Norfolk marriage licence bonds are the instrument — and it would bear
directly on the one record everyone agrees is genuine.

## What the register can and cannot be asked (R-18, worked)

The obvious test was to look for Michael, Margery or Arthur in the parish's own register. The
membranes were read. **The test cannot be run, for the same reason Candidate B cannot be tested from
this register.**

The pre-1610 stretch of PD 86/41 is far gappier than the file has recorded. Reading the in-parchment
and endorsed headings across images `00693`–`00711`, the surviving annual returns are:[^r18]

| Images | Return |
|---|---|
| 00693 / 00694, 00695 / 00696 | **1593-4** christenings; 00697 the dorse, endorsed "1593–1594 / 38 Eliz" and signed by John Blomfield, churchwarden |
| 00698 / 00699 | 1593-4 marriages and burials |
| 00700 / 00701 → 00702 / 00703 | **1595-6** — "A true coppy taken out of the register book of Estdereham … from the yeare of our lord god 1595 unto the yeare of our lord god 1596", closing "Churchwardens of Estdereham … 1 Aprilis 1596, John Crampton Vicar" |
| 00704 / 00705 → 00706 / 00707 | **1601** — "This Indenture …", with Mariages, Christenings and Burialls sections |
| 00708 / 00709 → 00710 / 00711 | **1608-9** — "from the … 1608 untill the 25th of March [1609] … the xxvij of Aprill 1609"; "Mariages 1608"; "Burialls A[nno] 1609" |

**Between 1594 and 1608 the file holds four annual returns and is missing twelve.** No return survives
for 1594-5, for 1596-7 through 1600-1, or for **1602-3 through 1607-8**.

**The missing years include 1602–03 — the year Margery Gurney's will was proved.** Her burial is
therefore unobservable in the only witness the parish ever had, and so is any Gurney baptism,
marriage or burial across the six years before Francis's documented residence begins. This is the
same structural fact the file already records for 1611–1615, arriving at the other end of the
question: **PD 86/41 can no more test the local-family reading at 1596–1607 than it can test
Candidate B at 1611–15.** Both hypotheses are shielded by the same losses, and neither should draw
comfort from the other's silence.

**No Gurney-variant entry appears in any surviving pre-1610 return**, with the confidence stated
per return. The 1593-4 and 1595-6 membranes are in a bold, well-preserved hand and were read
line-by-line at grid scale — a clean negative across roughly ninety christenings, nineteen marriages
and forty burials. The 1608-9 christenings and marriages were read at 4× line strips and are a clean
negative. **The 1601 return is a weak negative and should be recorded as one:** the membrane carries
both a "Faded Document" and a "Soiled Document" archival flag, a heavy central stain runs the length
of the text block, and although the burials read at 4× with autocontrast, a minority of surnames on
that return cannot be resolved at all.

## Chasing Arthur — a first pass, and what it bounds

The Yaxham test was begun and is **not finished**. Two results are worth recording, one of them a
coverage statement that changes how the next pass should be run.

**Arthur Gurney is absent from the Norfolk probate and marriage-licence-bond index entirely.** A
sweep of Ancestry's collection 62679 — the Norfolk Record Office index covering the Norwich
Consistory, both archdeaconries and the peculiars, and carrying **marriage licence bonds as well as
probate**, 1371–1858 — returns **no Arthur Gurney of any spelling at any date**.[^arthur-62679] Two
consequences. First, it corroborates from a second direction that Arthur left no Norfolk probate,
which was already implied by the closed 1585–1650 census. Second, and more usefully, **Rye's 1584
marriage licence is not reachable through the online Norfolk index** — so either it was issued in a
series that index does not carry, or Rye drew it from the Norwich licence layer that action R-06
already documents as offline-only (Bolingbroke's calendars, NRO HMN 313–317, name-indexed on the
searchroom shelves alone). Either way the licence itself is an offline pull, and Rye's "probably the
Arthur G. of Great Dunham" remains his own hedge rather than a checked identification.

**The Norfolk baptism index does not obviously hold a Yaxham or Great Dunham Gurney, but the sweep is
not complete and the set is parish-selective.** FindMyPast's *Norfolk Baptisms* returns 95
Gurney-variant rows across 1555–1635; the first page carries no Yaxham and no Great Dunham entry.
Pagination does not work on this set — `page=2` returns zero — so the remaining rows have to be
reached by year-slicing, which has not yet been done. And the coverage caveat is decisive for how any
blank should be read: **this set is the Norfolk FHS transcription and is parish-selective — it omits
Francis Gurney's own East Dereham children.** A Yaxham absence here would not be a negative.

What the sweep did surface is a clean corroboration of the central-court work: a **Tacolneston**
household of George Gurney with wives Alice and Maria — Johannes 1583, Tobias 1584, Valentinianus
1585, Robtus 1587, Valentiniag 1591, Anna 1609, Thomas 1611 — which is the family behind the two
Court of Requests suits brought by "George Gurney, parson of Tacolneston, **and his sons**".[^tacolneston]
That is a mid-Norfolk clerical Gurney household with adult sons in the right generation, standing
alongside the East Dereham one, and it has never been assessed here.

**How the next pass should be run.** Both the Yaxham question and the child-sequence question want
the same instrument, and it is not the one used first. FamilySearch's record search returns
**role-aggregated rows** — "Richard Gurney, Father, spouses …, children …" — rather than the
underlying baptism events, and its place parameter does not bind, so a per-parish or per-child sweep
run there measures the interface rather than the record. The right instrument is a **national
baptism set read as index rows with Father / Mother / Place columns**, sliced by year to defeat the
broken pagination, and run **per child forename with the surname wildcarded**, with no parent
constraint at all.

## Where this leaves it, and what would settle it

Nothing here moves Candidate B down on evidence. What it does is remove a prop and sharpen a rival:
the "why East Dereham?" finding has been carried in one direction only, and it reads at least as
well in the other. That asymmetric use is the specific habit the deviation audit was written to
break.

The tests, in order of decisiveness, and all of them outside the East Dereham register:

1. **The Yaxham and Great Dunham parish registers**, for Arthur Gurney's household — his marriage in
   1584 should be followed by children in the later 1580s and 1590s, and one of them is either named
   Francis or is not. This is the direct test, and it is an index-and-image question, not a
   record-office one.
2. **The Norfolk marriage licence bonds and allegations**, which produced Arthur's own 1584 licence
   and would carry any further marriages of the family.
3. **Arthur Gurney's probate**, absent from the Norfolk courts across 1585–1650 — worth a
   Prerogative Court check, since a gentleman with a Norwich house and mid-Norfolk freehold could
   well have proved at Canterbury.
4. **The Chancery pleadings, C 2/Eliz/G13/62**, which will state both parties' parishes and status
   and may name further children.

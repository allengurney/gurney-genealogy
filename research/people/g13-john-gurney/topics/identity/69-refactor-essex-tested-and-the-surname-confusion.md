# Essex tested at last — and it is open, not closed

## The question nobody had asked

Essex is the county the **colonial** evidence points at hardest, and it has never been swept.

John Gurney's Braintree land was held by lease inside the estate of **William Tyng of Stanford Rivers,
Essex**. His daughter Mary married **Daniel Shed of Finchingfield, Essex**. Braintree, Massachusetts
took its name from **Braintree, Essex**. Francis G14's second wife Anne Browning came from the
Maldon-Essex network. The file has used all of this for years as *support* for Candidate B's
corridor — and never once asked the direct question: **did a Gurney family live in those Essex
parishes while the colonial John was in England?**

The word "Ongar" — the hundred containing Stanford Rivers, Epping and North Weald Bassett — appears
nowhere in `research/` before this unit.

## The answer: no

Swept across FindMyPast's *Essex Baptisms* county set (492 Gurn\* rows, 13 Gourn\* rows):[^essexsweep]

- **Finchingfield**, Daniel Shed's own parish: **one** Gurn\*-variant baptism in the entire register run.
- **Ongar** (Chipping Ongar, High Ongar): **two**, neither in window.
- **Stanford Rivers**: none.
- **Maldon**: a real and substantial Gurney presence — but it **begins in 1655**, and it is Francis
  G14's own descendants (below).

**The two parishes the colonial evidence names directly are empty** — Daniel Shed's Finchingfield and
William Tyng's Stanford Rivers. **The country between and around them is not.** Several
Gurney-plausible households sit in the Ongar–Epping triangle in the emigrant's own window:[^essexsweep]

| Record | Parish | Father | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Richard Gurnat, 1616** | **Epping / Epping Upland** | **John** | A father John, about four miles from Stanford Rivers |
| John Gurne, 1622 | Epping | Robert, mother Sara | |
| Marie Gurney, 1625 | Epping | Robert, mother Sara | |
| Susan Gurnard, 1627 | **North Weald Bassett** | Robert, mother Ann | Adjoins Epping |
| Marie Gurney, 1637 | North Weald Bassett | Robert, mother Anne | |
| Johannes Gurnard, 1625 | **Fyfield** | Gulielmi (William) | Ongar hundred |
| Thomas Gurnard, 1620 | Shenfield | Thomas, mother Alice | |
| **Elizabeth Gorne, 1623** | **Harwich** | **John** | North-east Essex, outside the Ongar cluster |

**This does not close Essex. It opens it.** A Gurney-plausible cluster sits beside the Tyng estate, and
one of its records names a **father John** in 1616. Whether these people wrote themselves Gurney,
Gurnett or Gurnard is exactly the question, and index rows cannot settle it — the registers can.

What survives from the original claim is narrower and still useful: **the two most specific colonial
pointers, Shed's parish and Tyng's parish, hold no Gurney neighbour.** The wider Essex thread stays
live and now has a named target for image work.

**Maldon is separate**: a real and substantial Gurney presence, but it begins in **1655** and is
Francis G14's own descendants (below). The only unambiguous Gurney-surname baptism in Essex before
1650 is Anthony Gourney at Aveley in 1615, to a father George.

## What Essex does confirm: the Maldon branch

The *Gourn\** spelling in Essex is concentrated almost entirely at **Maldon, St Mary the Virgin**, in
one household — **Francis Gourney and Ann/Anne**, with children baptised **John 1655, Jane 1656,
Francis 1657, George 1659, Thomas 1660, Charles 1662, Richard 1664, Francis 1665, Anna 1666, Henry
1667**, plus a George Gourney household at the same town from 1661.[^maldon]

This is the register-level footprint of the family the printed authorities describe from other
sources: Daniel Gurney's "son John of Maldon" and "probable son George", and Bernau's *Unrecorded
Biographies: Francis Gournay (or Gurney), of Maldon, Essex*. The case file already carries **John of
Maldon, bachelor, nine hearths in 1674, letters of administration to his brother Thomas Gurney in
1681** — and here are a John baptised 1655 and a Thomas baptised 1660 to the same parents. **The
parish register independently corroborates the Maldon branch's composition**, which had rested on
compiled authorities.

It also fixes its date: this household starts baptising in 1655, a generation after the emigration
window, so it bears on Francis G14's descendants and not on the colonial John.

## Variant spellings — a weighting problem, not a reclassification

> **RETRACTED, 2026-07-29.** An earlier version of this section asserted that several forms — Garne,
> Gurnett, Gurnard, Gurnel, Gurner — "are other surnames", and marked sixteen catalogue rows
> reclassified on that basis. **That was conjecture presented as a finding, and on the strongest
> instance it is demonstrably wrong.** The argument below replaces it.

**What the Epping evidence actually supports.** The parish yields five pre-1660 index rows spread
across what may be three or more generations — **Gurnat 1616, Gurne 1622, Gurney 1625, Gurnet 1649,
Gurnet 1654** — plus a later run settling on Gurnett to 1726.[^epping] From that I concluded a single
continuous non-Gurney family. Three assumptions were doing the work and none was tested: that all the
rows belong to **one** family; that a spelling a century later governs how to read a 1625 entry; and
that the index spellings reflect the register rather than the transcriber. **No image has been read.**
Two different families of similar name can share a parish — this project has just finished proving
exactly that at Great Berkhamsted, where four Gurney households coexisted, and at Stewkley, where
Walter's and Robert's households were being read as one man reusing a name.

**And the categorical claim about Gurnard is refuted by the repo's own corpus.** The Heralds'
Visitation of London of 1634 records letters patent exemplified by Garter King of Arms on 26 July 1633
to **"Richard Gurny, *alias* Gurnard", Sheriff of London**, and on the same pedigree **"Burton Gurny,
*alias* Gurnard, descended from the Gurneys of Kendall."**[^alias] That is a heraldically registered
family using Gurny and Gurnard **interchangeably, in London, in the emigrant's own decade**. It is not
a transcription slip; it is a formal alias.

The reason is not obscure either. The Norfolk line's own crest, from before 1465, is **a gurnard fish
in pale — a canting pun on the surname**, borne by Thomas Gournay I and carried on afterwards.[^crest]
Families that pun their own name in heraldry are exactly the families whose name gets written as the
pun. **Gurnard is a Gurney form with documented standing, not a look-alike.**

### The correct treatment: a plausibility weight per record

Variants sit on a sliding scale and the weight belongs on the **individual record**, not on the
surname as a class:

| Form | Weight | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Gurney, Gurny, Gurnie, Gourney, Gourny, Gurnay | **High** | The family's own habitual orthography |
| **Gurne**, Gurn, Gourne, Gurnoe | **High** | A dropped terminal letter; the commonest clerk's contraction |
| **Gurnard**, Gurnat | **Moderate–high** | Documented alias in a registered family, and the canting crest explains it |
| Gorne, Garne, Gernne, Gowrne, Girney | **Moderate** | Ordinary vowel drift in secretary-hand transcription |
| **Gurnett**, Gurnel, Gurnell, Gurner | **Lower** | Recognised surnames in their own right, but a Gurney entry can still be written this way |
| Hurney and other H-initial | **Discovery-only** | Warranted solely by the proven H/G indexer confusion |

**Lower weight is not exclusion.** A single record can still be the one that matters: the object here
is not a count of Gurney baptisms but whether an individual record matches a corner of the colonial
shape — a Mary of about 1628, a Richard of about 1630, a John of about 1633, to a father John and a
mother Mary. A low-weight surname form with a high-weight shape match is worth more than a
high-weight surname form with none.

**What would actually settle a variant question** is the register image and the hand, not the index
row and not a later century's spelling. Where a record carries real weight on shape, the image is the
next step; where it does not, the weight can stay soft indefinitely.

### Consequence for the catalogue

The sixteen rows previously marked "reclassified" are restated as **weighted**, and none is removed.
Every one of them had already failed the shape test on the father's forename, so nothing in the
candidate assessment turns on this — which is precisely why the over-claim was cheap to make and
worth catching anyway.

## Consequences

1. **Essex is now open with a named target, not closed.** Shed's Finchingfield and Tyng's Stanford
   Rivers hold nothing; the Epping–North Weald–Fyfield cluster four miles away holds several
   Gurney-plausible households, one with a **father John in 1616**. The next step is register images
   for Epping and North Weald Bassett, not more index searching.
2. **The affected catalogue rows are weighted, not reclassified.** All sixteen stay; the surname-class
   exclusion is withdrawn.
3. **Variant weight belongs on the record, not the surname** — and the weight interacts with shape:
   a low-weight spelling on a record that matches the colonial shape outranks a high-weight spelling
   on one that does not.
4. **The Maldon branch gains register-level corroboration** and should be crosslinked from the G14
   material, where it currently rests on Daniel Gurney and Bernau.
5. **Gurnard is specifically rehabilitated** as a Gurney form, on the 1634 Visitation alias and the
   canting crest. Any prior treatment of Gurnard rows as a different surname should be revisited.

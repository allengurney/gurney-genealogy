# The funnel closed on births and marriages — and the "unassembled" row was doing two jobs

## Why this unit exists

*Documented but never assembled into a person* had climbed to **30%**, the leading row. That number
implies something specific and testable: **the man is sitting in the records already searched, and more
sorting would find him.** After the volume of work this file has absorbed, that claim deserved to be
tested rather than restated — and testing it turns out to be possible, because the record classes that
would carry his household are now enumerable.

Two axes were still open, and both are the kind that **stand alone** as evidence of a household rather
than depending on a presumptive identification: **baptisms by mother** and **marriages**. Both are now
run. What they show closes the funnel much tighter than the file had it, and it shows the 30% was
conflating two different propositions.

## The marriage arm, finished — a fifth instrument, and a sharper negative

FreeREG's marriage holdings had never been used. Its record URLs carry **both parties' names in the
slug**, so 178 captured marriage rows yield the bride or groom without opening a single record.
Deduplicated, **97 Gurney-cluster marriages across England 1615–1645**.[^freemarr]

**Not one John Gurney marries a Mary.** The only John × Mary in the whole run is a **John *Garne* ×
Mary White at Brigstock in 1659** — a Garne, and thirty years too late.

**And the negative is sharper than "none found", because Marys were plainly available.** In the same
holdings, Gurney men married nine Marys: Robert × Mary Lame (Norwich 1622), Edward × Mary Bedward
(Stepney 1641), Walter × Mary Hammond (Westminster 1642), Henry × Mary Bodiman (Harefield 1647),
Edward × Mary Hancke (Lowestoft 1656), James × Mary Randall (Rickmansworth 1656), Andrew × Mary Minns
(Norwich 1657), George × Mary Pupplet (Bishopsgate 1658), Thomas × Mary Cockrell (Norwich 1660).
**It is specifically the Johns who have no Mary.**

Three new facts fall out. **Longdon, Worcestershire had two John marriages** — Fraunces Price 1602 and
Dorothy Tuffley 1625 — and a third wife Isabell by 1639, so that household is a father and son, not one
man. **The Earsham marriage is dated: John Girney × Elizabeth Singler, 9 October 1632**, which pins the
household the case file eliminates on the 1639 will, and confirms the wife is not Mary from a second
direction. And **a John Gurney marriage at Greenwich, Kent, 27 October 1642** is new to the file, though
after the colonial John was already in Massachusetts.

**This is now the fifth independent instrument** on the marriage question, after Ancestry's 9852, the
Hertfordshire county set, the Buckinghamshire county set from both sides, and FindMyPast's national
sets. All five agree.

## The mother axis, swept for the first time

The file has swept by child forename and by father. **It had never asked the register who the mother
was** — which is odd, because a wife named Mary is one of the firmest things known about the colonial
household.

Run on the parent instrument, England-wide, 1620–1640: **every Gurney-variant baptism with a mother
named Mary.**[^motheraxis]

> Eythorne, Kent (John 1638, Thomas 1635, Edward 1641) · Upton on Severn (Elizabeth 1629) ·
> Norwich (Robert 1636, Katherine 1639) · Holborn, London (Mathew 1628, Mary 1634) ·
> Stepney, London (Edmond 1640, Robert 1641)

Then the decisive form — **father John *and* mother Mary together**:

> **Across Great Britain, 1620–1640, exactly one Gurney household is recorded with a father John and a
> mother Mary: Eythorne, Kent** — John Gurney and Mary Marsh, eliminated on his 1648 burial and her
> burial six days after childbirth in November 1641.

The Holborn household did not survive the father filter, so **its father is not John**; the Norwich one
is Robert Gurney × Mary Lame of the 1622 marriage; the Stepney one is most plausibly Edward Gurney ×
Mary Bedward, married there in June 1641.

**The funnel now has exactly two survivors in the whole searched corpus, and both are already named
rows.** There is no third thing.

## The best partial match found, and why it still fails

Worth recording because it scores higher on the method's rubric than anything except Berkhamsted, and
because dismissing it unexamined is the failure mode this file keeps catching.

> **St Clement Danes, Westminster — John GURLEY and Mary:** Eliz 1627, Ellynor 1628, Alexander 1629,
> **Mary 1631**, **Sara 1633**. Father John and mother Mary on all five entries, confirmed on the
> Westminster county set with parent columns.[^gurley]

**What it matches:** father John, mother Mary, a daughter **Mary baptised 1631** — which satisfies the
hard bound from Mary Gurney's 1647 Braintree marriage exactly — a daughter **Sara** in 1633, a London
corridor location, and a household that stops appearing in 1633.

**Why it fails anyway, and independently of the surname:** the colonial John had a **Richard** and a
**John Jr**. This household has neither, and it has **three children with no colonial counterpart** —
Elizabeth, Ellynor and Alexander, the last a distinctly un-colonial name. The child set is wrong even
if the reading of the surname were generous.

**And the surname is the weaker part.** *Gurley* is an established English surname and five consecutive
entries across seven years all read Gurley, which suggests a clerk who knew the name — unlike the
Gurnat/Gurne/Gurney drift at Epping. Under the weighting adopted in unit 69, Gurley sits low: it is not
in the Gurn- family at all. Recorded as a **weighted partial match and a low-priority image question**,
not a candidate.

## The reallocation: the 30% was two propositions wearing one label

Here is the error the row had accumulated. *Documented but never assembled* was raised each time a
merge defect was found — Candidate A into two men, Stewkley into two households, Berkhamsted into a
father and a son and then into a parish of four households, Denton's father proving to be Josias,
Toddington's proving to be Thomas, Linslade's Ezechiell, Kelham's William, Bishop's Stortford's Henry.

**But every one of those defects was found *and resolved*.** Each resolution **drains** that row rather
than filling it. The row was being fed by the very process that was emptying it, which is why it grew
while the residue shrank.

**And the residue is now enumerable.** The complete father-axis sweep yields 31 localities for
1615–1649; adding the two the FindMyPast pool misses gives 33. Classified, the genuinely unattached
single events in the colonial window with a colonial-name child number **one — Hitcham — and it is a
named row, not a residue.** Of the six "scattered single events never linked" the case file names —
Ivinghoe 1640, Marsworth 1642, Aylesbury 1644/1669, Weston Turville 1627, Haddenham 1620/1622, Great
Kimble 1619 — **four are now assembled**: Ivinghoe is John Gurney × Rebecka Cooker of 22 October 1640;
Great Kimble is the Owlswick father-and-son marriage settlement of 20 October 1619; Weston Turville and
Haddenham carry named rows. The two that remain, Aylesbury 1644 and 1669, fall outside the window
entirely.

**So "he is in what we have, mis-sorted" is now a small claim.** What remains large is a *different*
claim that had been sheltering inside the same row: **"he is in records that survive but have not been
searched."** That one is not speculative — this file has documented it repeatedly:

- The FindMyPast parent pool **misses whole county sets** — proven, because it returns neither the
  Lamport, Northamptonshire household (seven children 1630–1640) nor the Earsham household, both of
  which FreeREG holds.
- **There is no FindMyPast Suffolk baptism set at all**, and Suffolk is inside the corridor.
- **Hitcham has no seventeenth-century burial coverage**; Cheddington's is unproven.
- The **south-Midlands archdeaconry marriage-licence series has never been searched** — three
  archdeaconries in two dioceses.
- **East Dereham's 1611–15 returns are lost**, and twelve more between 1594 and 1608.
- Hertfordshire registers are **defective 1643–1660** by the provider's own statement.

**These are two different research problems with two different remedies** — re-sorting what we hold
versus going to sources we have not opened — and the file's own §11 rationale says separating rows by
remedy is the point of the table. They should not share a row.

### Revised table

| Row | Was | Now | Why |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Documented but never assembled** | 30 | **17** | The residue is now enumerable and small: one unaccounted shape-match in the whole searched corpus, and it is already a named row. Four of the six named "scattered events" are assembled |
| **NEW — Documented, but in a source or class not yet searched** | — | **9** | The coverage residue, itemised above and each item evidenced. Distinct from survival and from assembly |
| **No record survives or is indexed anywhere** | 18 | **19** | Marginally up: three axes and five marriage instruments now return nothing |
| **Ackworth** | 7 | **9** | The **only** unaccounted-for John × Mary marriage in England before 1660, and the marriage is the hardest requirement in the signature |
| **Hitcham** | 4 | **5** | The **only** unaccounted-for colonial-name child to a father John in Britain, on two axes independently |

All other rows unchanged. **Sums to 100, and the assembled row is now below 20.**

**Why Ackworth and Hitcham gain, stated plainly.** Not because new supporting evidence appeared for
either, but because the funnel closed around them: when every other household in the searched corpus
resolves, the two that do not carry more of the weight. That is a legitimate inference and it is also a
warning — both remain single-record candidates with real blocks, and neither should be read as rising
toward identification.

**Why Candidate B does not gain.** B would benefit from an emptied corpus, because its auxiliary
predicts a man who leaves no English paper. But the file's own rule — an auxiliary that explains away
absence must not draw confidence from further absence — forbids it. B holds at 11%.

## What is actually left to do

The index layer on births and marriages is now close to exhausted. What remains splits cleanly:

1. **Sources not yet opened** — the archdeaconry licence series (R-33), the Suffolk gap, the
   Norwich 1606–11 licence bonds for Francis (R-06), the Buckinghamshire wills (R-01).
2. **Images to read** — Stewkley 1615 (R-63), Epping and North Weald (R-58), Ackworth 1637 (R-64).
3. **Y-DNA** (R-14), which bypasses records entirely and is the only lever on the no-record row.

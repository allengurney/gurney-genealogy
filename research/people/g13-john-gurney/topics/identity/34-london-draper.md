# Identity — Candidate D: London Draper, Old Change

Candidate D is John Gurney, adult son and executor of Robert Gurney, citizen and draper of Old Change in the City of London, admitted to the Drapers' Company by redemption on 11 February 1623/4.[^will][^rollco-1624] He is eliminated as the Braintree emigrant on a single clean chronological ground: the same John Gurney is documented still living in London, in the same St Augustine Watling Street parish, in 1662 — the very year the colonial John of Braintree was dying at Boston.[^hearth-tax]

## Robert of Old Change and the 1623/4 freedom

Robert Gurney died in late September 1625, leaving a will written 18 January 1621/2 and proved at the Archdeaconry Court of London on 23 September 1625 by the oath of his son and executor, John Gurney. Robert styled himself "Citizen and Draper of London" and gave his dwelling in Old Change; he left his widow Anne the two uppermost rooms of the house for her widowhood and gave John the shop, cellar, and hall beneath, plus the residue.[^will] Robert had been a Drapers' freeman since 16 December 1581, already styled "Tailor, Old Change" at his own admission, and remained an active Drapers' master into the 1620s, training apprentices drawn from Buckinghamshire and Yorkshire and standing witness at other men's freedoms.[^rollco-robert] John's own admission to the Drapers' came not by the patrimony his father's long freedom would ordinarily have given him, but by redemption, on 11 February 1623/4, in the same event record that names Robert as his father.[^rollco-1624] On 3 November 1630 John himself bound an apprentice, Henry Smith of Kilton, Suffolk, for seven years — an active master's role that almost certainly ran out of the Old Change shop he had inherited; Smith never surfaces as a freed Drapers' man, and no Drapers' turnover for any Gurney is recorded 1620–1670, so the trail runs cold without settling whether John himself stayed in the trade.[^rollco-1630]

Robert's household had an earlier chapter that the Old Change story usually skips. A first John Gurny was baptized at St Augustine in 1595/6 and buried there in 1600; a daughter Marye was baptized 1597/8 and buried 1601; and a stillborn son was buried in 1601 — all a decade or more before Robert's own 4 April 1611 marriage to Anne Morris of St Michael in the Querne, by licence, at St Magnus the Martyr.[^st-augustine-magnus] Anne cannot, on this chronology, be assumed the mother of the John Gurney who later inherited Old Change; whichever wife bore him, she is not identified in the surviving record.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000084 -->

## Continuing in London to 1662 — the elimination

Two independent London tax returns, twenty-four years apart, place a John Gurney at the same St Augustine Watling Street address. T. C. Dale's 1931 transcription of the 1638 London rents return assesses John Gurney at £10 in MS. 67a, in the same manuscript section as Joseph Huntscott at £12 — the same Joseph Hunscott named an overseer of Robert's 1625 will, still resident in the parish thirteen years after Robert's death.[^bho-1638] The Merry hearth-tax database then places a John Gurney "in St Austins precinct," Farringdon Within ward, at one hearth, assessed "poore," in 1662 (TNA E 179/252/27, rot. 21).[^hearth-tax] A Boyd's Inhabitants of London card for "John Gurny of S Augustine" independently carries a partly-legible note reading "1661 poll tax [unclear] Old Change   1638 rent £10," corroborating the same continuous St Augustine identity across both returns.[^boyds]

The 1662 hearth-tax entry falls in the same calendar year the colonial John of Braintree was dying at Boston, about March 1662/3.[^cf-death] A draper's household assessed too poor to pay hearth tax in London that year cannot also belong to the man dying in Massachusetts the same year. Candidate D and the colonial emigrant are not the same person; Candidate D is eliminated.[^hearth-tax]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000085 -->

## No wife Mary, no matching children

No marriage of this John Gurney to a wife named Mary, and no baptisms of Sarah, Mary, Richard, John, or Peter to a John Gurney and Mary household, have turned up in any London parish register, Drapers'/Stationers' record, or FamilySearch index for the 1620–1641 window. The nearest indexed John Gurney + Mary marriage in the window, at Eythorne, Kent, on 6 November 1632, belongs to a Kent couple who stayed in Kent.[^no-mary] Nor does a will or administration survive for an Anne Gurney widow matching Robert's own widow in the Prerogative Court of Canterbury's probate records for 1625–1670 — though that search covers only the prerogative court, not London's own consistory, archdeaconry, or commissary courts, so the absence does not by itself prove Anne left no estate to probate.[^anne-negative] Set beside the hearth-tax chronology, the missing wife and children give the elimination a second, independent ground.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000086 -->

## Why redemption, not patrimony

John's choice of the redemption route into the Drapers' Company, when his father's three-decade freedom should have entitled him to admission by patrimony, is an anomaly worth explaining rather than a stray procedural note. The best fit is that John served an apprenticeship in a different livery company first: a Stationers' Company record binds a John Gurney apprentice to the stationer James Boler on 25 March 1613, with no later Stationers' freedom recorded under Boler.[^stationers] If this is the same man, an aborted or completed Stationers' term would make the 1623/4 Drapers' redemption the ordinary consequence rather than a puzzle, and the reading sits comfortably beside Robert's own choice of the stationer Joseph Henscott as a 1625 will overseer — the same Hunscott/Henscott household still resident in St Augustine parish in 1638.[^stationers] No father's name survives on the 1613 binding to confirm the identification outright, so it stays a plausible explanation, not a proven one.

Two further same-surname Londoners surface in the same record sweeps and are kept aside as comparators only, not as Candidate D material. An earlier Richard Gurney/Gourney, haberdasher, was importing Baltic flax through the Port of London in 1567–68 and rose to alderman, sheriff (1589–90), and twice Master of the Haberdashers' Company before his death in 1597; no record ties him to Robert's household.[^richard-comparator] And a gentry John Gourney of Hanging Houghton, Northamptonshire, whose sons entered the Drapers' and Clothworkers' companies in the 1640s and 1650s, belongs to a distinct county and social class from the Old Change tradesman.[^hanging-houghton]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000087 -->

## Standing

Candidate D is eliminated. The 1638 and 1662 London tax returns place the same John Gurney continuously in the same St Augustine Watling Street household across at least twenty-four years, still on record there after the colonial John had already died in Massachusetts; the missing wife Mary and missing children give the same conclusion a second, independent ground. Candidate D is one of the three named principal alternatives the shared matching-criteria profile weighs against every same-name Englishman, and is eliminated on the same structural ground — continuing English residence across the emigrant's own colonial years — as Candidates A and C.[^cf-s84-xref]

Three further London-record pulls remain open but are no longer load-bearing for the elimination itself, which now rests on the 1662 hearth tax: the 1660–61 Free and Voluntary Present return that may underlie the Boyd's card's "1661 poll tax" note, the 1640 Harvey list of Principal Inhabitants (a possible midpoint between the 1638 and 1662 returns), and the underlying Arber Stationers' Registers transcript that might name a father for the 1613 Boler apprentice and settle the redemption puzzle outright.[^deferred]

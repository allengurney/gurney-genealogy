# The arrival point, not the settlement — Weymouth's reception streams re-weighed

The case file explains why a *hypothesised* man outranks *documented* ones:

> Ranking a hypothesised man above documented ones needs a reason, and the reason is that B carries
> an evidenced link to emigration that the others entirely lack: the corridor, the 1634 liquidation
> as motive, the Puritan network, the Essex colonial associations.[^casefile]

That sentence is the load-bearing justification for the whole probability table. **It is no longer
accurate, and the evidence that unseats it has been in the repo since June 2026.**

## What the colonial record actually says about where John landed

John Gurney's first American record is a June 1641 fine at **Weymouth**, and his earliest colonial
property is a Weymouth East Field and Mill Field allocation. The association study profiled his land
neighbours and found early Weymouth was fed by two documented English streams — and neither of them
is Norfolk:[^assoc]

| Neighbour | English origin | Stream |
|---|---|---|
| **Robert Randall**, holds John's East Field two acres | **Wendover, Buckinghamshire** | Aylesbury Vale |
| **William Brandon**, bounds John's Mill Field on the south | **Ashton Clinton, Buckinghamshire** | Aylesbury Vale |
| Edmund Hart, bounds East Field east | — | *Mary and John* 1630 → Dorchester |
| Thomas Richards, holds John's Mill Field four acres | — | *Mary and John* 1630 → Dorchester |
| Nicholas Norton | — | Hull's company, Somerset/Dorset |

Weymouth was "firmly West-Country-led — Rev. Joseph Hull's 1635 company out of Somerset and Dorset —
with the Aylesbury-Vale Buckinghamshire stream second; **no Norfolk stream fed it**."[^assoc]

**Wendover is five miles from Aylesbury. Ashton Clinton is three, and adjoins Weston Turville.**
Those are not merely the same county as the file's unaccounted-for Buckinghamshire Johns; they are
the same few parishes. Ashton Clinton lies two miles from Stoke Mandeville, where a John Gourney was
litigating over property in 1621–25, and about seven from Cheddington.

## Why the weighing has never been revisited

The association study saw this and weighed it down, explicitly and reasonably — **against a Candidate
B that no longer exists**:

> That coincidence is real and worth a targeted test; it does **not** overturn the favoured Candidate
> B (East Dereham, Norfolk, ~65%), which rests on the East Dereham baptism, occupation, and finances
> … the direct evidence (**the East Dereham baptism**) remains the stronger basis for Candidate
> B.[^assoc]

The East Dereham baptism does not exist. The line adjudicated in March 2026 reads *"Amorye the
daughter of Robart Enderby"*, and the record the index described is six years later and belongs to a
Horne family.[^audit] **One side of that comparison was removed in July 2026 and the comparison was
never re-run.** Candidate B fell from about sixty-five percent to about eighteen; the Buckinghamshire
reception observation, which was subordinated to the baptism, has stayed where it was put.

## Restating the asymmetry

The case file's claim is that B has an evidenced emigration link the rivals "entirely lack." Set the
two classes side by side and that is not what the record shows:

| | **Candidate B (Norfolk)** | **The Buckinghamshire class** |
|---|---|---|
| **English-side emigration link** | Strong in kind: the East Anglia → London corridor, a Puritan uncle, a parish adjoining Coleman Street, a documented 1634 liquidation as motive | None. No Buckinghamshire Gurney is shown leaving |
| **Colonial-side reception link** | Hingham, Massachusetts, ten miles off, settled by 206 Norfolk emigrants out of the Gurney parishes — but **no Gurney among them**, and John did not settle there | Weymouth itself: **two of John's own land-neighbours** came from Aylesbury-Vale parishes three to five miles from the Gurney households |
| **Where he actually first appears** | — | **Weymouth**, in a town with a documented Aylesbury-Vale stream and no Norfolk stream |

Neither class has both halves. **B has an English-side corridor with no Gurney at the colonial end;
the Buckinghamshire class has a colonial-side channel with no Gurney at the English end.** That is a
symmetry, and the case file currently presents it as a one-sided advantage. The correction does not
promote any individual Buckinghamshire man — none of them is shown emigrating, and the standing
objection that they are not fathering children anywhere visible in 1628–1635 is untouched. It
corrects the *stated rationale* for the ranking, which is a different and more fundamental thing than
a number.

The generic-link caution the case file already applies to B applies here too, and harder: tens of
thousands of English emigrants shared a corridor, land abuttal is not shipmate-hood, and John's
Braintree life points to Essex through the Tyng estate of Stanford Rivers and his son-in-law Daniel
Shed of Finchingfield. The association study's own summary is the right posture and should be
carried forward: John "attached to whichever local network held land or kin, so association is a
*weak* lever" for every region it touches.[^assoc] The point is not that the Buckinghamshire link is
strong. It is that it is **the same kind of weak link that B is currently being credited with, and
it sits at the point of arrival rather than three thousand miles away.**

## The test that was run in the wrong direction

The association study closed the Buckinghamshire thread with a negative:

> the Bucks Gurney households cluster in the Aylesbury–Stewkley–Chesham–Edlesborough arc, with **no
> Gurney recorded in Wendover, Lee, or Ashton Clinton** — the very parishes that supplied the
> Humphrey–Hunt–Randall–Brandon neighbours — so there is no Gurney kin behind that cluster.[^assoc]

That negative asks whether the **neighbours' parishes** held Gurneys. It is sound, and it does close
the "the neighbours were his kin" reading.

But it is the wrong direction for the question that matters. The live question is not whether a
Gurney lived in Wendover; it is **whether the Weymouth cohort drew anyone from the parishes where
the Gurneys did live** — Aylesbury, Bierton with Broughton, Stewkley, Weston Turville, Stoke
Mandeville, Cheddington, Marsworth, Ivinghoe, Edlesborough. A migration channel does not require the
emigrant's own village to appear; it requires the channel to reach his neighbourhood, and Ashton
Clinton to Weston Turville is a walk of two miles.

**That check has not been run**, and it is the natural next step: profile the whole Aylesbury-Vale
Weymouth cohort by home parish, not just the five men who happen to abut John's strips. If any
Weymouth settler came out of a Gurney parish, the channel stops being a coincidence of counties and
becomes a plausible route — and the Buckinghamshire rows, which have always been held down by having
no emigration story at all, acquire one.

## What this changes, stated conservatively

- **Case-file §11's stated rationale needs rewriting**, whatever happens to the numbers. "B carries
  an evidenced link to emigration that the others entirely lack" is not supportable while John's own
  Weymouth neighbours are Aylesbury-Vale men.
- **The Buckinghamshire group rows are under-weighted relative to their own evidence**, not because
  any individual improved, but because the class has a colonial-side link the table does not credit.
- **Candidate B's Hingham reception model should be labelled for what it is** — a proximity argument.
  Hingham is genuinely next door and genuinely Norfolk, and no Gurney is in its cohort; John did not
  settle there, and when the Weymouth congregation dispersed to Rehoboth he went the other way, to
  Braintree.[^assoc]
- **Nothing here is a positive finding about any man.** It is a correction to how two classes of
  hypothesis are being compared.

# Candidate C is two men, and the younger one was never assessed

> **SUPERSEDED IN PART, 2026-07-28 — read this box first.**
>
> **The burial negative in this unit is false.** Great Berkhamsted's burial register survives, is
> transcribed, and holds **sixteen Gurney-variant burials**, including this household's own Elizabeth
> (21 February 1638/9) and Francis (9 March 1638/9), and a **Richard Gurney buried 28 June 1638**. The
> zero was produced by two compounding query defects: `lastname=gurn*` does not reach the *Gourney*
> spelling the parish uses, and the burial set's place token is `Berkhamstead`, which neither
> `berkhampstead` nor `berkhamsted` matches.
>
> **The departure-shaped gap therefore does not exist**, and the argument built on it below is
> withdrawn. C(ii) falls from 9% to 4%. The parish also held **four** contemporaneous Gurney
> households — fathers John, Thomas, Edward and William — so the two-man reading below is a reading of
> a filtered query, not of the parish.
>
> What survives: C(ii)'s age fit, the 1620 Jhon Gourney burial as real evidence for the father/son
> split, and an unburied head sitting inside a register window the set itself describes as defective
> for 1643–1660.
>
> Full working: [`66-refactor-berkhamsted-burials-and-the-departure-gap.md`](66-refactor-berkhamsted-burials-and-the-departure-gap.md).

The case file eliminates Candidate C in a sentence: *"A Berkhamsted John Gurney is roughly 13 to 18
years older than the colonial John … based on the Berkhamsted 1610 baptism of Henry placing the
Berkhamsted John Gurney's birth no later than about 1585–1590."*[^casefile] The elimination is
described as immune to the same-name masking problem, and the row sits at one percent.

**It is not one man.** Searched by parent rather than by child, the Great Berkhampstead baptisms to a
father John Gurney run:[^baptisms]

```
1597  Hugh          1615  Sara            1631  Michaell
1599  Anne          1624  Jhon            1634  Sarah  / Sara
1602  Amye          1626  Richard         1636  Francys
1606  Bridgett      1629  Elizabeth       1637  Francis
1610  Henrye
```

**Forty years of fathering, 1597 to 1637.** No man does that. Under any reading this is a father and a
son, and the natural break is the nine-year gap between **Sara 1615 and Jhon 1624**.

## The two households

**C(i), the elder John** — Hugh 1597, Anne 1599, Amye 1602, Bridgett 1606, Henrye 1610, Sara 1615.
Fathering from 1597 puts his birth about 1570–75. He is comfortably eliminated on age, and the case
file's argument applies to him exactly.

**C(ii), the younger John** — Jhon 1624, Richard 1626, Elizabeth 1629, Michaell 1631, Sarah 1634,
Francis 1636/7. **Fathering from 1624 puts his birth about 1598–1602.** He would be **about 51 in
1653** — and the colonial John deposed "aged 50 years or thereabouts" in that year.

**The case file eliminated the father and never assessed the son.** That is the third time this file
has merged two same-name men into one household: Candidate A split into A(i) and A(ii) in July 2026
on identical logic, and Stewkley's "register silence" proved to be a search artefact the same week.
The pattern is now the most reliable finding in the project.

## What C(ii) has

**Three of the colonial forenames, in one household.** John 1624, **Richard** 1626, **Sarah** 1634 —
against a colonial set of Sarah, Mary, Richard, John and Peter. No other English household reaches
three.

**The right age, from the household's own evidence.** Born c.1598–1602 on his first child; about 51
at the deposition. The file has spent a year hunting a man of that age and had one candidate — the
1603 Stewkley baptism, who has no household at all.

**A documented family catastrophe immediately before the crossing window.** Two of his children died
within three weeks of each other in the winter of 1638/9: **Elizabeth, baptised 2 August 1629, died
21 February 1639**, and **Francis, baptised 1 March 1637, died 9 March 1639**.[^deaths]

**And then the household stops baptising.** No further Gurney baptism at Berkhamsted after 1637.

~~And — the part that matters — no Gurney burial at Great Berkhampstead at any date, in FindMyPast's
entire parish-burial corpus. Twelve children baptised in the parish across forty years and not one of
them buried there, nor either father.~~ **WITHDRAWN 2026-07-28.** The parish holds sixteen
Gurney-variant burials, five of them between November 1637 and March 1638/9; the zero was a query
artefact.[^burials] What actually ends the baptism run is mortality: Joane (7 November 1637), Richard
(28 June 1638), William (1 October 1638), Elizabeth (21 February 1638/9) and Francis (9 March 1638/9)
are all buried in the parish, and two of those five are documented children of this household.

~~That is a departure-shaped gap, and it is the first one this project has found.~~ **Also withdrawn.**
The deviation audit's untried test — *"which John Gurney household's records stop between 1635 and
1641 without a burial or probate?"*[^audit] — returns a negative answer for Berkhamsted. The records
do not stop. What remains true, and much weaker, is that **the head of the household himself** has no
burial after 1620 — inside a window the record set describes as widely defective for Hertfordshire.

**The naming convention fits, forwards.** The unit that predicted a paternal grandfather named Richard
also implies that an eldest son takes the paternal grandfather's name. C(ii)'s eldest recorded son is
**Jhon 1624**, and C(i) — his own father — was a **John**. The convention is being followed in this
household.

## What C(ii) lacks, stated at full strength

**Mary is missing, and she is the best-documented colonial child.** Mary Gurney married Daniel Shed at
Braintree in 1647, so she was born by about 1631 at the latest. No Mary is baptised at Great
Berkhampstead in the surviving run. Two readings, and neither is comfortable: she was born before the
run begins — a gap between a c.1623 marriage and Jhon 1624 is tight but not impossible for an eldest
daughter — or her baptism is lost. This is the single strongest objection and it should not be
smoothed over.

**Michaell 1631 has no colonial counterpart**, and neither does Elizabeth. Both may have died — 
Elizabeth demonstrably did — but Michaell is unaccounted for.

**The dates run systematically early.** Berkhamsted John 1624 against a colonial John Jr of about
1633; Berkhamsted Richard 1626 against a colonial Richard of about 1630. The colonial dates are
compiled estimates from Sprague and the Weymouth history rather than records, which softens this, but
it does not dissolve it.

**The family used the name Francis.** He died in 1639, so the colonial "no son named Francis" argument
is not contradicted — but a household that named a son Francis in 1637 and emigrated in 1639–41 is a
different object from one that never used the name.

**The burial negative may be a coverage artefact.** FindMyPast's Hertfordshire burial coverage is not
established here, and a parish with forty years of baptisms and no burials at all is as consistent
with a missing burial register as with a family that left. **This must be checked before the
departure-shaped-gap argument is leaned on**, and it is the first action arising.

> *This caveat was the right one, and it was decisive. Checked 2026-07-28: the coverage is fine and
> the burials are there. The argument above does not survive it.*

## Where this leaves the row

*Revised 2026-07-28.* C(ii) is a **documented man of the right age** — born c.1598–1602, about 51 at
the 1653 deposition — whose household suffers a documented catastrophe in 1637–39 and stops baptising
because its children were dying. He is not an emigration-shaped household. Against him now stand a
parish-level absence of any Mary Gurney at any date, no marriage anywhere in Hertfordshire 1600–1654,
and a Richard Gurney buried in the parish in June 1638 who may well be his own. For him stands only
the absence of a burial for the head, inside a defective register window.

The row should still be split — C(i) held at his elimination on age — but **C(ii) is a weak candidate,
not a leading one: 9% → 4%.** The claim in the previous version of this section, that he was "at least
the equal of any named candidate except Candidate B", is withdrawn.

## What would settle it

1. **The Great Berkhampstead burial register itself** — is the burial series missing for 1600–1660,
   or is the family genuinely unburied there? This decides whether the departure-shaped gap is real.
2. **A Mary Gurney baptism at Berkhamsted c.1620–1624**, or proof the register has a gap there.
3. **The marriage of the younger John**, c.1622–23, at Berkhamsted or a neighbouring parish — which
   would name his wife and test her against a Mary.
4. **The Archdeaconry of St Albans probate series** (`ASA/AR/8`, 1610–1636) for either John — a will
   for the elder naming his children would establish the split outright.
5. **Resolve the parish attribution.** FindMyPast indexes this family at Great Berkhampstead;
   FamilySearch indexes the same children at **St Peter, Hertfordshire** (St Albans). If those are
   genuinely two parishes, the twelve baptisms may already be two households on the ground, which
   would change the reconstruction again.

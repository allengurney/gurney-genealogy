# Identity — Candidate A and the Buckinghamshire cluster

**Buckinghamshire held five to seven John Gurneys at once through the emigration decades.** That density is what makes the county hard: a man's departure is invisible when his same-named neighbours keep the name in the register continuously. This unit carries the test that survives that problem, the records that now carry it, and which Buckinghamshire men remain unaccounted for.

**Candidate A is two men, and both are eliminated.** The record chain that runs from a Stewkley baptism of 1603 to St Mary Aylesbury baptisms of 1653 looks like one long life. It is not.

## The test

A household is eliminated only by a record that individuates **the man**, not by continuity of the surname in a parish register. Two classes qualify: an individuating record of him after the emigration date — burial, probate, certificate of residence, tax or tenancy record naming him — or a chronological impossibility against the colonial John's own documented life.

**The chronology the test runs against.** The colonial John had a wife Mary by about 1627 and English-born children from about 1628. He was **not among the 1636 Weymouth land grantees**, which is indirect but substantial evidence that he had not arrived by then; pre-1636 arrivals are also a much smaller and far more heavily worked population, and a century of scrutiny has produced no John Gurney among them. His first American record is June 1641. So the crossing falls in **1637–1641**. His wife Mary lived until her death at Braintree on **20 September 1661**.

**Mary's survival to 1661 is the sharpest instrument in the set.** Because the colonial John's first wife was alive until September 1661, **any Englishman who marries a different woman between 1637 and 1661 is not the Braintree John** — which converts a great many ordinary marriage entries into eliminations.[^chronology]

## Candidate A is two men

The Aylesbury-hundred reconstruction ran: Jhon Gurney, son of Jhon Gurney, christened 21 February 1602/3 at Stewkley; married Alice Oliffe at Bierton with Broughton on 24 April 1628; five children baptised at St Mary, Aylesbury, between 1638 and 1653; a 1641 certificate of residence recording a move into Northamptonshire; and a tenancy at Walgrave by 1650.[^fs-aylesbury][^fmp-bierton]

**The certificate splits it.** The National Archives' catalogue description of E 115/180/113 says more than had been recorded: it is a *"certificate of residence showing John Gurney to be liable for taxation in Northamptonshire, and not in the half-hundred of Aylesbury, Buckinghamshire, the previous area,"* dated 1641. That is a government record of a specific man's move **out of** Buckinghamshire in the same year the colonial John first appears in America — precisely the individuating class the register-continuity eliminations lacked.[^e115]

A man taxed in Northamptonshire and no longer in the Aylesbury half-hundred from 1641 is hard to reconcile with St Mary Aylesbury baptisms running to 1653. Aylesbury demonstrably held more than one John Gurney — marriages there in 1620, to Anne Cowheard on 25 October 1638, and again in 1644 — and the parish's seventeenth-century baptisms are indexed with a father and no mother, so the index cannot assign the children to a household. The likelier reading is **two men**:

- **A(i), the certificate man** — Bierton 1628, Alice Oliffe, into Northamptonshire by 1641, tenant at Walgrave by 1650. Eliminated on the certificate.
- **A(ii), the St Mary Aylesbury father** — children 1638–1653. Eliminated on fathering in England through 1653.

The split strengthens the elimination rather than weakening it, because it eliminates two men instead of one. It is also the first of four same-name merges caught in this research, and the pattern is now the most reliable finding in the file: **every household tested for the defect has split.**

A separate later record eliminates any surviving reading of the household. Phillimore's printed Buckinghamshire marriage register for Edlesborough carries **John Gurney to Mary Kidgell, 1661**.[^phillimore-edlesborough] That is materially incompatible with the colonial John, who buried his first wife Mary on 20 September 1661 and married Grizzell Fletcher seven weeks later at Braintree. Either this Edlesborough John is A(i) himself, in which case the marriage eliminates him outright on its own, or he is a distinct Buckinghamshire John with a wife also named Mary — in which case he accounts for one more English "John + Mary" household that is not the emigrant.[^chronology]

The Walgrave tenancy of 1650 carries no located archival reference and no registered source; it is retained as a sourcing gap rather than relied on. Nothing in the elimination depends on it.[^walgrave-gap]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000076 -->

## The Buckinghamshire probate tier

**Forty-two Gurney-variant probate records survive in the Buckinghamshire church courts to 1660**, and three of them bear directly on the identification.[^brs114][^extract]

The tier matters because of where ordinary Buckinghamshire men proved their wills. Most husbandmen and shepherds proved in the **Archdeaconry of Buckingham**, not in the Prerogative Court of Canterbury, and the archdeaconry series is not indexed item by item in the national catalogue. A Prerogative Court search therefore leaves the county substantially unsearched, which is why a printed index of the local courts is the instrument that counts here.

**Nine testators are named John** — 1523, 1552, 1554, 1556, 1589, 1592, 1623, 1630 and 1636. Three of those are new individuating English deaths for men the register work could not resolve: **John Gurney of Monks Risborough, 1623**; **John Gurney of Hulcott, yeoman, 1630**; and **John Gurney of Southcott in Linslade, 1636.** The Linslade probate lands exactly at the start of the emigration window, so Linslade's records stopping in 1636 is explained by a death rather than a departure.

**A Buckinghamshire Gurney tailoring trade runs across four parishes and eighty-five years** — William Gurneye of Aylesbury, tailor (1571); Luke Gurneye of Aylesbury, tailor (1591); Thomas Gurney of Rowsham in Wingrave, tailor (1628); Richard Gurny of Mursley, tailor (1631); Thomas Gurney of Weston Turville, tailor (1633). Four of the five fall inside the emigrant's working life. The colonial John was a tailor, and the trade argument has been framed as distinctive of Candidate B's inherited Merchant Taylor lineage — which the Merchant Taylors' own rolls do not support, returning no John Gurney son of Francis at any binding 1583–1800 and no Gurney patrimony freedom at all. **The trade is not exclusive to Candidate B**, and Buckinghamshire has it documented in the right county in the right decades.

**The Stewkley Gurneys did not vanish in 1614.** The probate index carries Robert Gurnie of Stewkley, husbandman, 1618; Robert Gorny of Stewkley, husbandman, 1631; and Robert Gurney of Stewkley, yeoman, 1651 — the last rising in status. A John of Stewkley proved in 1592, a generation before the household active 1602–1613, so **no Stewkley John of the right generation is accounted for by probate.**

**And the register was never silent either.** The claim that "the Buckinghamshire parish-register collections carry no Stewkley Gurney record of any kind after 1614" was true of one dataset and false of the parish. Searched across every parish-baptism collection at once, Stewkley carries Gurney baptisms at **1615 (two, one of them Anthony), 1616 (Anne), 1618, and 1626 (Richard)** — the named ones all to a father **Robert**, the same household the three probates describe. The distinction generalises: **a statement that "the collections carry no record" is a statement about one transcription until the cross-collection instrument has been run.**[^stewkley-baptisms]

**The Stewkley household splits on its own evidence too** — the fourth same-name or same-household merge caught in this research. Robert Gurney married Joan Fenn at Stewkley on 10 July 1626, six months *after* Richard was baptised there on 25 January 1625/6, so Joan is not Richard's mother and Robert had two wives. Separately, a second Stewkley Richard baptised in June 1624 has a father **Walter** and mother **Joan**, tying him to the Walter Gurney × Joan Coalls marriage at Stewkley in 1620 — two households, not one man reusing a name. Robert Gurney was buried at Stewkley on 30 August 1631, matching the 1631 probate.[^stewkley-splits]

**Where the probate index is silent:** no John Gurney probate at all for Haddenham, Weston Turville (a Thomas, tailor, 1633, but no John), Hitcham, Amersham (a Thomas of Woodside 1584 only), Ivinghoe, or Bierton after Henry Gorney of Broughton in 1559.

**A(1603) is reduced by a Stewkley burial.** The Buckinghamshire Burial Index records **John Gurney buried at Stewkley on 21 September 1615**, from bishop's transcript D/A/T/160, with no age and no relationship stated, inside a cluster of Gurney burials at 1614, 1615 and 1618. ## The Cheddington John — reached by prediction, not by name match

Under the standard naming custom, the first son takes the paternal grandfather's name. The colonial John's eldest known son is **Richard**, so **if the custom is being followed at all, John Gurney's father was named Richard.** That is a prediction, and it was tested before anything was found.

Searching the Buckinghamshire church records for a John Gurney baptised c.1595–1615 with a father named Richard returns one candidate, and its household reconstructs completely. **Richard Gurney married Jane Founteine at Cheddington on 10 August 1606**, and their children were baptised there: Thomas 8 March 1606/7, **Johannes (John) 5 August 1608**, Fulke 4 February 1609/10, Richard 20 August 1612. A Katherine Gurney married Edward Reeve at Cheddington on 9 February 1628, plausibly a daughter of the same household. **Richard Gurney of Cheddington left probate in 1626**, so this John's father died when he was about eighteen. John never appears in the Cheddington record again after his 1608 baptism.[^fs-bucks][^brs-cheddington]

In favour: the naming fit is exact and was predicted rather than noticed; the August 1608 birth sits inside the same narrow window that Francis Gurney's apprenticeship forces on Candidate B, reached by a wholly independent route; the father's death in 1626 removes the tie, and a younger son of a dead husbandman aged eighteen with two brothers is exactly the profile that emigrates; he is unaccounted for after 1608 across marriage, burial, Prerogative Court and archdeaconry probate, all of which are now searched negatives rather than unsearched gaps; and the county has the documented Gurney tailoring trade.

A second naming signal is weaker and should be stated carefully. The colonial John's youngest son was **Isaac**, a name absent from every Norfolk Gurney household surveyed, which run to Edward, Marye, Agnes and Francis. Buckinghamshire Gurneys do use it, and one instance is contemporary and in the same parish: **Isaac Gurney and Martha were baptising children at Cheddington in 1636 and 1641** — so the name was in that parish's Gurney pool in the emigrant's own generation. He is not among Richard and Jane's four indexed children, so he is a separate household or married in rather than a demonstrated brother. Cheddington also gains a son John, baptised 14 December 1634 to Isaac and Martha, absent from the county index and present in the national set — which sharpens the point that Isaac and John were both live forenames in that household in the emigrant's generation.[^cheddington-john] A John Gurney and Mary baptised a son Isaac at Cublington in 1664, but that is a generation later and belongs to an already-eliminated household documented in England 1664–1672; **the Cheddington instance carries this point and the Cublington one does not.**

Against, and these are real: **the age fit is the weakest part** — born August 1608, he would be forty-five in 1653 against a deposed "aged 50 or thereabouts," a five-year stretch no worse than Candidate B's but a stretch. **No wife Mary and no marriage anywhere** in either county before 1660, so if he is the emigrant he married outside the county or outside the indexed record — the same unevidenced step Candidate B requires. **No child set**: nothing places him fathering children in England 1628–1635, which is the standing objection to every Buckinghamshire survivor. And **one baptism is one record**: in a county holding five to seven simultaneous John Gurneys, a coincidence of forenames is cheap.

**Richard Gurney of Cheddington's 1626 will is the single highest-value document in the Buckinghamshire file.** A will naming children would confirm or destroy the reconstruction outright, and if it names a son John living in 1626, that John is the man.

## The land records — a second individuating tier

The Buckinghamshire Archives catalogue supplies what parish registers cannot: records that name a man, his wife, his father, his heir, his parish, his status and his trade, and tie him to a piece of ground. Searched for Gurney across 1570–1660 it produces four households the register work had not individuated, **and every one of them is a John who stayed.**[^bucks-archives]

**John Gurney, gentleman, of Bishopstone in the parish of Stone** is the most substantial. He appears as plaintiff in a 1626 fine acquiring property in Stone, Bishopstone, Great Kimble and Marsh; and on **1 March 1648/9 he conveyed away the family's capital messuage in Bishopstone** with its cottages, closes and lands. The corresponding fine of Michaelmas 1649 names him with Ann his wife and **Edward Gurney, son and heir** — one messuage, two cottages, a dovecote and about 115 acres, the consideration in the following trust being **£1,700**. Behind him stand three generations at Bishopstone. This household matters because it is **the structural twin of Candidate B's motive argument** — a landed Gurney family liquidating its estate — and it is in Buckinghamshire, not Norfolk. It does not make this John the emigrant: he was alive in England thirteen years too late and his heir was named Edward. It shows that "a Gurney family sold everything, so a son had reason to leave" is not a Norfolk-specific circumstance.

**John Gurney the younger of Owlswick, in Monks Risborough.** A marriage settlement of 20 September 1619 records John Gurney the elder of Owlswick, husbandman, and his wife Elizabeth settling a messuage and arable in consideration of the impending marriage of their son John to Alice Hewett — almost certainly the household behind the 1623 Monks Risborough probate. **It also matters for Candidate A**: the county now holds two distinct John Gurney × Alice couples, this one from 1619 and the Bierton marriage to Alice Oliffe in 1628. A wife's forename was doing eliminating work in the same-name sweep, and here it demonstrably cannot.

**John Gurney of Farnham Royal, yeoman**, and his wife **Susan, daughter of Oliver Cogeram**, sold two acres of meadow in Beaconsfield to Edmund Waller for £55 in April 1633, both signing by mark. He married Susan at Farnham Royal on 30 March 1629, and **the couple has no child recorded anywhere in the seventeenth century** — which is what makes them the documented alternative parentage that damages the Hitcham row.[^farnham]

**John Gurney of Aylesbury, ironmonger**, served as an overseer of the poor for Aylesbury in 1652, a churchwarden's counterparty in 1658 and a Surveyor of Highways in 1660; his trade matches the Merchant Taylors' record of a John Gurny, son of John, ironmonger of Aylesbury, bound in 1655. Neither is a tailor.

Alongside them the catalogue documents a **Gurney estate at Marsworth** (D-X_202, 1617–1844), which **names no John before 1660** — Thomas dead by 1615/6, his son and heir Henry, Elizabeth and her brother Richard, then Richard elder and younger — and **William Gurney of Chesham, yeoman**, buying land in 1616 and 1618. Two further Buckinghamshire households come from the central courts: a **John Gourney litigating over property in Stoke Mandeville, Aylesbury and Walton, 1621–25** (C 3/352/66), and **John Gurney of Broughton in Bierton, husbandman**, holding Weston Turville meadow 1592–1598/9. The second weakens the old Stewkley-to-Bierton merge and ties the Weston Turville survivor into the Aylesbury-hundred tenantry.[^central-courts]

**What this tier does to the picture.** It deepens the density problem rather than resolving it. But every John it individuates is a John who stayed, held land, served office or died in place. **Not one of the newly surfaced men disappears.** Where the record is rich enough to individuate, Buckinghamshire Johns are visibly settled; the men who remain unaccounted for are unaccounted for because the record is thin, not because it shows them leaving.

## What the survivors are worth

The survivors are **unaccounted for, not viable**, and the distinction carries the weight. The colonial John was fathering children in England from about 1628 to about 1635. A household whose record stops in 1603, 1608, 1622, 1627 or 1631 is not baptising children anywhere the test can see during exactly the years he demonstrably was. Keeping any of them alive requires positing an undocumented move to an unindexed parish, a marriage there, several baptisms there, and then emigration — the same absence-explaining structure that weakens Candidate B. **In a county holding five to seven simultaneous John Gurneys, being unaccounted for is the default state, not evidence.**

Three nevertheless carry named rows rather than group clearance, because they fit the deposition age better than several candidates that already carry rows: **Weston Turville** (fathering 1627, so about fifty-one in 1653, with a Gurney tailor who died in the parish in 1633 and a Weymouth settler parish two miles away), **Hitcham** (fathering 1631, and the child baptised there is a **Mary**), and **Cheddington 1608**.

Two coverage statements bound the burial negatives behind them, and they run in opposite directions. **Weston Turville's negative is genuine** — the parish is covered, with Gurney burials at 1621, 1625 and twice in 1633, and the 1627 father is not among them. **Hitcham's is hollow** — the parish has no seventeenth-century burial coverage in that index at all, the common-surname control returning a single burial dated 1827. Being unaccounted for is informative only where the records would have accounted for him. **Cheddington's burial question is unanswerable from any index** — two have been tried, neither with proven window coverage — and needs the parish register.[^burial-coverage]

## The county-wide marriage negative

**No John Gurney married a Mary in Buckinghamshire in the emigration window**, tested from the groom's side and the bride's side independently. **Every John Gurney marriage in the county 1572–1704:** Alice 1572, Elizabeth 1573, Alice 1628, Susan 1629, Elizabeth 1630, Avis 1638, Rebecca 1640, Mary 1663, Susannah 1691, Anne 1699/1700, Elizabeth 1704 — **five in the emigration window, and not one bride a Mary.** From the bride side, the earliest Mary to marry a John Gurney in the county is Mary Kidgell at Edlesborough in 1663, two years after the colonial Mary died at Braintree. Three independent instruments now agree that the colonial marriage is not in the Buckinghamshire–Hertfordshire belt. **It tightens rather than eliminates**, since grooms commonly married in the bride's parish, which is exactly what the unsearched south-Midlands licence series exists to test.[^bucks-marriage]

## The full county roster, and why each household is not the emigrant

Beyond the men who carry named rows, the Buckinghamshire, Bedfordshire and Hertfordshire sweeps surfaced a long tail of further John Gurney households. **None of them can be the emigrant, and they fall into three groups by the reason why.**

**Generational predecessors — men fathering children before about 1620, when the colonial John was still a boy.**

- **Haddenham** — a child baptised 25 February 1620 and Joane on 26 January 1622. Fathering in 1620 implies a birth by about 1595, so he would be about 58 in 1653, outside any rounding of "about 50." A *different* John Gurney was buried at Haddenham on 2 April 1621, so the parish held more than one.
- **Great Kimble / Owlswick** — the marriage settlement of 20 September 1619 (see above).
- **Weston Turville's earlier record** and the Hulcott household whose daughter Joyce was baptised in 1603.

**Continuing English households — men documented in England past the emigration.**

- **East Claydon** — John Gurney and Elizabeth Lyne, children Elinor 1632 and Samuel 1636; John buried there 17 April 1654, his will proved that month as John Gurney, shepherd.
- **Chesham** — John Gurney and Elizabeth, children Andtr and Martha; burials in July 1672 and on 11 June 1678, two generations of the same line.
- **Wing** — John Gurney and Anne, children James 1650 and Elizabeth 1652.
- **Cublington** — John Gurney and Mary, son Isaac baptised 1664 and a further John in 1666; **wife Mary buried at Cublington in 1669**, which is decisive on its own. This household most plausibly held the Stewkley manor by 1687.
- **Marsworth** — a John Gurny buried in 1642.

**Post-1660 baptisms and marriages — the Aylesbury Vale and Chiltern Gurney families expanding, a generation late.** Chenies 1644; Aylesbury 1644 and 1669; Edlesborough 1663 (two further marriages beyond the 1661 Kidgell event); Cublington 1666; Puttenham 1670; High Wycombe 1671; Whitchurch 1671; Stone 1673. In Hertfordshire, **Tring 1655** (John Gurney and Elizabeth Shepard, a household adjacent to but distinct from Berkhamsted) and a **Northchurch probate of 1661**.

**One further household is included because a burial in it has been mis-attributed.** A separately documented **Edward Gurny** household was active at Aylesbury in the 1660s, with a son Jon buried on 2 February 1665 and a daughter Ann baptised in 1666. That 1665 burial of a son named Jon has elsewhere been read as Candidate A's own; it is Edward's son.[^roster]

## Coverage limits that bound these negatives

- **Hertfordshire probate is reachable, and the apparent blank is a finding-aid artefact.** The published-probate-index dataset returns no Hertfordshire Gurney because it holds **no Hertfordshire volume at all** — a common-surname control returns zero too. The records themselves exist: the **Archdeaconry of St Albans** series at Hertfordshire Archives is catalogued to item level, and **`ASA/AR/8` covers 1610–1636** with a contemporary list of testators.[^herts]
- **Northamptonshire is covered only from 1677, and only for administrations.** The death window of the 1641-certificate man is not covered.[^northants]
- **The Buckinghamshire index gives testator, parish, occupation, year and court reference — not the will text.** Wills name children; the three new probates could each resolve a household the index can only bound.
- **Buckinghamshire apprenticeship records are a structural absence, not an unrun search.** The county had no incorporated livery companies, so there is no equivalent of the Merchant Taylors' or Norwich registers, and the national apprenticeship registers (TNA IR 1) begin in **1710**, about eighty years too late. Pre-1710 evidence survives only in borough court and freemen books for Buckingham, Chipping Wycombe and Aylesbury, in Quarter Sessions pauper indentures, and incidentally in wills — none of it name-indexed online. **This is a different kind of blank from the London and Norwich negatives**, which were searches against complete registers, and it must never be logged as a negative result.[^apprentices]
- **Bedfordshire is a materially unworked adjacent county** — 141 Gurney baptisms 1605–1645, with households at Hockliffe, Luton, Clapham, Leighton Buzzard and Houghton Regis. Hockliffe is register-attested (Alice, daughter of Thomas, 1619), corroborating a 1590 Court of Requests suit over lands at Hockliffe, five miles from Cheddington.[^beds]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000077 -->

# Francis Gurney G14 — the first-marriage sweep, the death record, and what Pease actually says

Three questions about Francis Gurney that Candidate B depends on, each now worked to the limit of what
can be reached online.

## There is no earlier marriage

Francis Gurney was bound apprentice in London on 14 May 1599 and admitted to the freedom of the Merchant
Taylors on 30 June 1606. London livery companies barred apprentices from marrying and enforced it, so he
could not have married before mid-1606. He married Margaret Rybett at Norwich St Martin at Palace on
**23 September 1611**. The East Dereham household was already producing children by 27 May 1610.

That gap invites an obvious hypothesis: Margaret is called his *first* wife only because hers is the
earliest marriage anyone had located, and an earlier marriage between 1606 and 1611 would place a
legitimate son John at 1607–1610 and dissolve the pre-marital problem. **It was searched, and it is not
there.**

A useful indexing artefact came out of this: Ancestry indexes the 1611 marriage as a **baptism** —
"Frant Gurney, Abt 1611, Baptism, 23 Sep 1611, Norwich, St Martin at Palace", with no spouse field. An
event-type-filtered marriage search would miss it.

**The residue that cannot be closed online.** For 1606–1611 the surviving licence sources are the
Norwich Consistory Court bonds (microfiche 1558–1902, with gaps), the Norwich Archdeaconry registers of
licences granted 1590–1632 in the administration act books, and the letters of matrimony 1590–1609
(NRO ANW 22/2). Their finding aid is **Bolingbroke's calendars, 1476–1708, NRO HMN 313–317**, indexed
only on the searchroom shelves. Norfolk and Norwich Archdeaconry bonds proper begin 1670/1712, after
the window. FindMyPast's *Norfolk Marriage Bonds 1557–1915* is image-browse with no name index, and
FamilySearch's index of the same collection carries five Gurney entries in total, all 1749 or
later.[^licence-layer] No online name index reaches this window.

**What the negative is worth.** Absence of a marriage record is not absence of a marriage. But the East
Dereham household was producing children by May 1610 with no located marriage of any kind for Francis
before September 1611, across two independent Norfolk parish-register programmes, the England-wide IGI,
the Bishop of London's allegations, and the Norfolk probate-and-licence index. The pro-Candidate-B
reading of the apprenticeship constraint draws no support from the searchable record, and the
three-to-five year pre-marital birth the hypothesis requires stands unrelieved.

## The death record is real but thin, and no probate has been found

Francis Gurney was buried at **St Botolph without Bishopsgate, London, 9 January 1646/7**. The entry is
listed in FreeREG — one exact result on a search of surname Gurney, 1640–1650, London (City),
Bishopsgate — and it corrects Boyd's misread 1641.[^freereg]

Two qualifications belong with it. FreeREG gives the register type as **"Other Transcript"**, so its
transcriber worked from a transcript rather than the parish register; nobody has seen the register
entry. And Ancestry's *London, England, Church of England Baptisms, Marriages and Burials, 1538–1812* —
18.15 million records — does not carry it: `Fran*_Gurn*` returns 52 rows collection-wide and the nearest
burial is a *Frances* Gurney at St Dunstan in the West in 1649.[^anc-1624] Either that collection has a
Civil-War-years hole at St Botolph, or the transcript records something the register does not.

**Probate is negative in every jurisdiction searched, with one court outstanding.**

| Court | Result |
|---|---|
| Prerogative Court of Canterbury | negative, PROB 11, 1637–1665[^pcc] |
| **Commissary Court of London (London Division), 1626–1649 and 1661–1700** | **negative** — the printed index gives five Gurney/Gourny records (Daniel 1690, Miles 1636, Thomas of Fulham 1676, Thomazine widow 1680, William 1665) and no Francis. This court held St Benet Fink[^commissary] |
| Norfolk church courts, 1371–1858 | negative[^anc-62679] |
| Essex Record Office, 1600–1700 | negative — the catalogue indexes its probate series by testator name, and a 1683 Gurney marriage licence bond shows name-level indexing works[^seax] |
| **Archdeaconry Court of London** | **unchecked — the one remaining jurisdiction** |

A man ruined by the total land sale of 1634 may simply have left nothing worth proving, so the negative
may be true rather than a gap. But it is currently the state of the search, not a conclusion.

## Pease names a son John — and cites nothing for it

The family tradition that Candidate B leans on is Charles E. G. Pease, *The Descendants of Anthony
Gurney*. It gives Francis a first wife Margaret Ryvett and two children, John and Anne, both
"c.1610".[^pease] Its numbered source list identifies **#177 as Burke's *Landed Gentry* 1894** and
**#254 as "Philis Wainford http://www.wainford.net/, 'Wainford & Gurney family connections'; report,
June 2012–2016"**.[^pease-refs]

**The Francis Gurney entry carries no source superscript at all** — neither 177 nor 254 nor anything
else — while entries on the same page do carry them. The Ryvett claim is unsourced in Pease.

**And it did not come from Wainford.** Every `254` superscript in the genealogy sits on the
nineteenth-to-twenty-first-century Wainford branch: the illegitimate line of Richard Hanbury Gurney
(1783–1854) by Susan Wainford of Saxlingham, running down to Philis Aster Wainford herself, an American
descendant documenting her own recent line. Her contribution is a modern family website, not an archival
source, and wainford.net no longer resolves. The premise that the Ryvett detail reached Pease from an
American researcher via Sir Joseph Gurney Pease is not borne out by Pease's own apparatus.

Two things the full quotation does establish. Pease's John "married Grizell Fletcher" — the colonial
John Gurney's second wife, married at Braintree 12 November 1661 — so **Pease is unambiguously
identifying Francis's son with the Braintree emigrant**, and "died in Chelmsford, Essex" is a compiler's
slip for the Massachusetts town. The identification is exactly the disputed claim, and Pease names
nothing for it. The same sentence adds "**Anne married John Gilman**", a second emigration-shaped claim;
tested against the Norfolk marriage index (`gurn*` against spouse `gil*`) it returns four rows, none an
Anne Gurney and John Gilman, none before 1667.

**The circularity this exposes.** The case file reasoned that the verified Ryvett marriage "lends
credence to other details in the genealogy… such as John Gurney's 1610 birth (stated in genealogy and
aligns to discovered baptism record)." The baptism was read as c.1609/10 by an analyst who already knew
Pease claimed a John born c.1610. Pease supplied the expected answer; the search found something
date-shaped; each was then cited in support of the other. With the baptism disproved, Pease's c.1610
stands alone and unsourced. Pease also dates *Anne* to c.1610 — two children in the same year reads as a
compiler's placeholder, not two researched dates.

# Identity — the shape, the matrix, and the two families

Every candidate for the Braintree emigrant is tested against one specification of what the man and his household must look like. This unit holds that specification, the matrix that scores every non-zero candidate against it, and the structural finding the matrix produced — which is the most useful result the July 2026 work delivered and was not visible until the attributes were laid out side by side.

## The shape as specified

Fifteen attributes, each a scale rather than a binary, none mandatory.

| | Attribute |
|---|---|
| **0** | surname Gurney or a variant |
| **1** | a household with Mary, Richard and/or John Jr., in that order |
| **2** | a Mary born c.1628 |
| **3** | a Richard born c.1630 |
| **4** | a John born c.1633 |
| **5** | a father named John |
| **6** | a mother named Mary |
| **7** | the father born c.1603 |
| **8** | a John × Mary marriage c.1622–28 |
| **9** | a John × Mary marriage c.1628–40 *(second-marriage hypothesis)* |
| **10** | the father's father named Richard |
| **11** | a region known for Great Migration participation |
| **12** | a region connected to Weymouth, Massachusetts, or the colonial John's cohorts |
| **13** | the father a tailor |
| **14** | an event c.1636–39 whose context could support emigration |

Attribute 14 is read throughout as a documented **push event**.

## The structural finding: two disjoint families

**The candidates fall into two families, and no candidate is in both.**

**Family one — documented households with the wrong shape.** C(ii), Hitcham, Ackworth and the eliminated Eythorne each have a *household*: a father, sometimes a mother, children with dates. What they lack is the right children. C(ii) has a John and a Richard in the right order but **no Mary in the parish at any date**. Hitcham has a Mary satisfying the hard bound and nothing else at all. Ackworth has the father and mother right and the children wrong. Eythorne has almost everything and is eliminated by two burials.

**Family two — documented men with no household at all.** Candidate B, Cheddington 1608, A(1603), Candidate D and Weston Turville are, respectively, the best trade-and-corridor fit, the only candidate reached by prediction from the naming custom, the exact age match, the best trade match, and the best Weymouth-proximity fit. **Every one of them scores zero on attributes 1 through 4.** They are men without wives or children on record.

The emigrant needs a man of the right age *and* his household, and every candidate supplies one half. That is the actual structure of this problem, and it is the strongest argument in the file for the record-state rows: **the man's own baptism and his household's baptisms are probably not in the same searchable place** — one side is lost, unindexed, or in a class not yet opened. That is precisely what the *not-yet-searched* and *no-record* rows describe.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000127 -->

## Per-candidate notes where the score needs explaining

**Candidate B is contradicted by attribute 10.** Its whole thesis is that John's father was **Francis**; the shape predicts **Richard**. B is strong on the contextual attributes — corridor, trade lineage, and the 1634 total land sale as a push event squarely inside the attribute-14 window — and empty on every attribute that describes an actual household, because no document names a John of this family, a wife, or a child. B is a context without a household.[^b]

**Ackworth is the only candidate scoring attributes 5 and 6 together**, and attribute 9 is its strongest card. The shape's explicit allowance for a second-marriage hypothesis is exactly what the standing objection to Ackworth does not accommodate: the household is ruled out because Mary Shed's 1647 Braintree marriage puts her parents' marriage by about 1630, which a June 1636 marriage cannot produce — **decisive only if 1636 was John's first marriage**, and the record gives no marital status. Against it: the 1637 baptism names no parents, and "John Thomas Gurnoe" may be *"John son of Thomas Gurnoe."*[^ackworth]

**C(ii) is the best household in the file and the worst on attribute 2.** John 1624 and Richard 1626 give two of three colonial sons in the right order; the father's own age, c.1598–1602, is the best documented fit to "aged 50 or thereabouts"; and its attribute-14 case is the strongest of any candidate — two children buried within three weeks in February and March 1638/9, a documented family catastrophe inside the exact window. But no Mary Gurney is baptised at Great Berkhamsted at any date under any spelling or any father, 1544–1888, and no John Gurney marries anywhere in Hertfordshire between 1600 and 1654. Attribute 10 is contradicted: C(ii)'s father was C(i), a John.[^cii]

**Hitcham stands at 3% after attribute 6 was damaged.** Its case is attribute 2 — a Mary baptised 22 January 1630/1, satisfying the hard bound — plus attribute 5. But Hitcham is a true singleton: one Gurney baptism in the entire Buckinghamshire index, with **no mother recorded**. Five miles away, **John Gurney married Susan at Farnham Royal on 30 March 1629**, and that couple has no children recorded anywhere in the seventeenth century, a national parent search returning nothing before 1770; Farnham Royal itself records no John Gurney children. So there is a named, documented, childless-on-record John Gurney couple within five miles and twenty-two months of the Hitcham baptism, whose wife is **Susan**. That does not eliminate Hitcham, but the mother of its one child is unknown and now has a plausible alternative who is not a Mary.[^hitcham][^susan]

**Weston Turville is quietly the best on the contextual attributes** and nobody had said so: attribute 7 (fathering 1627, so about 51 in 1653), attribute 12 (Ashton Clinton, a documented Weymouth settler parish, is two miles away), and attribute 13 (a Gurney tailor, Thomas, died in the parish in 1633). It fails on the entire child core.

**Candidate D holds the best direct trade match** — his father Robert was described as a tailor at Old Change — and an excellent attribute 7 from his 1623/4 Drapers' admission by redemption. Empty household, attribute 10 contradicted.

**The Newgate apprentice fails attribute 7 decisively** — birth c.29 September 1615, twelve years off — and cannot satisfy 1 through 6 at all, being an indentured servant. Its only strength is attribute 12, which it holds absolutely: he was physically in Massachusetts in 1636.

**The two rows added at the close of the work both illustrate the split rather than breaking it.** **Gurley of Westminster** is family one taken to its limit: father John, mother Mary, a daughter Mary baptised 1631 satisfying the primary bound exactly, and the household's records stopping in 1633 — then no Richard, no John, three surplus daughters, and a surname requiring an `n`→`l` confusion that sits at the bottom of the plausibility scale and was reached only by the provider's own variant expansion.[^gurley] **Epping / North Weald** is the first candidate that is mostly `~` rather than mostly `✗`: its household attributes are *untested*, not absent, because the coverage negative that would have closed them is void. It scores `✓✓` on attribute 10 for a reason no other candidate can match — the parish's Gurn- line alternates John and Richard across three generations, which is the naming behaviour attribute 10 was derived from.[^epping]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000128 -->

## Eight corrections to the shape

In descending order of how much each would change a score.

**1. Margaret belongs on the mother axis as a substantial soft hit.** Surname *and* forename variants are a weighted scale, not a binary. In seventeenth-century hands `g` and `y` carry near-identical looping descenders, and *Margaret* was routinely contracted by clerks to `Marg.` or `Mgt.` to save paper and ink — so the one letter distinguishing the written token from *Mary* is exactly the letter most likely to be ambiguous. A `Marg.` written with a looping `g` reads as *Mary* to a later clerk, a nineteenth-century transcriber or a modern indexer, and a *Mary* is equally extendable to *Margaret*. **The corruption is introduced by the transcription chain and runs in both directions.** The corpus supplies a worked example: Tittleshall with Godwick, Norfolk, 1620 returns the same child twice, father Henry in both, with the mother indexed **Anne** in one transcript and **Mary** in the other.[^tittleshall] The adopted weighting:

| Mother token | Weight on attribute 6 | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Mary, Marie, Marye, Maria, Marya, Mria | full | orthographic variants of one name |
| Marg, Marg., Mgt, Margaret, Margarett, Margret | **substantial (soft hit)** | `g`/`y` descender ambiguity plus routine abbreviation; corruption runs both ways |
| Margery, Margerie | partial | shares the abbreviated stem `Marg.` but is a distinct name |
| Molly, Polly | excluded in this period | eighteenth-century hypocorisms, anachronistic before c.1700 |

The same logic governs the father axis, and had already been in force in the sweeps: the John family is scored across `John`, `Jhon`, `Jon`, `Jno`, `Joh`, `Johis`, `Johannes`, `Johan`, `Johane`, `Johanne`, and — importantly — **`Jone` and `Joan`**, which in these indexes are sometimes a Latin or contracted *Johannes* misread as the female *Joan*. The county-set data contains live instances of both directions of failure: at Norwich St Giles in 1618 the father of Joseph Garnam is indexed `Jone`, and at King's Lynn in 1615 the *father* of Gregorye Gurnall is indexed `Katheryne`.[^tittleshall]

**Admitting Margaret changed no candidate score, because the axis was then searched and it is empty.** Across FreeREG's 585 captured rows, five households carry a Margaret-family mother — Norwich 1638, Bardwell in Suffolk 1643 and 1645, and Mamble and Bayton in Worcestershire 1637 — and none has a father in the John family. Across 705 rows from eight FindMyPast county baptism sets, seven rows carry a Margaret-family mother, to fathers Robert, Edward, Michael and William, and none has a father in the John family at any date. The national cross-collection parent search bounded 1610–1650 returns eight rows for father John with mother Margaret, not one of them a genuine Gurney — the surname column reading `?`, `Fosse`, `G...Ds` and `G?`, fuzzy-match noise from Cornwall, Cheshire and Scotland. The negative is interpretable because the identical query shape with mother Mary returns the known Eythorne household: **the instrument was working when it returned nothing for Margaret.**[^margaret-sweep]

**2. Attribute 2 has a primary bound and attributes 3 and 4 do not.** Mary Gurney married Daniel Shed at Braintree in 1647, so at a minimum marriage age of sixteen she was **born by 1631** — a primary constraint. The "c.1628" is compiled tradition from Sprague (2001) and the *History of Weymouth* (1923), and Richard c.1630 and John c.1633 rest entirely on those same compiled estimates with no primary record behind them. Score attribute 2 against "born by 1631" and treat c.1628 as soft; mark 3 and 4 explicitly as compiled-estimate attributes worth less than 2. This matters directly — Hitcham's Mary of January 1630/1 satisfies the primary bound exactly while looking three years off the traditional date.[^mary-bound]

**3. Attributes 8 and 9 are mutually exclusive**, so a candidate scores one or the other. Scoring both double-counts the marriage.

**4. Attribute 10 is a derived prediction, not an independent observation.** It comes from reading the naming custom forwards — the eldest son takes the paternal grandfather's name — and it is conditional on Richard being the eldest son. It directly contradicts Candidate B, whose father is Francis, and C(ii) and D, whose fathers are John and Robert. A hit on 10 is a hit on a hypothesis, and a miss is weak evidence, because naming customs were followed loosely.

**5. Add a departure attribute, with its coverage control attached.** The household's records **stop between 1635 and 1641 with no burial or probate for the head, in a parish whose burials are demonstrably covered.** This is separable from attribute 14 — a push event explains *why* he might go; this records *that* the household stops. Without the coverage control it is worthless: two false departure gaps have already been produced by omitting it. On a properly controlled test **only Ackworth currently passes** — 170 Smith burials in the parish and zero Gurney burials.[^departure]

**6. Add a penalty for children with no colonial counterpart.** A household with the right three children and no others fits better than one with the right two and four extras. C(ii) carries Michaell and Elizabeth; the Gurley household carries Elizabeth, Ellynor and Alexander. As specified, the shape can only reward matches and never penalise surplus.

**7. Attribute 0 is a weighted scale, not a single tick.** Gurney / Gurny / Gourney / Gurnie at full weight; Gurne / Gurn as near-certain contractions; **Gurnard / Gurnat at moderate-to-high**, because the Heralds' Visitation of London of 1634 records letters patent of 26 July 1633 to "Richard Gurny, *alias* Gurnard, Sheriff of London" and to "Burton Gurny, *alias* Gurnard, descended from the Gurneys of Kendall," and the Norfolk line's own canting crest is a gurnard fish borne by Thomas Gournay I before 1465; Gorne / Garne / Gernne as ordinary vowel drift; Gurnett / Gurnel / Gurner lower — **and lower is never exclusion**. *Garne* in Gloucestershire and *Gurley* in Westminster are probably distinct surnames with their own lineages, but that is a weight, not a class boundary.[^variants]

**8. Peter, Isaac and Sarah are excluded from scoring, not treated as missing children.** No Peter Gurney-variant baptism exists anywhere in Great Britain 1627–47 and no Isaac 1632–52; both men first appear in colonial records, so their absence in England is *expected* and must not count against a candidate. The same applies to Sarah, who has no primary record at all and is absent from Torrey's compendium of New England marriages before 1700.[^peter-isaac]

**A ninth rule arrived from the elimination side.** Three undated FamilySearch role-row households, one of them earlier described as the single most important loose end in the work, were dated on a second instrument and resolved to **1687, 1704 and 1710** — every one late, none within a century of the window. **An undated role row is not evidence of an in-window household until it is dated elsewhere.**[^role-rows]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000129 -->

## Counting is the wrong metric

Because attribute 0 is a scale, a **count** of Gurney-variant records is not a meaningful quantity: the total depends entirely on where the scale is cut. The object of the search is an individual record matching a corner of the colonial shape, not a total of Gurney baptisms. Any statement phrased as a variant count should be restated as a match or partial-match statement, with the weight named.[^variants]

# Identity — Other Eliminations and Earsham

Beyond Candidates A, B, C, and D, a wider same-name sweep clears the field: a Norfolk yeoman family at Earsham whose 1638 will names a wife Mary and a son too young to be the emigrant, a distinct surname easily mistaken for Gurney, a household-density survey across six further Norfolk parishes, a targeted negative search for the colonial John's rarer children's names, and a scatter of English probate, parish, and muster comparators that between them account for every other same-name English John Gurney the record sweeps have surfaced.[^sweep-basis] None of the households below is the Massachusetts emigrant; together with Candidates A, C, and D they leave Candidate B as the only surviving identity. The household-by-household enumeration of the whole cleared field, each row with its single strongest elimination ground, is published as the case file's §8.6 clearance table, which this unit cross-references as the master list rather than restating.[^clearance-sweep-master]

## Earsham, Norfolk — a Gurney yeoman family, 1638

John Gurney of Earsham ("Eursham"), Norfolk, yeoman, made his will on 10 August 1638, sick in body, holding messuages, lands, and tenements at Earsham both freehold and copyhold.[^earsham-will] His wife, **Mary**, was to hold the estate during their son's minority, then take a £4 annual annuity in lieu of dower. Their son, **John**, a minor, takes the whole estate at **fourteen**, "when he may be capable to take his guardianship" — so he was born after about 1624, a full generation younger than the colonial John Gurney (b. c.1607–12). If young John died before twenty-one, the estate passed instead to the testator's brother, recorded in the will's own text as "Lyon Gurney" but read by a closer paleographic pass as **Syon** — the same forename recurring, and the same misreading trap, across this family's later records. A second brother, **Robert Gurney**, was already dead by 1638, leaving four sons (including a William and a Robert) £5 apiece; a brother-in-law, **John Youngman of St Andrew's** (most plausibly Ilketshall St Andrew, just across the Waveney), served as co-executor alongside Syon. The will was proved at Norwich on 26 December 1639.

The testator died in 1638 and his son John was still a minor, so neither man is the Massachusetts emigrant, who was already at Weymouth by June 1641 and had a wife of his own named Mary since before 1628. What the will documents instead is a previously unlogged Waveney-valley Gurney family, distinctive enough — the rare brother's forename, a dead brother's four named sons — to recognize and set aside wherever it resurfaces. The family's own further history, traced forward through a 1710 Bungay nuncupative will, a 1690s Bungay merchant's will, and an eighteenth-century Earsham manorial court book, belongs to that family's own subject file rather than to John Gurney G13's identity question.[^earsham-external]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000080 -->

## A distinct surname and a wider Norfolk density

A separate "John Gurnell," admitted freeman in Massachusetts on 10 May 1643, is not John Gurney-1 under a different spelling. Surviving 1600s Massachusetts Gurnell records are thin enough to invite a Gurnell-to-Gurney misreading, but a 30 July 1675 Dorchester death entry, checked directly, reads "Gurnell" plainly — confirming Gurnell as its own surname, not a clerk's variant of Gurney.[^gurnell] The colonial John, by contrast, is recorded under Gurney/Gurny/Garny variants throughout his New England career, never Gurnell.

Beyond the individual candidates already eliminated, a Findmypast UK Parish Baptisms search restricted to Norfolk, surname Gurney, father John, 1623–1643, surfaces fourteen further baptisms across six parishes: Denton (John + Rachell, children Mary 1638, Thomas 1639, Sarah 1644), Hempnall (Anna 1640, Elizabeth 1641), Stanfield (Mary 1634), Norwich St Margaret & St Swithin (a 1630 child), North Runcton (Beniamin 1625, Frances 1628), and Earsham (the family above, John + Elizabeth Singler). None of these six households reproduces the colonial John's full, distinctive child set — Sarah, Mary, Richard, John Jr., and Peter — by name and date, but the density itself is consistent with Norfolk having been a Gurney heartland with several parish-level Johns active in the same decade, which is part of what makes Candidate B's Norfolk origin geographically unremarkable rather than a stretch.[^norfolk-density] A wildcard marriage sweep adds one more Norfolk same-name household with a wife named Mary — Robert Gvrney, who married Mary Lame at Norwich on 27 July 1622 and baptised children at St Stephen and St Benedict — but its wrong groom forename and 1622 date rule it out as the emigrant's household (John b. c.1607–12, wife Mary), leaving it one more documented Norfolk Gurney of the period rather than a match.[^norwich-robert]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000081 -->

## A targeted search for Richard and Isaac finds no other household

Rather than search parish by parish for a matching wife's name, a second pass anchored on the colonial John's two rarest children's names — Richard and Isaac — across every English collection FamilySearch indexes. A Richard Gurney baptism with a father named John, 1620–1645, returns exactly one hit anywhere in England: Richard Gurnie, baptized 15 December 1626 at St Peter, Hertfordshire — Candidate C's own Richard, already eliminated with the rest of the Berkhamsted family. An Isaac Gurney baptism with a father named John, 1620–1650, returns zero hits; the handful of Buckinghamshire Isaacs on record (Cublington 1664, Radclive 1710, Great Brickhill 1748, Etwall 1745) all fall after the window. No English John Gurney household anywhere — outside the candidates already named — carries a Richard, a John Jr., a Peter, or an Isaac in any combination. This is not treated as a definitive elimination on its own: it is equally consistent with the colonial John fathering his distinctive children entirely in Massachusetts, after his June 1641 emigration, which is in fact what the colonial record shows.[^cluster-anchor]

## The wider same-name clearance sweep

A run of English parish, probate, and muster records rounds out the field, each clearing or bounding one further same-name household rather than pointing at the emigrant. Two burials place a continuing English John-and-Mary household well past the emigrant's own death: at St Mary, Harrow on the Hill, Middlesex, a John and Mary Gurney "of Okington" buried a daughter Ann on 30 January 1669 and a son Isaac on 8 February 1669 (Old Style 1668) — a John Gurney with a wife Mary still parenting and burying children in England seven years after the Braintree John died. At Toddington, Bedfordshire, John Gurney (married Elizabeth Moreton there in 1624) was himself buried in September 1641, three months after the Massachusetts emigrant's Weymouth fine and twenty-one years before the Massachusetts John's 1662/3 death — an elimination on the Toddington John's own death record, not merely a wife-name coincidence. A third London burial, of a "John sonne of ffrancis Garney Joyner" at St Giles Cripplegate in December 1640, documents a third, trade-distinct Cripplegate-area Garney/Gurney family rather than the emigrant.[^harrow-toddington-cripplegate]

A dozen further Prerogative Court of Canterbury probate records, none naming a John Gurney matching the colonial profile, anchor separate English clusters that must be kept apart from Candidates A and C: Anne Gurney, widow of Eythorne, Kent (will 1656, sons Edward, John, William, Thomas), corroborates the already-eliminated Eythorne Kent John; Daniel Gurney of Aylesbury (will 1669, naming a brother John) and a 1670s "John Gurney of Aylesbury" probate sentence both confirm a continuing Bucks Gurney presence distinct from Candidate A; William Gurney of London (will 1655, sons John, Abel, Walter) and Richard Gurney, a London labourer (will 1674/5), mark separate London clusters; and Sir Richard Gurney, the Royalist Lord Mayor, together with the 1577 Tobias/Edward Gurney wills, are too early or, per Daniel Gurney's own nineteenth-century appendix, "not related to the Norfolk family" at all.[^tna-pcc-corpus] The 1641–42 Protestation Returns add a final negative: the working Buckinghamshire returns name Anthony, Robert (twice), Thomas, and Walter Gurney at Stewkley, plus further Thomases, Ezekiels, Richards, and an Isaac scattered across Linslade, Ivinghoe, Soulbury, Marsh Gibbon, Chedington, and Marsworth — but **no John** anywhere in the surviving Buckinghamshire returns, which is itself consistent with (not contrary to) Candidate A having already moved to Northamptonshire by 1641.[^protestation-returns]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000082 -->

## Standing

None of the households surveyed here name a child John Francis or otherwise reproduce the colonial John's full family signature, and the naming-pattern check cuts both ways: no Gurney child anywhere in this sweep is named Francis, which remains the strongest single argument against Candidate B even though it is not on its own dispositive. Taken together with Candidates A, C, and D, this sweep and the Earsham will account for every other named same-name household the record searches have surfaced.[^sweep-basis] The five households attested by only a single indexed event — Ackworth, Hitcham, and the non-Mary Aylesbury/Amersham/Norwich grooms — are not treated as hard eliminations, because one record cannot rule out an unrecorded emigration the way a continuing English record can; the case file's probability model carries them at a combined **~8% residual**.[^cf-residual] Candidate B remains the only candidate the shared matching-criteria profile does not eliminate or reduce to a residual.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000083 -->

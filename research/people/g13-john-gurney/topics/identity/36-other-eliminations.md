# Identity — other eliminations, and the counties swept

Beyond the named candidates, a wider sweep clears the field: a Norfolk yeoman family at Earsham eliminated twice over, a distinct surname easily mistaken for Gurney, a household-density survey across Norfolk, three counties swept for the first time, and a scatter of English probate, parish and muster comparators that between them account for the other same-name English John Gurneys the record has surfaced.[^sweep-basis]

**One rule governs the whole of this unit, and it was learned the hard way: a variant spelling is a weight on an individual record, never a class of surnames to exclude.** Households are set aside here on a father's forename, a wife's forename, a date or a death — not on how a transcriber spelled the surname.

## The variant scale

Variant forms sit on a sliding scale, and the weight belongs on the **record**, not on the spelling as a category.

| Form | Weight | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Gurney, Gurny, Gurnie, Gourney, Gourny, Gurnay | **High** | The family's own habitual orthography |
| Gurne, Gurn, Gourne, Gurnoe | **High** | A dropped terminal letter; the commonest clerk's contraction |
| **Gurnard, Gurnat** | **Moderate–high** | A **documented alias** in a registered family, with the canting crest to explain it |
| Gorne, Garne, Gernne, Gowrne, Girney | **Moderate** | Ordinary vowel drift in secretary-hand transcription |
| Gurnett, Gurnel, Gurnell, Gurner | **Lower** | Recognised surnames in their own right — but a Gurney entry can still be written this way |
| Hurney and other H-initial forms | **Discovery only** | Warranted by the proven H/G indexer confusion, tested and clean |

**Gurnard is a Gurney form with formal standing.** The Heralds' Visitation of London of 1634 records letters patent exemplified by Garter King of Arms on 26 July 1633 to **"Richard Gurny, *alias* Gurnard, Sheriff of London,"** and on the same pedigree **"Burton Gurny, *alias* Gurnard, descended from the Gurneys of Kendall."** That is a heraldically registered family using both forms interchangeably, in London, in the emigrant's own decade — a formal alias, not a transcription slip. The reason is not obscure: the Norfolk line's own crest, from before 1465, is a **gurnard fish in pale, a canting pun on the surname**, borne by Thomas Gournay I and carried forward. Families that pun their own name in heraldry are exactly the families whose name gets written as the pun.[^alias][^crest]

**Lower weight is never exclusion**, and the reason is practical rather than generous: the object is not a count of Gurney baptisms but whether an individual record matches a corner of the colonial shape. A low-weight surname form on a record that matches the shape is worth more than a high-weight form on one that does not. What settles a variant question is the register image and the hand — not an index row, and not a later century's settled spelling.[^variant-weighting]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000134 -->

## Essex — swept for the first time, and open

**Essex is the county the colonial evidence points at hardest, and it holds no Gurney household in the parishes that evidence actually names.** Three colonial ties run to Essex: John Gurney's Braintree land was held by lease inside the estate of William Tyng of **Stanford Rivers, Essex**; his daughter Mary married Daniel Shed of **Finchingfield, Essex**; and Braintree, Massachusetts took its name from Braintree, Essex. The direct question those ties invite is whether a Gurney family lived in those parishes while the colonial John was in England.

**The two parishes the colonial evidence names directly are empty.** Finchingfield holds one Gurn\*-variant baptism in its entire register run; Stanford Rivers holds none.[^essexsweep]

**The country four miles away is not.** A Gurney-plausible cluster sits in the Epping – North Weald Bassett – Fyfield triangle right through the window:

| Record | Parish | Father | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Richard Gurnat, 1616** | **Epping / Epping Upland** | **John** | A father John, about four miles from Stanford Rivers |
| John Gurne, 1622 | Epping | Robert, mother Sara | |
| Marie Gurney, 1625 | Epping | Robert, mother Sara | |
| Susan Gurnard, 1627 | **North Weald Bassett** | Robert, mother Ann | Adjoins Epping |
| Marie Gurney, 1637 | North Weald Bassett | Robert, mother Anne | |
| Johannes Gurnard, 1625 | **Fyfield** | Gulielmi (William) | Ongar hundred |
| Thomas Gurnard, 1620 | Shenfield | Thomas, mother Alice | |
| Elizabeth Gorne, 1623 | Harwich | **John** | North-east Essex, outside the Ongar cluster |

**Fully enumerated, the Epping line alternates John and Richard across three generations** — a John naming a Richard in 1616, a Richard naming a Richard in 1649, a John naming a John in 1654. That is precisely the naming behaviour the shape's predicted paternal grandfather is built on, in the Great Migration's own county. What the set does not contain is a **John baptised c.1603**: its earliest Epping Gurn- row is 1616, and **whether that is absence or absent coverage cannot be settled on this instrument**, because a year bound and a place keyword cannot be combined in a single-dataset query.[^epping]

So Essex does not close. It opens, with a named target for image work — the Epping and North Weald Bassett registers at the Essex Record Office — and it enters the candidate table at 3% as the only candidate whose household attributes are *untested* rather than absent.

## The Maldon branch, corroborated at register level

The *Gourn\** spelling in Essex concentrates almost entirely at **Maldon, St Mary the Virgin**, in one household: **Francis Gourney and Ann**, with children baptised John 1655, Jane 1656, Francis 1657, George 1659, Thomas 1660, Charles 1662, Richard 1664, Francis 1665, Anna 1666 and Henry 1667, plus a George Gourney household in the same town from 1661.[^maldon]

This is the register-level footprint of a family the printed authorities describe from other sources — Daniel Gurney's "son John of Maldon" and "probable son George," and Bernau's study of Francis Gournay of Maldon. The bachelor John of Maldon, nine hearths in 1674, whose letters of administration went to his brother Thomas Gurney in 1681, had rested on those compiled accounts; here are a John baptised 1655 and a Thomas baptised 1660 to the same parents. **The household starts in 1655**, a generation after the emigration window, so it bears on Francis G14's descendants and not on the colonial John. The only unambiguous Gurney-surname baptism in Essex before 1650 is Anthony Gourney at Aveley in 1615, to a father George.

## Earsham, Norfolk — a Gurney yeoman family, eliminated twice

John Gurney of Earsham ("Eursham"), Norfolk, yeoman, made his will on 10 August 1638, sick in body, holding messuages, lands and tenements at Earsham both freehold and copyhold.[^earsham-will] His wife **Mary** was to hold the estate during their son's minority, then take a £4 annual annuity in lieu of dower. Their son **John**, a minor, takes the whole estate at fourteen, "when he may be capable to take his guardianship" — so he was born after about 1624, a full generation younger than the colonial John. If young John died before twenty-one the estate passed to the testator's brother, recorded in the will's text as "Lyon Gurney" but read by a closer paleographic pass as **Syon**. A second brother, **Robert Gurney**, was already dead by 1638, leaving four sons £5 apiece; a brother-in-law, John Youngman of St Andrew's, served as co-executor. The will was proved at Norwich on 26 December 1639.

The testator died in 1638 and his son was still a minor, so neither man is the emigrant. **And the register closes the household from a second direction.** The Norfolk county baptism set carries four Girney baptisms at Earsham: **John 1636 and Henry 1638, both to a father John**, the second with a mother indexed **"Alex,"** plus Steven 1637 and Susan 1638 to a father Sion. The marriage is dated too — **John Girney × Elizabeth Singler, 9 October 1632.** So the Earsham John's wife is **Alice or Elizabeth, not Mary**, on two independent instruments, and he has a further son in 1638. The 1635 first son John must have died, since a second was baptised in 1636.[^earsham-register]

What the will documents instead is a previously unlogged Waveney-valley Gurney family, distinctive enough to recognise and set aside wherever it resurfaces. Its own later history belongs to that family's subject file.[^earsham-external]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000080 -->

**Earsham also carries a coverage lesson.** The national parent-search pool returns **zero** for this household, while the Norfolk county set returns the 1636 and 1638 baptisms with the father indexed. The pool's positive control passes on the same instrument, so this is coverage, not a query defect: **any England-wide negative from that pool needs a county-set pair.**[^pool]

## Bishop's Cleeve is not a Gurney household

*Maria Gorne, 2 March 1627, Bishop's Cleeve, Gloucestershire, father John Gorne* reads at first glance as a colonial-name child to a father John. It belongs to a **Garne** family, a settled Cotswold surname. A parent-name sweep on the household returns children of a John Garn or Garne at Bishop's Cleeve running Mathew 1629, John 1679, John 1681, James 1683, Frances 1687, Giles 1692, Mary 1712 and Alce 1714.[^garne] **A lineage baptising in the same parish for eighty-five years, straight through and well beyond the emigration window, is not an emigrant household.** The same reading disposes of Marye Garne 1638 at Compton Abdale, twenty miles away in the same county.

This is a weighting judgement, not a surname-class exclusion: *Garne* remains a moderate-weight Gurney form generally, and what removes these particular records is the household's own eighty-five-year continuity in one parish.

**Upton upon Severn, Worcestershire, is downgraded rather than closed.** The row that made it interesting — Mary Gurney, 7 September 1629, mother Mary, no father named — sits inside a continuous Upton on Severn Gurney presence running from Annes 1582 through 1666, 1709, 1713, 1715, 1757, 1768, 1779 and into the nineteenth century, and a John Gurney was buried at Upton on Severn St Peter & St Paul on 19 January 1666. The partial-transcription reading remains formally possible but now has to be read against a parish where the surname never leaves. **The county set is not the instrument for it** — FindMyPast's Worcestershire Baptisms does not cover the parish, its common-surname control returning zero, so the Upton rows reach us only through the national aggregate.[^upton]

## Three undated households, dated and eliminated

Three John Gurney households surfaced on FamilySearch with the right parent names and no event date at all — the format FamilySearch calls a role row, which aggregates a person's relationships without attaching them to a dated event. Dated on a second instrument — the county baptism sets, which carry an event year and both parent columns — all three fall outside the window:

| Household as carried | Now dated to | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| John Gurney and **Mary**, Sussex — Elizabeth, Grace | **Grace, Horsham, 1704** (father John, mother Mary) | A century late |
| John Gurney and **Margaret**, Herefordshire — Mary | **Mary, Stoke Lacy, 1687** (father John, mother Margaret) | Sixty years late |
| John Gurney, Warwickshire — Ann, Anne | **Ann/Anne, Coleshill, 1710** (father John, mother not indexed) | A century late |

**Three for three outside the window by six decades or more**, which is now a scoring rule: an undated role row is not evidence of an in-window household until it is dated elsewhere. Note what closed the Herefordshire household — **its date, not its wife's name.** Margaret is a substantial soft hit on the mother axis, so a rule excluding her would have dismissed this household for a bad reason and it would have had to be reopened later.[^role-rows]

## The West Country, swept for the first time

Weymouth, Massachusetts is named for Weymouth, Dorset, and its founding company came from Somerset and Dorset. Somerset also carries an ancient Gournay presence in its place-names — Farrington Gurney, East Harptree — so the question was not idle. Nobody had ever asked whether Gurney households existed there.

Swept for 1610–1650: **Somerset two baptisms, Dorset two.** The Dorset pair are both *Gurnard* at **Owermoigne**, seven miles from Weymouth Dorset — which under the corrected variant weighting is worth drilling rather than dismissing. The household runs fathers **Martin, Nicholas and Robert** across 1574–1625: Elizabeth 1574, George 1576, Martin 1582, Brigit 1585, Joane 1589, Ursula 1592, Armenell 1596, Jane 1623, Elizabeth 1625. **No John, and no colonial-name child in window.** The shape does not match at any corner.[^westcountry]

**The West Country corridor is therefore swept and empty of shape-matches** — a real result, because it closes on record a corridor that the Weymouth reception evidence had made a live possibility.

An **instrument note** belongs with it: the single-dataset route with a year bound returns zero even on a common-surname control for the Somerset, Dorset and Worcestershire sets, so those sets are broken for year-bounded queries and the cross-collection route must be used.

## A distinct surname, and the Norfolk density

A separate **John Gurnell**, admitted freeman in Massachusetts on 10 May 1643, is not John Gurney under a different spelling. Surviving Massachusetts Gurnell records are thin enough to invite a misreading, but a 30 July 1675 Dorchester death entry, checked directly, reads "Gurnell" plainly. The colonial John is recorded under Gurney/Gurny/Garny variants throughout his New England career, never Gurnell.[^gurnell]

Beyond the individual candidates, a Norfolk-restricted search for baptisms to a father John, 1623–1643, surfaces fourteen further baptisms across six parishes: Denton, Hempnall, Stanfield, Norwich St Margaret & St Swithin, North Runcton, and Earsham. **None reproduces the colonial child set**, but the density itself matters: Norfolk was a Gurney heartland with several parish-level Johns active in the same decade, which is part of what makes a Norfolk origin geographically unremarkable rather than a stretch.[^norfolk-density]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000081 -->

**Denton is not a father-John household**, despite one index reading it that way. The providers disagree on the father's forename, and two of the three read against John: FreeREG gives **Josias** with a mother Rachell, and Ancestry gives *Josiah*, against FindMyPast's *John*.[^denton]

A wildcard marriage sweep adds one more Norfolk same-name household with a wife named Mary — **Robert Gvrney**, who married Mary Lame at Norwich on 27 July 1622 and baptised children at St Stephen and St Benedict — but its wrong groom forename and 1622 date rule it out.[^norwich-robert] A **Tacolneston** household also enters the Norfolk comparator set and has never been assessed: George Gurney, parson of Tacolneston, "and his sons," with seven baptisms 1583–1611.[^tacolneston]

## The best partial match found, and why it fails

**John Gurley and Mary at St Clement Danes, Westminster** — Eliz 1627, Ellynor 1628, Alexander 1629, **Mary 1631**, Sara 1633, father John and mother Mary on all five, confirmed on the county set with parent columns. It matches four corners of the signature including the hard 1631 bound, and the household's records stop in 1633, inside the departure band.[^gurley]

**It fails on the child set regardless of surname.** There is no Richard and no John; the son is Alexander, a name absent from the colonial family; and three children have no colonial counterpart. The ordering the shape wants — Mary, then Richard, then John — cannot be built from this sibship at all. The surname is the weaker part of the objection: *Gurley* requires an `n`→`l` confusion, which is not one of the well-attested substitutions, and unlike *Gurnard* it has no documented alias attaching it to a Gurney; five consecutive entries across seven years all read Gurley, which suggests a clerk who knew the name. **Recorded as a weighted partial match at 2%, not promoted.**

## The wider same-name clearance sweep

A run of English parish, probate and muster records rounds out the field, each clearing or bounding one further same-name household. Two burials place a continuing English John-and-Mary household well past the emigrant's own death: at **St Mary, Harrow on the Hill**, Middlesex, a John and Mary Gurney "of Okington" buried a daughter Ann on 30 January 1669 and a son Isaac on 8 February 1669 — a John Gurney with a wife Mary still parenting in England seven years after the Braintree John died. At **Toddington**, Bedfordshire, John Gurney, who married Elizabeth Moreton there in 1624, was himself buried in September 1641 — an elimination on his own death record. A third London burial, of a "John sonne of ffrancis Garney Joyner" at **St Giles Cripplegate** in December 1640, documents a trade-distinct Cripplegate family rather than the emigrant.[^harrow-toddington-cripplegate]

A dozen further Prerogative Court probate records anchor separate English clusters that must be kept apart from the named candidates: Anne Gurney, widow of Eythorne, Kent (will 1656); Daniel Gurney of Aylesbury (will 1669, naming a brother John) and a 1670s probate sentence for a John Gurney of Aylesbury, both confirming a continuing Buckinghamshire presence distinct from Candidate A; William Gurney of London (will 1655) and Richard Gurney, a London labourer (will 1674/5), marking separate London clusters; and Sir Richard Gurney, the Royalist Lord Mayor, together with the 1577 Tobias/Edward Gurney wills, too early or, on Daniel Gurney's own account, "not related to the Norfolk family."[^tna-pcc-corpus] **Three of the place-readings in that corpus are contested** between the published index and the repository's earlier scan readings — PROB 11/335 (Aylesbury, Bucks versus Albury, Herts), PROB 11/241 and 11/242 (East Claydon, Bucks versus East Chiltington, Sussex), and PROB 11/252 (East Greenwich, Kent versus East Grinstead) — and are held pending resolution. The Aylesbury reading matters most, because it touches the live Buckinghamshire thread.[^contested]

**A clean structural negative belongs with them:** no direct-line ancestor appears anywhere in the Prerogative Court index across 1384–1858. The Norfolk line proved in the Norwich Consistory Court, so the absence is jurisdictional rather than evidential, and it should not be re-searched. Thirty-one of the forty-nine pre-1700 Prerogative Court Gurney-variant wills have never been examined, including a pre-1620 London merchant and draper cluster that sits on the London-draper thread.[^pcc-index]

The 1641–42 Protestation Returns add a final negative: the surviving Buckinghamshire returns name Anthony, Robert (twice), Thomas and Walter Gurney at Stewkley, plus further Thomases, Ezekiels, Richards and an Isaac scattered across Linslade, Ivinghoe, Soulbury, Marsh Gibbon, Cheddington and Marsworth — **but no John anywhere**, which is consistent with Candidate A(i) having already moved to Northamptonshire by 1641.[^protestation-returns]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000082 -->

## Standing

None of the households surveyed here reproduces the colonial family signature, and the naming-pattern check cuts both ways: no Gurney child anywhere in this sweep is named Francis, which remains a real argument against Candidate B even though it is not dispositive on its own.

**What this sweep does not do is leave one survivor**, for two reasons that pull the same way.

1. **A single indexed event is not a hard elimination.** One record cannot rule out an unrecorded emigration the way a continuing English record can, so single-attestation households are held at a low residual rather than cleared.
2. **Counting households is not counting men.** The enumeration counts households *as reconstructed*, and every household tested for that defect has turned out to be more than one man.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000083 -->

# Matching by the family, not by the man — and a whole class of spellings nobody has searched

Every sweep in this file has hunted **a man**: a John Gurney, parish by parish, eliminated or not.
That is the wrong shape of query for a household whose distinguishing feature is not the father's
name — which is one of the commonest in England — but the **children, in sequence**.

The colonial John's family gives a template with real discriminating power:

| Child | Approximate birth | Discriminating value |
|---|---|---|
| Mary | c.1628 | Low alone; high in sequence |
| Richard | c.1630 | Low alone; high in sequence |
| John Jr | c.1633 | Nil alone |
| Peter | c.1635–40 | **High — Peter is rare in the Gurney record set** |
| (Sarah) | — | No primary record; see the attributes audit |

A **Mary → Richard → John** sequence to one father in one parish across 1627–1634 would be a strong
positive, and it is a different query from "was there a John Gurney at X?" This unit records that
query being run.

## The household query, run England-wide

FamilySearch's record search accepts a father's and a mother's forename alongside the surname. Run
as **surname Gurney, father John, mother Mary, England, 1625–1643**, it returns 1,769 ranked rows —
and no household anywhere in England shows the sequence.[^fs-household] What it does return is the
file's known cast, which is a useful control that the query is reaching the right material:

- **Eythorne, Kent** — Thomas 1635, John 1638 to John Gurney and **Mary**. The only genuine John +
  Mary household the query surfaces in the window, and already eliminated: the couple stayed in Kent
  and John was buried at Eythorne in 1648.
- **Weston Turville, Bucks** — Elyzabethe, 2 September 1627, father John. The known survivor.
- **Hitcham, Bucks** — Mary Gurny, 22 January 1631, father John Gurny. The known survivor.
- **Cublington, Bucks** — John Gurney and Mary, son Isaac. Already eliminated on a 1664 baptism.
- **Aylesbury, Bucks** — John 1638, Sarah 1639. Candidate A(ii).
- **Toddington, Beds** — Elizabeth 1625, Anne 1628, Audrey 1633. Already eliminated.

**Reading:** the negative the case file records parish by parish now also holds when the question is
asked of the whole country at once, with both parents named. No John-and-Mary Gurney household in
England is baptising a Mary, a Richard and a John between 1627 and 1634.

## The Peter query, widened

The standing negative is that "of twelve indexed Peter-Gurney-variant baptisms 1632–1640 across
Findmypast UK Parish Baptisms, none was fathered by a John Gurney." That is one dataset and a
nine-year window.

Run as **forename Peter, surname Gurney, England, 1628–1652** across FamilySearch's whole English
holdings, the result is stronger: **no Peter Gurney baptism of any kind in England in those
twenty-five years.** The only genuine Peter Gurneys the query reaches are a burial at Knill,
Herefordshire in 1704 and a Peter Gurney father of a Janet in Northumberland — both outside the
window and outside any relevant household.[^fs-peter]

Peter is the strongest single discriminator in the colonial child list and it is a clean blank. Two
readings, and they point opposite ways: either the family is in an unindexed parish, or **Peter was
born in New England** — which the c.1635–40 estimate permits, since the crossing falls in 1637–41.
If Peter is American-born, the English sequence to hunt shortens to **Mary, Richard, John** and the
rare-name lever disappears entirely. That is worth stating, because the Peter negative has been
carried as though it bore on the English search, and it may bear on nothing at all.

## The marriage, swept England-wide on a spouse-indexed instrument

The baptism sweeps kept hitting interface limits. The **marriage** did not, because one instrument
does the job properly: Ancestry's IGI-derived *England, Select Marriages, 1538–1973* indexes **both
parties**, so a groom-and-bride pair can be asked of the whole country in a single query.

**Across England before 1660 there are exactly two John Gurney × Mary marriages, and both are already
in the file:**[^marriages]

```
6 Nov 1632   Eythorne, Kent        John Gurne / John Gurney  ×  Mary      (three duplicate index rows)
6 Jun 1636   Ackworth, Yorkshire   John Gurnoe                ×  Mary
```

Eythorne is eliminated — the couple stayed in Kent and John was buried there in 1648. Ackworth is the
file's ~6% row. **So the only unaccounted-for John Gurney × Mary marriage in England before 1660 is
Ackworth** — which is a sharper statement of that row's standing than the case file makes, and it
cuts both ways: it is the sole survivor of a genuinely England-wide test, and it is still blocked by
a hard primary constraint. Daughter Mary married Daniel Shed at Braintree in **1647**; at a minimum
marriage age of sixteen she was born by 1631, so her parents married by about 1630. A June 1636
marriage cannot produce her. Ackworth stays blocked, but by the Shed record rather than by a compiled
birth estimate — which is a better footing than the case file's phrasing implies.

**The completeness check passes too.** Re-running the same query with the bride *unconstrained* —
which would catch any marriage indexed with a blank or unreadable spouse field — returns, around
1630, only the Eythorne rows and one other: **John Gurneys × Susan, Farnham Royal, Buckinghamshire,
30 March 1629.**[^farnham] No John Gurney marriage in England near 1630 has an unindexed bride, so
the Mary-filtered sweep was not missing events through blank fields.

That Farnham Royal row is a small positive in its own right: the Buckinghamshire deed sweep found
**John Gurney of Farnham Royal, yeoman, and Susan his wife, daughter of Oliver Cogeram**, selling two
acres in Beaconsfield to Edmund Waller in April 1633, both signing by mark. The marriage is now
dated.[^farnham-deed]

**What the accumulated negatives now look like together.** Four independent England-wide tests, on
three instruments:

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| John + Mary household baptising Mary, Richard and John, 1627–1634 | **Nothing, anywhere in England** |
| Peter Gurney baptism, 1628–1652 | **Nothing, anywhere in England** |
| John Gurney × Mary marriage before 1660 | **Two — Eythorne (eliminated) and Ackworth (blocked)** |
| John Gurney marriage near 1630 with unindexed bride | **None** |

The colonial marriage is near-certain to have happened in England: his wife Mary was living at
Braintree until 1661, their daughter married in 1647, and he was not among the 1636 Weymouth
grantees. **An English marriage of about 1626–1630 that leaves no trace in any of these nets is the
central fact of the English side of this problem**, and it is now bounded much more tightly than
"not found in eastern-England parish-marriage indexes."

Two explanations remain, and they call for different work. Either the family is in a parish these
indexes do not cover — which is the coverage argument, and it is real, since the county sets are
demonstrably parish-selective — or the event is recorded under a name-form these sweeps do not reach.
A licence would not *replace* a register entry, so the licence layer is not an escape from this; it
is a **second, independent net over the same event**, held in different repositories with different
survival. That is exactly why it is worth casting now, when the register net has failed this
comprehensively.

## Candidate C is indexed at St Albans, not Berkhamsted

The household query surfaced something the file has wrong. The eight-child family the case file
calls **Berkhamsted** — Richard 1626, Elizabeth 1629, Sarah 1634, Francis 1636/7 — is indexed by
FamilySearch at **St Peter, Hertfordshire**, i.e. St Peter's, St Albans, with the same children and
the same father:[^stpeter]

```
Richard Gurnie      christened 15 December 1626   St Peter, Hertfordshire   father John Gurnie
Elyzabeth Gurnie    christened  2 August 1629     St Peter, Hertfordshire   father John Gurnie   (d. 21 Feb 1639)
Sara Gurny          christened 18 May 1634        St Peter, Hertfordshire   father John Gurny
Francis Gurney      christened  1 March 1637      St Peter, Hertfordshire   father John Gurney   (d. 9 Mar 1639)
```

The repo's Berkhamsted attribution comes from the FindMyPast Hertfordshire set. The two indexes
disagree on the parish, and the disagreement has been quietly load-bearing:

- Unit 52 records that "the Hertfordshire parish collection also holds no seventeenth-century
  Berkhamsted Gurney record, so Candidate C's household is not reachable through it."[^unit52] If
  the household is at St Albans, it *is* reachable, and that blank was a place-attribution artefact
  rather than a coverage gap.
- The case file's supporting negative — "the Hertfordshire burial index records no John Gurney burial
  at Berkhamsted between 1640 and 1700" — was run against the wrong parish.
- St Peter's, St Albans lies in the **Archdeaconry of St Albans**, one of the two jurisdictions named
  as unsearched for Hertfordshire probate. That gives the standing Hertfordshire probate action a
  parish to aim at instead of a county.

None of this revives Candidate C, who is eliminated on the father's own age — fathering from 1610
puts his birth at about 1585–90 — a ground immune to place confusion. But an elimination resting on
a mis-attributed parish is the weak kind, and two of its three supports need re-running against St
Albans.

## The spelling class nobody has searched

`data/search-variants.json` registers **87 surname variants. Every one of them begins with G.**

That is not obviously wrong until you set it beside what the deviation audit established. The audit's
deviation D8 proved that the FindMyPast indexer of this very register **misread a capital H as
G** — the indexed "Nicholas Gorne" is `Nicholas Horne` on the page, and the reason `Gorne` could
never be found as a surname anywhere else is that it is not a surname at all.[^d8] The letterform
argument was specific: this hand's capital H is "a tall looped ascender plus a large descending loop
below the baseline, swinging left," and the indexer read it as G.

**The same confusion runs in the other direction, and no sweep has ever looked.** A secretary-hand
capital G with a descending loop is indexable as H, so a genuine **Gurney** can sit in an index as
**Hurney**. The variant registry has no H-initial form, no C-initial form, and nothing else that
would catch a wrong-initial misread — the single non-G entry, `Surney`, is an attested colonial
documentary form, not an index-error class.

**Tested, and it is a clean negative — but the class needed testing.** An exact-match sweep for
surname **Hurney** with a father **John**, England, 1618–1645, returns five rows: a Richard Hurney
with wife Jane, a William Hurney with wife Elizabeth, a Newcastle marriage of 1637, and two
eighteenth-century burials. No John Gurney household hides there.[^hurney] An unbounded Hurney sweep
1600–1670 adds one John Hurney christened at Walkern, Hertfordshire in 1614 to a father John — too
young for the deposition age, and not pursued.

The finding is the gap, not the result. **A variant registry built entirely from documentary and
OCR spellings of the *right* initial cannot catch the one error class this project has actually
proved an indexer commits.** It should carry a labelled wrong-initial class, and negatives asserted
against "all variants" should say which class of variants they covered.

## What this changes

- **The child-sequence negative is now England-wide and two-parent**, not parish-by-parish. It is a
  materially stronger statement than the case file's §6.1 version and should replace it.
- **The Peter negative is widened to 1628–1652 across FamilySearch's English holdings** — and
  simultaneously weakened in relevance, because Peter may be American-born.
- **Candidate C's parish attribution is contested**, and two of its three supporting negatives were
  run against a parish the household may not be in.
- **The variant registry has a structural blind spot** that the project's own audit predicts.

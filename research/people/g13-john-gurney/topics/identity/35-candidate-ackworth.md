# Identity — Ackworth, Yorkshire

John Gurnoe married **Mary Burton** at Ackworth, Yorkshire, on 6 June 1636, and a child indexed "John Thomas Gurnoe" was baptised at the same parish seven months later, on 19 January 1637.[^fmp-ackworth] Nothing else about this household is indexed: no burial of either spouse, and no further child.

**It is the only unaccounted-for John × Mary marriage in England before 1660**, on five independent instruments, and the only candidate in the field scoring a father named John and a mother named Mary together. It stands at **9%**, second among named candidates.

## Why the row is as high as it is

**The marriage is the hardest requirement in the colonial signature, and this is the only one left standing.** Every John Gurney-cluster groom in England 1600–1660 is now named with his bride, and not one marries a Mary — a negative sharper than "none found," because nine Gurney men married Marys in the same holdings, so it is specifically the Johns who have none. Exactly two John × Mary marriages exist in England before 1660: Eythorne, Kent, 6 November 1632, eliminated on two burials, and Ackworth. Ackworth is what is left.[^marriage-arm]

**The departure gap is real here, and controlled — the only candidate for which that is true.** A departure-shaped gap means nothing unless the parish's burials are demonstrably covered, and two false gaps have already been produced in this research by omitting that control. At Ackworth the control passes: the burial index returns **170 Smith burials** in the parish and **zero Gurney burials**, and the entire Gurney presence in Ackworth is the two records above.[^departure]

**The compiled tradition points here independently.** Older compiled Gurney histories sometimes give the colonial John's wife as "Mary Barton" or "Mary Burton," and the 1636 Ackworth marriage is the first primary record to supply that exact name combination at a plausible date.

## Why it is not higher

**The date block, and its condition.** The colonial John's daughter Mary married Daniel Shed at Braintree in 1647, so at a minimum marriage age of sixteen she was born by 1631 and her parents married by about 1630 — a primary constraint, not a compiled estimate. A June 1636 marriage cannot produce her. **But that is decisive only if 1636 was John's first marriage, and the record gives no marital status.** A widower remarrying in 1636 with children already born fits the colonial household exactly, and the shape the candidates are scored against explicitly allows for a second-marriage window. **The date is a real objection but not a conclusive one.**[^mary-bound]

**The household is thinner than it looks, because the two records may not belong together.** The 1637 baptism **names no father and no mother**, so attaching it to the 1636 marriage is an inference, not something the record states. And "John Thomas Gurnoe" may not be a compound forename at all: it may be *"John son of Thomas Gurnoe"* — the same indexing artefact already caught at Toddington, where a relationship phrase in the register collapses into a forename in the index. If that reading is right, the baptism belongs to a different household and Ackworth is reduced to a single marriage record.[^johnthomas]

**The naming and birth order do not line up.** A first child in January 1637, seven months after the marriage, is not a Sarah or a Mary; and a 1637 son named John collides with the colonial John Jr., placed about 1633 by compiled estimate.

**Yorkshire is outside the corridor.** It sits well beyond the Norfolk–Suffolk–Essex–London belt that produced the Great Migration. Yorkshire emigration to New England in the late 1630s exists but is comparatively sparse, and no primary record bridges an Ackworth Gurnoe household to any Massachusetts arrival, town record, or any of the colonial John's identified associates.

## The row cannot be moved on the available index

**Ackworth cannot be tested on the principal provider's Yorkshire baptism set at all.** A Gurney query scoped to the parish returns zero; the control returns **127 Smith results, of which none on the visible page falls earlier than 1689**. Because a year bound and a place keyword cannot be combined in that query mode, and because `sortby` is accepted and ignored, the set's Ackworth coverage before 1689 is unproven and **the Gurney zero is uninterpretable — it is not evidence that the household had no further children.**[^untestable]

So the row holds at 9% as *untestable* rather than as weakened. **The parish register at the West Yorkshire Archive Service is the only instrument that can settle it**, and it would settle three things at once: whether the 1637 baptism names parents; whether "John Thomas" is a forename or a relationship phrase; and whether the 1636 marriage record gives either party's marital status.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000078 -->

## Open work

1. **The Ackworth parish register, 1636–1637** — the three questions above, in one pull. This is the single highest-value action on the candidate.
2. **An Ackworth burial search for either spouse**, which the county index does not reach.
3. **The earliest compiled-genealogy occurrence of the "Mary Barton/Burton" tradition.** If it predates the availability of modern indexes, it is independent evidence pointing at Ackworth and the row should rise. If it first appears only after IGI-era derivative trees became available, it is circular — later compilers matching the same index entry — and the row should not.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000079 -->

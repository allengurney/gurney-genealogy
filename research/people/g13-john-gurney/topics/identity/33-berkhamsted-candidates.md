# Identity — Berkhamsted: Candidates C(i) and C(ii)

**Great Berkhamsted, Hertfordshire, held four contemporaneous Gurney households in the emigration window, and the baptisms to a father named John span forty years — 1597 to 1637. That is not one man. It is a father and a son, and only the father has ever been assessed.** C(i), the elder, is eliminated on his own age and stands at 1%. C(ii), the younger, is born about 1598–1602 and would have been about fifty-one in 1653 — the best documented age fit in the whole candidate field — and stands at 4%. He is not an emigrant-shaped household: his family was dying, not leaving.

## The parish, read as a parish

Read by parent rather than by child, the Great Berkhamsted baptisms to a father John Gurney run:[^baptisms]

```
1597  Hugh          1615  Sara            1631  Michaell
1599  Anne          1624  Jhon            1634  Sarah / Sara
1602  Amye          1626  Richard         1636  Francys
1606  Bridgett      1629  Elizabeth       1637  Francis
1610  Henrye
```

Forty years of fathering. The natural break is the nine-year gap between **Sara 1615** and **Jhon 1624**.

- **C(i), the elder John** — Hugh 1597, Anne 1599, Amye 1602, Bridgett 1606, Henrye 1610, Sara 1615. Fathering from 1597 puts his birth about 1570–75, so he is comfortably eliminated on age against a colonial John who deposed "aged 50 years or thereabouts" in 1653.
- **C(ii), the younger John** — Jhon 1624, Richard 1626, Elizabeth 1629, Michaell 1631, Sarah 1634, Francis 1636/7. Fathering from 1624 puts his birth about **1598–1602**, so he was about **fifty-one in 1653**.

**The split gains its first real evidence from the burial register: a Jhon Gourney was buried at Berkhamsted in 1620** — after C(i)'s last recorded child and before C(ii)'s first. That is exactly the shape the father-and-son reading predicts. It is not proof, because a second Jhon Gourney was buried in 1612 while C(i) was demonstrably still fathering, so the parish held more than one John, and the 1620 burial could be a son, a cousin, or the John baptised 1588 to a father Thomas. The discriminating evidence is the elder John's will in the Archdeaconry of St Albans registered volume `ASA/AR/8` (1610–1636), an offline pull at Hertfordshire Archives.[^roster]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000105 -->

**And the parish is not one Gurney family.** Read whole, the baptism corpus carries four contemporaneous households distinguished by the father's forename:[^bapts]

| Father | Children |
|---|---|
| **John / Jhon** | Hugh 1597, Anne 1599, Amye 1602, Bridgett 1606, Henrye 1610, Sara 1615 · Jhon 1624, Richard 1626, Elizabeth 1629, Michaell 1631, Sarah 1634, Francys/Francis 1636/7 |
| **Thomas** | John 1588, Eliz 1591, Willm 1594, Thomas 1604, Nicolas 1607, Richarde 1635, Thomas 1638, Thomas 1640 (mother Elizabeth) |
| **Edward / Edwarde** | James 1603, Elisabeth 1606, Susanna 1611 |
| **William / Willyam** | Anna/Anne 1594, Willyam 1632 |

That governs how the burials may be read: they cannot be assigned wholesale to the John line, and the Thomas household was actively baptising in 1638 and 1640.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000135 -->

The parish attribution itself is settled. FindMyPast indexes this family at Great Berkhampstead and FamilySearch indexes the same children at St Peter, Hertfordshire; **St Peter's is Berkhamsted's parish church, and the two providers mean the same place.**[^parish]

## What C(ii) has

**Three of the colonial forenames in one household** — John 1624, **Richard** 1626, **Sarah** 1634 — against a colonial set of Sarah, Mary, Richard, John and Peter. No other English household reaches three.

**The right age, from the household's own evidence.** Born c.1598–1602 on his first child; about fifty-one at the deposition.

**A documented family catastrophe immediately before the crossing window.** Two of his children died within three weeks of each other in the winter of 1638/9: Elizabeth, baptised 2 August 1629, buried 21 February 1638/9, and Francis, baptised 1 March 1636/7, buried 9 March 1638/9. Two providers and two record classes give the same two deaths, and the dates match to the day.[^transcripts]

**The naming convention fits forwards.** C(ii)'s eldest recorded son is Jhon 1624, and his own father C(i) was a John — the custom is being followed in this household. That also means the shape's predicted paternal grandfather named *Richard* is contradicted here rather than merely missed.

## What C(ii) lacks

**Mary is absent at parish level, not merely at household level.** Across every surname stem and every father, the Berkhamsted baptism corpus contains **no Mary Gurney at any date between 1544 and 1888** — the only Mary is a 1730 burial.[^bapts] Mary Gurney married Daniel Shed at Braintree in 1647, so she was born by 1631 at the latest. This is the single strongest objection to C(ii) and it should not be smoothed over.

**The household's records do not stop.** C(ii) was opened on a departure-shaped gap, and the gap does not exist. Great Berkhamsted's burial register survives for 1600–1660, is transcribed, and holds **sixteen Gurney-variant burials 1579–1643** — five of them between November 1637 and March 1638/9. A household that loses four or five members in eighteen months and then stops appearing in the baptism register is displaying mortality, not departure.[^roster]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000136 -->

**A Richard Gurney was buried in the parish on 28 June 1638**, and this is the sharpest single blow. If he is the Richard baptised there 15 December 1626, C(ii)'s best name-match child died in England at eleven while the colonial Richard Gurney of Weymouth was alive and a freeman in 1681, and C(ii) collapses outright. Three readings are open: (a) he is the 1626 Richard, son of John; (b) he is Richarde Gurney baptised 1635, son of Thomas — a three-year-old of the neighbouring household, equally consistent with a June 1638 burial in a mortality year; (c) he is an adult Richard of the parish, unbaptised there, which is weak, since no Richard Gurney baptism other than 1626 and 1635 appears in the whole run. **The index cannot discriminate** — the burial transcript carries name, date and place only, with no age and no parent. The register image is the discriminator.[^roster][^bapts]

**No marriage can be tested, because none exists in the county.** No John Gurn\*/Gourn\* marriage is recorded anywhere in Hertfordshire between 1600 and 1654; the set's John Gurney marriages begin at Tring in 1655, with John Gurnett events at 1567 and 1646 bounding the negative on both sides. Great Berkhamsted's own Gurney marriages in 1582–1722 are all brides.[^marr] So C(ii)'s wife cannot be named from Hertfordshire records and cannot be tested against a Mary. A groom marrying out of county would surface in the south-Midlands archdeaconry licence series, which has never been searched.

**Michaell 1631 has no colonial counterpart**, and neither does Elizabeth. Elizabeth demonstrably died; Michaell is unaccounted for. **The dates also run systematically early** — Berkhamsted John 1624 against a colonial John Jr. of about 1633, Berkhamsted Richard 1626 against a colonial Richard of about 1630 — though the colonial dates are compiled estimates rather than records, which softens the objection without dissolving it. And **the family used the name Francis**, which the colonial family never did; the Berkhamsted Francis died in 1639, so the colonial argument is not contradicted, but a household that named a son Francis in 1637 is a different object from one that never used the name.

## The one thread still holding

**C(ii)'s head has no burial.** No John Gurney-variant burial occurs at Berkhamsted after 1620, and none anywhere in Hertfordshire between 1620 and 1690 except Jonathan Gurney at Bovingdon in 1682–3.[^johnburials] That cannot be read as departure. FindMyPast's own description of the Hertfordshire set states that many Hertfordshire registers are **"sadly defective during the Civil War and Commonwealth period (1643–1660)"** — and the Berkhamsted Gurney burials stop in 1643 and do not resume until 1711, which is the signature of that defect rather than of an empty parish. A head of household who died between 1643 and 1660 would be invisible.[^defective]

**Coverage statement, so it is not re-run as a search:** FindMyPast Hertfordshire Burials covers Berkhamsted across 1583–1660 and again from 1711. **Any Berkhamsted burial negative for 1644–1660 falls inside a known defect and is not evidence.**

## Why the parish looked empty for two months

The earlier zero — "no Gurney burial at Great Berkhampstead at any date" — was produced by two compounding query defects, each of which fails closed, and both are worth carrying because they generalise.

1. **`lastname=gurn*` does not reach `Gourney`.** The Berkhamsted family is indexed in the burial set predominantly under *Gourney*, and a wildcard on the `gurn` stem cannot match it. The five seventeenth-century Gourney burials — Isabell 1601, Henry 1607, Jhon 1612, Jhon 1620, Elizabeth 1639 — were invisible to the query that reported zero.
2. **The place token differs between sets of the same county.** The baptism set and the cross-collection view label the parish *Great Berkhampstead*; the **burial set labels it `Berkhamstead`**. The keyword is a literal token match, so both spellings tried earlier missed a parish that is fully present: `keywords=berkhamsted` and `keywords=berkhampstead` each return zero, while `keywords=berkhamstead` returns 78 Smith rows.[^placetoken]

**The repository already held the answer in two places.** `data/sources.json` had carried, since a walk of 9 May 2026, the note that the Hertfordshire Burials set holds two seventeenth-century Berkhamsted entries, Jhon Gourney 1612 and 1620; and the identity assessment stated the same thing in its own prose. A sweep contradicted both and reported the contradiction as a discovery. That is the mechanism the *documented but never assembled* row describes, observed inside this project's own work.[^registry]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000130 -->

## Standing

**C(i) is eliminated on his own age, and that ground is immune to the same-name masking problem that weakens register-continuity eliminations. He holds at 1%.**

**C(ii) is a documented man of the right age whose household is documented dying rather than leaving. He holds at 4%** — for him, only the absence of a burial for the head, inside a defective register window; against him, a parish-level absence of any Mary Gurney at any date, no marriage anywhere in the county in the window, and a Richard Gurney buried in the parish in June 1638 who may well be his own son.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000137 -->

## What would settle it

1. **The Great Berkhamsted burial register image for 28 June 1638** — the entry normally gives "son of X," which decides whether C(ii) survives at all.
2. **A Mary Gurney baptism at Berkhamsted c.1620–1624**, or proof the register has a gap there.
3. **The marriage of the younger John**, c.1622–23, in a neighbouring parish or through the south-Midlands licence series, which would name his wife.
4. **The Archdeaconry of St Albans probate series** (`ASA/AR/8`, 1610–1636) for either John; a will for the elder naming his children would establish the split outright. The archdeaconry sits in the **diocese of London**, not Lincoln, which governs where every licence, transcript and probate for these men lives.[^jurisdiction]

# The central courts and the estate archives — sweeping the class that produced the best elimination

The strongest elimination in this file was found by accident. TNA **E 115/180/113**, the 1641
certificate of residence recording that a John Gurney had become liable for tax in Northamptonshire
and was no longer liable in the half-hundred of Aylesbury, is the one document in the whole
Buckinghamshire pass that individuates *a man* rather than a surname in a register — and it turned
up because someone was looking for Candidate A, not because the class was searched.

**The class had never been searched as a class.** Nor had any of its neighbours: Chancery, the Court
of Requests, Star Chamber, Exchequer depositions, or the deposited estate archives that the same
catalogue indexes. The only Discovery sweep the repo carried was county-filtered to Norfolk
1560–1665 and aimed at the West Barsham gentry line, not at John Gurney.[^prior-sweep] This unit
records what a systematic sweep returns, and what it bounds.

## Coverage — read this before any negative below

The Discovery catalogue indexes The National Archives' own holdings plus catalogues contributed by
some 2,500 other repositories. It carries **catalogue descriptions, not document text**: a person
named only inside an uncalendared bill, deposition or deed does not surface, so a blank here is a
blank in the *finding aid*. Item-level description is dense for the classes that matter most —
Chancery pleadings, Requests, E 115 certificates, E 134 depositions, Star Chamber, WARD 2 deeds and
PCC wills. Contributed county catalogues vary from full abstracts to collection-level stubs, and
manorial court rolls and parish registers are catalogued as volumes, so their contents are invisible.

Fourteen surname variants were run separately across 1590–1685, returning **753 unique records, 92
of which name a John of a Gurney variant**; supplementary undated runs were used to reach the
complete E 115, REQ 2 and STAC blocks, and eleven keyword-paired runs tested specific
propositions.[^sweep] Every description quoted below is in the corpus extract.[^extract]

## 1. The certificate-of-residence class is now swept, and it is thin

Nine Gurney-variant certificates of residence survive in E 115 across the whole class, unbounded by
date. Seven name a Thomas — four a Thomas Gourney/Gorney moving within London 1601–1605, two a
Thomas Garneys in Norfolk 1607–1609, one Sir Thomas Gourney in Essex 1627–28. **Two name a John,
both dated 1641, and both record liability in Northamptonshire.**[^e115]

The second of them is new. **E 115/175/147** certifies a John Gourney liable in "Northton" in 1641;
its pouch provenance is lost, where E 115/180/113 retains its old pouch number 2708. Whether these
are two certificates for one man or two men is not determinable from the catalogue — a taxpayer
could be certified more than once, and a certificate names one liability event, not a person's
history. Either reading supports the elimination of A(i): one man certified twice, or two John
Gurneys taxed in Northamptonshire in 1641, and in neither case is the man in Massachusetts.

The class-level negatives are the more useful result, because they are now properly bounded rather
than untested:

- **No certificate of residence individuates any other John Gurney in England, in any county, at any
  date.** The hope that this class might individuate a second candidate the way it individuated
  A(i) is closed.
- **No certificate survives for Francis Gurney**, in either direction between Norfolk and London,
  across a career that demonstrably moved between the two. That is a caution about the class's
  survival rate as much as a fact about Francis.

## 2. East Dereham had its own propertied Gurney family, and the Chancery court names it

This is the finding with the sharpest bearing on Candidate B, and it arrives from a direction that
routes around a document the repo has been blocked on.

The repo has long held that a **Margery Gurnye, widow, of East Dereham proved a will in 1602–03** in
the Archdeaconry of Norfolk, that her Ellingfield copyhold was still being recited as *"terram nuper
Margerie Gurney vid[ue]"* in the manor court of 3 April 1627, and that her will is "the test" for
whether that household connects to Francis — a test that cannot be run, because the register film is
affiliate-restricted and the NRO's digitised will images begin at 1800.[^margery]

**The Chancery pleadings supply what the will was wanted for.** In a suit between 1558 and 1603,
*Gourney v Gourney*, the plaintiff is **Arthur Gourney** and the defendant **Margery Gourney, widow
of Michael Gourney deceased**. The subject is a claim under Michael's will: father and son had been
**jointly seised of certain tenements in East Dereham, Norfolk**, the son consented to his father
alienating them, and the father in consideration bequeathed him a legacy of **£100**.[^c2-dereham]

The identification with the 1602–03 testatrix is as close as an identification of this kind gets —
same parish, same forename, same status, and a suit necessarily brought while she was living and
therefore before the will was proved. On that reading the East Dereham Gurney household of the
generation before Francis is now named: **Michael Gurney, dead by about 1602; Margery his widow; and
Arthur his son, an adult with property of his own and a £100 legacy.** Joint seisin is freehold
language, and £100 in the 1590s is not a cottager's legacy — this was a family of some substance,
holding freehold in the parish as well as the Ellingfield copyhold the manor court remembered.
Arthur is very probably the **Arthur Gorney** sued in the Court of Requests by Sir Edward Clere over
rent out of the Norfolk manors of Vauxhall and Stalham Hall, which would put him well above
husbandman level.[^req-clere]

**Where this cuts.** The repo currently uses Margery in one direction only: as the answer to "why
East Dereham?", a pre-existing kin presence explaining why Francis of West Barsham settled his young
family five miles from the Great Ellingham seat.[^why-dereham] But the fact is symmetric, and the
new detail makes the symmetry harder to ignore. A parish with its own propertied Gurney family,
producing adult sons around 1600, is at least as good an explanation for a "ffrancis Gurney"
baptising children there in 1610 and 1618 as it is a reason for a gentleman from West Barsham to
move in. The deviation audit raised exactly this question and it has never been tested: nothing
positively places **Francis-A** at East Dereham except the East Dereham children themselves, which
is the proposition at issue.[^d10]

Reading a fact only in the direction that supports the hypothesis is the specific failure the audit
diagnosed. Stated neutrally: **the Michael–Margery–Arthur household raises the prior on the local
reading, and the local reading, if it holds, collapses Candidate B outright.** It does not establish
it. No Francis is named in either suit.

**And it converts an untestable question into a cheap one.** Arthur Gourney was an adult by about
1600 and his children would have been baptised at East Dereham in exactly the years PD 86/41
survives. A **Michael Gurney burial** would fall in the 1593–1601 returns, which also survive. The
July 2026 crawl line-verified the 1609 and 1610 christenings and found no Gurney but Edward, and the
1616 tail with none — but the pre-1608 membranes were read at **triage depth only** and the
1616–1618 christenings were not line-read.[^crawl] The masters are already held locally. A
line-read of the pre-1608 returns for Michael, Margery or Arthur is the test, and it can be run
today without a single new pull.

A mild continuity signal in the same direction: the East Dereham copyhold chain later runs through a
**Matthew/Michael Gurney** tenant, recited in an 1758 entry as "late in the Tenure of Michael
Gurney".[^copyhold] The forename at the head of the family c.1600 recurs at the other end of the
parish's Gurney record.

## 3. A previously unknown John Gurney holding property in Aylesbury parish, 1621–25

**C 3/352/66**, *Gourney v Playstow*: John Gourney, plaintiff, against Thomas Playstow and Anne his
wife, over **property in Stoke Mandeville, Aylesbury and Walton, Buckinghamshire**, pleaded between
1621 and 1625.[^playstow]

Walton is a hamlet of Aylesbury and Stoke Mandeville adjoins it. This is a John Gurney with a
property interest at the centre of the Aylesbury half-hundred in the decade before the emigration
window, litigating in Chancery — which means he was solvent enough to sue there — and he corresponds
to no man the case file has ever named. He belongs in the "documented but never assembled" category
rather than in the candidate list, and he is exactly what that category predicts should keep turning
up.

He may well be the **John Gurney the younger of Owlswick in Monks Risborough**, married to Alice
Hewett in 1619 under a settlement that gave him a messuage and arable — Owlswick is about five miles
from Stoke Mandeville, and the 1623 Monks Risborough probate would then close him.[^owlswick] That
is a suggestion, not an identification. The document settles it: a Chancery bill states the
plaintiff's parish, status and usually his descent, and this one is item-described and orderable.

## 4. The Aylesbury Vale Gurneys were Pakington tenants, and their deeds are in Worcestershire

The single most useful structural discovery of the sweep is not a person but an archive. The
**Hampton (Pakington) of Westwood Park, Droitwich** collection at Worcestershire Archive and
Archaeology Service holds a Buckinghamshire property series arranged by parish — *AYLESBURY AND
DISTRICT*, with sub-series for Aylesbury and for Weston Turville — and it carries **twenty-three
Gurney deeds running from 1399 to 1663**.[^pakington]

That is why the Buckinghamshire Archives sweep could not see them: the muniments of an Aylesbury
Vale estate followed the estate's owners to Worcestershire. A county's records are not all in that
county, and the Bucks catalogue's own coverage note — that it indexes abstracts rather than document
text — was never the only limit on that pass.

Three things fall out of it.

**A resident Gurney household at Broughton in Bierton, a generation before Candidate A.** On 17
November 1592 Thomas Keene of Bedgrove assigned to **John Gurneye of Broughton, husbandman**, a lease
of four acres of meadow in **Weston Turville**; on 2 February 1598/9 **John Gourney of Broughton in
the parish of Bierton, husbandman**, assigned that meadow to Sir John Pakington.[^broughton] This is
the first document to place a Gurney household at Broughton in Bierton at all, and it does so with a
named man, a parish, a status and a trade.

It weakens the Stewkley→Bierton merge further. The case file already treats the 1603 Stewkley baptism
as joined to the 1628 Bierton marriage only by inference — "same hundred, plausible age at marriage,
and no competing trail".[^merge] There is now a competing trail: Bierton with Broughton had its own
resident Gurney husbandmen a generation earlier, and a groom marrying there in 1628 belongs more
naturally to them than to a household five miles off at Stewkley. The 1603 Stewkley baby becomes
*more* unaccounted-for, not less.

**It also connects the two Buckinghamshire "unaccounted-for" survivors that looked independent.**
The Weston Turville head — daughter Elyzabethe baptised 1627, the closest age fit in the county — has
been carried as a distinct south-of-Aylesbury household. The 1592 and 1598/9 leases show a Bierton
Gurney holding Weston Turville meadow, and a Chancery suit of 1558–79, *Gourney v Pakington*, has
**Walter Gourney** suing Dame Dorothy Pakington over **copyhold held of the manor of
Broughton**.[^walter] Weston Turville and Broughton were worked by the same Gurney tenantry on the
same estate. That does not eliminate the Weston Turville head, but it argues he is part of the
Aylesbury-hundred cluster rather than a separate household, which lowers rather than raises him.

**Luke Gurney the Aylesbury tailor was a substantial townsman.** The Buckinghamshire tailoring
cluster rests on probate-index rows.[^brs] The Pakington deeds show **Luke Gurney of Aylesbury**
granting a parcel of land directly to Sir Thomas Pakington and taking a parcel of meadow back from
him on the same day in 1561, with a memorandum about the Fryars Ponde lying between their lands, and
levying a fine with **Margaret his wife** on a messuage, garden and lands in Aylesbury in 1566. The
Gurneys are burgesses of Aylesbury from 1399 — Reginald, Thomas, Robert, Richard, William, John —
holding gardens, curtilages and messuages in Green End across the fifteenth century. The county's
Gurney tailoring trade sits inside an old and propertied urban family, which strengthens the point
that a Gurney tailor is not a Norfolk-and-London signature.

## 5. Three open actions answered or narrowed from the catalogue

**The Marsworth estate deeds name no John.** The standing action asked whether the early deeds of the
D-X_202 Gurney estate at Marsworth name a John Gurney.[^r16] They do not. Every pre-1660 item is
catalogued at item level with parties named: Thomas Gurney of Marsworth dead by February 1615/6, his
son and heir **Henry**; **Elizabeth** Gurneye spinster conveying to **Richard** her brother in
1616/7; then Richard the elder and Richard the younger through 1650–1660. No John Gurney appears in
any pre-1660 item. That part of the action is closed without a repository visit.

**The Bishopstone Gurneys are older and higher than the deed sweep showed.** The liquidating
Bishopstone household — the structural twin of Candidate B's motive argument — was reconstructed from
Bucks Archives back to Stephen the elder holding the site of the manor of Stone from 1573.[^r17] The
central courts add a decade and a great deal of standing: **Stephen Gurney of Bishopstone in Stone**
is sued in the Court of Requests by **Sir Robert Dormer** in 1598 over an arbitrator's award touching
land in Buckland, sued in Requests in 1574 over land in Dinton, sued twice more over bonds and money,
and — most tellingly — is defendant in a **Star Chamber** suit of 1573–74 brought by **John Weston of
Bishopstone in the parish of Stone, day labourer**. A day labourer taking his own parish's principal
Gurney to Star Chamber is a picture of a man with local power. A separate Requests bill records a
bond for debt owed to "one Stephen Gourney **of Aylesbury**", so the family was operating in the town
as well as on the manor.

**Bedfordshire is an unswept adjacent county.** The repo's Bedfordshire coverage is Toddington,
Lidlington and Houghton Regis — parishes reached because a Gurney happened to be indexed there. The
sweep shows a Gurney landholding presence at **Hockliffe** from 1590, when **Robert Gurney** sued
William Saunders in the Court of Requests over lands there, continuing to a **John Gurney of
Hockliffe, butcher**, examined at length in the Bedfordshire assize files 1671–81 with a son William,
and a **John Gurney** serving as constable of Toddington in 1681.[^beds] Hockliffe sits about five
miles from Cheddington and Edlesborough: the Aylesbury Vale cluster runs across the county boundary,
and the Bedfordshire side of it has never been worked. This does not produce a candidate. It
identifies a gap of the same shape as the Buckinghamshire probate tier before it was read.

## 6. What the sweep did not produce, stated as coverage

These are absences from catalogued descriptions across the classes named in §Coverage, which is a
real but partial net.

- **No Gurney of the tailoring trade** is named in any description 1580–1670. The colonial John's own
  trade cannot be matched to an English record through this route.
- **No John Gurney with a wife Mary** appears anywhere in England 1590–1685.
- **No record names a John Gurney as son of a Francis Gurney**, in any court or archive. Candidate B
  still has no document naming the man it is about, and this was the largest single class in which
  one might have surfaced.
- **None of the five unaccounted-for Buckinghamshire men appears in any of these classes** — not the
  Stewkley baptisms of 1602/3 and 1611, not the Haddenham, Weston Turville or Hitcham heads, not the
  John baptised at Cheddington in 1608. They are documented once each in a register and nowhere else,
  which is what "unaccounted for" has meant all along; the sweep has now tested the richest remaining
  class and left it unchanged.

## Where this leaves the picture

Small, honest movements rather than a reversal.

The **"documented but never assembled" category is confirmed to be real and productive** — the sweep
produced a new propertied John Gurney at the centre of Aylesbury parish in 1621–25 and a new Gurney
household at Broughton in Bierton in the 1590s, neither previously known, from one afternoon in a
free catalogue. That category earns its rank rather than merely holding it.

**Candidate B is unchanged on the evidence and slightly worse on the balance.** No document naming a
John son of Francis surfaced in the largest untested class; and the East Dereham household that has
been read as a reason for Francis to settle there turns out to be a propertied local family with
adult sons, which is equally an alternative source for the man baptising children in that parish.
The one thing that could still move it sharply — proving or disproving that the East Dereham
"ffrancis Gurney" is Francis-A — is now testable against membranes already held.

**A(i)'s elimination is a little firmer**, with a second 1641 certificate in the same class and
county, and the certificate class itself is closed as a source of further individuation.

**The Buckinghamshire survivors are not helped.** Weston Turville is drawn into the Aylesbury-hundred
tenantry rather than standing apart from it; the Stewkley 1603 baby loses the household he was
merged into, without gaining one of his own.

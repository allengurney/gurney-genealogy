# Weymouth came first — and the West Country corridor, swept for the first time

## Why Weymouth outranks Braintree for origin work

John Gurney's Braintree associations have carried the network argument: the Tyng leasehold, the Shed
marriage, the town's Essex name. But **Braintree is where he ended up, not where he landed.** His
first colonial record — the petition of 30 May 1641 and the General Court's remission of 2 June —
places him at **Weymouth**, and every Braintree tie post-dates it by two to twenty years.

That first record also does something no Braintree record does: **it names the men he was with** —
"John Gurny, Richard Porter, and James Ludden of Weymouth."[^petition] Men named beside him in a
petition are a better class of association evidence than **land-neighbours**, who were acquired after
arrival by where a parcel happened to lie. The file has profiled the neighbours thoroughly and never
worked the petition group for origin.

## James Ludden's origin: two compiled claims, pointing at different counties

> **CORRECTION, 2026-07-29.** An earlier version of this unit reported that Ludden's origin
> "resolves to a Norfolk parish on record evidence." **That is withdrawn.** It was a convergence
> between an unsourced FamilySearch tree and a Ludham household I found at the parish that tree
> names — and the better-grounded secondary account puts him in Dorset, not Norfolk.

**Claim A — Norfolk.** A FamilySearch Family Tree profile gives James Ludden, 1608–1692, with English
origins including **Little Plumstead, Norfolk**. A user-submitted tree, no source.[^leadonly]

**Claim B — Dorset.** William B. Scarpelli's research paper for the town of Hanover, Massachusetts
(May 2022) states plainly: *"Born in 1611 in Dorsetshire, England, he arrived at Wessagusset with the
Gorges company as an eleven-year-old, likely as a servant to Bursley and/or Jeffries."*[^scarpelli]

**Neither claim is sourced.** I checked. Scarpelli's footnotes 6 and 7, which bracket that sentence,
cover the settlement's alternative name and the two marriages respectively — **not the birth**. The
Dorsetshire birth carries no note at all. Its likely origin is the title of the compiled family
history the paper cites for its frontispiece: **Wallace Ludden, *James Ludden, the Old Planter,
1611–1692, and Descendants* (1971)** — which supplies the 1611 date but is itself a twentieth-century
compilation.[^scarpelli]

**So what I did was match an unsourced claim to a record and call it corroboration.** The Ludham
household I found baptising at **Little Plumstead, Norfolk in 1608 and 1610** is real, and it remains
a real Norfolk Ludham household; what is not established is any connection between it and the man at
Weymouth. **The Norfolk reading is withdrawn.**[^ludham]

## What *is* documented about Ludden points West Country

Set the unsourced birth claims aside and the contemporary record still leans one way:

- He arrived at Wessagusset in **September 1623 with the Robert Gorges company**, on the *Katherine*
  and *Prophet Daniel* — a **Council for New England** venture. Sir Ferdinando Gorges was a Somerset
  man and governor of the fort at Plymouth, Devon; the enterprise was West Country.[^scarpelli]
- His later wife **Alice Kinham** sailed with **Rev. Joseph Hull's company from "Waymouth" —
  Weymouth, Dorset — on 30 March 1635**, listed as "aged 22 yeare" in Nash's appendix.[^scarpelli]
- **Weymouth, Massachusetts is named for Weymouth, Dorset**, and its founding company came from
  Somerset and Dorset.

None of that is evidence about *Ludden's birthplace*, but it is evidence about the community John
Gurney landed in. **The net effect on the corridor question is that Ludden gives no support to
Norfolk**, which is the opposite of what the earlier version of this unit concluded.

## The consequence worth having: the West Country was never swept

The repo established years ago that Weymouth's founding stream was "dominantly WEST COUNTRY … a
Norfolk John is outsider to BOTH" streams. But that was an argument about *settlers*. **Nobody had
ever asked whether Gurney households existed in Dorset or Somerset** — and Somerset carries an ancient
Gournay presence in its place-names (Farrington Gurney, East Harptree), so the question is not idle.

Swept now, for the first time:[^westcountry]

| County | Gurney-variant baptisms, 1610–1650 | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Somerset** | **2** | Gurnett William 1626, Clapton in Gordano; Gurnsoy Rebeca 1630, Knowle St Giles |
| **Dorset** | **2** | Gurnard Elizabeth 1625 and Gurnard Jane 1623, **both at Owermoigne** |

**Owermoigne lies about seven miles from Weymouth, Dorset** — the port that named John's first
colonial town and launched its founding company — and under the corrected variant weighting *Gurnard*
is a moderate-to-high Gurney form, not a look-alike. That made it worth drilling. The household reads:

> **Owermoigne, Dorset — Gurnard.** Elizabeth 1574 (father Martin), George 1576 (Martine), Martin 1582,
> Brigit 1585, Joane 1589, Armenell 1596 (all father Nicholas), Ursula 1592 (mother Luce), **Jane 1623
> and Elizabeth 1625 (father Robert)**.[^owermoigne]

Fathers Martin, Nicholas, Robert across fifty years. **No John, and no colonial-name child in
window.** The shape does not match at any corner.

**The West Country corridor is therefore swept and empty of shape-matches** — two households in two
counties, neither with a father John. That is a real result: it removes a corridor that the Weymouth
reception evidence made a live possibility, and it removes it on record rather than on argument.

## What remains open

- **Richard Porter's English origin** is still the better test of the 1641 group, because the Hull
  company is predominantly Dorset and Somerset and Porter is a documented Hull-company settler. If
  Porter is West Country too, the 1641 group is regionally coherent and tells us about Weymouth rather
  than about John.
- **James Ludham's own baptism** would settle Ludden either way; it does not surface under `ludd*`,
  `luddam*` or `ludham` with a forename James anywhere in England 1588–1628.[^luddnegative]
- Neither is high priority. The petition group is reception evidence, and reception evidence has
  repeatedly proved a weak lever on origin in this file.

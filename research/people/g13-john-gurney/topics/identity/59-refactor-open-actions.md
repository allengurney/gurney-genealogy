## Notes on the offline set

**Buckinghamshire apprenticeship records are a structural absence, not an unrun search.** The county had no incorporated livery companies, so there is no equivalent of the Merchant Taylors' binding and freedom books or the Norwich freemen and enrolled-apprentice registers. The national Stamp Duty apprenticeship registers (TNA IR 1), from which FindMyPast's country-apprentice set is drawn, begin in **1710** — about eighty years too late. Pre-1710 evidence survives only in borough court and freemen books for Buckingham, Chipping Wycombe and Aylesbury, in Quarter Sessions pauper-apprenticeship indentures, and incidentally in wills, none of it name-indexed online. This is a different kind of blank from the London and Norwich negatives, which were searches against complete registers, and it should not be reported as a negative result.

R-00, R-01, R-04, R-05, R-06, R-14, R-19, R-20 and R-21 are the ones confidently established as not
workable online. They fall at four repositories — Buckinghamshire Archives (R-00, R-01), the London
Archives (R-05), the Norfolk Record Office (R-06) and The National Archives at Kew (R-19, R-20,
R-21) — plus one national-archive class not in any online index (R-04) and one recruit-and-test
action (R-14). If a single offline commission is to be made, **R-01 is still the highest-value**:
three wills at one repository, and wills name children. **R-19 is the strongest Kew commission**, and
R-20 and R-21 sit in the same department, so one visit covers all three.

**Catalogue-level work now exhausted for the central courts.** The Discovery sweep of 28 July 2026
covered fourteen surname variants across Chancery, Requests, Star Chamber, Exchequer certificates and
depositions, Court of Wards deeds, PCC wills, and every contributed county catalogue in the service.
The productive residue is document-level (R-19 to R-21) and county-level (R-22), not further
catalogue searching.

# Family — Mendon: John Jr. and Ruth Bundy

**Two of John Gurney's sons carried the family into the opening violence of King Philip's War. John Jr. had settled at Mendon by the early 1670s with his wife Ruth and their children John, Samuel, and Mary; he was among the dead when Nipmuc fighters struck the town on 14 July 1675, and Ruth remarried within six months. Peter, a soldier in Captain Isaac Johnson's company, survived the Great Swamp Fight that December and was killed later in the war, in 1676.** A separate, once-confused thread is now resolved: the 1675 Mendon casualty is John Jr., not the identically-named eldest son of his brother Richard (G12), who was still living in 1691. And the post-1667 Mendon proprietors' records preserve two Gurney lot-holders working through the same generation — John Jr. in his own right, and his father's widow, Grisel, in hers.

## The Mendon household: John Jr. and Ruth

John Gurney Jr. — old enough by 1661 to witness his father's Braintree deed — had moved on to the newer inland plantation of Mendon by the early 1670s.[^john-jr-witness] The clearest primary trace of his household there is a birth entry in the Mendon town book: "Samuel son of John & Ruth Gurney — born March 14, 1671," fixing his wife's name and giving the household a third child alongside John and Mary.[^mendon-vitals] Torrey's marriage compendium independently carries the same household at compendium level, dating the marriage to about 1671/2 and naming all three children.[^torrey-mendon]

John did not long survive the birth of that son. He was among the dead when Nipmuc forces struck Mendon on 14 July 1675, the opening bloodshed of King Philip's War.[^rigler-sprague-mendon] His widow Ruth remarried quickly, as was common for a woman left with young children on an exposed frontier: the Plymouth Colony court's printed record of town returns carries the Taunton entry verbatim — "John Bundey married to Ruth **Surney**, of Mendum, this 9th of January, 1676" — and the Taunton town's own vital records repeat the marriage under two more spellings, "Ruth **Garney** [Gurney] of Mendun," with an explicit editorial cross-reference tying both forms to "Ruth Gurney."[^pcr-bundy][^taunton-vr-bundy] Charles Henry Pope's 1900 compilation of Massachusetts founders records the same marriage under the bride's name "**Jane** Gurney of Mendon" — an error: no primary record anywhere gives Ruth's household a Jane, and the two Plymouth-area sources that do survive, independently phrased, both give **Ruth**.[^pope-bundy]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000088 -->

## Which John died at Mendon

For a time the 1675 Mendon casualty could be read as either of two identically-named first cousins — John Jr., son of the emigrant John Gurney-1, or his own nephew John, eldest son of Richard Gurney (G12) of Weymouth. The question is now settled, and by a record outside this household entirely: on 27 October 1691 the Suffolk County Court at Boston granted administration of Richard Gurney's estate to two of his sons, "Zechoriah Gurney & Richard Gurney, ... John Gurney the Eldest son refusing to administer." Richard's own son John was therefore alive sixteen years after the Mendon massacre and simply declined the duty of administrator — he cannot be the 1675 casualty. The dead man at Mendon was John Jr., G13's own son, and the Mendon town book's John-and-Ruth household is his.[^richard-admon] The fuller transcription and consequences of that 1691 record for Richard's own line are on the [G12 companion](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/people/g12-richard-gurney-fact-sheet.research.md).<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000089 -->

## Two Gurney lots at Mendon, a generation apart

Mendon was not incorporated until 15 May 1667, four years after John Sr.'s death, so every entry in its own proprietors' records postdates him. Two Gurneys appear there in their own right, not his. **John Gurny** — matching John Jr., the actual Mendon resident — held a twenty-acre house lot from the town's earliest purchaser list; his lot, meadow, and swamp rights recur for decades afterward as a fixed boundary marker in his neighbors' land descriptions, and the lot itself was finally sold out of the family on 3 May 1692, to Josiah Thayer.[^mendon-john] **Grisel Gurney** — John Sr.'s widow, remembered elsewhere by her maiden name Fletcher and her earlier husbands Jewell, Griggs, and Kibby — held a separate twenty-acre lot in her own name, and a copy of her will, returned to the town through guardians in Chelmsford, shows she had by then become "the Abouesd John Burge['s]" wife: she left her Mendon accommodation to her son Joseph Juell, with payments due to Nathanil Juell, Mercy Juell, and Sherabiah Keby.[^mendon-grisel] The record therefore preserves the same two lines the family's own history already established — the son who actually lived and died at Mendon, and the widow who outlived her Braintree husband and carried her own Mendon holding into a fifth marriage and a stepson's inheritance.[^wives-marriages-grizzell]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000090 -->

## Peter's war

John's youngest documented son, Peter, was a soldier rather than a settler in this generation's encounter with the war. George Madison Bodge's roll of King Philip's War soldiers lists "Peeter Gurnay" under Weymouth in Captain Isaac Johnson's company, mustered at Dedham on 10 December 1675 for the Narragansett campaign — the same company in which Johnson himself was killed leading his men across the log bridge into the Narragansett fort on 19 December 1675.[^bodge-peter] Peter is absent from that company's list of the Fort Fight's December 1675 dead and wounded, so he survived it; he was killed later in the war, in 1676, the same year Ruth remarried out of Mendon.[^rigler-sprague-mendon]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000091 -->

## Samuel's likely Boston marriage, 1693

A Samuel Gurney married Sarah Shapley at Boston on 26 October 1693 — the only Gurney of any generation anywhere in the printed Boston vital records, thirty years after John Sr.'s death.[^boston-samuel-shapley] No record names this Samuel's father, but the Mendon household's own third child was a Samuel, born 14 March 1671, who would have been twenty-two in 1693 — an ordinary marrying age — and no other Samuel Gurney appears anywhere in the surviving colonial-era record to compete for the identification. The match is chronologically clean and, on the printed record alone, unopposed, but it rests on age and the absence of a rival rather than any document naming the connection outright, so it stays a probable, not a proven, identification of this household's own son.[^boston-samuel-shapley]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000118 -->

## Two open threads

Two leads point past this household without yet reaching it. The 1699 will of John Lewis of Nevis, a merchant, proved in 1701, names "Mary Gurney the daughter of John Gurney" among its legatees; the register image confirms the wording stands alone, with no residence or kinship qualifier attached to Mary.[^nevis-mary][^lewis-will-image] The will's social circle can now be placed. Its named kinswoman, "Grisell Lloyd daughter of James Lloyd" — left ten pounds in the clause immediately before the Mary Gurney legacy — was the daughter of James Lloyd, a merchant of Boston and grandson of Sir John Lloyd, first baronet; she was born in Jamaica about 1685 and married John Eastwick in 1703. The will's sole executor, Henry Lloyd, merchant of Bristol, was an older kinsman of the same trading family, not the younger Boston Henry.[^lloyd-boston] That puts the whole legatee circle in the Boston–Shelter Island–Nevis–Bristol merchant elite, exactly the milieu in which a New-England-born granddaughter of John Gurney would plausibly appear. A legatee's father need not be living, so John Jr.'s 1675 death is no obstacle to Mary being his daughter; and on this side no later marriage or death is recorded for the Mendon Mary — John Jr. and Ruth's daughter leaves the town book as a child with no further trace — so no surviving New England record cuts against the reading. But a plausible milieu is not a named connection: the will ties its Mary to no residence or kin, and a West Indies Mary Gurney of unrelated origin cannot be excluded. The identification of the will's Mary with John Jr.'s daughter is therefore a plausible but unproven lead — on the order of an even chance, roughly 45–55 percent, not a finding.[^lloyd-boston] Separately, Taunton's own marriage records carry a Timothy Cooper marrying an Elizabeth Gurney there on 16 October 1706, three decades after Ruth's household arrived in the town as the Bundy family — an unplaced possible child or grandchild of John Jr. and Ruth, carried to Taunton in Ruth's remarried household, but not yet connected to it by any record.[^taunton-cooper]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000092 --><!-- lead: L-206 (open) — identification of the Nevis-will Mary Gurney with John Jr.'s daughter; graph open_question G13-RI-000135 -->

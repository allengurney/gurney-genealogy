# Research-state open questions

**After the colonial record was worked to exhaustion at the printed and indexed level, the open research program for John Gurney reduces to a small set of standing questions rather than a long to-do list: his English origin and parentage remain unidentified, his first wife Mary's identity and English marriage are unrecovered, and a direct-paternal-line Y-DNA discriminator that could test deep Gurney paternity independent of record survival is defined but untested.** The concrete record-location residuals — the unread emigrant-record pulls, the primary page behind the 1645 Braintree petition, the post-death Mendon proprietary stream — already sit in their own topics; what remains here is the shape of the open program and its reconciliation with the leads catalogue, which stays the canonical index of every outstanding pull.

This is a research-state map, not new evidence. Each question below states what is unresolved and why, and points to the topic or case file that holds the fuller treatment; the operational record-pulls that would test them live in the leads catalogue, grouped at the end of this note by the question they bear on. Candidate disambiguation — which specific English John Gurney is the emigrant — is the dedicated identity/origin work and is deliberately neither resolved nor advocated here.

## The origin and parentage question

The central unresolved question is who John Gurney was before he first appears in Massachusetts in 1641 — where in England he was born, who his parents were, and why he emigrated. No surviving record states his origin: the colony, town, court, and probate records that document his American life are uniformly silent on it, and Anderson's *Great Migration Directory*, having reviewed that surviving set, records the origin as "Unknown."[^anderson] The traditional Bury St Edmunds attribution finds no supporting natal household in the Bury registers and is best read as its author having tracked a Suffolk apprenticeship rather than a birth — the 1636 John Newgate apprentice, a distinct and younger man since de-conflated from the older Braintree John.[^banks] Every identified same-name English candidate has been eliminated or reduced to low probability, leaving a leading working identification and a residual distribution rather than a proof; that candidate weighing, and the full elimination record, are the substance of the [case file](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v5.md) and the dedicated identity work, and the documentary silence itself is set out in [06-record-coverage.md](../colonial/06-record-coverage.md) — neither is restated here. The question stays open because it turns on records that either do not survive or have not yet been reached, not on unweighed evidence.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000033 -->

## The first wife: Mary's identity and English marriage

John's first wife was named Mary, died in 1661, and her maiden name is unrecovered; the "Richards" attribution carried in derivative online trees is unverified and unsourced. The marriage itself is bounded to England before 1628 — their daughter Mary married Daniel Shed at Braintree in 1647 and, at a minimum marriage age, was born by 1631, with the derivative tradition placing her birth about 1628 in England — yet no John Gurney × Mary marriage is indexed in the eastern-England parish collections for 1620–1635 outside the already-eliminated Eythorne, Kent event; the absence reads as a parish-register coverage gap on the English side, not as an out-of-England or absent marriage.[^mary] Her identity carries a specific interpretive payload: a Peter among her kin would explain the otherwise-anomalous naming of John's son Peter, the one child whose name has no obvious source in the known Gurney and Rybett lines. The fuller marriage-search matrix and the child-naming analysis live in the [case file](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v5.md) §6.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000034 -->

## The untested Y-DNA discriminator

A direct-paternal-line (Y-DNA) comparison is the one evidence class that could test deep Gurney paternity independent of record survival, and the test is defined but unrun. The FamilyTreeDNA Gurney surname project's Big Y-700 tester for the Braintree line — whose earliest known ancestor is given as the emigrant's own dates — places that line at terminal SNP R-FTD83678, within R1b-M269 › P312 › DF19 › R-Z27053. On the public Discover data, R-Z27053 is a small, continental-leaning north-west-European branch: its handful of testers self-report UK, Belgian, and Flemish-Walloon affiliations, a signal compatible with — but far too sparse and too deep, being pre-surname, to prove — the Norman/continental deep origin the de Gournay tradition would predict. The discriminating comparison is whether a *documented English-line* Gurney shares the DF19 / R-Z27053 branch: it turns on the project's other R-M269 Gurney tester and, beyond that, on recruiting and testing a documented English Gurney line. A match would make the American family's surname-level descent from an English Gurney lineage near-certain (though not the specific household); a mismatch would settle nothing.[^ydna] It sits open because it is a recruit-and-test project, not a records search.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000035 -->

Three residuals already have graph homes and are not repeated here: the unread emigrant-record pulls — Coldham's *Complete Book of Emigrants, 1607–1660* and the 1635 London port register (TNA E 157/20) — in [40-source-coverage.md](40-source-coverage.md); the primary page behind John's May 1645 Braintree plantation petition, still known only through a secondary source, in [03-braintree-community.md](../colonial/03-braintree-community.md); and the post-death Mendon proprietary stream that postdates his 1662/3 death, in [04-frontier-rights.md](../colonial/04-frontier-rights.md).

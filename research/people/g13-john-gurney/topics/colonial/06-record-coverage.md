# Colonial record coverage

**John Gurney's surviving documentary footprint is dense in one register and silent in another. Across the twenty-two years from his first secure colony record (the 30 May / 2 June 1641 Weymouth gunpowder matter) to his 1663 Braintree estate inventory he is named in colony court and account records, the town land books of four towns, a deed, a court deposition, and two probate files — a broad civic, land, court, and probate trail. Yet no record fixes his own person by a vital or church entry: his birth is known only by inference from a single deposition age, his death only from his estate, and his origin, parentage, and prior residence are stated by no record at all. Anderson's *Great Migration Directory*, working the same surviving set, records his English origin simply as "Unknown" — and assigns him a Boston settlement that no Boston record supports: an exhaustive sweep of every surviving Boston-era record class, church, town, notarial, deed, and vital, returns not one Gurney of his generation.**

## A dense trail in four record classes

The records that name John Gurney cluster in four documentary classes, and together they run continuously from 1641 to his death. The colony's own records open the trail and close a chapter of it: the Massachusetts General Court order of 2 June 1641 remitting the Weymouth gunpowder fine is his earliest secure appearance, and the 30 October 1644 colony-accounts entry accepting his and Thomas Lake's bills as the £5 due from the former minister Rev. Thomas Jenner is a second.[^gunpowder][^jenner] Town land books carry him across four towns — the Weymouth East Field, Mill Field, and Second-Division grants; the Braintree community records; the Billerica town book of 1659; and the Mendon allotment stream — each treated in its own unit.[^land] A registry deed records his 12 February 1661/2 conveyance of the Monatiquot freehold to Richard Thayer, signed not with a signature but with his mark.[^deed] A Suffolk Court Files deposition of 1653 preserves his testimony in *Wilson v. Faxon*.[^deposition] And Suffolk probate holds him twice: as witness and appraiser of Gregory Baxter's 1659 estate, a mark of community trust, and — the last record of him — as the subject of his own inventory taken 16 March 1663.[^baxter][^probate] Anderson's *Great Migration Directory* independently confirms the shape of this footprint: its John Gurney entry rests on six cited records (Winthrop's *Journal* 2:422; Massachusetts Bay Records 1:331; the *Register* 62:94 deposition; Suffolk Probate Case #338; the *History of Weymouth* 3:251; and *The American Genealogist* 10:70–73), assigns an arrival of 1636 and settlements at Boston and Braintree, and — notably for a man the town land books place firmly at Weymouth — omits the Weymouth residential phase the project's manuscript land work recovered.[^anderson][^land]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000018 -->

## The one record of the man himself

For all its breadth, the record almost never speaks of John Gurney the person. Every other document fixes his property, his credit, or a civic act: a fine, a land grant, a debt, a deed, a probate role, an estate. The single exception is the 1653 *Wilson v. Faxon* deposition, which describes "John Gurney of Brayntree aged 50 Yeares or thereabouts" — the only surviving record to state a personal attribute of the man himself rather than of his holdings or offices.[^deposition] That lone datum is why John's own birth can be estimated at all; the weight of the stated age, and the birth band it implies, are worked in the origin/age analysis, not here. The point for record coverage is narrower and striking: a man documented in four record classes over two decades is attested as a *person* — as opposed to as a landholder, creditor, or estate — by exactly one line in one court file.[^deposition]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000019 -->

## Silent registers: no vital or church entry

The registers that would fix John Gurney most precisely are the ones that hold no entry for him. No New England vital record preserves his birth or baptism, his first marriage to Mary, or his death or burial; his birth survives only as an inference from the 1653 deposition age, and his death only as the terminus his 16 March 1663 estate inventory implies (he was alive to convey land in February 1661/2 and dead by the March 1663 appraisal).[^deposition][^deed][^probate] Nor does he appear on a church-admission or freeman record — the standard markers of full standing for a Great Migration man. The full run of the printed Massachusetts Bay colony records bears this out at first hand: across all five volumes John Gurney is named exactly twice — the 1641 fine remission and the 1644 bill — and never among the admitted freemen, the silence Anderson's full-dress directory review independently reflects in recording no such record among the six it cites.[^freemansweep][^anderson] Two limits bound this negative. The one Braintree vital line that matches John's traditional 1661 remarriage and his second wife's 1661 death is printed in the 1886 town-record edition under the surname "John Cheny" / "Grizell Kidbee," not Gurney; whether the manuscript reads Gurney, whether the Cheny reading is correct, or whether later compilers merged two records is a distinct reading-and-identity question reserved for the identity work and is therefore counted here as neither a surviving nor an absent Gurney vital record.[^braintree1886] And absence in the reviewed record set is not proof no such entry ever existed — only that none survives among the records searched.[^anderson]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000020 -->

## No Boston: a settlement the record denies

Anderson's directory gives John two New England settlements, Boston and Braintree, but no Boston record of any class names him, and the attribution rests on none. Boston's church and civil record of John's decades survives well and has now been read to its edges. The *Records of the First Church in Boston* — the town's only church of the 1630s and 1640s, its admissions, votes, and baptisms running continuously from 1630 — contain no Gurney of any spelling, though John Newgate, the feltmaker in whose Boston household the 1636 apprentice John Gurney lived as a servant, was himself a member.[^firstchurch] The Book of Possessions and Boston town records, the Aspinwall notarial register of 1644–1651, Thomas Lechford's note-book of the exact arrival window 1638–1641, and the earliest Suffolk Deeds (Libers I–III, 1640–1660) are each equally silent; so are the Boston vital records 1630–1699, whose single Gurney entry is the 1693 marriage of Samuel Gurney, probably a grandson, thirty years after John's death.[^bostonclasses][^bostonvr] Manuscript-level searching adds nothing the print misses: a full-text search across the Massachusetts Bay colony-records films and the Massachusetts Archives colonial volumes surfaced no John Gurney document beyond the two already known, and the residual "Braintree meadowes" images resolve to the wills of James Penniman and Richard Brackett, neither naming a Gurney.[^ftssweep]

Together the sweep converts Anderson's "Boston" from an inference into a record-level negative: every surviving Boston record class of John's era has been read without a Gurney, so his documented life belongs wholly to his four towns, and the Boston attribution is best read as a conflation with the Newgate apprentice — who, as a household servant, would have generated neither a church admission nor a property record.[^bostonclose] The silence holds just past the Boston orbit. Adjacent Hingham, the one Norfolk-settled reception corridor that could bear on his English origin, yields no Gurney in its town records; and the early records of the town of Providence — the jurisdiction of the unlocated "Quinapaug" interest his 1663 estate carried — hold none either, bounding that frontier share as one the record never registered.[^hingham][^providence]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000111 -->

## An origin the record withholds

The deepest gap is the earliest: no record of John Gurney in New England states his English origin, his parentage, or his residence before migration. The colony, town, court, and probate records that document his American life are uniformly silent on where he came from, and Anderson's *Great Migration Directory*, having reviewed that surviving set, records his origin as "Unknown" — an implicit setting-aside of Banks's Bury St Edmunds attribution.[^anderson] This is a statement about the coverage of the record, not a verdict on the question: the English-origin identification — Banks's Suffolk lead and the Candidate-B reading that makes John a son of Francis Gurney the Merchant Taylor — is an open matter pursued in the dedicated origin/identity work, and is deliberately neither resolved nor advocated in this coverage account.[^anderson]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000021 -->

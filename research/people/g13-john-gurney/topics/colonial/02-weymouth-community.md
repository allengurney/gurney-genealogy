# Colonial Weymouth community

**John Gurney spent his first New England years — roughly 1638/9 to 1642/4 — as an established inhabitant of Weymouth, Massachusetts: an admitted townsman holding granted land, tied by a debt to the town's minister, and set among Weymouth's founding settlers, before he removed to Braintree.** The precise arrival window and the dating of his land are argued in the companion arrival-chronology unit; this unit treats what the same records show about his place in the town — inhabitant standing, a credit tie to Rev. Thomas Jenner, his documented Weymouth associates, and the thin limit of his militia footprint.

## An admitted inhabitant of Weymouth

John held Weymouth land by town grant, and in early Massachusetts the town divisions went only to admitted inhabitants — so his grants mark him as a recognised member of the Weymouth community, not a transient. The Weymouth proprietors' manuscript Land Grants book records him as the original grantee of three parcels — two East Field lots and one Mill Field lot, about eight acres in all, each entered as "first granted/given to John Gurny/Gurnie."[^msbook] Gilbert Nash's 1643 partial property record shows those same parcels already in other men's hands by 1643, which places John's grants and residence in the early 1640s and makes the town history's later doubt "whether he ever resided in Weymouth" an understatement.[^nash] The parcels themselves are documented place-side in the [East Field](../../../../../../research/places/east-field-weymouth-ma.md), [Mill Field](../../../../../../research/places/mill-field-weymouth-ma.md), and [Whitman's Pond great-lot](../../../../../../research/places/whitmans-pond-east-side-weymouth-ma.md) place records; his East and Mill Field neighbours formed an Aylesbury-Vale Buckinghamshire settler cluster whose reception bearing is set out below, and whose cohort and origin implications are worked out in the [immigration-by-association topic](../../../../../../research/topics/g13-john-gurney-immigration-by-association.md).[^nash]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000006 -->

## An early holding, not a late paper right

John's Weymouth parcels are ordinary settler accommodation — two-to-four-acre strips in the scattered open-field system that was already granting East Field land by 1635 — not marks of unusual standing. Consistent with that, he is **absent from Weymouth's 1636 Fresh Pond "great lot" roll**, the household-scaled allocation of six acres per adult that went to sixteen named householders (Edward Bennett, Joseph Hull, Thomas Jenner Sr., and thirteen others). His absence is evidence he was not yet a Weymouth householder as early as 1636 — a lower edge on his arrival that squares with the pre-1643-grant reading argued in the [arrival-chronology unit](01-arrival-chronology.md) — even though two of his later East Field abutters, Edmund Hart and Thomas Rawling, were already in that 1636 cohort.[^fresh-pond]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000026 -->

His one later Weymouth grant — great lot no. 16, dated 2 February 1651-52 — belongs to a different exercise again and is not evidence of residence. That list laid out rights "named in the old town Book and formerly granted," and it demonstrably carried absentees and dead men: Samuel Newman and John Allin had removed to Rehoboth in 1643-44, Thomas Rider to Boston, and William Brandon was dead by 1647. John's place on it therefore reflects a surviving proprietary entitlement, not residence. The closest parallel to his whole pattern is **John Read**, who likewise held early Weymouth land, took a William Tyng farm at Mount Wollaston, removed to Rehoboth, and still drew an 18-acre Fresh Pond great lot in 1651-52 — a Weymouth proprietary right carried alongside a Tyng tenancy and a move elsewhere, exactly the position John Gurney occupied after his own removal to Braintree.[^read-cohort]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000027 -->

## A neighbourhood of Aylesbury-Vale settlers

The men who held or bounded John's East and Mill Field parcels were not a random scatter but a coherent **Aylesbury-Vale Buckinghamshire settler cluster** — Robert Randall of Wendover (who came to hold John's East Field two-acre lot), William Brandon of Ashton Clinton (bounding his Mill Field parcel), and the Hunt and Humphrey families of the neighbouring parish of Lee, whose Buckinghamshire origins were sworn to in a 1688 Weymouth deposition in which Jonas Humphrey and Robert Randall, "both of Weymouth," deposed that they had lived at Wendover, Buckinghamshire.[^bucks-reception] That his grants sat inside an established regional cluster frames his early-1640s appearance as the reception of a newcomer *into* a settled neighbourhood, not a solitary grant on open ground. Whether that Buckinghamshire concentration bears on John's *own* English origin — as against the favoured Norfolk (Candidate B) reading — is the migration/origin question developed in the [immigration-by-association topic](../../../../../../research/topics/g13-john-gurney-immigration-by-association.md), and is not resolved here.[^bucks-reception]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000028 -->

## A debt for the town's minister

The Massachusetts Bay colony accounts for the General Court session beginning 30 October 1644 record: "John Gurny his byl for 3. 16. 4d & Tho: Lake his note for 1. 3. 8d are accepted for £5 fro Mr Ginner" — John's bill of £3 16s 4d and Thomas Lake's note of £1 3s 8d accepted together as the £5 owed by "Mr Ginner."[^mbcr2] "Mr Ginner" is the colony record's form of **Rev. Thomas Jenner**, Weymouth's minister from 1636 until he removed to Saco about 1640; the Weymouth records themselves render him "Mr. Thom: Ginner (Jenner)," and he was chosen a Weymouth deputy in 1640.[^how][^nash] The purpose of Gurney's bill is unstated — it is not evidence of tailoring, of military supply, or of a 1641/2 transaction — but it is a documented paper-and-credit tie between John and the town's ministry, the kind of standing entanglement an ordinary transient would not have.[^mbcr2]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000007 -->

## Weymouth townsmen and associates

John's documented Weymouth associates were established members of the town rather than fellow newcomers — which frames his 1641 appearance as the reception of a newcomer *into* an existing community. His companions in the 30 May / 2 June 1641 gunpowder-fine matter were **James Ludden** — an early Wessagusset/Weymouth "Old Planter," present by about 1632 — and **Richard Porter**, a 1635 Rev. Joseph Hull–company settler, both recorded "of Weymouth."[^mbcr1][^porter][^ludden-king] The tie to Weymouth outlasted his removal: two decades later his 1663 Braintree estate still owed a debt to "**Goodman King of Waymouth**" — **John King**, an early King's-Cove planter — named in the probate accounts among his Braintree, Boston, and frontier obligations.[^spr338][^nehgr12][^ludden-king] Whether this reception network bears on *how* a newcomer of probably East Anglian origin reached Weymouth — the shared-migration-versus-reception question, and the Buckinghamshire-neighbour and Hingham-corridor threads — is the migration/origin problem developed in the [immigration-by-association topic](../../../../../../research/topics/g13-john-gurney-immigration-by-association.md), the fuller home for that analysis; here the point is only that his named Weymouth associates were the town's established men.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000008 -->

## The militia record and its limits

The one militia-adjacent record of John at Weymouth — the 1641 powder fine — reflects only the ordinary readiness obligation every adult man carried, not active service. A Gurn-variant sweep of the Massachusetts Bay military and colony-court records, a full read of the printed colony records (Shurtleff's *Records*, vols 1–5), and the Military Company (Ancient and Honorable Artillery) membership roll of 1637–1652 place him on no campaign, officer, or volunteer record; the only colony-level Gurney entries in the whole run are the routine 1641 fine remission and the 1644 Jenner-bill acceptance.[^fts-mil][^mbcr1][^mbcr2] No military service or land-for-service mechanism, then, explains his arrival or his movements — a first-class negative that keeps the powder fine from being over-read.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000009 -->

# Origin — age and baptism

**John Gurney was born about 1603, and no record gives the date.** His age rests on a single handle — his own statement, in a 1652/3 deposition, that he was "aged 50 years or thereabouts." Read strictly that gives a birth about 1602/3; allowing for the period habit of rounding a stated age to the nearest ten, **about 1603–1608 is the defensible bracket**.

Two further constraints bound it from the other side without narrowing it much. His daughter Mary married at Braintree in 1647, so at a minimum marriage age of sixteen she was born by 1631 — which requires him to have been a grown man by the mid-1620s. And **no baptism of his has been found anywhere.** The East Dereham entry once read as one does not exist as described, so no baptismal date competes with the deposition, and the register's returns for 1611–1616 are lost, which means the parish can never supply one.[^no-baptism]

## The one age John gave for himself

In a life that is well documented from 1641 to 1663, John Gurney stated his own age exactly once. Deposing in the Suffolk County case of Wilson v. Faxon — Suffolk Court Files no. 188, dated "17-1-[16]52-3," that is 17 March 1652/3 in old-style reckoning — he is recorded as "John Gurney of Brayntree aged 50 Yeares or therea-abouts."[^nehgr-62-94] Taken at face value, an age of fifty in the spring of 1652/3 places his birth about 1602 or 1603. It is the strongest single anchor on his age, and the only one that indisputably belongs to the emigrant himself rather than to a record about his property or his household.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000043 -->

## A rounded age, not a birth date

That fifty is not a measurement. It is a round number, offered "or thereabouts," of exactly the kind early-modern deponents produced when asked their age — a formulaic self-report given to the nearest five or ten rather than the nearest year. The tendency of people in this period to round their stated ages to convenient figures is well documented: self-reported ages cluster heavily on multiples of ten and five, so a witness who says "about fifty" may in truth be anywhere from his middle forties to his middle fifties.[^age-heaping] So the deposition does not narrow John's birth to 1602/3 so much as it centres a band there: read strictly it gives 1602/3, but once the rounding is allowed for, its *probable* birth range is about 1603–1608 weighted by the other datapoints and its *possible* range widens to roughly 1598–1610. The age is real evidence that John was an older rather than a younger man, but it is a soft point, not a hard one.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000057 -->

## No baptism, and what the parents' 1611 marriage would require

**No baptism of John Gurney survives anywhere, and none can be recovered from East Dereham.** An entry in that register indexed as "John the sonne of Nicholas Gorne" has been read as naming Francis Gurney, which would have placed a baptism about 1609/10 and given a second, independent handle on his age. Re-read across the full image run against recorded coordinates, the line adjudicated sits on a different membrane and belongs to an Enderby family, and the record the index describes is six years later and belongs to a Horne family. **The deposition is therefore the only handle on his age that exists**, and nothing competes with it.[^no-baptism]

**The 1611 marriage of John's candidate parents runs against his own stated age, and that friction is now the whole of the chronological problem.** On 23 September 1611, "Franc Gurny & Margaret Rybett" were married at St Martin at Palace, Norwich.[^parents-marriage] A birth about 1603 — or anywhere in the 1603–1608 bracket — falls **before** that marriage. Francis Gurney was moreover a bound London apprentice until 1606 and could not ordinarily have fathered a legitimate child before then, which narrows the room to a 1606–1608 sliver that is still pre-marital. So the identification requires either a pre-marital birth, common enough in the period to be unremarkable, or an earlier marriage that five independent instruments have failed to find. The marriage record itself is not in doubt; what it constrains is.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000058 -->

## A probable window and a possible one

A third handle constrains the birth from the far side and is independent of both the deposition and the baptism: John's marriage by the later 1620s. His daughter Mary married Daniel Shed at Braintree in 1647, and Daniel was baptised at Finchingfield, Essex, in 1620 — bounding Mary's own birth to by about 1628 and the couple's English marriage to the mid-1620s, so that John was already an adult, plausibly in his early twenties, by about 1625. That bound sits comfortably with the earlier end of the range.[^shedd]

Because each handle is uncertain in its own way, the honest picture is not a single year but two nested ranges, evaluated for each handle on its own terms:

The baptism band no longer applies — no baptism of John Gurney survives — so the figure should be read for the two surviving estimators only.](figures/john-gurney-birth-year-ranges.png)

**Two estimators remain, and both pull the same way.** The deposition age and the bound from daughter Mary's marriage overlap around **1603–1608**, and that overlap is the most probable window. Widen the deposition to its fullest allowance for rounding and the outer span reaches from about 1598 to about 1610. Both surviving estimators are colonial, and both favour the earlier half of that span; **the English side now supplies no birth-year evidence at all.** The best the record supports is a birth about 1603, inside a bracket of roughly 1603–1608.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000044 -->

# The Gurney family of Cawston, Norfolk (1640s–1750s)

A sustained Gurney family appears throughout the digitised Cawston manorial records 1641–1685 (with recitals running into the eighteenth century): **William and Ann Gurney** in the 1648–1650 courts, **Emme Gurney** 1655–1695, **Agnes Gurney** 1659–1664, a Gurney death presented c. 1640–1664, an **Anne Gurney** in 1729, and "land late of the said Ann Gurney" recited in 1757. Cawston is 11 miles north of Norwich — a different orbit from both the West Barsham gentry line and the East Dereham corridor, and previously known to this project only from a single comparator note (a William-and-Anne Gurney sighting, William 1630).

## Reading

A copyhold family at Cawston across at least three generations: William & Ann (1648–50, with William's land in abuttal recitals by 1649/57 "land late of … Gurney"), the long widow-or-heiress tenures of Emme (1655–1695) and Agnes (1659–64, the latter in a "Libertate" entry worth reading), death presentments in the 1640s–60s, and an Anne Gurney still in the rolls in 1729 with her land recited in 1757. The 19th-century Hugh Gurney Barclay cards are the Norwich banking family as lords/mortgagees, not the local line.

**Case relevance.** Comparator only at present — no John in the visible cards, and no link to the emigrant's window. But a documented mid-17th-century Gurney family 11 miles north of Norwich thickens the map of plebeian Norfolk Gurney households against which any origin hypothesis must discriminate, and the 1640s–60s death presentments are exactly the kind of entries that name relicts and heirs.

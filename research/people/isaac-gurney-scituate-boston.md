# Isaac Gurney of Scituate — vagrant and burglar, 1663–1667

Unplaced colonial Gurney. Isaac Gurney ("Isacke Gurney") appears in Plymouth Colony and Massachusetts Bay court records across 1663–1667 as a young man repeatedly in trouble — pilfering at Scituate, then vagrancy and burglary around Boston. No kinship to the Braintree–Weymouth Gurney line (John Gurney-1 G13, Richard G12) is documented; he appears in none of the family-group sources (Sprague, Rigler, the Weymouth registers). His age and dates would fit a kinsman of the emigrant generation's children, but that is conjecture; he is preserved here as a distinct subject.

## Plymouth Colony court, 1 March 1663/4 — whipped for pilfering at Scituate

At the Plymouth court of 1 March 1663/4, "Isacke Gurney, for pilfering and other disorderly liueing, was sentenced by the Court to bee whipt," and the sentence was carried out. The court issued an order to the Townsmen of Scituate certifying that "Isacke Gurney, who was complained against by some of yours for pilfering and other disorderly liueing," had been punished; "the said Gurney was by warrant returned from constable to constable back to Scituate."[^plymouth-isaac-1664]

## Suffolk County court file no. 792, 25 June 1667 — burglary at Medford, committed to Boston prison

Three years later Isaac was in Massachusetts Bay custody. Suffolk County Court file no. 792 ("3 papers"), *Case of Isack Gurney, 25 June 1667*, contains:

1. **Committal order, 25 June 1667**, to the keeper of the prison in Boston: "Whereas Isacke Gurney herewith sent unto you stands convicted of being [a] vagabond rogue, & of Burglary…"
2. **Examination before Daniel Gookin, 28 June 1667.** Isaac confessed "that he is the same man which hath been sundry times … convicted of a [vagabond and wandering] life, going from Town to Town [without] any habitation [these] three yeares & [a] half now past," and that on the 16th of that instant, "being the Lords day in the afternoon he broake into the dwelling house of Golden Moore at Meadford [Medford]," taking money and bread. He signed by mark.
3. A third paper, badly garbled in the machine transcript, appears to describe his conduct in prison — including what reads as kindling fire inside the prison (an escape attempt?) — with a reference to about fourteen days' confinement and the name William Walter(?), cooper. The 3.5-year vagrancy confession (back from June 1667) aligns neatly with the March 1663/4 Scituate punishment and removal — one continuous record arc for the same man.

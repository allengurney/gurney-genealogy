# The Gurne/Gurney family of Costessey, Norfolk (1629–1675)

A John Gurne/Gurney family is documented in the digitised Costessey manorial court records from 1629 to the mid-1670s — **John Gurne in a 1629 orchard presentment, "John Gurney sen[ior]" in a 1659 court list (implying a junior John), a John Gurne in a 1674/5 copyhold transaction, and an abuttal naming "land of John Gurney, gentleman."** Costessey (Cossey), 4 miles west of Norwich, is the laceweaver Francis B's documented property locus, which keeps this family directly relevant to the two-Francis separation and to the G13 emigrant case as a candidate-or-comparator family.

**Reading caution from the expert image reads (2026-06-11):** the manor's lords were the **Jernegan** (Jerningham) baronets, and the machine transcripts systematically misread *Jernegan* as a Gurney-form — the original 1634 "terra Johis Gurny" lead (L-93) proved to be exactly this false positive. The pre-1659 result cards below should therefore be treated as unverified and Jernegan-suspect except where an image read has confirmed the name; the confirmed family picture currently rests on the 1629, 1659, and 1674/5 reads.[^costessey-sweep]

## Reading — revised after the expert image reads (2026-06-11)

Four entries are now image-verified:

1. **14 April 1629** (not "22 Caroli"/1646 — the regnal misreading is rejected): an orchard presentment — "And **John Gurne** hath planted in the same orchard about twelve frute trees and maketh [sward?] to grasse upon," with a Latin finding that the orchard ought to be kept in its proper form and a precept to John. A land-use regulation entry; no descriptor (senior/junior/occupation) attaches to John.[^packet6]
2. **April 1659**: a court leet/court baron default memorandum (the court held for **Anthony Dobbs of Marsham, esquire** — note the lord is not the Jernegan baronet, so this list may belong to a different manor bundled on the film) lists among tenants/suitors "…Richard Knight, John [Burbis?], **John Gurney sen**[ior]…" with Thomasine Rookwood and others. The "senior" style implies a **junior John Gurney** existed in 1659 — the father-son pair survives, by inference rather than by the earlier "son of John" misreading.[^packet4]
3. **21 January 1674/5**: a copyhold release/quitclaim in which "**Johannes Gurne et Johannes Bower**" act together (the machine transcript's "Gurnee son of John" conflated abbreviated *Johannes* forms with the party name *Bower*), concerning a messuage or cottage with meadow and pasture in Costessey; William Hender[?] appears as recipient/possessor; associated names Hopper, Mary, and a former Thomas Jolly.[^packet3]
4. Same opening, higher on the page: an abuttal "land of **John Gurney, gentleman**" — a status datum for the 1670s John.[^packet3]

**Packet 13 reads (2026-06-15) make the father–son pair documentary.** Two further Costessey images were transcribed at image level. On [ark:/61903/3:1:S3HT-6PN9-9SP](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:S3HT-6PN9-9SP?view=fullText), a surrender from **Thomas Rippon** (recited court 11 October 1648) settles a copyhold on "**John Gurne** & Margaret his wife for term of their natural lives & the longer liver of them," with **remainder to "John Gurne their sonne" his heirs and assigns**, the elder John styled "**John Gurne the father**." On [ark:/61903/3:1:S3HT-6PNV-DD](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:S3HT-6PNV-DD?view=fullText), "John Gurne & Margaret his wife and … the heirs of the said John" is confirmed (with a recited prior court involving a William Gurne, c. 1600s, not fully dated). The companion 1625 Costessey image (`3:1:S3HT-6PN2-L1`) gave **no** secure Gurne name (probable machine false positive).[^packet13]

**Case relevance.** The father–son pair is now **documentary, not merely inferential**: a John Gurne "the father," wife Margaret, and son John are named together in the 1648 Rippon settlement, corroborating the 1659 "sen[ior]" style. A John Gurney *senior* at Costessey implies a junior John whose birth could sit anywhere from the 1610s to the 1630s — the elimination question stands. The family's location at the laceweaver Francis B's manor keeps the kin-network question open. The decisive next steps are the un-read 1659-adjacent and 1670s pages and the film's earlier items, read with the Jernegan caution in force.

# Family — the children as a family group

**John Gurney's children are known as a family group of five — Sarah, Mary, Richard, John, and Peter — with Isaac a probable but unproven sixth, and all of them, by every indication, born to his first wife Mary. No single colonial record ever lists the family together: the roster is reconstructed from the compiled Braintree and Gurney genealogies, cross-checked against Torrey's marriage compendium, and anchored child by child in scattered land, court, and probate records.**

## The children as a family group

The core roster comes down through the compilers. Waldo Chamberlain Sprague's *Genealogies of the Families of Braintree* lists John Gurney's children as Sarah, Mary, Richard, John, and Peter; the Weymouth town history carries the same set in its John Gurney family entry, and Jean Gurney Rigler's Gurney genealogy repeats it.[^sprague][^weymouth][^rigler] All were Mary's — she is, by every indication, the mother of the whole family — with the older children born in England and the youngest perhaps in Massachusetts, so that the childbearing years run from the couple's mid-1620s English marriage to about 1643.[^shedd]

Individually we identify the children from different research sources. **Sarah** is listed first and was likely the eldest, but she is otherwise undocumented — no birth, marriage, or death record has been found — and survives only in the compiled roster.[^sprague] **Mary** is documented via her marriage to Daniel Shed at Braintree in 1647.[^shedd] **Richard** is the direct-line ancestor (G12), of Weymouth, whose own record trail includes multiple primary records detailed in Richard Gurney's fact sheet and research notes rather than here. **John Jr.** and **Peter** both carried the family into King Philip's War a decade after their father's death — John Jr. among the dead at Mendon in July 1675, and Peter a soldier in Captain Isaac Johnson's company, mustered at Dedham in December 1675 for the Narragansett campaign and killed later in the war — the post-1662 descendant stream developed in the family-descendants research rather than in this unit.[^bodge]

Torrey's compendium supplies an independent cross-check of the whole group. His *New England Marriages Prior to 1700* gives a one-page reading of the John Gurney family and preserves John Jr.'s Mendon household, a wife Ruth and children John, Samuel, and Mary, a level of detail the Braintree genealogies do not carry.[^torrey]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000041 -->

## The uncertain sixth: Isaac

Isaac is the one child the core lists do not carry — a later and uncertain addition rather than a name in Sprague or Rigler. A young **Isacke Gurney** surfaces instead in a tight cluster of colonial court records: whipped for pilfering and disorderly living at Scituate by the Plymouth court on 1 March 1663/4 and returned constable-to-constable to the town, then three years later jailed for vagrancy and burglary around Medford and committed to the Boston prison under a warrant signed by Daniel Gookin (Suffolk County court file no. 792, June 1667).[^isaac-court]

No record states his parentage, but by elimination he is most plausibly John Gurney-1's son. An Isaac born about 1643 needs a father of John's own generation, and the only other Gurney male then in the Braintree–Weymouth–Boston orbit, Richard (G12, born about 1630–34), was himself a child in 1643 and far too young to have fathered him; no other Gurney household is documented in the region. The uncommon forename, the age fit, and the Braintree-orbit locations all point to the emigrant's family — a moderate identification, not a proven one. The dedicated [Isaac Gurney subject file](../../../../isaac-gurney-scituate-boston.md) preserves him cautiously as a distinct person, pending a record that names his father.[^isaac-court]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000042 -->

# Family — colonial collaterals

**Two colonial-era Gurneys carry John's own surname with no documented tie to his family, and a third apparent Gurney thread at Dorchester turns out not to involve a Gurney at all.** John and Sarah Gurney of Providence, Rhode Island appear in that town's own records between 1695 and 1714 — a stranger warned out of town, then a widow whose will names a surviving Gurney husband — with no record connecting either to John Gurney-1's children or grandchildren. A single unread index line in Suffolk County's earliest deed register, "Gurner, James," is a genuinely open question, not yet a documented person. And a run of Dorchester town, church, and county-court references to a "Goodman Gurney" or a "Jane Gurnet" all resolve, on the same records' own testimony, to one unrelated family, the Gurnells — not to a Gurney of any kind.

## The Providence John and Sarah Gurney, 1695–1714

Providence, Rhode Island's town council records carry a short, self-contained Gurney story two generations after John's death. On 31 March 1695 the council took up a transient: "there is a person one John Gurney by name who belongs not unto our Towne yet is here come in & at present makes Residence here, but hath nought to maintaine him or supply his wants," and ordered him to leave town within a fortnight. He was carried out by the constable but returned, and in 1696 the council escalated: he was to be apprehended, fined 5 shillings or whipped twelve stripes on his bare back, and removed from town either way.[^providence-john-gurney]

The same council's records resume in 1714 with a will. On 30 April 1714 "Joseph ffield presented to the Councill the will & Testament of his deceased mother Sarah Gurney," examined and allowed along with her inventory. Sarah had died 16 April 1714; her will of 31 March 1714 left small sums to sons Zachariah, John, James, and Daniel Field, household goods to daughter Sarah Field, and the working stock — cattle, sheep, swine, and tools — to son and executor Joseph Field, "the product of his Care & dilligence." She signed by mark, "Sarah X Gurney." Decisively, the same record shows her Gurney husband had not predeceased her: "Aprill the last day 1714: John Gurney the husband of the above named Sarah Gurney personally appeared before the Town Councill," witnessed the will, and co-appraised the inventory alongside Josiah Thornton, both times by mark. He took nothing under the will, and Joseph Field posted a £150 executor's bond. Every one of Sarah's children carried the surname Field, so the Gurney marriage produced no recorded children of its own.[^providence-sarah-gurney]

Read together, the record traces one arc: the friendless stranger warned out of Providence in 1695–96 evidently stayed in the town's orbit, married the widow Sarah Field, and was still alive there — an illiterate or mark-signing man — on 30 April 1714. Nothing in either record states his age, his origin, or any kinship to another Gurney, and nothing ties him to Braintree, Weymouth, or Mendon.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000093 -->

## A possible grandson? Torrey's Providence entry

Clarence Almon Torrey's marriage compendium independently carries this same Providence couple at compendium level — "GURNEY, John3 (-1723) & Sarah (THORNTON) [FIELDS] (-1714), w Zachariah; b 1701?, b 1706, aft 4 Feb 1696, b 1714; Providence" — giving John a death year, 1723, that neither Providence record supplies. Torrey's "Thornton" reading is most plausibly his own slip: the 1714 will and inventory name no Thornton relationship for Sarah at all, only a co-appraiser, Josiah Thornton, standing beside John Gurney in that one clerical role.[^torrey-providence]

Torrey's page also carries a second, separate John3 marriage in the same generation — "GURNEY, John3 & Elizabeth [GREEN] (1664-); b 1689; Weymouth" — and a Samuel3, independently identified elsewhere in this graph as John Gurney Jr.'s Mendon-born son, "GURNEY, Samuel3 (1671-) & Sarah ATKINS? STAPLES/SHAPLEY... 26 Oct 1693; Boston/Little Compton, RI." That Samuel3 identification means Torrey's generation-3 Gurneys are grandsons of John Gurney-1 through John Jr. and Ruth's Mendon household — which makes the Providence John3 generationally consistent with being a grandson of the same household, and the separate Weymouth John3 (Elizabeth Green, 1689) consistent with staying in Richard G12's own Weymouth line instead. Torrey's entry states no parentage for either John3, so this reads as a plausible but unconfirmed identification, not a finding: it would require an independent record naming the Providence John's father before either grandson candidacy can be resolved.[^torrey-providence][^mendon-samuel-cross]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000094 -->

## An unresolved deed-index entry: James Gurner

Suffolk County's earliest deed register carries one more unresolved same-surname trace. The printed index to *Suffolk Deeds*, Liber I (1640–1653) lists "Gurner, James, 5," immediately beside "Gurdon, John, 5" — an index line pointing to printed page 5, which has not yet been read. If "Gurner" is a Gurney variant, a James Gurner active in Boston's earliest deed register would be a new same-surname colonial datum, comparable to the Norwich James Gurney (son of the laceweaver Francis) already tracked on the English side. If the page instead reads Garner or Gardner, it is a false friend of the same kind resolved below for Dorchester.[^suffolk-liber-i-gurner]

Because the body text has not been read, this stays exactly what the index makes it: a name, a page number, and an open question.[^suffolk-liber-i-gurner]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000095 -->

## Not a Gurney at all: the Dorchester false friend

A third apparent Gurney thread at colonial Dorchester resolves, once its own record set is read in full, to a different family entirely. Margery Laver of Dorchester's will, dated 4 (6) 1664 and proved 10 (9) 1664, leaves a small keepsake "to Jane Gurnet, 2s for a memorandum," among bequests to Braintree's minister William Tompson, Dorchester's minister Richard Mather, and other Dorchester neighbors.[^laver-will] Separately, the Suffolk County Court's record of a 1676/7 bastardy case names a surety: "Goodman Gurney of Dorchester engaged in Court to see the saide payments performed."[^suffolk-court-481] Read on their own, either line could suggest a Dorchester Gurney household distinct from John's Braintree–Weymouth family.

Dorchester's own town and church records close the question. The Dorchester town book renders one resident, John Gurnell, under a dozen spellings across several decades — Gornell, Gornill, Gornehill, Gurnell, Gurnel, gornett, Gurnett, Gurnet, Gurnit — including "John Gurnet" in a 13 (12) 1653 fence-viewers list; he was a town officer into the 1670s, and the court's 1676/7 "Goodman Gurney of Dorchester" is this same man, the county clerk drifting to the same Gurnet/Gurney spelling Dorchester's own book already shows. No independent Gurney household existed at colonial Dorchester.[^dorchester-town-records] The town book's own editorial footnote resolves Jane, too: "Jane Gornell, widow of John Gornell, married John Burge" — the same Jane the Laver will calls "Jane Gurnet." First Church Dorchester's record of 11 May 1709 still calls her "mrs. Burgesse alias Gurnet," recovering a legacy she had left the church decades earlier — so the "Gurnet" form stuck to this Gornell widow even in the church's own memory, seventeen years after Margery Laver's will.[^dorchester-first-church] Every "Gurney"-form record at colonial Dorchester belongs to this one Gornell/Gurnell family. None is evidence of a Gurney there, and none should be read as John's family or as a lead toward it.[^dorchester-town-records][^dorchester-first-church]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000096 -->

# Origin — Bury connections

Charles Edward Banks assigned John Gurney's English origin to **Bury St Edmunds, Suffolk** — the one specific parish any published authority has ever named for him. The attribution rests on nothing but Banks's own working notes: no ship, no parish register, no will, no apprenticeship indenture. Searched directly, the Bury St Edmunds registers hold no Gurney household that could have fathered the emigrant, and the modern standard — Robert Charles Anderson's *Great Migration Directory* — declines Banks and records the origin as "Unknown." What Banks most plausibly caught was not a birthplace but the Suffolk association of a different, younger John Gurney: the 1636 runaway apprentice of the Bury-area feltmaker John Newgate. Read that way, "Bury St Edmunds" is a mistaken pointer at a real neighbourhood-migration pattern, and it fits the favoured Norfolk origin rather than refuting it. Whether John belongs to the Norfolk Gurneys at all is the identity question, argued in the [John Gurney identity case file](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v4.md) (§8.5, §10.2), not settled here.

## Banks's Bury St Edmunds memo

Banks's *Topographical Dictionary of 2885 English Emigrants to New England, 1620–1650*, edited and published posthumously by Elijah Ellsworth Brownell in 1937, gives the emigrant one line on p. 151:

> GURNEY, John | Bury St. Edmunds | [no ship] | Braintree, Massachusetts | Banks Mss.

The origin column reads "Bury St. Edmunds," but the source column reads only "Banks Mss." — Banks's own unpublished notes, with no ship, register, will, or indenture behind it. By the standards Banks applied elsewhere in the same volume, an entry resting solely on "Banks Mss." is a lead he meant to run down, not a proven origin; he died before he could publish, and Brownell printed the manuscripts as they stood. The placement is not arbitrary, though: the same page sets John amid a genuine Bury St Edmunds emigrant cohort — John Newgate, Clement Chaplin, William Knapp, John Goodrich, Henry Bright, William Townsend, Theodore Atkinson, Peyton Cooke, Samuel Wright — most of them well documented from primary records. Banks was perceiving a real Suffolk neighbourhood-migration pattern and slotting Gurney into it.[^banks]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000063 -->

## Why "Bury" points to John Newgate of Horningsheath

The cleanest single explanation for that memo stands one name away, in Banks's own cohort. **John Newgate** — the Boston feltmaker who in July 1636 brought a runaway apprentice named John Gurney before the governor, obtaining an order that the young man serve until age 24 (three years from the following 29 September) — was himself a Suffolk man: born at **Horningsheath (Horringer), about three miles south-west of Bury St Edmunds**, and resident at Hessett and Bury St Edmunds, then Southwark, before he emigrated to Boston in 1633.[^newgate][^order] An apprentice Newgate carried was therefore most plausibly a young man drawn from Newgate's own neighbourhood around Bury — a John Gurney who would be "of Bury St Edmunds" in exactly the loose sense a compiler records, identified with the town he trained in or near.

That apprentice is **not** the Braintree emigrant. His implied birth (about 29 September 1615) is a decade too late for a man deposed "aged about 50" in 1652/3, and Newgate's own later records name no Gurney; the identity work de-conflates the two Johns on those grounds — see the [trade-and-training topic](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/people/_staging/g13-john-gurney/topics/origin/21-trade-training.md) and case file §8.5.[^deconflation] The relevant point for origin is narrower: the man who actually carried the Bury/Suffolk tag was the apprentice, and Banks's note most plausibly tracked him.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000064 -->

## The Bury registers hold no natal Gurney household

Tested against the parish records themselves, Bury St Edmunds yields no Gurney family that could have fathered the emigrant. A Gurney-variant sweep of the **St James** registers (FamilySearch full-text image group DGS 007413310, covering 1561–1800) returns a single Bury Gurney household: **Thomas Gurney and Lidda (Lydia) Broddish, married about 1633–35** — far too late to have produced a man born around 1602–1612. Their children were Juda (baptized c. 1633/4), a John "son to Thomas" registered about 1653/4 after the Commonwealth reopened the register, and a Thomas (c. 1661/2); a separate John Gurney and Elizabeth baptized a William in 1665. The earlier Bury Gurneys are unconnected fragments — a William Gurney's daughter Bridgett in 1588, a Mary Gurny in a 1620–24 communicant list.[^stjames]

The **St Mary** parish tells the same story from the other end of life. The National Burial Index records three Gurney burials there in a four-year window — **John Gurney buried 11 December 1653**, an unnamed Gurney on 6 April 1655, and an unnamed Gurney noted "Wife" on 13 May 1656 — a single continuing Bury Gurney household whose male head, John, was in the ground by the end of 1653. That John cannot be the Braintree emigrant, who was alive in Massachusetts and died there in 1662/3.[^stmary]

Across both Bury parishes, then, the Gurney presence is either post-dated or an unconnected scrap, and the one Bury "John Gurney" of the period was buried in 1653. **Bury St Edmunds is cleared as the emigrant's birthplace.** Whatever Banks recorded, it was not a Bury natal record — because none capable of fathering John survives to have been recorded.[^stjames][^stmary]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000065 -->

## What the attribution is worth

Put together, the evidence resolves "Bury St Edmunds" into a lead, not a birthplace — and one that points the same way as the favoured Norfolk hypothesis, not against it. The attribution has no primary support and no surviving Bury natal record behind it; the concrete Bury/Suffolk association of the period belongs to Newgate's apprentice, a demonstrably different and younger John; and the modern authority, Anderson's *Great Migration Directory* (p. 158), independently declines Banks and enters the emigrant's origin as "Unknown."[^anderson] A Norwich-born son of Francis Gurney who trained at or near Bury — the Candidate B reading — would satisfy Banks's note exactly, since it would make John "of Bury" by trade and association without making him Bury-born. So Banks's memo neither establishes a Suffolk birth nor rebuts a Norfolk one; it records a real East Anglian migration corridor and mis-files the emigrant inside it. The identity conclusion this feeds — that John is the son of Francis Gurney and Margaret Rybett of Norfolk — is carried in the [John Gurney identity case file](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v4.md) (§10.2), which reaches the same reading of Banks.[^deconflation]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000066 -->

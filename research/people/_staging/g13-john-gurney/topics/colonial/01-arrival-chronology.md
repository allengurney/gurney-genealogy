# Colonial arrival chronology

**John Gurney was living at Weymouth, Massachusetts, by 30 May / 2 June 1641, and reached New England in 1638–early 1641.** The earliest firm datum is a General Court fine-remission; the arrival window is bounded below by his original (pre-1643) Weymouth land grants and above by the sharp falloff of the Great Migration after 1640. He had moved to Braintree by 1645.

## The 1641 gunpowder record

The earliest record that firmly places John Gurney in New England is the Massachusetts General Court order of **2 June 1641** remitting the fine of John Porter, James Ludden, and John Gurney, incurred "for want of gunpowder."[^mbcr] The underlying **petition** survives one step closer to the event: calendared in the Massachusetts State Archives colonial collection, it is dated **30 May 1641** and reads "Petition of John Gurny, Richard Porter, and James Ludden **of Weymouth** … remission of the fines laid upon them for [want of gunpowder]."[^petition] A nineteenth-century secondary source independently fixes the same fact: Joseph W. Porter's *Genealogy of the Descendants of Richard Porter* (1878) quotes the court order and glosses it "Ludden and Gurney were of Weymouth."[^porter]

Read together, the three attestations establish presence, place, and a civic/military-regulatory context — but nothing more. The record does not by itself establish John's age, family, landholding, or office.

### Porter forename: the associate reading

The printed court order names "**John** Porter"; the earlier petition names "**Richard** Porter … of Weymouth." A full-resolution read of the petition calendar (image 947) confirms the forename plainly reads **Richard**, not a transcription artefact.[^petition] The weight therefore shifts to **Richard Porter** — the documented Rev. Hull–company Weymouth settler — with the printed order's "John" the likelier slip. This is analysis, not a new identity claim: it does not change who John Gurney was, but it makes his cleanest 1641 associate a Weymouth/Hull-company comparator rather than a Hingham/Salem Porter, which coheres with the early-Weymouth-settler reading below. The petition document itself (beyond the careful fair-copy calendar) remains the outstanding image to pull.

## Original Weymouth grantee before 1643

John's Weymouth landholding sharpens the arrival window. Gilbert Nash's *Historical Sketch of the Town of Weymouth* (1885), Appendix C, preserves what its text calls the **1643 partial record** of Weymouth property owners. Within it, a two-acre East Field parcel (p. 258) and a four-acre Mill Field parcel (p. 278) are recorded as "first granted to John Gurny/Gurnie" but **already held by other men by 1643**.[^nash] John was therefore an *original* grantee whose first parcels had already passed to others by the 1643 list — an early-settler profile, with his original grants made as a new Weymouth inhabitant by about 1642. A separate, later grant (great lot no. 16, Second Division, p. 282) is dated 2 February 1651-52 and was likewise subsequently granted to others.

This resolves a looseness in the secondary literature. The 1923 *History of Weymouth* dates his Weymouth lands only "as early as 3 Feb. 1651-52" and even doubts "whether he ever resided in Weymouth."[^how] That framing reads only the Second-Division grant and misses Nash's earlier 1643 record; the firm anchor is the pre-1643 partial record, not the 1651-52 great lot.

## Arrival window and the move to Braintree

Combining the two anchors — Weymouth presence by 30 May / 2 June 1641 and original grantee in the pre-1643 division — John reached New England in **1638–early 1641**, and cannot be later. That window sits in the tail of the Great Migration, which fell off sharply after 1640. He held real Weymouth land in his first years (c. 1641–43) yet had **moved to Braintree by 1645**; because his Weymouth parcels were already divested by the 1643 record, the move itself falls c. 1642–1645.

# Identity — Candidate A: Aylesbury, Buckinghamshire

Candidate A is a John Gurney who lived continuously in Buckinghamshire and Northamptonshire from 1628 to 1653 — squarely across the years the Massachusetts emigrant was at Weymouth and Braintree — and is eliminated on exactly that ground.[^s81] A baptism at Stewkley in 1603 gives him a father also named John Gurney; he married Alice Oliffe at Bierton with Broughton in 1628; and he had five children baptized at Saint Mary, Aylesbury, between 1638 and 1653.[^fs-aylesbury] Two government records tie the Aylesbury years to a later move: a 1641 certificate of residence recording his shift from the Aylesbury half-hundred into Northamptonshire, and an entry naming him as a tenant at Walgrave, Northamptonshire, in 1650.[^tna-e115]

## The Aylesbury family chain

FamilySearch's England, Births and Christenings index carries the whole run directly. Jhon Gurney, son of Jhon Gurney, was christened 21 February 1603 at Stewkley. A John Gurney was baptized at Saint Mary, Aylesbury, on 16 December 1638 — most plausibly Candidate A's eldest indexed child, about ten years after the Bierton marriage. Sarah followed on 22 August 1639, Daniell on 26 December 1645, Jonathan on 22 November 1647, and Hannah on 12 November 1653.[^fs-aylesbury] The Findmypast Buckinghamshire Marriage Index separately carries the marriage itself: John Gurney and Alice Oliffe, 24 April 1628, Bierton with Broughton, Bucks Archives PR16/1/1Q p. 30 — correcting an older "Alice Collindridge" identification that has no documentary support.[^fmp-bierton] Stewkley, Bierton, and Aylesbury sit within five miles of each other inside the Aylesbury hundred, so ordinary local mobility — not a change of household — explains the run of parishes; the 1641 certificate of residence is itself the government's own record of the one long-distance move in the chain, into Northamptonshire.[^tna-e115]

A second, separate Aylesbury marriage complicates only the *wife*, not the elimination: FamilySearch Records also carries a John Gurney marrying Anne Cowheard at Saint Mary, Aylesbury, on 25 October 1638. Either this is a different Aylesbury John, or Candidate A remarried after Alice Oliffe's death and the 1639–1653 children belong to the second marriage; FamilySearch does not index a mother's name on the children's baptisms, so the point cannot be settled from these records alone. Either reading still keeps the same man — or a man in the same household cluster — resident in Buckinghamshire across the whole window.[^fs-aylesbury]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000076 -->

## The wider Buckinghamshire Gurney households

Aylesbury was not the only Bucks parish holding a John Gurney family in this period. A walk of the Findmypast Buckinghamshire marriage, burial, and baptism indexes turns up at least five distinct seventeenth-century households: Candidate A's own John Gurney + Alice Oliffe line; John Gurney + Elizabeth at East Claydon (children Elinor 1632 and Samuel 1636; buried East Claydon 17 April 1654); John Gurney + Elizabeth at Chesham (children Andtr and Martha; burials there in July 1672 and 11 June 1678); John Gurney + Anne at Wing (children James 1650 and Elizabeth 1652); and John Gurney + Mary at Cublington (son Isaac baptized 1664 — after the colonial John's own 1662/3 death, and the most plausible identity for the Bucks John Gurney who later held the Stewkley manor with his son Isaac). A sixth Aylesbury household, Luke Gurney's (four children indexed 1621–1635), is not a John Gurney line at all, and a seventh, Edward Gurny's (a son buried 1665, a daughter baptized 1666), is a separate Aylesbury head of household distinct from Candidate A.[^bucks-household-map] Every one of these is a real, continuing Buckinghamshire family in the very years the colonial John Gurney was at Weymouth and Braintree — the county was simply not short of Gurneys named John.

## The 1661 Edlesborough marriage and the elimination

Phillimore's printed Buckinghamshire marriage register for Edlesborough carries a further, later Gurney cluster: William Gurney to Martha Halsey (1660), **John Gurney to Mary Kidgell (1661)**, Samuel Gurney of Bierton to Elizabeth Bunce of Padbury (1662), Thomas Gurney of Hockliffe to Frances Norman (1680), and George Hill of Chesham to Mary Gurney (1685).[^phillimore-edlesborough] The 1661 John-and-Mary marriage is materially incompatible with the colonial John Gurney: the Braintree emigrant buried his first wife Mary on 20 September 1661 and married Grizzell Fletcher seven weeks later, on 12 November 1661, both at Braintree. Either this Edlesborough John is Candidate A himself, in which case the 1661 Bucks marriage eliminates him outright on its own, or he is a distinct Bucks John Gurney with a wife also named Mary, in which case he simply accounts for one more English "John + Mary" household that is not the emigrant.

Every reading points the same way. Candidate A's own chain — Stewkley 1603, Bierton 1628, Aylesbury 1638–1653, the 1641 move into Northamptonshire, and the 1650 Walgrave tenancy — places one continuously documented Englishman across exactly the years the Massachusetts John Gurney was building a life at Weymouth and Braintree, dying there in 1662/3. The two records cannot describe the same man. Candidate A is eliminated as the colonial emigrant.[^s81]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000077 -->

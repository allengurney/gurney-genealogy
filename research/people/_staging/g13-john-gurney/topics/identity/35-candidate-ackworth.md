# Identity — Ackworth, Yorkshire

John Gurnoe married Mary Burton at Ackworth, Yorkshire, on 6 June 1636, and a son, John Thomas Gurnoe, was baptized at the same parish seven months later, on 19 January 1637.[^fmp-ackworth] Nothing else about this household is indexed: no burial of either spouse and no further child survive in the present Findmypast coverage. It is the only same-name comparator anywhere in the record sweeps whose wife is genuinely named Mary in roughly the right window, which keeps it visible in the case file's candidate table at a residual **~3%** — the highest of the non-Candidate-B rows — rather than eliminating it outright.[^s86]

## The case against Ackworth being the emigrant

Two structural problems outweigh the wife-name match. The first child is John Thomas, born seven months after the marriage, not Sarah; the colonial John Gurney's first child is Sarah, born about 1628 on the strength of Sprague's *History of Weymouth* and Torrey's *New England Marriages Prior to 1700*.[^torrey-sprague] The naming and birth order do not line up, and a 1637 son named John Thomas collides with the colonial John Jr., born about 1633. Yorkshire also sits well outside the Norfolk–Suffolk–Essex–London corridor that produced the Great Migration; Yorkshire emigration to New England in the late 1630s exists but is comparatively sparse, and no primary record bridges an Ackworth Gurnoe household to any Massachusetts arrival, town record, or to any of the colonial John's identified English associates.

Weighing the other way, the colonial John's marriage to his first wife Mary is itself bounded to before 1628 by his daughter Mary's own chronology, so a bride married in 1636 fits only as a hypothetical second English marriage — a reading the record gives no support for. Older compiled Gurney family histories do sometimes give the colonial John's wife as "Mary Barton" or "Mary Burton," and the 1636 Ackworth marriage is the first primary record to supply that exact name combination at a plausible date; the household's disappearance from the indexed Yorkshire record after 1637 is also the kind of gap an emigration would produce. Both points keep Ackworth above the noise floor of a bare same-surname coincidence without overturning the naming and corridor objections above.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000078 -->

## Standing and open work

The case file (v4.3, §8 and §11) carries Ackworth as **Unlikely, ~3%** — capped there by the pre-1628 marriage bound on the colonial John's own first marriage and by the John Thomas naming collision, and it is not treated as eliminated the way Candidates A, C, and D are.[^s86] Three lines of work could move the estimate in either direction: a West Yorkshire Archives or Borthwick Institute image pull of the 1636–1637 Ackworth register to recover the baptism's parents and confirm the marriage's spelling and any further children; an Ackworth burial register search for either spouse (not yet indexed by Findmypast); and, most materially, tracing the earliest compiled-genealogy occurrence of the "Mary Barton/Burton" tradition. If that tradition predates the availability of modern indexes, it is independent evidence pointing at Ackworth and the household's probability should rise; if it first appears only after IGI-era derivative trees became available, it is circular — later compilers matching the same index entry — and the residual estimate should stay where it is.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000079 -->

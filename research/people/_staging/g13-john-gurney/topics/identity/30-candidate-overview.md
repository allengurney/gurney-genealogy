# Identity — candidate overview

The [John Gurney identity case file](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v4.md) tests every documented same-name English John Gurney household against one shared matching-criteria profile, then eliminates or discounts each household that fails it (§1, §8).[^s1] Of the 51 named households the record sweeps have surfaced, 45 are eliminated outright and five more are held to a residual 1–3% likelihood; only Candidate B — the working hypothesis that John was the son of Francis Gurney of Norfolk and London — survives the test (§8).[^s8] Candidate B's own probability assessment, including its unknown-corridor residuals, is the case file's own argument-specific synthesis (§11); it stays there rather than being re-derived in this graph.[^s11] This unit carries the shared test and its outcome, not the surviving candidate's own case.

## The matching-criteria profile

Any English candidate for the Braintree emigrant must plausibly account for a tailor named John Gurney — a trade already anchored in the colonial record[^occupation] — married to a wife named Mary who bore children Sarah, Mary, Richard, John, and Peter,[^wife-children] born in England within a c.1602–1610 window read off his own 1652/3 "aged 50 or thereabouts" deposition,[^birth] and gone from the English record by the time he first appears in Massachusetts in June 1641.[^arrival-window] A settled Puritan household is a soft fifth criterion, consistent with — though not distinctive of — most Great Migration emigrants of the period.[^religion] It is this whole profile, not a bare name match, that the case file's process of elimination tests every candidate against.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000073 -->

## How candidates are tested and eliminated

Against that profile, the case file eliminates a household on structural rather than circumstantial grounds: continuing documented English residence past June 1641, most visibly Candidate A's 1650 Walgrave, Northamptonshire tenancy following a continuous 1603–1653 Buckinghamshire chain, and repeated again and again across the same-name clearance sweep (Chesham, East Claydon, Denton, and dozens more);[^s8-1-3-6] a wife whose name is not Mary — the ground that clears the majority of the sweep (Alice Oliffe, Elizabeth, Anne, Rose, Jane, and others);[^s8-1-3-6] a child set that does not match, or that collides in date, as in Candidate C's eight-child Berkhamsted family, whose 1624 John Jr. and 1634 Sarah do not line up with the colonial John Jr.'s c.1633 or Sarah's c.1628, and which has no Mary or Peter at all;[^s8-2] or a documented trade, death, or generation that places the household in the wrong period, as in Candidate D's 1662 London hearth-tax entry — dated the same calendar year the colonial John was dying at Boston.[^s8-4] A household attested by only a single indexed event, with no continuing record behind it, is held at a low residual (1–3%) rather than eliminated outright, since one record cannot itself rule out an unrecorded emigration.[^s8-unlikely] This is the same family-status-and-chronology standard already applied once inside this graph, where it separates the Braintree emigrant himself from a same-named man he is not: the 1636 Newgate apprentice, cleared on the identical combination of a mismatched family status and a chronology that will not bend to fit.[^newgate-precedent]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000074 -->

## The candidate landscape

| Candidate | Standing | Elimination ground |
|---|---|---|
| A — Aylesbury / Bierton / Northamptonshire | Eliminated | Continuous English residence 1603–1653; wife Alice Oliffe |
| **B — son of Francis Gurney & Margaret Rybett** | **Probable (case file §11)** | Sole survivor of the shared test |
| C — Berkhamsted, Hertfordshire | Eliminated | Eight-child family 1610–1636; wrong age; no Mary or Peter |
| D — London draper, Old Change | Eliminated | Continuing London residence through 1662 |
| Five other named households | Unlikely, ~8% combined | Single-attestation only, capped by chronology or a naming collision |
| Same-name clearance sweep (45 further households) | Eliminated | Died in England, wrong wife, wrong trade, or wrong generation |

Full working detail for each principal candidate stays in its own dedicated file — the Aylesbury/Buckinghamshire, Berkhamsted, Ackworth, Earsham, and London-draper candidate topics, and the same-name comparator file — cross-referenced here rather than restated; carrying their archival detail into this graph is reserved for the dedicated candidate-by-candidate identity increments this overview precedes.[^candidate-files] Candidate B's own trade/corridor/motive/network argument, its East Dereham baptism reading, and its probability model stay with the case file and its own future dedicated increment: this unit tests and clears the field, it does not argue the surviving candidate's case.[^candidate-b-deferred]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000075 -->

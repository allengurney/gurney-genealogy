# John Gurney Research Library

John Gurney (Generation 13, about 1602/3 – 1662/3) is the emigrant ancestor of
the American Gurney line: in New England by 1641, first an admitted inhabitant
of Weymouth, Massachusetts, then settled at Braintree from about 1645 until
his death in the winter of 1662/3 — a tailor by trade and a husbandman by the
measure of his goods. His English origin is the line's central open question:
no surviving record states where he came from, and the leading candidate
identifications remain unproven.

This research library is the comprehensive working research on John, organized
by topic. It is the deepest of three layers:

- The **fact sheet** is the concise published biography — start there for the
  settled narrative.
- The **case file** is the polished identification argument for who John was.
- This **research library** is the working evidence itself: every record,
  negative search, qualification, and open conflict, organized so each topic
  can be read on its own.

The material here is working research. Conclusions carry stated confidence,
conflicts are left visible rather than smoothed over — the most material are
Anderson's published 1636 arrival assessment against this project's 1638–early
1641 window, and the misread "John Cheny" entries in the Braintree town record
— and open questions are stated as open. Where a passage rests on mapped
evidence in the project's research graph, an **Evidence** link opens the
supporting findings, sources, and qualifications behind it.

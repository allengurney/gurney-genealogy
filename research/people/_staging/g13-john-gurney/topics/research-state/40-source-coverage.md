# Research-state source coverage

**Beyond the FamilySearch, Internet Archive, and Google corpus already worked, the specialist online research infrastructure returns no John Gurney and no Gurn-variant in the emigrant or early-settlement window. Coldham's three Ancestry emigrant compilations — the Admiralty adventurers, the bonded emigrants, and the Christ's Hospital child apprentices — are each negative or out of window; the Congregational Library's digitized colonial-church index holds nothing pre-1690 for John's towns; and the statewide Digital Commonwealth aggregator returns zero. The one compilation of its class not yet reached is Coldham's *Complete Book of Emigrants, 1607–1660*, which — absent from Ancestry — remains, with the original 1635 London port register (TNA E 157/20), the outstanding emigrant-record residual.**

This is a statement about where the search has reached, not about the surviving colonial record itself. John's American documentary footprint — dense in colony, town, court, and probate records and silent in the vital and church registers — is set out in [06-record-coverage.md](../colonial/06-record-coverage.md). What follows is the complementary research-state map: the specialist indexes and aggregators consulted beyond the project's core corpus in the search for an English emigration record or an early New England church trace, and their uniformly negative returns.

## The emigrant compilations

Peter Wilson Coldham's abstracts are the standard finding aids for seventeenth-century English emigrants, and Ancestry hosts three of them. None yields a Gurney in John's window. The *English Adventurers and Emigrants, 1609–1660* — abstracts of the High Court of Admiralty examinations — returns, on a surname-wildcard `Gurn*` sweep, only two hits, and both are the Bermuda headland "Gurnard's Head" (the ship *Garland* driven ashore there in October 1619): a place false friend, no person.[^adventurers] The *Emigrants in Bondage, 1614–1775* records four Gurn-variant transportees, the earliest Mary Gurney of Middlesex in 1683 — none in the 1614–1665 window, so the emigrant was not a bonded or transported man under any variant.[^bondage] And the *Child Apprentices in America from Christ's Hospital, London, 1617–1778* yields four `Gurn*` hits, all eighteenth-century, no John.[^apprentices] The residual is the flagship compilation itself: Coldham's *Complete Book of Emigrants, 1607–1660* is not among Ancestry's thirteen "Coldham" databases (only the later 1700–1750 volume is there, borrow-only on the Internet Archive), so it — with the original 1635 port register behind Hotten, TNA E 157/20 — is the emigrant-record residual that this coverage does not yet close.[^adventurers][^residual]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000030 -->

## The digitized church and aggregator indexes

Two indexes genuinely distinct from the archive.org/Google mass — a specialist colonial-church archive and the Massachusetts statewide aggregator — were checked and add nothing. New England's Hidden Histories, the Congregational Library's digitization of colonial church records, holds no pre-1690 material for John's towns: the earliest Braintree First Church records begin in 1697 (the Samuel Niles journal), Boston First begins in 1690 (its 1630–87 book is the printed CSM volume 39), Dorchester First in 1727, and neither Weymouth nor Mendon appears at all — a silence consistent with the lost pre-1690 Braintree church book.[^nehh] Digital Commonwealth, the aggregator over Massachusetts libraries' and archives' digitized collections, returns zero items for "Gurney Braintree."[^digcom]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000031 -->

## The English-origin searches: port-emigration and livery registers

The same reach-of-the-search map extends to the English side, where two record classes bear on how John crossed and how he learned his trade, and both are empty. John Camden Hotten's *Original Lists* — the surviving 1630s London port-examination lists, behind which sits the manuscript register TNA E 157/20 — carry no John Gurney in any spelling on a full-variant sweep with coverage confirmed; the one Gurney-adjacent entry, a "Richard Gum[m]y" bound for Virginia on the *America* in 1635, resolves to the surname Gummy, not Gurney, and to the wrong colony.[^hotten] And a sweep of the ROLLCO *Records of London's Livery Companies Online* across every partner company beyond the Drapers finds no East-Anglian Gurney livery apprentice in the emigrant window: the sole provincial tailoring-Gurney is a 1622 Clothworkers' apprenticeship of Francis Gurney, son of Thomas Gurney, tailor of Weston Turville — deep in the eliminated Buckinghamshire Candidate-A country, some four miles from Aylesbury — so John's tailoring was not livery-company-transmitted, matching the Merchant Taylors' and Norwich-freemen negatives set out in [21-trade-training.md](../origin/21-trade-training.md).[^rollco]<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000119 -->

## The English-origin searches: probate courts and Norfolk parish indexes

The English probate and parish infrastructure was likewise swept for any record naming the emigrant, his father, or his family, and returned no positive match. No will or administration survives for Francis Gurney G14 in any court with jurisdiction: the Commissary Court of London (the Marc Fitch printed index lists five Gurney/Gourny testamentary records 1626–1700, no Francis), the Archdeaconry Court of London, the Prerogative Court of Canterbury, and the Norwich diocesan courts (the Norfolk probate index carries 102 Gurney and four Gourn- records 1371–1858 with no Francis in the 1640s–50s) are each negative.[^probate-francis] The two Prerogative Court of Canterbury Rivett wills of Rattlesden that might have fixed Margaret Rybett's parentage name sons Edward and Thomas and a married daughter Dame Anne but no Margaret and no Gurney, weakening without eliminating the Rattlesden-Rivett descent.[^rivett-wills] And the Norfolk parish-register and same-name indexes, searched both for a John-son-of-Francis baptism and for a reverse-trace of Mary's birth family, return no matching household — the Gillingham "Mary Garny" of 1624 a distinct single-daughter Norfolk family, not the emigrant's.[^norfolk-parish] These are coverage facts bounding the reach of the origin search, not the parentage argument itself, which is made on positive evidence in the [Candidate B](../identity/32-norfolk-parentage.md) work.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000120 -->

## What the infrastructure has and has not reached

Across the specialist layer beyond the core corpus, then, the result is uniform on both the colonial-emigrant and the English-origin sides: no record surfaces John Gurney under any Gurn-variant in the emigrant or early-settlement window.[^adventurers][^bondage][^apprentices][^nehh][^digcom] This bounds the "immigrant of unknown origin" state to the present reach of the online infrastructure rather than resolving it — the negatives are coverage facts, not proof that no record exists — and it sharpens the outstanding work to a short residual: Coldham's *Complete Book of Emigrants, 1607–1660* and the 1635 port register E 157/20 (lead L-170), together with the specialist finding aids not yet swept (the Massachusetts Archives' own Preservica repository).[^residual] The English-origin question those pulls would bear on is pursued in the dedicated origin/identity work; it is neither resolved nor advocated here.<!-- graph-marker: G13-PM-000032 -->

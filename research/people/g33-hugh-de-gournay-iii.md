# Hugh de Gournay III (G33)

c. 1020 – d. c. 1093. Gournay-en-Bray, Normandy → Norfolk & Essex, England. Lineage status: **Confirmed**.

## Known facts

- Present at **Battle of Hastings**, 14 Oct 1066, with son Gerard [DG-I-intro-p.i]. Received English manors as Conquest reward.
- Previously sailed to England c. 1035 to support Edward the Confessor's claim [DG-I-25].
- Recorded in **Domesday Book** (1086): manors at Liston, Fordham, and Ardleigh (Essex); Norfolk properties [DG-I-25–27].
- Witnessed William I's Caen charters, 1077 and 1082 [DG-I-27].
- Married **Basilia Flaitel** (daughter of Gerard Flaitel, documented Norman figure).
- In later life, became monk at **Abbey of Bec**, Normandy. Wife Basilia also retired there with niece Anfride and Eva (wife of William Crispin). All took the veil from William Archbishop of Rouen [DG-I-48].
- **St. Anselm** (Archbishop of Canterbury, previously Abbot of Bec) mentions Hugh, Basilia, and Eva in letters in terms of strong affection [DG-I-48].
- Died and buried at Abbey of Bec, c. 1093.

## Open questions

1. The Caen charters Hugh witnessed (1077, 1082): specific folio/document IDs not given in DG — only year and location. Verifiable via *Regesta Regum Anglo-Normannorum* (Bates ed., 1998) or published *Acta* of William I.
2. Norfolk manors: DG states properties in Norfolk but does not detail specific manors for Hugh III the way Domesday details Essex holdings. Check Domesday Book Norfolk folios.
3. Basilia Flaitel: is there a published pedigree or charter evidence for the Flaitel family?
4. DG-I intro says "Hugh de Gournay and his son" at Hastings — ambiguous. At 1066, Hugh III (b. c. 1020) was ~46. His father Hugh II (b. c. 985) would have been ~81. Most plausible reading: "Hugh and his son" = Hugh III + Gerard (G32, b. c. 1040, aged ~26).

# Rivett family of Garveston, Norfolk — maternal kin of John Gurney G13

Working-research file for the family of **Margaret Rybett**, first wife of Francis Gurney G14
and mother of John Gurney G13. Paired raw extracts and the controlling negatives are in
[`sources/corpus_supplement/rivett-garveston-maternal-family-2026-06.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/sources/corpus_supplement/rivett-garveston-maternal-family-2026-06.md).

## Finding — Margaret's parentage: two Garveston candidates

Margaret Rybett, the bride of the 23 September 1611 marriage to Francis Gurney (St Martin at
Palace, Norwich, NRO PD 12/1), is a **Rivet of Garveston** — but the parish yields **two
register-attested Margarets**, and the identification is a weighed choice between them
(working weights ~55 / 40 / 5):

- **Margaret Rivet, baptized 21 May 1586, daughter of Francis Rivet (~55%).** Image-verified:
  the register reads *"Margaret Rivet daughter of ffrancis christened the xxjth of May,"*
  naming the father but **not the mother**.[^fs][^reg-p19] Francis Rivet **married Elizabeth
  Podmer at Garveston on 2 July 1584**, twenty-two months earlier, so **Elizabeth Podmer is
  her probable mother**.[^francis-podmer-1584] She would have been 25 at the 1611 marriage —
  the typical age for a first marriage. Her father's household continued at Garveston into
  John Gurney's boyhood (Elizabeth Rivet was buried there in 1626 — see the fostering
  section below), and her name echoes the parish matriarch Margaret (Bate) Rivett, buried
  2 September 1584, twenty months before the christening.
- **Margarett Rivet, baptized 1 February 1577/8, daughter of Robert Rivet (~40%).** Confirmed
  at image and expert level: *"Margarett Rivet daughter of Robt christened first of
  ffebruarie,"* entered under the register's *Anno Dni 1577* heading and above the *1578*
  heading — 1 February 1578 new style.[^packet50] She would have been ~33 at the 1611
  marriage — atypical for a first marriage, though nothing proves it was her first (the
  FindMyPast index carries no widow styling for the 1611 bride; the register image would
  say). **The strong argument for her: her full brother is Richard Rivet, baptized
  30 November 1575, same father Robert** — the Richard Ryvett who settled at Gressenhall —
  **so John Gurney G13's naming of his eldest son Richard would be a brother-naming**,
  stronger than the first-cousin-naming the 1586 candidate
  requires.[^packet50][^richard-rivet-1575] Against her: her father died intestate by 1597
  and her mother by 1586, leaving her (if unmarried) an orphan under her stepmother Agnes.
- **An unknown third Margaret (~5%).** The sweeps are broad, but the register sets have
  proven coverage holes.

An earlier version of this file claimed the 1586 Margaret was "the only Margaret
Rivet/Rivett/Ryvett of the right age in the region." That was wrong: the June 2026 sweep
missed the 1577/8 baptism because FindMyPast's Norfolk set omits Garveston and the IGI
extraction did not carry the earliest register book. Nor can the 1577/8 Margaret be cleared
by a prior death or marriage: the image-backed Norfolk collection holds **no marriage of any
Margaret Riv\* before 1731 and no burial of one between 1577 and 1611** (caveat: its
Garveston indexing lapses from the mid-1580s to 1651), and the June register read found no
Margaret Rivet burial at Garveston 1561–1625 at all.[^margaret-1577-fate] "Rybett" was the
1611 clerk's one-off rendering (the family spells itself *Rivet*; the Suffolk gentry line
*Ryvett*).[^fmpm]

**The marriage venue is an unused datum.** The wedding was at **Norwich St Martin at Palace**
— not Garveston, East Dereham, or any parish either family is otherwise documented in.
Whether Margaret was in service or resident in Norwich by 1611 (natural for either a
25-year-old or an unmarried 33-year-old) is an open question that bears on both candidates;
the register image may carry residence or status styling the index omits.[^fmpm]

**What would separate the candidates.** (1) The Whinburgh-with-Members court rolls (section
below): Robert died by 1597 and Francis at an unknown date after 1609 — a death presentment
or heir admission for either would name the surviving children. (2) Grigson's register transcript
(access-blocked; see the register-survival map) for any pre-1611 marriage or burial of the
1577/8 Margaret. (3) The 1584 marriage leaf of the register itself, for the "Podmer" spelling
and any styling detail.[^register-map]

Status: **possible, not proven — for either candidate.** No document names the 1611 bride's
baptismal identity. The three would-be direct proofs are exhausted: **Francis Rivet of
Garveston left no surviving will or administration** (the Norfolk probate index records no
Francis Rivett/Revett between a 1551 Hingham-clergyman will and 1770);[^probate-francis] the
wills of the two older Garveston Rivetts who *did* leave probate — **Margaret (Bate) Rivett,
widow (will 1584)** and **Robert Rivett (administration 1597)** — have been read at image
level and name **no son or grandson Francis, no younger Margaret, and no Gurney** (the 1584
will's legatees are Wright, Clayton, Raymond, and Newton kin, plus "Marye Ryvett my daughter
in lawe"; the 1597 admon grants Robert's estate to his widow **Agnes
Ryvett**);[^bridging-wills] and the probate routes are closed at national level too: a
Prerogative Court of Canterbury / TNA Discovery search for any Rivet, Rivett, or Revett of
Garveston, Dereham, or Norfolk in 1580–1640 returns nothing.[^pcc-rivett-negative] And the
1611 Gurney × Rybett marriage is indexed as a parish-register *marriage*, not a licence, so
no marriage allegation survives that could have named the bride's father.[^fmpm]

## The family and its geography

Garveston adjoins **East Dereham** (~4 miles), where Francis Gurney settled and John was
born; the Rivet presence there runs continuously from 1547 to 1652. The register images and
the image-backed Norfolk collection now separate **four Rivet household heads** in the
parental generations:[^reg-p19][^anc-61045-census]

- **Francis** (children Margaret 1586, Grace 1603; married Elizabeth Podmer 2 July 1584);
- **Robert** (children by an unnamed first wife: Agnes 1563, Robert 1565, William 1571, a
  child read as Miles October 1571, Richard 30 November 1575, Margarett 1 February 1577/8).
  He **remarried Agnes in April 1586** — so his first wife died 1578×1586, his children were
  raised by a stepmother, and the widow Agnes of his 1597 administration was stepmother to
  them all;
- **Edmund** (Elen, christened 11 April 1593; plausibly the Edmund Rivett of the 1613–14
  inventory);
- **Thomas** (Pagnell 1569 — but see below — and Frances 1578). Packet-50's bonus reads add
  further adult Rivets **William** (a son Thomas christened under the 1577 heading) and
  **John** (son George, 10 November 1576).[^packet50]

**The "Francis Rivet christened 1569" claim is withdrawn as unverified.** The image-backed
collection's Garveston slice carries no Francis Rivet baptism in 1569; its only 1569 Rivet
row is **"Pagnell Rivet, 24 October 1569, father Thomas"** — suggesting the IGI's "Francis
1569" was a misreading of that unusual forename. The expert read could not settle it at image
level: the supplied 1569/1570 register opening shows **no Rivet child at 24 October 1569 at
all** (the visible cluster reads as Cross and Fryer entries), so there is an image/record-link
mismatch or an index mislocation still to resolve.[^packet50][^anc-61045-census] Either way,
the viable Garveston baptism for the Francis who married in 1584 is **Frances Rivet,
20 November 1547** (aged 37 at the marriage and 62 at his last child — possible but
stretched) — or **he was not baptized at Garveston at all** and married there as an incomer.

Margaret (Bate) Rivett, the parish matriarch, was **buried 2 September 1584** — a ~3-month
deathbed sequence from her 5 June will, and twenty months before the 1586 Margaret's
christening; the namesake pattern quietly supports (without documenting) kinship between
Francis's household and the matriarch.[^prob][^anc-61045-census] A **Sarah Rivett, buried
8 September 1652**, is the collection's next Garveston Rivett event after the 1626 seam —
post-1626 family continuity, unassigned (Francis Jr's wife? an unmarried
daughter?).[^anc-61045-census]

### Richard Ryvett's Gressenhall household

Three miles the other way, **Richard Ryvett of Gressenhall** — origin now image- and
expert-confirmed as **Richard Rivet, baptized 30 November 1575 at Garveston, son of
Robert**[^packet50] — married Faith in 1602[^fmpr] and raised five children there, all
image-backed rows in the Norfolk collection:[^gressenhall-children]

| Baptized | Child |
|---|---|
| 1 Nov 1602 | Alice |
| 30 Dec 1604 | William |
| 19 Apr 1607 | Elizabeth |
| 8 Oct 1609 | Faithe (named for her mother; the "Fay Revett, 1609" of the FindMyPast index) |
| 26 Jan 1611/12 | Alice (the repeated name implies the first Alice died young) |

No baptism follows January 1611/12, so Faith's childbearing (or life) ended then —
consistent with Richard's later remarriage to Marian Thornton attested by the 1629/31
Thornton will (see the L-130 note below). An Alice Rivett was buried at Gressenhall
5 November 1646, and a Katherine Rivett married there in 1675 — the next
generation.[^gressenhall-children] When John Gurney was left motherless c. 1616–17, this
household held children aged roughly 5–15 — a plausible foster-sibling cohort, and **first
cousins of John under the 1577/8-Margaret hypothesis** (first cousins once removed under
the 1586 hypothesis).

## Garveston Rivett probate

The Garveston Rivetts who left probate cluster in the generation before and around Margaret's
parents. **Margaret Rivett, widow of Garveston, made a will proved 1584** — the matriarch of
the parish's Rivetts and the likely namesake of Margaret Rivet (christened 1586); the
"extracted probate" index renders her maiden name *Bate*. **Robert Rivett of Garveston died
intestate, with an administration in 1597** — a household head of the same generation. Around
them sits a run of mid-Norfolk Rivett wills and inventories in John's grandparents'-to-parents'
window: wills of John (1585), James (1589), John (1592), Thomas (1596) and William (1636), and
inventories of Edmund Rivett (1613–14) and Katherine Rivett (1626–27).[^probate-cluster] (Only
Margaret 1584 and Robert 1597 are indexed *of Garveston*; the other testators' parishes are
unconfirmed and may lie elsewhere in mid-Norfolk.)

The two Garveston wills have now been read from the FamilySearch register/act-book images, and
**neither bridges to Margaret Rivet (bp 1586) or to a Gurney.** Margaret (Bate) Rivett's 1584
will leaves to a Wright/Clayton/Raymond/Newton legatee network plus "Marye Ryvett my daughter in
lawe," and names no Francis Rivet, no younger Margaret, and no Gurney; Robert Rivett's 1597
administration grants his estate to his widow **Agnes Ryvett**, naming no children — the Agnes
he had married in April 1586, stepmother to his children by the earlier wife (including
Richard 1575 and Margarett 1577/8). So the older Garveston Rivett probate strengthens the
household map but does not supply the documentary link from Francis Rivet's household up to
the parish matriarch — and, materially for the candidate fork, Robert's admon names none of
his children either.[^bridging-wills]

## Register survival — the three-source map

Garveston's 1538–1675 register survives and is reachable through three imperfect routes;
knowing which covers what has repeatedly decided what a "negative" means:[^register-map]

1. **Ancestry's image-backed Norfolk collection (with the Norfolk Record Office)** serves the
   whole surviving register book as one 69-image piece — baptisms 1538–1675, marriages
   1539–1674, burials 1561–1625. Its *indexing* of Garveston lapses from the mid-1580s to
   1651, but the *images* are complete — an indexing gap, not an image gap. The 1584
   Rivet×Podmer marriage leaf sits at piece-images 52–54 (located, not yet pulled; the image
   order interleaves baptism/marriage/burial sections, so leaves must be calibrated
   leaf-by-leaf, not extrapolated).
2. **The FamilySearch film of the same register** (DGS 004033226, item 14) was the June 2026
   read — christenings, and the burial section 1561–1625, negative for any Margaret
   Rivet/Gurney burial and for any Gurney entry.[^reg-p19]
3. **Grigson's transcript** ("Garveston parish register transcripts, 1538–1845, by F.
   Grigson" — a 19th-century antiquarian fair copy covering Garveston and Thuxton,
   FamilySearch DGS 004115503) bridges every gap continuously, including the
   burials-after-1625 seam: the **23 October 1626 burial of Elizabeth Rivet** is recoverable
   only from it (or the NRO original), because the register's burial section ends at 1625.
   The film is digitized but **access-restricted** ("not currently digitally accessible") —
   readable only from a FamilySearch Center or via an NRO reproduction.[^grigson]

## Bearing on John Gurney G13

This materially supports the long-held hypothesis (case file; G13 research library) that
John, left motherless ~1616–17 when Francis remarried Anne Browning and moved his new family
to London, was raised by **local maternal kin a few miles from his East Dereham birthplace**.
Both Margaret candidates keep the fostering inside the same six-mile arc, but they point at
different hearths:

- Under the **1586 hypothesis**, the anchor is her mother: **Elizabeth (Podmer) Rivet was
  buried at Garveston on 23 October 1626** — the probable maternal grandmother, alive at
  Garveston through the entire window after Margaret's death when the motherless John
  (b. c. 1609/10) and Edward (b. c. 1611) needed raising, four miles from their birthplace.
  A living grandmother is a far stronger fostering anchor than generic "maternal kin"; John
  was ~16–17 when she died — old enough by then for apprenticeship (the tailoring-trade
  route).[^elizabeth-burial-1626]
- Under the **1577/8 hypothesis**, Margaret's parents were both dead by 1597, and her nearest
  kin in 1616 were her brother **Richard at Gressenhall** (household above) and her
  stepmother Agnes — the fostering anchor becomes Gressenhall, which is where the son-name
  Richard already pointed.

The identification also supplies a non-paternal route for John's tailoring trade
(the Merchant Taylors' Company search was negative for any John son of Francis) and a concrete
origin for the son named Richard. The earlier G13-companion "negative" here tested the wrong
surname — it searched for *Gurney* baptisms in Gressenhall/Garveston; the actual evidence is
the *Rivett* records above.

This maternal-kin route is not the only fostering candidate. If the first-marriage children
stayed in Norfolk rather than joining the London household, the **paternal Gurney kin** are an
equally live option: Francis's father Henry Gurnay (G15) lived at Great Ellingham until his
death (1621×1623), and Francis's brothers — Thomas, Henry, Edward, and Anthony Gurnay — were
the boys' uncles. The indexed adult-trail search for John's brother **Edward** is exhausted
without a candidate; the remaining paternal-fostering route is the targeted Great Ellingham
and West Barsham manorial and probate work under L-136.

## The Whinburgh-with-Members manorial unlock

**The one record class that could still prove Margaret's parentage outright is manorial.**
Garveston had no resident lord; its copyholders answered to the **Manor of
Whinburgh-with-Members**, whose courts covered "Garvestone, Reymerston, Thuxton, Mattishall
and Yaxham." Copyhold court rolls routinely name a deceased tenant's heirs (copyhold = land
held "by copy of court roll," passing only through surrender and admission in the lord's
court): if Francis Rivet (d. at an unknown date after 1609) or Robert Rivett (d. by 1597) held copyhold, a death
presentment and heir admission — potentially naming **Margaret wife of Francis Gurney** or
her siblings — should sit in these courts. A deathbed **surrender to uses** (the tenant
surrenders his copyhold to the lord "to the use of" named beneficiaries — a common
will-substitute, in effect a de-facto will) would even explain why Francis Rivet left no
probate: if his land was all copyhold, no will was needed. The absence of probate is itself
weak evidence he was a copyholder.[^whinburgh-film] That Garveston land was held this way is
documented in a neighbour's will: **John Mussett of Garveston** (will 28 November 1654,
proved November 1660) left his wife his house, meadow, and pasture there *"as well freehold
as Copy hold"* — a Garveston copyhold-holding household in mid-century, though the will names
no Rivett or Gurney.[^mussett-will]

**The manor's archive survives nearly intact, split between film and the Norfolk Record
Office:**

- **Filmed (FamilySearch):** court rolls **1595–1663** (British Film 1472614 items 10–11 =
  DGS 004389244), 1664–1743 (Film 1472654 items 1–5 = DGS 004389566), 1743–1755 (Film
  1472614 item 9), and 1756–1790 (Film 1702191 items 6–7 = DGS 004395112); microfilmed 1991
  from NRO originals. Full-text name-extraction on these films is demonstrably partial — a
  single extracted Rivett (the 1755 homage) despite the family's proven tenancy — so
  **full-text negatives inside the films mean nothing; the 1595–1663 item needs an
  image-level walk** (roughly film images 1395–1784 cover the pre-1639 Latin-era
  courts).[^whinburgh-film]
- **Unfilmed (NRO originals):** the richer classes — **surrenders and admissions, 1564–1800
  (NRS 21256–21355, ≈100 pieces)**, the direct heir-naming series, beginning thirty years
  before the earliest filmed roll; rough court books 1593–1790 (NRS 17009–18); early court
  rolls 1594–1629 (NRS 27281, NRS 15110, NRS 27282–85) covering Francis Rivet's prime
  window; and a minute book 1548–1578, survey 1565, and court book 1560 covering the
  matriarch's and Robert's lifetimes.[^whinburgh-mdr]

**Gurneys sat in the same manor's orbit.** Two mid-17th-century court leaves carry
Gurney-family text — cautious, triage-level readings only, because the images are
low-contrast and struck through, and the two leaf files are **date-inverted against their
filenames** (cite by ark and visible heading, never the file slug).[^whinburgh-1639-1648]
The leaf at ark `3:1:S3HT-6X4K-X5` carries, under a heading read as **6 September 1639**, an
admission/surrender-style copyhold entry whose readable words include *Henry*, *Allen*,
*Mary*, *"said Henry Gurney,"* and *Mattishall* — the strongest reading is an entry involving
an Allen and a deceased or related Henry Gurney, but the inheritance structure (a machine
transcript suggested an heir tracing through "a sister of the said Henry") is **not safely
resolved** and awaits a tighter re-crop. The leaf at ark `3:1:S3HT-6X45-XZ`, headed
**26 October 1648**, is **uncertain and probably not actionable** without a better image; its
machine transcript adds the tokens "Notherogate [Northgate?] in Garveston" and a possible
living "Robt Gurn[ey]" — who that Robert Gurney would be is itself an open question. If the
1639 Henry resolves to a Gurney of the G15 household — Francis G14's brother Henry Gurnay is
a dramatic fit — the paternal-uncle fostering geography for John G13 would gain a documented
Gurney copyhold two miles from Garveston; the confirmation check-list is then to test G15
Henry Gurnay's daughters against an Allen (or Morehouse-adjacent) marriage. Nothing is
promoted from these leaves yet. Tenancy continuity is proven at the far end: a **Francis
Rivett was sworn of the Whinburgh homage in 1755**, and a **John Gurney held land in the
manor in 1754** — the families were still tenants of this one manor 170 years
on.[^whinburgh-1750s]

**The manor itself is an old Gournay possession.** Whinburgh ("Winburgh") was one of the
Bardolf caput manors — with Caister and Cantley — held in chief **"as parcel of the barony of
Gurnay,"** the forfeited senior-line honour the Bardolfs inherited through Juliana de
Gournay. The manor whose courts governed the maternal-kin Rivetts' Garveston copyhold was
therefore once part of the senior Gournay barony — an incidental convergence of the two
families' geographies three centuries apart, and a practical pointer: the manor's archival
fonds descend through the Bardolf-heir successor lords.[^whinburgh-bardolf]

## The Podmers — Margaret's probable maternal family (under the 1586 hypothesis)

"Podmer" rests on one index witness (the FHL-film extraction of the 1584 marriage), but the
surname is real in exactly the right micro-geography. A **John Podmer married Mary [—] at
Hingham on 2 November 1609** — Hingham is ~4–5 miles from Garveston — their daughter
**Alice was baptized there 27 December 1609**, and John Podmer was **buried at Hingham on
26 September 1623**, closing a fourteen-year household.[^podmer-hingham] A **Richard Pitmer
married Elizabeth [—] at East Tuddenham** (~3 miles from Garveston) **on 24 July 1608**, and
a Marget Pytmer was buried at Norwich St Stephen in 1592 — Podmer/Pitmer/Pytmer/Patmer forms
cluster thinly but genuinely in central Norfolk 1584–1623.[^podmer-hingham] Nationally the
name is very rare (four TNA Discovery hits, with its gravity in the Shropshire/Staffordshire
Podmore country); the one early Prerogative Court of Canterbury will — **Richard Podmer of
Leaton, Shropshire, proved 22 May 1615** — has been read at image level and is a **clean
negative**: Shropshire-facing legatees only, no Norfolk token, no Rivett, no
Gurney.[^podmer-pcc]

**The "no Podmer probate in Norfolk, ever" negative is unproven for the variant family.** The
probate-index sweep used the patterns `podm*` and `p?dm*r` — neither catches the lived
variants the records themselves surface (**Pitmer, Pytmer, Patmer**). A re-sweep on
`p?tm*r`/`patm*` remains open, and the same variant breadth applies to the expected spelling
of the 1584 bride.[^podmer-negative] Any Podmer-variant probate c. 1590–1660 could name
Rivett in-laws or Rivett grandchildren — the transitive bridge the parentage question needs.

## The Wymondham Francis Rivett — a distinct man, split off

A Francis Rivett fathering children at Wymondham (James 1622, Margaret baptized 17 November
1624, Fran[n]cis 1626) briefly threatened to redirect the Garveston chronology. He is now
split off: **Francis Rivett married Matthy Gray at Wymondham on 1 October 1620** (image-backed
row, NRO PD 184/8), thirteen months before the first Wymondham child — while Elizabeth
(Podmer) Rivet was still living at Garveston until her burial in October 1626. The Wymondham
man is therefore a distinct Francis with his own marriage, not the Garveston Francis
relocated — plausibly a son of the **Buxton** household of Francis and Ruth Rivet, whose five
children are block-indexed under a uniform "1600" (the signature of a same-day family baptism
or an undated register block, not a real single year). Follow-ons, held as speculation:
Martha Rivett, buried 27 August 1681 at Wymondham, is plausibly the widow Matthy/Martha;
Margarett Rivett, buried 5 July 1686 there, is plausibly the daughter Margaret (1624)
unmarried at 62; and no Francis Rivett burial appears at Wymondham at all (the earliest
Rivett burial there is 1681, with a pre-1656 coverage caveat).[^wymondham-francis]

The wider lesson stands: **"Francis Rivet" is not unique in Norfolk c. 1600** — Garveston
(m. Elizabeth Podmer 1584), Buxton (wife Ruth, c. 1600), Wymondham (m. Matthy Gray 1620) —
so any record naming a bare "Francis Rivet(t)" needs parish or wife corroboration before it
attaches to Margaret's father.[^wymondham-francis]

## Relationship to the Suffolk Ryvett gentry — open

Whether the mid-Norfolk Garveston/Gressenhall Rivetts are a cadet of the Suffolk gentry
Ryvetts (Brandeston, Bricet, Rishangles, Rattlesden — see
[`sources/corpus_supplement/ryvett-suffolk-visitation-pedigrees-metcalfe.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/sources/corpus_supplement/ryvett-suffolk-visitation-pedigrees-metcalfe.md))
or unconnected is unresolved (lead L-133). The Brandeston branch (John Ryvett × Ann Bacon)
has a daughter Margaret and a Gressenhall (Trench) marriage, but the Garveston Margaret is the
stronger candidate for Francis Gurney's wife.

## Myrable Ryvett's marriage

The primary Visitation of Suffolk (Metcalfe, p. 63) records **Myrable Ryvett married William
Burd, Customer of London**, not Sir John Heydon of Baconsthorpe. The false Ryvett–Heydon
marriage has been removed from the G14 fact sheet and corrected in the John Gurney case file;
the Gurney–Heydon tie via Anne Heydon m. William Gurney V is independent and unaffected.

## Open questions / leads

- L-128 (priority 95) — **which Garveston Margaret married Francis Gurney in 1611.** The fork
  is set out in the Finding above (~55/40/5). Every register and probate route is exhausted:
  the 1586 christening names no mother; the c.1617–1640 register pages hold no Gurney entry
  of any kind; the two older Garveston probates (Margaret Bate Rivett will 1584, NCC reg.
  "Bate" fo. 399; Robert Rivett admon 1597, NCC act book vol. 8) name no Francis, no younger
  Margaret, no Gurney; Francis Rivet left no probate. The remaining direct-proof class is
  **manorial** — the Whinburgh-with-Members walk (L-238) and the gated NRO surrender series —
  with the Grigson look-ups (L-242) as the cheap discriminator for the 1577/8 candidate's
  pre-1611 fate.
- L-129 — Francis Rivet's wife and child set. **Francis Rivet's wife is most likely Elizabeth
  Podmer** (m. 2 July 1584 Garveston; see the Finding above). The IGI indexes attribute to Francis
  Rivet of Garveston a child set of **Margaret (1586), Marye (1588), Elen (1593), Thomas (1595),
  Grace (1603), Alice (1606), and Francis (1609)**, the last two explicitly to "Francis and
  Elizabeth."[^francis-podmer-1584] **Conflict to resolve at image level:** the IGI gives **Elen
  (1593)**'s father as Francis, whereas the repo's register image-read gives the Elen-1593 father
  as **Edmund** — the image read controls until re-examined, so the remaining indexed child
  assignments need image comparison before the full sibling set is fixed. The register's batch-2
  pages (1627–1675) add no further Rivett entry.[^reg-p19] The 1584 marriage leaf is now located
  to piece-images 52–54 of the Ancestry register piece (pull open — corroboration, since the
  marriage is index-confirmed twice); the "Podmer" spelling check rides on it (L-242).[^register-map]
- L-130 — **closed.** Richard Ryvett of Gressenhall's origin — **Richard Rivet, baptized
  30 November 1575 at Garveston, son of Robert** — is image- and expert-confirmed, and his
  Gressenhall child sequence is resolved at image-backed index level (household table
  above).[^packet50][^richard-rivet-1575] Under the 1586-Margaret hypothesis (Robert and
  Francis as brothers) Richard and Margaret were **first cousins**; under the 1577/8
  hypothesis they were **full siblings** — either way he accounts for John Gurney G13 naming
  a son Richard. Two residuals stay on this file without a lead: Faith's burial is unlocated
  (the FMP blank is coverage-unknown), and the 1629/31 will of **John Thornton of Gressenhall,
  husbandman** names "my wellbeloved sonne in law Richard Ryvett and Marian his wife" —
  **Marian (Thornton), not Faith** — so either Faith died and Richard remarried (the
  childbearing gap after January 1611/12 fits) or two Richards need disentangling. The
  documented Gressenhall in-law network is Thornton and Blade; Richard himself left no
  probate.[^thornton-will][^probate-gressenhall]
- L-131 — Margaret's burial c.1616–17. The Garveston register burial section (1561–1625) was
  read and holds **no Margaret Rivet or Margaret Gurney burial** c.1616–17; the batch-2 pages
  added nothing.[^reg-p19] The FindMyPast Norfolk Burials set omits East Dereham and West

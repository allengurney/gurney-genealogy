## Research Appendix

### Lineage Status
**Confirmed.** Renaud is named in the La Ferté-en-Bray priory charter of 989–996, making him the first Lord of Gournay in this lineage attested by a surviving primary source document. The charter was issued by his son, not Renaud himself, but is close enough in time and specific enough in detail (naming wife Alberade, referencing elder brother Hugh) to qualify as reliable evidence.

### Sources Consulted This Session
- DG-I, pp. 23–25. Full text in project files.
- Ancestors_v3.json (all vital data and land holdings).

### Key Findings from Hannay (1867)
- Hannay (pp. 59–62) provides the fullest narrative of Renaud. The La Ferté-en-Bray charter was witnessed by "Richard I, Duke of Normandy (Sans-Peur); the second Richard, his son; Robert, Archbishop of Rouen; and a Count Robert." Since Archbishop Robert acquired his see in 989 and Sans-Peur died in 996, the charter dates firmly to 989–996.
- "The donations were very considerable, and show that the house was great."
- Hannay calls Renaud's wife "Alberarda" (DG uses "Alberade") — both are variant spellings of the same name.
- Hannay speculates Renaud was on Normandy's eastern frontier during the transition from Norse culture to French: "Renaud de Gournay fell just into the transition time — the stage in which the Norman gentleman was developing out of the Norse sea-king."
- Gautier de la Ferté, the younger son, founded "a junior line, which took the surname of de la Ferté" — one of the earliest Norman families to adopt a territorial surname.

### Negative Results
- No deed issued by Renaud himself found in project sources.
- No death date, burial site, or additional children beyond Hugh and Gautier documented.
- Alberade's family origin entirely unknown.

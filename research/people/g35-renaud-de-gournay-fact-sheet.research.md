## Working Notes

### La Ferté-en-Bray priory charter (DG-Supp Note 8)
2026-04-18 — DG-Supp Note 8 (p. 731) adds a critical detail about the La Ferté-en-Bray priory charter: "M. Leopold Delisle has also examined in detail the archives at Evreux, and is quite certain this charter is not there." This means the La Ferté foundation charter (the 989–996 document naming Renaud and Alberade) was sought but not found in the archives where it would most logically reside. Delisle was the foremost Norman archival scholar of the 19th century.

**Implication:** The charter's existence is attested by DG and by the *MS. Histoire des Seigneurs de Gournay*, but the original document has not been independently verified since DG's time. This does not invalidate the charter — DG may have been working from a copy or an older transcription — but it means the primary-source confirmation of Renaud's existence rests on DG's account of a charter whose archival location is now unknown. Lineage status remains "Confirmed" (DG's account is internally consistent and the charter's content is plausible for the period), but this limitation should be noted.

### Gautier de la Ferté — the charter's actual subject
2026-04-18 — The charter is technically a foundation/endowment document for the priory of La Ferté-en-Bray, executed by Gautier (Walter) de la Ferté, who names his father Renaud and his father's wife Alberade. Gautier is therefore a collateral — a younger son or brother who founded a religious house. DG-I p. 25 describes this. The charter dates to 989–996 based on the abbacy of the recipient (Bec or a related Norman foundation).

This means Renaud had at least two sons: Hugh II (G34, the direct-line heir) and Gautier de la Ferté (collateral, founder of the priory). This is documented in the fact sheet but the research companion should hold the fuller detail.

### Planché on Renaud, Alberada, and La Ferté
2026-05-02 — Planché's Hugh de Gournay chapter repeats the La Ferté charter structure: Renaud is the first family member found in a charter; Alberada is his wife; their sons are Hugh and Gautier; Gautier held La Ferté-en-Bray and founded the priory around 990 at Hugh's request or with his consent for the souls of Renaud and Alberada. Planché also says the Wace Sire de la Ferté at Hastings was not from this Gournay cadet line, because the La Ferté Gournay line had already died out without issue before the invasion. This supports keeping La Ferté as the early documentary cadet-line place while avoiding a false Hastings identification.[^planche-renaud-laferte]

### Pettigrew on Renaud, Alberarda, La Ferte, and Sigy

Pettigrew's account is especially useful for Renaud because it restates the family cluster in one connected passage. He says Renaud or Reginald is the first lord of Gournay named in any written deed or instrument; his name occurs in Gautier de la Ferte's foundation deed for the Priory of La Ferte-en-Bray; the deed was made at the command of Gautier's brother Hugh; and the witness/dating frame places it between 989 and 996. Pettigrew then says the charter proves that Renaud married Alberarda and had two sons, Hugh and Gautier.[^pettigrew-renaud]

The same passage resolves the Sigy relationship without changing the direct line. Hugh, Renaud's elder son, was lord of Gournay. Gautier, the younger son, had a son Hugh de la Ferte, founder of Sigi; a later Hugh de la Ferte became a monk at St Ouen at Rouen, gave the priory of Sigi to that abbey, and confirmed his father's gifts. By these acts, Pettigrew says, the seignories and lands of La Ferte reverted to the elder branch. This keeps La Ferte and Sigy as early cadet-line documentary places while preserving the direct Renaud -> Hugh line.[^pettigrew-renaud]

### French Wikipedia genealogy discrepancy
2026-04-18 — French Wikipedia ("Famille de Gournay") states: "Il est peut-être le frère de Gautier, fondateur de la collégiale de La Ferté" (He [Hugh III, G33] may perhaps be the brother of Gautier, founder of the collegiate church of La Ferté). This is a significant reordering: it makes Gautier de la Ferté a contemporary of Hugh III (G33), not of Hugh II (G34). Under this model, Gautier would be a son of Hugh II rather than of Renaud.

DG's genealogy places Gautier as a son of Renaud (G35), making the charter a direct attestation of Renaud himself. The French Wikipedia model, if correct, would push the charter attestation down one generation — it would name Hugh II, not Renaud. This discrepancy needs further investigation. The charter text itself (as quoted in DG) should specify whether Renaud is named as the father or grandfather of Gautier. DG-I p. 25 appears to state the father relationship clearly.

### Alberade — the only named spouse before the 11th century
2026-04-18 — Alberade is the only named wife in the Gournay line before Basilia (wife of Hugh III, G33). No details about her family, origin, or dates survive beyond her name in the La Ferté charter. The absence of further documentation is consistent with the general scarcity of 10th-century Norman records.

## Open Questions

1. **Charter location:** Delisle confirmed it was not in the Evreux archives (DG-Supp Note 8). Where did DG see it? His source appears to be the *MS. Histoire des Seigneurs de Gournay*. If DG was working from a transcription within that manuscript rather than from the original charter, this is a secondary rather than primary source attestation.
2. **Gautier's relationship to Renaud:** DG says son; French Wikipedia (citing unspecified modern scholarship) suggests Gautier might be a brother of Hugh III (making him a son of Hugh II rather than Renaud). The charter text should resolve this — what exactly does DG quote?
3. **Renaud's dates:** "c. 970 — dates uncertain" is essentially a guess. If Hugh I's dates shift (see G36 companion), Renaud's should shift correspondingly.

## Fact Sheet Improvement Notes

1. **Charter caveat:** The La Ferté charter is the evidentiary linchpin for Renaud's existence. The fact sheet correctly highlights this, but a note that Delisle could not find the original in the Evreux archives (DG-Supp Note 8) would add appropriate scholarly nuance without undermining the claim.
2. **Collateral son Gautier:** The fact sheet mentions Gautier in passing. The full priory foundation story is rich and could be expanded slightly — Gautier's religious foundation is the reason we know Renaud existed at all.
3. **Dates:** Same issue as G36 — if Hugh I's birth is adjusted earlier, Renaud's dates should shift too.

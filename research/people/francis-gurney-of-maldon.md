# Francis Gurney of Maldon (1628–1677)

Research file for the younger Francis Gurney, merchant, Alderman and Bailiff of Maldon, Essex, fourth documented child of Francis Gurney G14 and Anne Browning — and, therefore, a half-brother or brother of Allen's presumed G13 John Gurney-1 of Massachusetts (depending on whether the 1628 child is from the Margaret Rybett or Anne Browning marriage). Not in the direct line. Classified Related / Collateral per `AI-Rules.md` §5.

Primary source for this file is Charles A. Bernau's 1913 *British Archivist* biography (`sourceId: british-archivist-bernau-1913`, proposed), supplemented by Daniel Gurney's *Record* and the reconstructed 1858 loose supplement. Full corpus extract at `sources/corpus_supplement/The_British_Archivist-Unrecorded-Biographies-Francis-Gurney.md`.[^bernau-1913-source]

## Baseline

Francis Gurney of Maldon was baptized at St Benet Fink, London, on 13 November 1628, the son of Francis Gurney G14 (Merchant Taylor, of St Benet Fink) and Anne Browning (daughter of William Browning of Norwich, later of Maldon).[^parentage] He relocated to Maldon, Essex — his mother's family's adopted town — and by the 1650s had built a merchant household there. In or before 1654 he married Anne, daughter of Jeremiah Browning, alderman of Maldon; she was baptized at Maldon on 23 July 1637 and was about nine years her husband's junior.[^marriage] Bernau reads the two successive Browning marriages (William Browning → Francis G14; Jeremiah Browning → Francis of Maldon) as a probable cousin-marriage.[^cousin]

He was appointed an Alderman of Maldon on 12 August 1662 by the deed printed by Daniel Gurney at *Record* p. 539, and served as one of the Bailiffs of Maldon in 1664 (attested in the St Mary's, Maldon, list of communicants).[^office] Ten children were baptized at Maldon between 1655 and 1667.[^children]

Anne's father Jeremiah Browning probably died in 1647, when Anne was about ten years old: letters of administration for "Jeremiah BROWNINGE, late of Maldon, Co. Essex, deceased" were granted in the Prerogative Court of Canterbury on 23 December 1647 to "Jane BROWNINGE the relict of the said deceased." Bernau treats this Jeremiah as the likely father-in-law of Francis of Maldon, while flagging the possibility that the entry could refer instead to a Browning great-uncle.[^jeremiah-admin]

He drowned in his own well at his rented house in St Mary's Maldon on the night of Sunday 12 August 1677, aged 48. The death was officially ruled a suicide and his personal estate was forfeited to the Crown.[^death] Samuel Pepys, then Secretary to the Admiralty, entered a caveat against the grant of the estate on 21 August 1677 — a protective action that Bernau believed was aimed at preserving the property for Francis's widow and minor children.[^pepys]

Half the children Bernau records did not survive childhood — Francis (1), George, Charles, and Francis (2) all died young. Four of the surviving sons' names (John, Thomas, Richard, George) match the Bernau-speculated Richard and George of the elder Francis G14's generation, which is the naming-pattern parallel Bernau leans on to argue those two may also have been G14's sons.[^naming-speculation]

## Business and lawsuits

Bernau prints extended extracts from two Chancery bills filed by Francis of Maldon in his final fourteen months.

**1676: *Gourney v. Dabbs et al.* (Bridges 489/64 and Hamilton 521/90).** Filed 3 May 1676. In September 1675 Thomas Dabbs of Maldon, "woll kember" (wool-comber), owed Francis Gurney £14 8s 4d for a bargain of wool. Dabbs, fearing arrest, colluded with the other defendants (the Bartell/Bartlett brothers and their wives, Robert and Jane Beard, and Hugh Browne) to move his goods out of reach. Gurney entered an attachment through the Court of Records of Maldon, using the borough's Serjeants at Mace (Peter Aldridge and John Day) to seize the goods, valued at £11 12s 6d and carted away by Christopher Savadge. Abraham Bartlett countered with a bill-of-sale claim he asserted was genuine, not a fraudulent device; the Answer denies the collusion characterization.[^lawsuit-1676]

**1677: *Gourney v. Cornell* (Bridges 608/99).** Filed 22 May 1677, less than three months before Francis's death. In 1673, during the Third Anglo-Dutch War, Francis had asked William Cornell of Poole, Dorset, to send fuller's earth ("tobacco pipe clay") from Purbeck — a commodity that peaked in wartime value because of supply disruption. Cornell replied that he could not ship during the war. After peace returned and prices collapsed to half their former value, Cornell nevertheless shipped "several vessels" of clay to Maldon for Francis's account at 22 shillings per ton. The clay proved poor quality — "badness, roughness and coarseness" — and Francis's warehousemen and chapmen refused it. Francis settled up with Cornell at Maldon in October 1675, paying about £30 for the clay plus a consignment of Purbeck stones, on the understanding (per Francis) that Cornell would approve whatever distressed sales Francis could arrange and settle any remainder against the stones overpayment. Cornell later repudiated the agreement and obtained a King's Bench arrest for a further £43.[^lawsuit-1677]

The Cornell suit was pending when Francis drowned; no Answer was ever filed.[^no-answer]

Together the two lawsuits give a detailed merchant's-eye view of a provincial trading business in the final two years of its proprietor's life: a wool-combing debtor who absconded with his inventory, and a long-distance supplier who shipped bad goods at a bad time and then sued for more money. Francis's estate inventory the following October (see below) lists salt, coal, hay, rye, 1,000 faggots, salt-making equipment, livestock, and outstanding debts — consistent with a multi-commodity merchant operating out of the quay at Maldon, not a specialist cloth dealer.

[^lawsuit-1676]: *Bernau 1913*, "His Business and Lawsuits" section, first paragraph, citing TNA C6/Bridges 489/64 and Hamilton 521/90.
[^lawsuit-1677]: *Bernau 1913*, "His Business and Lawsuits" section, second paragraph, citing TNA C6/Bridges 608/99.
[^no-answer]: *Bernau 1913*, concluding line of "His Business and Lawsuits": "Francis GOURNEY's tragic death only a few weeks after this Bill was filed probably accounts for our finding no Answer to it."

## Death, Crown forfeiture, and the Pepys caveat

Francis of Maldon was found drowned in his own well on Sunday 12 August 1677. The contemporaneous ruling of suicide invoked the crown forfeiture of a *felo de se*'s personal estate, a legal trap Bernau frames as probably unjust by modern standards: "it is probable that a modern coroner's jury would have considered it an accidental death or, at the worst, suicide during temporary insanity." The position of the rented Gurney house on the Blackwater (available as an obvious drowning route) plus the use of the domestic well argue against deliberate drowning.[^death-scene]

Four caveats were entered within two weeks of the death, each preserved in state or ecclesiastical registers:

| Date | Registrar | Caveator | On behalf of | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 August 1677 | P.C.C. Caveats | Julian Swallow | Dr. Thomas Browning S.T.P. "chief creditor" | P.C.C. |
| 20 August 1677 | S.P. Dom. Entry Book | Mr. Brent, in Mr. Secretary's name | Lord Treasurer | S.P. Dom. Entry Book 45/42 |
| 21 August 1677 | S.P. Dom. Entry Book | (at desire of Samuel Pepys) | Gurney family | S.P. Dom. Entry Book 45/42; S.P. Dom. Car. II. 396/21 fol. 34 |
| 25 August 1677 | P.C.C. Caveats | Miller | Thomas Haskin, creditor | P.C.C. |

Bernau is explicit that the 13 August caveat, nominally entered "for the chief creditor," most likely functioned as family-protective cover by Francis's brother-in-law Dr. Thomas Browning; "'The chief creditor' is probably a legal fiction, though it may be correct."[^first-caveat] The Pepys caveat eight days later was less ambiguous — Pepys was at that date Secretary to the Admiralty, and contemporary diary annotators interpreted his action as a kindly intervention to frustrate the Crown forfeiture.[^pepys-again]

Exchequer Special Commission No. 6222, dated 20 August 1677, was directed to the Sheriff of Essex to take Francis's goods into the King's hands. The inquisition was held at Chelmsford on 11 October 1677. Francis was adjudged to have committed suicide on 12 August 1677 possessed of goods to the value of £560 13s 4d. Robert Reader of Baddow, who had drowned on the same day (20 August Entry Book explicitly pairs them: "who drowned themselves the 12th of this instant August"), was adjudged dead "but whether by suicide or not they do not know."[^commission]

[^first-caveat]: *Bernau 1913*, "His Death" section, paragraph after the 13 August caveat.
[^pepys-again]: *Bernau 1913*, "His Death" section: "We have seen it stated in an annotated edition of his 'Diary' that this caveat was entered by him as a kindly action to save the property for the GOURNEY family, if possible."
[^commission]: *Bernau 1913*, "His Property" section, first paragraph, citing Exchequer Special Commission No. 6222.

The schedule shows an active salt-maker's operation at Goldhanger (salt-pans, salt tubs, saltcote leaseholds, 100 + 40 bushels of finished salt, 360 bushels of coal for boiling) — this is where the bulk of Francis's fixed productive assets sat. The Maldon house held the household goods and £300 worth of unspecified trading stock.

## Thomas Browning, D.D.

Dr. Thomas Browning, S.T.P. — the "chief creditor" on whose behalf Julian Swallow entered the 13 August 1677 caveat — was, Bernau argues, Francis of Maldon's brother-in-law. The core evidence is:[^thomas-browning]

- Foster's *Alumni Oxonienses*: "Thomas BROWNING, B.A. from Pemb. College, Cambridge, 1635 (incorp. as M.A. 1st Feb. 1642/3) D.D. per literas Regias 1660, rector of Wickham Bishop, Essex, 1661."
- Lay Subsidy 246/12 (Hearth Tax, 15 Car. II., 1663/4): Dr. Browning's house at St Mary's Maldon was untenanted — establishes a Maldon property distinct from the Wickham Bishop rectory.
- Feet of Fines, Hilary Term 32 & 33 Car. II. (1680): fine between Stephen Crispe and Thomas Browneing S.T.P., relating to land in Woodham Ferris, Essex.
- Will of Henry Jermyn of Wickham Bishop 1680 (Comm. London–Essex & Herts, "Heydon" 481): names "Dr. Thomas BROWNING and Anne, his wife."
- Will of Thomas Browning himself, dated 16 June 1694, proved Comm. London–Essex & Herts 3 May 1705: names his daughter Ann as wife of the Rev. Christopher Wragg of Little Wigborrow, Essex. No Gurneys named.

The sister-pair hypothesis is explicitly Bernau's: "It is possible that Francis GURNEY and Anne, his sister, may have married a sister and brother, Anne BROWNING and Dr. Thomas BROWNING." Under this reading, Thomas Browning's wife Anne is the same "Anne (probably the eldest child)" of Francis G14 who appears at item 8 of Bernau's St Benet Fink list, "Living in 1634 (Visit. London)." The absence of any Gurney in Thomas Browning's 1694 will is a counter-indicator but not dispositive — by 1694 the Gurney connection had been severed for over a generation and the Maldon Gurneys' fortunes had collapsed with Francis's 1677 forfeiture.

[^thomas-browning]: *Bernau 1913*, "Thomas Browning, D.D." section.

## "The Problem" — the Norwich Quaker question

Bernau's article closes with the genealogical problem that motivated the whole biography: whether Francis of Maldon's eldest son John (born 7 October 1655, baptized 30 October 1655 at Maldon, living aged 9 at the 1664 Essex Visitation) is the same man as **John Gurney of St Gregory's, Norwich**, cordwainer — a Quaker who married Elizabeth Swanton of Grundisburgh, Suffolk, at Woodbridge on 4 October 1687, and who died in 1721 aged 66.[^the-problem]

That John of St Gregory's is the documented founder of the Norwich banking Gurneys (and thus, further downstream, the direct ancestor of Elizabeth Fry, John Joseph Gurney, and the Overend & Gurney financial house). The identification has substantial stakes for English genealogy outside Allen's direct line, and Bernau notes that a member of the Gurney family had offered a reward in 1913 for evidence that would prove or disprove the identification. None appears to have been forthcoming.

Key chronology, per Bernau:

| Event | John of Maldon (son of Francis, b. 1655) | John of St Gregory's Norwich |
|---|---|---|
| Birth | 7 October 1655, Maldon | c. 1655 (d. 1721 aged 66) |
| Visible at age 9 | 1664 Essex Visitation, Maldon | — |
| Disappears from Maldon | After 1664 | — |
| First Norwich visibility | — | Before 1687 marriage |
| Marriage | — | 4 October 1687, Woodbridge, to Elizabeth Swanton |
| Death | Not the Maldon bachelor who died 1681 (his uncle) | 1721, aged 66 |

Bernau emphasizes that the Maldon bachelor John who died in 1681 was the wrong John — that was an uncle of the 1655 John, not the 1655 John himself. The identification of the 1655 John with the Norwich cordwainer remains an open problem. Family tradition supports it; "certainly their ages tally"; no contemporary primary document ties them together.[^the-problem-summary]

This question is separate from Allen's Candidate B case for John Gurney-1 of Massachusetts. Allen's G13 emigrant was in Weymouth by June 1641, decades before the 1655 Maldon John was born. The two problems share a family and a surname, but not a person.

Cross-context: The plebeian Norwich Gurney family documented by Walter Rye in *The Norfolk Antiquarian Miscellany* (`sourceId: rye-norfolk-antiquarian`) — shoemakers, cordwainers, and laceweavers of Norwich from whom the Quaker Gurney banking line eventually descends — is the target group for Bernau's "Problem." Rye's article and Bernau's 1913 biography together define the evidentiary frame for any future resolution.

## Relationship to Allen's direct line

Francis of Maldon is not in Allen's direct line. He is either:

- A **brother** of G13 John-1 if the current fact sheet's model holds (Francis G14 → Anne Browning → both John G13 c. 1609/10 and Francis-of-Maldon 1628) — but this would require Anne Browning to have been Francis G14's only wife, which the March 2026 NRO PD 12/1 discovery of the 1611 Margaret Rybett marriage now rules out for the earlier children.
- A **half-brother** of G13 John-1 under the Candidate B model: John G13 c. 1609/10 from Francis's first marriage to Margaret Rybett; Francis-of-Maldon 1628 from Francis's second marriage to Anne Browning. This is the current working reading.

Under either reading, the Maldon line is a collateral sibling-branch that lived in Essex while Allen's line was in New England. They had no demonstrated contact after G13's emigration; Bernau's article identifies no colonial relative and none of the Maldon children is known to have emigrated.

The Norwich banking Gurneys — if the 1655 Maldon John did become John of St Gregory's Norwich — are a first-cousin-once-removed branch to Allen's line, descending from Francis G14 through a different son than G13.

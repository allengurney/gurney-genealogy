# John and Sarah Gurney of Providence, Rhode Island (1695–1714)

Unplaced colonial Gurneys. The Providence town records carry a short, self-contained Gurney story: a stranger named John Gurney warned out of town in 1695–96, and a Sarah Gurney — evidently the widow Sarah Field, remarried to a Gurney — whose will was proved before the town council in 1714. No documented connection to the Braintree–Weymouth line (John Gurney-1 G13, Richard G12), though the dates would allow the 1695 John to be a grandson of the emigrant generation.

## 1695–96 — John Gurney, a stranger, warned out of Providence

At a council meeting of 31 March 1695: "Whereas there is a person one John Gurney by name who belongs not unto our Towne yet is here come in & at present makes Residence here, but hath nought to maintaine him or supply his wants … The Councill doe … order that the said John Gurney doe betweene this day & the 14th day of this instant depart ou[t of the town]." He was carried out by the constable but returned, and the council ordered "that the said Gurney be dealt withall & proceeded against according as the law" directs. In 1696 the council determined "that the said John Gurney shall by warrant from a majestrate, be apprehended & shall pay 5s in money, or else be whipt upon his Naked Body with 12 stripes & be by the Constable Emediatly after Execution or payment Removed out of ye Towne."[^providence-john-gurney]

## 1714 — Sarah Gurney's will, presented by her son Joseph Field

At the council meeting of Friday 30 April 1714: "This day Joseph ffield presented to the Councill the will & Testament of his deceased mother Sarah Gurney, for their Examination thereof, and also the Inventory of her Estate; the which were both of them by the Councill Examined & Allowed. The witnesses to the will sworne; & also the apprisers of the Enventary & Joseph ffield … made Oath thereunto."[^providence-sarah-gurney]

The will, inventory, and original probate file are now all in hand, and they settle the family's shape — with one correction to the first reconstruction. **Sarah died 16 April 1714; her will of 31 March 1714** gives 5s each to sons **Zachariah, John, James ("if he live to come home"), and Daniel Field**, the household brass, pewter, iron, and bedding to daughter **Sarah Field**, and the cattle, sheep, swine, and working tools — "the product of his Care & dilligence" — to son and executor **Joseph Field**. She signed "Sarah X Gurney her mark." Decisively: **"Aprill the last day 1714: John Gurney the husband of the above named Sarah Gurney personally appeared before the Town Councill"** — her Gurney husband *survived* her (he did not predecease, as first guessed), witnessed the will, and co-appraised the inventory with Josiah Thornton, both times **by mark**; he took nothing under the will, and Joseph Field entered a £150 executor's bond. All children are Fields — the Gurney marriage produced no recorded children. Extracts at [`sources/corpus_supplement/providence-sarah-gurney-will-1714-extracts.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/sources/corpus_supplement/providence-sarah-gurney-will-1714-extracts.md).[^providence-sarah-gurney][^sarah-will-1714]

So the story runs: the stranger John Gurney warned out in 1695–96 evidently stayed in the Providence orbit, married the widow Sarah Field, and was still alive — an illiterate or mark-signing man — on 30 April 1714. Josiah Thornton's role as co-appraiser and councilman is the likely seed of Torrey's "Sarah (Thornton), widow" reading; Torrey's actual entry remains to be compared.

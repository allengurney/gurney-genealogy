# Research case-file rules

Human-facing overview:
- `research/case-files/README.md`

## Purpose
Case files are for sustained investigations with competing hypotheses, evidence weighing, chronology, and probability assessment.

## Expected shape
A case file should usually include:
- the question
- competing hypotheses
- timeline
- evidence ledger or structured analysis
- source discussion
- assessment of strengths and weaknesses
- current conclusion and confidence level
- open items

## Analytical standard
- Do not compress uncertainty into false certainty.
- Preserve the argument trail.
- Keep rejected paths visible when they materially explain why a conclusion was reached.

## Citation rigor
- Cite every material evidentiary assertion.
- Cite interpretive turning points.
- When a probability estimate is given, make the supporting basis visible nearby.

## Relationship to other layers
- Subject-level findings that belong in a person, place, or topic file should be crosslinked there.
- The case file is not a substitute for updating the relevant subject files.

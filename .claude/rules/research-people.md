# Research people rules

Human-facing overview:
- `research/README.md`
- `research/people/README.md`

## Purpose
This directory is the main working layer for people:
- ancestor companions
- pre-fact-sheet ancestor files
- spouses and non-ancestor subjects
- candidate identities
- disambiguation targets

## What belongs here
- working notes with substance
- source extracts
- transcriptions
- open questions
- negative results
- conflicting information
- hypotheses
- crosslinks
- supporting detail that would overwhelm a fact sheet

## Working-notes discipline
- Record actual substance, not vague placeholders.
- Prefer topical headings over pure date-order.
- Use dates inside entries when helpful, but organize primarily by issue, source, or finding.
- Capture what a record says and why it matters.

## Relationship to fact sheets
- If there is a paired fact sheet, this file is the evidentiary and analytical companion.
- Keep the two aligned.
- Do not assume that everything here belongs in the published narrative.

## Citation rigor
- New factual claims, quotations, transcriptions, and inferential claims should be cited.
- Research files may contain denser note-taking than fact sheets, but they should still be source-traceable.
- When a claim is provisional, say so explicitly and cite the basis.

## Findings vs source provenance
- Findings about the person stay here.
- Do not offload substantive person-level findings into `sources/validations/`.
- Source validation files should point back here, not replace this file.

## Filename conventions
- Paired companion: `g{NN}-{slug}-fact-sheet.research.md`
- Pre-fact-sheet ancestor: `g{NN}-{slug}.md`
- Non-ancestor subject: descriptive slug, no generation prefix

## General Lessons

### Machine transcript posture

- Use machine transcripts, FamilySearch Full-Text Search snippets, OCR, and poor inherited transcriptions as locator aids.
- Do not promote a name, kinship, date, or place from a handwriting transcript unless the image supports it.
- A poor transcript can still identify approximate line position, repeated formulae, associated names, and candidate words for comparator searches.

### Crop posture

- Start with a grid or locator crop before trying tight reading crops.
- For manuscript lines, preserve ascenders, descenders, spacing, and neighboring ink. A tight crop that clips letterforms is worse than a wider crop.
- Prefer batch-generated candidate strips over repeated single-coordinate recrops.

### Name variants

- For Gurney/Gournay work, compare the target form against neighboring capital G, long-s, terminal y/e, minims, and common abbreviation marks in the same hand.
- Treat dictionary-snapped OCR variants as leads, not evidence. Common snap targets include Gorney, Gorne, Gorner, Gurnee, Gornesly, Gorness, and Gorneses; re-evaluate by geography because some variants may be real local surnames.

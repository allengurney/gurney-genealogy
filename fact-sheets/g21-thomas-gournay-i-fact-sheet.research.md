## Research Appendix

### Lineage Status
**Confirmed.** Thomas I is named in Daniel Gurney, <em>Record</em> (1848) pedigree p. 286 as the nephew and collateral heir of Sir John Gurney V (d. 1408), with his wife (Catherine Kerville of Watlington) and son (Thomas II) identified. His uncle Sir John Gurney V is independently confirmed by the extensive History of Parliament Online biography, which is one of the richest sources for any pre-1600 ancestor in the entire lineage. His father (Robert Gurney, G22) and mother (Joan de Norwich) are covered in the existing G22 fact sheet and in Daniel Gurney, <em>Record</em> (1848) pp. 278–280. The collateral inheritance pattern (Sir John V → Edmund underage → Thomas I as nephew) is explicit in DG.

### Friction with the project JSON — for review
1. **Dates c. 1408 – c. 1450.** Project JSON gives the active period. This is approximate — the 1408 date reflects his uncle's death (and the earliest point at which he could have inherited), not his birth. A birth date of c. 1390 is the reasonable estimate given that his son Thomas II was born c. 1430.
2. **Name.** Project JSON uses "Thomas de Gournay" or similar. DG uses "Thomas Gournay, Esq. I." The fact sheet uses **"Thomas Gournay I"** (the older spelling, in line with the Wauncy-era family convention that dominated until the 16th-century shift to "Gurney").

### Sir John Gurney V (d. 1408) — the uncle whose death made Thomas I heir
Sir John Gurney V is documented in the History of Parliament's <em>House of Commons 1386–1421</em> volume (ed. Roskell, Clark, and Rawcliffe, 1993), with a biographical entry freely available online. Highlights of his career (all independently confirmed by the HoP biography):

- **Parentage**: son and heir of Edmund Gurney (d. 1387) of Harpley — Edmund was a successful Norfolk lawyer who served John of Gaunt as joint steward of his East Anglian estates almost continuously from 1372 to 1387. His mother was Katherine, daughter and eventual heir of Sir William Wauncy of Depden, Suffolk, and West Barsham, Norfolk. (This is the marriage that brought West Barsham into the family in 1372.)
- **Marriage**: before November 1392, to Alice, daughter and eventual sole heir of John Heylesdon (d. 1384), London mercer and former alderman of London. The marriage brought the London City warehouse "La Selde Coronata," the Heylesdon and Drayton manors, houses in Norwich, and advowsons of two chantries.
- **Career as a lawyer**: began by 1382; retained as steward of the Norfolk estates of Richard Fitzalan, 10th Earl of Arundel, by 1386; remained a Fitzalan retainer through the earl's political rise and fall.
- **Crown offices**: multiple Norfolk commissions of inquiry 1384–1407; commissions of array; gaol delivery at Great Yarmouth 1394; JP for Norfolk; Sheriff of Norfolk and Suffolk while Parliament was still in session in 1399 (appointed by Henry IV); later escheator of the joint bailiwick.
- **Parliaments**: returned by Norfolk to the 1399 Parliament that deposed Richard II and acclaimed Henry IV; returned again to the Coventry Parliament of 1404 (Henry IV's so-called "Unlearned Parliament," which had the specific feature of excluding lawyers).
- **Associates**: Sir Thomas Erpingham (the famous Lancastrian stalwart of Agincourt); Sir John Strange (later chief usher of the King's hall); John Wynter (receiver-general for the prince of Wales); the Fitzalan earls of Arundel.
- **Later disputes**: involved in disputes with the duchy of Lancaster tenants at South Creak over common grazing rights ("South Lings") where he claimed free warren pertaining to his lordship of West Barsham.
- **Estate at death**: able to leave "eight manors in Norfolk and another in Suffolk," plus the London warehouse and Norwich houses.
- **Heir**: a single son Edmund, who died before reaching majority. The estate therefore fell collaterally to Sir John's nephew Thomas I (son of Sir John's brother Robert by Joan de Norwich).

This level of national-scale political documentation is exceptional for the pre-1600 Gurneys. Sir John V — Thomas I's uncle — is more fully documented in contemporary Crown records than any Gurney ancestor before him or after him until the 19th-century Quaker bankers.

### Landholdings inherited by Thomas I
Via the collateral succession from Sir John Gurney V, Thomas I came into possession of approximately the following portfolio (estimated from the HoP biography and DG pedigree):
- **West Barsham manor**, Norfolk — the Wauncy inheritance, family seat since 1372.
- **Harpley manor**, Norfolk — the medieval family seat since at least 1183.
- **Hardingham ("Swathings") manor**, Norfolk — long-standing family holding from the Henry II period.
- **"Loundhall" in Saxthorpe**, Norfolk — acquired by Edmund Gurney (d. 1387) in exchange for other premises.
- **Moiety of the Nerford manor at Houghton**, Norfolk — purchased by Sir John V in 1399, bordering on Harpley.
- **Hellesdon and Drayton manors**, Norfolk — inherited from the Heylesdon marriage.
- **Advowsons of Hellesdon and Drayton churches** and of two chantries Heylesdon had founded.
- **Houses in Norwich**.
- **Depden, Suffolk** — Wauncy inheritance.
- **"La Selde Coronata"** warehouse in the City of London.
- **South Creak landholdings**, Norfolk.

This is a portfolio of **at least nine manors across Norfolk and Suffolk plus London and Norwich commercial property**. Whether Thomas I retained all of it or whether parts were alienated during the collateral succession (for example to pay debts or settle Alice Heylesdon's dower) is not clear from the sources consulted. By the time of his grandson William Gurney IV's 1507 will, the working core had narrowed to West Barsham, Harpley, Hingham, and the Pockthorpe-by-Norwich town house — suggesting some consolidation or loss over the intervening century.

### Negative Results
- **No personal documentary trace** of Thomas I in Crown records, parliamentary rolls, commissions of the peace, or History of Parliament searches. He appears only as a name in the pedigree and as the inheriting nephew.
- **No will, IPM, or probate record** for Thomas I has been identified. Compare to his son Thomas II (will proved 1471) and his uncle Sir John V (will referenced in HoP biography) — both of whose wills survive.
- **Exact year of birth and death unknown.** c. 1390 and c. 1450 are reasonable estimates based on his parents' activity and his son's generation, but neither is directly attested.
- **Catherine Kerville's parents, siblings, and dates are not given in DG.** The Kerville family of Watlington is catalogued in Daniel Gurney, <em>Supplement</em> (1858) p. 795 but without substantial detail.
- **The exact moment of collateral inheritance** — i.e., when young Edmund (Sir John V's son) died — is not recorded.
- **Whether Thomas I had siblings** is not addressed in the sources consulted. The DG pedigree shows only him as the issue of Robert G22 and Joan de Norwich, but this may reflect documentary gaps.

### Open Questions for Future Research
1. **Daniel Gurney, <em>Supplement to the Record of the House of Gournay</em> (King's Lynn: Thew & Son, 1858), pp. 789–795** — should contain the fullest treatment of Thomas I and his immediate family, including the Kerville marriage, the Boking v. Gournay fine (a land dispute involving a "Thomas Gournay" that may be G21 or his son G20), and the inheritance from Sir John V. Worth retrieving in full.
2. **The fine between Nicholas Boking and Thomas Goumay** (Daniel Gurney, <em>Supplement</em> (1858) p. 795) — a land dispute. Which Thomas? If G21, it is the only known Crown-level document naming him directly.
3. **The death date of Edmund** (Sir John V's son) — would pinpoint when Thomas I came into the inheritance. Might be in TNA Chancery Inquisitions Post Mortem or Patent Rolls around 1408–1415.
4. **Sir John Gurney V's will** — referenced in HoP but not transcribed there. Would reveal whether Thomas I was named as default heir in the will itself or whether the collateral descent was enforced only after Edmund's later death.
5. **The Kerville family of Watlington, Norfolk** — pedigree and dates. Blomefield's <em>History of Norfolk</em> for Watlington parish should give the context.
6. **Bodleian MS Tanner 175** — Henry G15's commonplace book preserves family records back to Edward III and likely contains material on Thomas I.
7. **La Selde Coronata** in the City of London — what City ward was it in? Does the name survive in medieval London topographical records? A 2019 archaeological survey of the eastern City (Fenchurch Street / Leadenhall area) might reference it.

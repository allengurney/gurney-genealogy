<section class="fact-panel fact-panel-highlights" id="highlights">
<h2 class="unnumbered">Highlights</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Brought Boleyn descent into the Norfolk Gurney line.</strong> William V's wife Anne Heydon was a daughter of Anne Boleyn the elder of Blickling, sister of Sir William Boleyn, who was the grandfather of Queen Anne Boleyn. Through Anne Heydon, William V's son Anthony G17 was a <strong>second cousin of Queen Anne Boleyn</strong> — a kinship that placed the early 16th-century Norfolk Gurneys within the wider Boleyn-Howard cousinage of the eastern counties. The connection is independently documented by Wikipedia, by the WikiTree Sir Henry Heydon profile, and by <em>East Anglian Notes & Queries</em>. <sup class="fn"><a href="#n6" id="ref-6">6</a></sup></li>
  <li><strong>His father-in-law Sir Henry Heydon built Baconsthorpe Castle.</strong> Sir Henry Heydon (d. 1503/4) was a Privy Councillor to Henry VII, steward of the Norfolk estates of the Duke of Norfolk, and the builder of Baconsthorpe Castle — a moated and gatehouse-fortified country house begun in the 1480s and now an English Heritage ruin. Anne Heydon was raised at Baconsthorpe; her husband William V would have been a regular visitor. The castle is documented in detail in Francis Blomefield's <em>History of Norfolk</em>, vol. vi (1807), in the parish entry for Baconsthorpe. <sup class="fn"><a href="#n7" id="ref-7">7</a></sup></li>
  <li><strong>Anne's siblings married into the highest Norfolk gentry.</strong> Through Anne Heydon, William V's children gained a remarkable network of in-laws: Anne's sister Bridget Heydon married Sir William Paston of Caister (the Paston Letters family); her sister Dorothy married a Lord Cobham (a granddaughter of Dorothy and Lord Cobham, Elizabeth Brooke, would become the second wife of Sir Thomas Wyatt the poet); and her sister Amy married Sir Roger Lestrange of Hunstanton. The Lestrange marriage is the kinship that explains why two generations later William V's great-great-grandson Francis Gurney (G14) became the financial agent to the Lestranges of Hunstanton from 1612 to 1636 — they were distant cousins. <sup class="fn"><a href="#n8" id="ref-8">8</a></sup></li>
  <li><strong>"Of Irstead" — the Norfolk Broads identification.</strong> Daniel Gurney's pedigree identifies William V as "of Irstead." The connection to Irstead manor came in through his Heydon father-in-law: Sir Henry Heydon had received the Irstead manors (Overhall and Netherhall) by conditional bequest from John Groos's will of 1 March 1487, per Blomefield's parish entry for Irstead in volume xi. Whether William V actually maintained a residence at Irstead or simply held it nominally during his short adult life is unclear. <sup class="fn"><a href="#n9" id="ref-9">9</a></sup></li>
  <li><strong>Died vita patris, leaving his young son Anthony as eventual heir.</strong> William V died before his father William IV, who died on 18 January 1507/8 (per Daniel Gurney's pedigree). Their son Anthony — only nine or ten years old — inherited as a "boy lord" when William IV died, and the Norfolk Gurney estates were managed during Anthony's minority through wardship arrangements involving the Heydon kin. This is the second consecutive generation in which the senior heir died before his father, making the line skip a generation in inheritance terms. <sup class="fn"><a href="#n10" id="ref-10">10</a></sup></li>
</ul>

<section class="fact-section fact-narrative" id="narrative">
<h2 class="unnumbered">Narrative</h2>

William Gurney V is one of the more lightly documented generations in the Norfolk Gurney line — partly because he died <em>vita patris</em> and never held the principal estates in his own right, and partly because the surviving record about him is dominated by his much more famous Heydon in-laws. He matters in the lineage less for what he himself did than for whom he married, and for the network of cousinage that his marriage brought into the Gurney family.

He was born around 1465 — Daniel Gurney's pedigree gives no birth year, and the Ancestry composite trees give c. 1468. His father was William Gurney IV (G19), the Norfolk escheator and council member to the Duke of Norfolk; his mother was Anne Calthorpe, only daughter of Sir William Calthorpe of Burnham Thorpe, KB, by his first wife Elizabeth Grey of Ruthyn (whose own death in 1437 is independently documented in the Calthorpe family literature). Through his Calthorpe mother, William V was a kinsman of one of the leading Norfolk gentry families of the late 15th century, with connections to the Drurys of Hawstead, the Hasildens, the Stapletons of Ingham, and ultimately to the de la Poles, Earls of Suffolk.

Around 1484 — modern Heydon scholarship reads Sir Henry Heydon's surviving will of 1503/4 as placing the marriage "shortly after 28 May 1484" — William V married Anne Heydon, daughter of Sir Henry Heydon of Baconsthorpe Castle. Sir Henry Heydon (d. 1503/4) was a Privy Councillor to Henry VII, steward of the Norfolk estates of the Duke of Norfolk, and the builder of Baconsthorpe Castle (begun in the 1480s, now an English Heritage ruin). Anne Heydon's mother — Sir Henry's wife — was Anne Boleyn the elder of Blickling, sister of Sir William Boleyn, who was the paternal grandfather of Queen Anne Boleyn. This kinship made William V's son Anthony G17 a second cousin of Queen Anne Boleyn through his Heydon mother.

Anne Heydon's siblings married into a remarkably wide network: her sister Bridget Heydon married Sir William Paston of Caister (the Paston Letters family); her sister Dorothy married a Lord Cobham (a granddaughter of whom, Elizabeth Brooke, would become the second wife of Sir Thomas Wyatt the poet); and her sister Amy Heydon married Sir Roger Lestrange of Hunstanton. The Lestrange marriage is the kinship that explains why two generations later William V's great-great-grandson Francis Gurney (G14) would become the trusted financial agent to the Lestranges of Hunstanton from 1612 to 1636: they were distant cousins, and Norfolk gentry preferred to do their financial business with kin.

Daniel Gurney's pedigree identifies William V as "of Irstead." The connection to Irstead manor came in through his Heydon father-in-law: per Francis Blomefield's parish entry for Irstead in volume xi, Sir Henry Heydon had received the Irstead manors (Overhall and Netherhall) by conditional bequest from John Groos's will of 1 March 1487. Whether William V actually maintained a residence at Irstead or simply held it nominally during his short adult life is unclear from the surviving sources.

William V died before his father William Gurney IV, who in turn died on 18 January 1507/8 (per Daniel Gurney's pedigree, supported by Blomefield's mention of "William Gournay, junior" in 14 Henry VII / 1499 — see the G19 fact sheet). At William IV's death, the Norfolk Gurney estates passed to William V's young son Anthony — about nine years old — who inherited as a "boy lord." Wardship arrangements during Anthony's minority would have involved his Heydon kinsmen; given that Anne Heydon had remarried Sir Lionel Dymoke of Ashby, Lincolnshire, by this date, Anthony's wardship was probably administered from Norfolk by his maternal uncles or by the Heydons of Baconsthorpe.

Anne Heydon survived her son's eventual marriage and her grandson's birth, and died around 1521. Her remarriage to Sir Lionel Dymoke and her death are independently documented in modern Dymoke and Heydon family literature.

William V's role in the Gurney lineage is therefore primarily as a hinge: the man whose marriage brought the family into the Heydon-Boleyn-Howard-Lestrange cousinage of late-medieval Norfolk, but who himself never lived to take possession of the estates that his marriage helped to enrich. His son Anthony, his grandson Francis (who also died <em>vita patris</em>), and his great-grandson Henry (G15) the Elizabethan poet would all live longer in the historical record.
</section>

## Research Appendix

### Lineage Status
**Confirmed by multiple independent witnesses, but most of the documentation is about William V's wife and her family rather than William V himself.** The Heydon-side documentation is exceptionally rich:
- **Wikipedia**, "Sir Henry Heydon" — citing the Prerogative Court of Canterbury will of 1503/4.
- **WikiTree**, profile for Sir Henry Heydon of Baconsthorpe Castle — listing all five known daughters and their marriages, including Anne to William Gurney.
- **Francis Blomefield**, <em>History of Norfolk</em>, vol. vi (1807), "Hundred of South Erpingham: Baconsthorp," pp. 502–513 — full genealogy of the Heydons of Baconsthorpe Castle.
- **<em>The East Anglian: Notes and Queries on Subjects Connected with the Counties of Suffolk, Cambridge, Essex and Norfolk</em>**, vol. ii (1866) — antiquarian notes on the Heydon-Boleyn cousinage.
- **Modern academic literature** on the Boleyn family, especially Eric Ives, <em>The Life and Death of Anne Boleyn</em> (Oxford: Blackwell, 2004).

The Calthorpe-side documentation for William V's mother is also substantial:
- **James Lee-Warner**, "The Calthorps of Burnham," <em>Norfolk Archaeology</em> vol. ix (1884), pp. 1–19.
- **Christopher William Carr-Calthrop**, <em>Notes on the Families of Calthorpe and Calthrop in Norfolk, Lincolnshire and Elsewhere</em> (3rd ed., London, 1933).
- **John Sell Cotman & Samuel Rush Meyrick**, <em>Engravings of Sepulchral Brasses in Norfolk: Tending to Illustrate the Ecclesiastical, Military, and Civil Costume as Well as to Preserve Memorials of Ancient Families in That County</em>, vol. 1 (London: Bohn, 1838).
- **Sir William Calthorpe's full will** of 31 May 1494, printed in <em>The East Anglian: Notes and Queries</em>, vol. ii, p. 210, naming his daughter Anne (wife of William Gurney) as a beneficiary.
- **Geni**, **WikiTree** (Calthorpe-7), and **Find a Grave** Memorial #81101154 — all independently identifying Anne (wife of William Gurney) as Sir William Calthorpe's only daughter by Elizabeth Grey.

For William V himself, the principal source remains **Daniel Gurney's <em>Record</em> (1848), pedigree p. 287, and <em>Supplement</em> (1858), pp. 868 ff.** The Pease/Pennyghael Gurney genealogy adds Heydon and Calthorpe context but no new dates for William V personally.

### Friction with the project JSON — for review

1. **Birth and death year span.** The project `ancestors_v3.json` gives "fl. c. 1490 – 1530." Both ends are problematic. William V's marriage "shortly after 28 May 1484" implies a birth year no later than the mid-1460s. The "1530" terminal date is incompatible with William V having died <em>vita patris</em> before his father William IV (d. 1507/8). Recommend updating to **"c. 1465 – before 18 January 1507/8 (vita patris)."**

2. **Marriage order.** Daniel Gurney's <em>Supplement</em> (1858) gave Anne Heydon's marriage order as Dymoke first, Gurney second. Modern scholarship reading Sir Henry Heydon's surviving will of 1503/4 reverses the order: Gurney first ("shortly after 28 May 1484"), Dymoke second. The reversed order is more consistent with the documented dates of William V's father William IV (d. 1508) and Sir Lionel Dymoke (d. 17 August 1519). The project JSON should adopt the modern reversed order.

### The Heydon-Boleyn-Howard cousinage in detail
Sir Henry Heydon (d. 1503/4) had married Anne Boleyn the elder of Blickling, sister of **Sir William Boleyn** (d. 1505), who was the paternal grandfather of:
- **Queen Anne Boleyn** (c. 1501/7–1536), through her father Sir Thomas Boleyn
- **Mary Boleyn** (c. 1499/1500–1543), Anne's elder sister and former mistress of Henry VIII
- **George Boleyn, Viscount Rochford** (c. 1503/4–1536), Anne's brother
- **Queen Elizabeth I** (1533–1603), Anne's daughter

This means William V's son Anthony G17 was a **second cousin of Queen Anne Boleyn, Mary Boleyn, and George Boleyn**, and a **first cousin twice removed of Queen Elizabeth I**. The Norfolk Gurneys' tradition of having Lady Catherine Howard as a godmother to Henry G15 in 1548/9 is consistent with this surviving cousinage on the Howard-Boleyn side a generation later.

Anne Heydon's other siblings and their marriages, per the WikiTree Sir Henry Heydon profile:
- **Bridget Heydon** married **Sir William Paston** of Caister (the Paston Letters family).
- **Dorothy Heydon** married **Lord Cobham** — granddaughter Elizabeth Brooke (later second wife of Sir Thomas Wyatt the poet).
- **Amy Heydon** married **Sir Roger Lestrange** of Hunstanton — the cousinage that two generations later explains Francis Gurney G14's appointment as financial agent to the Lestranges.
- **Sir John Heydon** (the Heydon heir) inherited Baconsthorpe Castle and continued the family in north Norfolk.

### The Calthorpe connection (William V's mother's side)
Sir William Calthorpe of Burnham Thorpe, KB (1410–1494), was twice married. His first marriage was to **Elizabeth Grey** (d. 1437), daughter of Sir Reynold (Reginald) Grey, 3rd Baron Grey of Ruthyn. By Elizabeth he had **two sons (Sir John and William) and one daughter — Anne, wife of William Gurney**. Sir William's second marriage was to Elizabeth Stapleton, daughter and coheiress of Sir Miles Stapleton, by whom he had several further children including Anne (wife of Sir Robert Drury, Speaker of the House of Commons), Elizabeth (wife of Francis Hasilden), Sir Francis Calthorpe, and Edward Calthorpe.

This means William V's mother Anne Calthorpe was the senior surviving daughter of one of the leading north-Norfolk gentry families of the late 15th century, with kinship through the Greys to the Plantagenet royal line and through the Stapletons (her stepmother's line) to the de la Poles, Earls of Suffolk.

The Calthorpe identification is independently confirmed across at least eight non-Daniel-Gurney sources (Lee-Warner 1884, Cotman & Meyrick 1838, Carr-Calthrop 1933, the East Anglian Notes & Queries printing of Sir William's will, Wikipedia, WikiTree, Geni, and Find a Grave). This makes the G19 William Gurney IV "name correction" (William, not Edward William) very strongly supported — see the G19 fact sheet.

### The Irstead manor question
Daniel Gurney's pedigree identifies William V as "of Irstead." But Francis Blomefield's specific entry for the Irstead manor (vol. xi, 1810, "Tunstede Hundred: Irstead," pp. 46–49) does not name any Gurney as a holder. Instead, Blomefield records that:

- John Groos of Irstead made his will on 1 March 1487, conditionally bequeathing his manors of Irstead (called Overhall and Netherhall) to Sir Henry Heydon (William V's father-in-law) "on certain conditions."
- In 32 Henry VIII (1540) — i.e., a generation after William V's death — the manor was held by Sir Richard Southwell of Wood Rising, a major Henrician courtier.

This means:
- The Gurney connection to Irstead came indirectly through the Heydons.
- The principal Irstead manor may have left the Heydon-Gurney interest by 1540 — i.e., during Anthony G17's lifetime.
- Daniel Gurney's "of Irstead" identification may reflect a residence rather than a manorial title, or may reflect a smaller tenement at Irstead distinct from the principal manor.

Blomefield's separate entry for Great Ellingham (vol. i, p. 488) does record Henry G15 as holding "Irsted manor of the Bishop Norwich" in 1572. This is a different Irstead tenure — held of the bishop, not of the king — which suggests there were two Irstead properties or that the Gurney family retained a residual smaller holding even after the principal Heydon manor passed to Southwell. This is an open research question that would need to be resolved by reference to the Norfolk Record Office Irstead deeds and to the Norwich diocesan rentals.

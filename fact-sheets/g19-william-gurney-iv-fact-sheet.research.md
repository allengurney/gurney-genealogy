## Research Appendix

### Lineage Status
**Confirmed by independent topographical witness.** William Gurney IV is independently documented by **Francis Blomefield** in <em>An Essay Towards a Topographical History of the County of Norfolk</em>, vol. vii (London: William Miller, 1807), "Gallow and Brothercross Hundreds: East-Barsham," pp. 53–65, which records that in 14 Henry VII (1499) "**William Gournay, junior**, and Thomas Sefoule, Esq. had a grant of the custody of the manors and lands of Roger Wood of East-Barsham, son and heir of John Wood, from John Earl of Oxford, to whom King Henry VII had granted the custody of the person and lands of William Viscount Beaumont." This is **independent corroboration that a "William Gournay, junior" was active in the Norfolk gentry land market in 1499**, in the very period and place where Daniel Gurney places William Gurney IV. The "junior" qualifier reinforces that he was distinguished from another, older William — almost certainly his father William Gurney III. This Blomefield reference is independent of any Gurney genealogist and is the strongest non-Daniel-Gurney corroboration that the man's name was William, not Edward.

He is further documented in Daniel Gurney, <em>Record</em> (1848), pedigree p. 287, with full title and offices, dates of activity (1494, 1507) and death (1508). Daniel Gurney, <em>Supplement to the Record of the House of Gournay</em> (King's Lynn: Thew & Son, 1858), p. 817 ff., contains the William Gurney IV chapter and references his Inquisition Post Mortem ("Inquisitio Post Mortem Willelmi Gurney Senioris"). His wife Anne Calthorpe is independently confirmed by multiple Calthorpe-side sources (Wikipedia, WikiTree, Find a Grave, Cotman & Meyrick's <em>Sepulchral Brasses</em>, Carr-Calthrop's 1933 family history, Lee-Warner's "The Calthorps of Burnham" in <em>Norfolk Archaeology</em> ix (1884), and Sir William Calthorpe's full will printed in <em>The East Anglian: Notes and Queries</em> vol. ii p. 210) — all of which uniformly identify Sir William Calthorpe KB's only daughter by his first wife Elizabeth Grey as "Anne, wife of William Gurney."

### MAJOR CORRECTION TO PROJECT JSON

The project `ancestors_v3.json` identifies G19 as **"Edward William Gurney."** This name appears nowhere in any primary or secondary source consulted in this research session. **It is unsupported.** The man's correct name is **William Gurney IV.**

**Evidence:**

1. **Francis Blomefield, <em>An Essay Towards a Topographical History of the County of Norfolk</em>, vol. vii (1807), "Gallow and Brothercross Hundreds: East-Barsham," pp. 53–65** — independent topographical witness, naming "**William Gournay, junior**" as receiving the custody of the East-Barsham manors of Roger Wood from John Earl of Oxford in 14 Henry VII (1499). This is independent of any Gurney genealogist and confirms the name as William.

2. **Daniel Gurney, <em>Record</em> (1848), pedigree p. 287** (the foundational genealogy) names him explicitly: <em>"William Gurnet, Esq. IV. son and heir, of West Barsham, and of Pockthorpe by Norwich, living 1494, used the wrestling collar as a crest, escheator for Norfolk; died 1508."</em> No "Edward" anywhere.

3. **Daniel Gurney, <em>Record</em> (1848), p. 282**, names him as "William Gurney" in narrative.

4. **Daniel Gurney, <em>Supplement to the Record of the House of Gournay</em> (King's Lynn: Thew & Son, 1858)**, p. 817 ff. — devotes a full chapter heading "William Gurney IV" with no alternate name.

5. **Daniel Gurney, <em>Supplement</em> (1858)**, p. 817, includes the "Inquisitio Post Mortem Willelmi Gurney Senioris" — the Latin death inquisition refers to him as <em>Willelmus Gurney senior</em> (William Gurney the elder), not Edward.

6. **Wikipedia article on "William Calthorpe"** (his father-in-law) names his only daughter from his first marriage as "Anne, wife of William Gurney."

7. **WikiTree "Calthorpe-7"** profile names her as "Anne (Calthorpe) Gurney" married to William Gurney.

8. **Find a Grave memorial #81101154** for Sir William Calthorpe lists his daughter Anne as "wife of William Gurney."

9. **James Lee-Warner, "The Calthorps of Burnham," <em>Norfolk Archaeology</em> ix (1884), pp. 1–19** — independent Norfolk antiquarian survey, identifying the same Anne Calthorpe as wife of William Gurney.

10. **Christopher William Carr-Calthrop, <em>Notes on the Families of Calthorpe and Calthrop in Norfolk, Lincolnshire and elsewhere</em>** (3rd ed., London, 1933) — also gives the same identification.

11. **John Sell Cotman & Samuel Rush Meyrick, <em>Engravings of Sepulchral Brasses in Norfolk</em>, vol. 1 (London: Bohn, 1838)** — Calthorpe family memorial brasses.

12. **Sir William Calthorpe's full will of 31 May 1494, printed in <em>The East Anglian; or, Notes and Queries</em> vol. ii (1866), p. 210** — names his daughter Anne as wife of William Gurney.

13. **Étienne Pattou, <em>Seigneurs de Gournay</em>** (racineshistoire.free.fr) — does not list any "Edward William Gurney" in this generation.

14. **Sir Henry Spelman's Gurney pedigree** (preserved in Daniel Gurney via the Spelman MSS) — names William Gurney IV.

**Recommendation:** Update `ancestors_v3.json` to change the G19 entry from `"name": "Edward William Gurney"` to `"name": "William Gurney IV"`. The "Edward" is most likely a transcription error in the JSON, perhaps from a confused reading of "escheator for Norfolk in the reign of Edward IV" — a phrase about his Crown office that may have been misread as a forename.

### Friction with the project JSON — for review

1. **Name (above).** "Edward William Gurney" → "William Gurney IV."

2. **Death date 18 January 1508** at Burnham Thorpe. Project JSON has this and it agrees with DG's "died 1508." The precise day and place (18 January, Burnham Thorpe) is plausible — it would come from his Inquisition Post Mortem and is consistent with him visiting his Calthorpe in-laws in his last illness.

3. **Birth year c. 1430.** Project JSON gives "b. c. 1430." DG does not give a birth year. c. 1430 would make him 78 at his death — possible but on the older side. A birth date c. 1450 would place him in his late twenties when he inherited from his father in 1471 and is more consistent with his being "of council to the Duke of Norfolk" by 1477. Recommend updating the JSON to "b. c. 1450" or simply "b. mid-15th century."

### Landholdings
- **West Barsham Hall and manor, Norfolk** — primary seat. Inherited from his father Thomas II in 1471. *Visible today: surviving 16th-century north wing of West Barsham Hall, Grade II listed.*
- **Pockthorpe-by-Norwich, Norfolk** — town house in the parish immediately outside Norwich's eastern walls. Standard Norfolk gentry pattern of having a winter house in or near the city.
- **Hingham-Gurneys manor** — held of the Bardolph heirs.
- **Hardingham (Swathings) manor** — long-standing family holding from the Henry II period.
- **Harpley manor** — would have remained in the family from earlier generations. Henry G15 would later repurchase it in 1587 after a period out of family hands.
- **Sheep flock**: at least 700 sheep at West Barsham at the time of his 1507 will. Comparable to substantial East Anglian sheep-farming households of the period.
- **Burnham Thorpe association** — he died here, suggesting either an independent landholding, a stay with his Calthorpe in-laws, or both.

### Sir William Calthorpe KB — full context
William IV's father-in-law was a major figure of late-medieval Norfolk knightly life. Born 30 January 1410 at Burnham Thorpe; son of Sir John Calthorpe and Amy Wythe; inherited the Calthorpe estates as a child after his grandfather's death in 1421. Manumised one of his villeins on 28 June 1443. Lancastrian; arrested 20 May 1450; pardoned 1458. Knight of the Bath at the coronation of Elizabeth Woodville in the Tower of London on Ascension Day 1465. High Sheriff of Norfolk and Suffolk multiple times (1442, 1458, 1469, 1479). Steward of the household of the Duke of Norfolk in 1479. Locum tenens for the Duke of Norfolk during the minority of his heir Henry, Earl of Exeter. Made presentations to the Rectory of Beeston (1460, 1481, 1492) and Hempstede (1479, 1485). Buried in the Church of the White Friars, Norwich, beside his first wife Elizabeth Grey. His will (31 May 1494, proved 23 May 1495) is published in <em>East Anglian Notes & Queries</em>, vol. ii, p. 210. He had a town house in St Martin at the Palace parish, Norwich.

This is the level of Norfolk knightly family that William Gurney IV married into. The Calthorpe alliance was one of the most prestigious available to a Norfolk gentry household in the late 15th century, equivalent in standing to the alliances his ancestor Edmund Gurney (G23) had made with the Wauncys in 1372 or that his descendants would make with the Heydons (1484) and Lovells (1519).

### The 1518 Prioress of Thetford
William IV's daughter Elizabeth Gurney's election as Prioress of Thetford in 1518 is the most distinguished individual achievement of any of his children. Thetford had been one of the great Norfolk religious houses since the 11th century. Within twenty years of Elizabeth's election, her house would be dissolved by Henry VIII (the Cluniac and other Norfolk priories suppressed in waves between 1536 and 1540). Elizabeth's name does not appear in standard surveys of dissolved religious houses readily searchable online, suggesting she may have died before the dissolution; a check of <em>Victoria County History: Norfolk</em> for Thetford Priory and of David Knowles & R. Neville Hadcock, <em>Medieval Religious Houses: England and Wales</em>, would resolve this.

### Negative Results
- **The full text of William IV's 1507 will has not been recovered in this session.** It would normally be in the Norwich Consistory Court probate registers (Norfolk Record Office) or transcribed in Daniel Gurney, <em>Supplement</em> (1858) pp. 816–820. A direct check would yield the names of all his children, the exact disposition of West Barsham, and likely details about the 700 sheep and his debts.
- **The Inquisition Post Mortem of 1508** is referenced in Daniel Gurney, <em>Supplement</em> (1858) p. 817 but the full text has not been recovered. It would survive in Court of Wards / Chancery records at TNA.
- **Burial location not recorded.** He died at Burnham Thorpe but most likely was returned to West Barsham for burial.
- **Birth year is approximate.** The "c. 1430" in the project JSON is unsupported by DG; "c. 1450" is more probable but still an estimate.

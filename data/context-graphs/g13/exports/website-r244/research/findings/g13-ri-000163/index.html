Rye's two-Francis separation, anchored by the 1627 Parmentergate apprenticeship of James Gurney

Walter Rye first separated two contemporary Francis Gurneys: the West Barsham gentleman and Merchant Taylor, at East Dereham c.1608–1618 and St Benet Fink 1619–1637; and a Norwich plebeian laceweaver of St Peter Mountergate, later of St Giles Cripplegate 1638–1640, wife Mary, to whom the Costessey property belongs. The Norwich corporate registers separate them by simultaneous independent record: James Gurney, son of a Francis of St Peter Parmentergate — the laceweaver's parish — was bound apprentice at Michaelmas 1627 for seven years and admitted a freeman of Norwich on 8 January 1648, while Francis the Merchant Taylor was baptising children at St Benet Fink in London in the same year. The same registers carry no John Gurney freedom or enrolled apprenticeship anywhere in the emigrant window.

The 1615/Southwark tradition belongs to Newgate's apprentice, not the emigrant

The 1615-birth/Southwark tradition is best explained as the true biography of the 1636 Newgate apprentice, misattached to the older Braintree emigrant. Read backward, the 1636 court order's "serve until age 24, three years from the following 29 September" implies a birth of 29 September 1615 — exactly the date all three American traditions carry — and Newgate himself lived at Southwark before his 1633 emigration, supplying the "near London Bridge"/"Brent" detail as neatly as the birth date. That apprentice is not the Braintree emigrant, who was deposed "aged about 50" in 1652/3 (implying birth c.1602/3, a decade earlier) and whom Newgate's own later will and estate papers do not name. American family memory most plausibly preserved the apprentice's real biography — his birth year, his London-area origin, his tie to Newgate — and attached it to the wrong, older John Gurney.

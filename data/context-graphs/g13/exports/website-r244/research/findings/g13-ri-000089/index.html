Passenger silence is expected in the 1639-41 Great-Migration tail

The absence of a passenger record for John's crossing is what Great-Migration timing predicts. His arrival is bounded to 1638-early 1641, the tail of a migration that crested in 1638 and fell off sharply after 1640; and named Boston-area passenger lists survive far worse for 1639-mid-1641 - the window most likely to hold his crossing - than for the earlier peak years, the surviving evidence there being ship-arrival notices and clearance licences with passenger counts but no names. A man arriving then would very often leave no listed name even under perfect record-keeping, so his absence from the surviving lists is the expected outcome of poor list survival, not evidence about his party or origin.

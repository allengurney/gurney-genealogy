John's trade points back to a Norfolk textile family

John's tailoring is an origin-bearing handle that points back toward a Norfolk textile family. He is styled a tailor in his own Braintree records, and in this period a craft passed most often from father to son. The father the identity work proposes for him, Francis Gurney, was himself a Merchant Taylor - apprenticed in London in 1599, free of the Company in 1606 - whose commercial life ran through Norwich and who in 1622 joined a scheme to teach the poor of King's Lynn to spin worsted-yarn, alongside a Great Ellingham woolchapman; the wider Gurney family sat in East Anglia's worsted-and-woollen economy for generations. So the trade sets a specific expectation - a tailor's son of a tailoring family should be traceable to a master - and strengthens, without proving, the association with Norfolk, the Gurney family, and Francis in particular. The parentage conclusion is held to the identity work.

The jest-book's West Barsham Gurney anecdotes are kin-sourced through the L'Estrange-Lewkenor marriage

The Gurney anecdotes in Sir Nicholas L'Estrange's manuscript jest-book - printed in Thoms's 1839 Anecdotes and Traditions and long treated as detached hearsay - are kin-sourced family testimony. Muskett records that Sir Nicholas L'Estrange, 1st Bt. of Hunstanton (d. 1655), the jest-book's compiler, married Anne Lewkenor of Denham, daughter of Sir Edward Lewkenor II and so niece of Martha (Lewkenor) Gurney and first cousin of Edward Gournay of West Barsham. The compiler's own wife was therefore first cousin to the heir the anecdotes describe, moving them from hearsay to family testimony. The upgrade cuts both ways: it explains 'Frances Hood' as the jest-book's rendering of Edward's wife Frances Hovell, but it also shows that the mistaken 'died in 1614' - which the wills instead place at 1621x1623, the 1614 date belonging to Henry G15's revoked earlier will - was already circulating inside the family.

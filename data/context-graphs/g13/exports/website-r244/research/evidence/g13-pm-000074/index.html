Evidence — The elimination scaffold: structural grounds, not a blanket same-surname suspicion

Against the matching-criteria profile, a household is eliminated on structural rather than circumstantial grounds: continuing documented English residence past June 1641; a wife whose name is not Mary; a child set that does not match or that collides in date; or a documented trade, death, or generation placing the household in the wrong period. A household attested by only a single indexed event with no continuing record is held at a low residual (1–3%) rather than eliminated outright, since one record cannot itself rule out an unrecorded emigration. This is the same family-status-and-chronology standard already applied once inside this graph, where it separates the Braintree emigrant from the 1636 Newgate apprentice.

Evidence — American arms tradition, corroborated in situ at Norfolk (inheritance unverified)

A separate American family tradition holds that the American Gurneys retained arms connecting them to the Norfolk Gurneys: American Biography (1926) states "the Gurney arms kept by the American Gurneys show connection with Norfolk (England) Gurneys," and the Lysander Franklin Gurney sketch (1912) likewise reports arms cherished within the American branch. The claimed blazon, argent, a cross engrailed gules, is independently and contemporaneously attested in Norfolk itself: carved on the West Barsham chancel brass of Edward Gournay (d. 1641) and recorded again among the Harpley church shields, per an 1871 published account of the Gournay heraldry and an independent 1887 parish-by-parish Norfolk survey. The claimed blazon is therefore genuine to the Norfolk Gournays; only the American line's claimed inheritance of it is unverified.

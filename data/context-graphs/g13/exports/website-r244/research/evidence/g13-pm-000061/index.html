Evidence — The Weymouth land-and-neighbour reception network

John's first colonial ground was a Weymouth land-and-neighbourhood network, not a party he arrived with. His East and Mill Field parcels - town grants taken up as an admitted inhabitant in his arrival years and largely passed on by the 1643 property record - sat among neighbours drawn from Weymouth's two documented settler streams, an Aylesbury-Vale Buckinghamshire group and a West-Country/Dorchester group, several granted their own land in the same few years. The associate rings widen from the men named with him in 1641 through the neighbours who held or absorbed his parcels to his later Braintree probate circle, but none resolves into a shared shipload. Read as immigration by association, the evidence shows a newcomer entering an existing community by land, locality, and acquaintance.

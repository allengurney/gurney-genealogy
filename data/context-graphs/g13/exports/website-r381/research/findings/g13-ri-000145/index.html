Candidate B: the leading named candidate at about 11 percent, on one primary record and a circumstantial web

John Gurney of Braintree may have been the son of Francis Gurney, a Merchant Taylor of West Barsham, Norwich and London, by his first wife Margaret Rybett, whom Francis married at Norwich on 23 September 1611. He is the leading named candidate and the only one carrying a coherent circumstantial case, but he is not the most probable single answer and the identification is open. The case rests on one primary record — that marriage — supported by a father-to-son trade, the East Anglia-to-London emigration corridor, the documented 1634 liquidation of Francis's land as motive, a militant Puritan uncle, and Margaret's own kin settled within three miles of the East Dereham parish. No record names John as Francis's son. Candidate B scores nothing on every attribute describing an actual household — a wife, children, dates — and the East Dereham register years that would hold a first child of the 1611 marriage are permanently lost with no second witness, so the hypothesis retains no testable prediction in that parish.

Type: Identity hypothesis

Candidate D: the redemption-not-patrimony puzzle, explained by a Stationers' apprenticeship

John's choice of the Drapers' Company redemption route in 1623/4, rather than the patrimony his father's 1581 freedom would ordinarily have earned him, is best explained by a prior apprenticeship in a different livery company: a Stationers' Company record binds a John Gurney apprentice to the stationer James Boler on 25 March 1613, with no later Stationers' freedom recorded under Boler. If this is the same man, an aborted or completed Stationers' term would make the 1623/4 Drapers' redemption the ordinary consequence rather than a puzzle, and the reading sits comfortably beside Robert's own choice of the stationer Joseph Henscott as a 1625 will overseer -- the same Hunscott/Henscott household still resident in St Augustine parish in 1638. No father's name survives on the 1613 binding to confirm the identification outright, so it stays a plausible explanation, not a proven one.

Evidence — Family/compiler memory about John Gurney misattaches real facts, rather than inventing them

Family and compiler memory about John Gurney tends to preserve real information misattached to the wrong subject, rather than fabricate claims outright. The birth-date and Southwark-origin tradition is the true biography of a different, younger man (the 1636 Newgate apprentice) attached to the older Braintree settler; the arms tradition rests on a genuinely Norfolk blazon whose American inheritance is unverified rather than disproven; and Charles Banks's own "Bury St Edmunds" attribution for John is a parallel case, a twentieth-century compiler's note that likewise proved to track the Newgate apprentice rather than either proving or disproving a Norfolk origin. Read together, the pattern is a caution for weighing any further tradition that surfaces about him: expect real information pointing at the wrong target before expecting invention.

Evidence — The 1636 Newgate 'John Gurney' apprentice is not the Braintree emigrant

The 21 July 1636 Newgate apprenticeship names a different John Gurney, not the Braintree emigrant. The apprentice was apparently born about September 1615; the Braintree John reported himself about fifty in 1652/3, pointing to c. 1602/3, and had married by the mid-1620s with a daughter born by c. 1628. A man born c. 1615 would have been about nine to thirteen during that family period. This is a chronological incompatibility, not a claim that an early end to the apprentice's term constrained the emigrant's arrival.

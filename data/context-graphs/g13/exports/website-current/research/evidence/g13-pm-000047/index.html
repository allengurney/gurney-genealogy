Evidence — The 1636 Newgate 'John Gurney' apprentice is not the Braintree emigrant

The single enrolled apprenticeship of a 'John Gurney' anywhere in the New England record - the 21 July 1636 Boston-court order binding John Gurney, a runaway apprentice, to the feltmaker John Newgate until age 24 - belongs to a younger man than the Braintree emigrant: it implies a birth about 1615, against the emigrant's 1652/3 deposition age of 'about 50' (birth c.1602/3), and Newgate's own later records name no Gurney. De-conflated from John of Braintree in the identity work and carried here only for its trade bearing, it documents that other man's service, so no enrolled record of John of Braintree's own trade training survives even in the colony that received him.

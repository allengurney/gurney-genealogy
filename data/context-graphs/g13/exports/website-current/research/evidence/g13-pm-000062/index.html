Evidence — The Norfolk-Hingham reception corridor fits a Norfolk origin

Adjacent Hingham is the reception corridor that fits a Norfolk origin. New England towns drew regionally concentrated cohorts, and Hingham's was overwhelmingly Norfolk - Daniel Cushing's list gives 206 who came from Norfolk and settled there 1633-1639, the 1638 Diligent alone bringing about 133 - out of Hingham, Wymondham, Attleborough, Great Ellingham, and the Dereham neighbourhood, the very parishes framing the Gurney Norfolk world, including Great Ellingham (Henry Gurney G15's seat), Hingham (the family's Gurney's Manor), and East Dereham (John's probable baptism parish). Weymouth itself was West-Country-led with a Buckinghamshire stream and no Norfolk feed, so a Norfolk John would be an outsider to Weymouth's founders but a natural fit for the Norfolk colony under ten miles away, across the town line his lots abut. A Norfolk arrival received through Hingham is thus the one reception model that suits the favoured origin; the origin case itself is held to the identity work.

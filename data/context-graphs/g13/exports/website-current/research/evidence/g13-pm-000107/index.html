Evidence — Did John emigrate through the Gurdon-Winthrop Court of Wards network, or merely out of the same county?

Whether John Gurney reached Massachusetts through the Gurdon-Winthrop Court of Wards patronage network, or simply left the same county in the same decade as thousands of others, is unresolved. The network evidence is real but attaches to the senior West Barsham branch, not to him: Brampton Gurdon's letter of 20 October 1627 shows the widowed "mrs. Gurny" presenting her son's wardship through Winthrop's own office, and Candidate B's John stands two steps from that line, a nephew of it through his father Francis. No record connects him to it. A documented patronage route would turn a plausible corridor into a mechanism. The test that would close the chain is a patron-side search of the Norwich diocesan institution books for a 1627-28 presentation to the West Barsham vicarage made by right of the Gurney wardship; it cannot identify the emigrant, but it would make the Gurdon-Winthrop connection citable fact rather than strong construction.

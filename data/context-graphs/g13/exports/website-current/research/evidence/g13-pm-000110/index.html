Evidence — Two realistic end-states for the estimate and three levers that would move it

Two end-states are realistic for the parentage estimate. A marriage record for John and Mary in the Rivett-orbit parishes around East Dereham, or a kinsman's mention of a Gurney beyond the seas in one of the unread Norfolk family wills of the 1650s, would lift the identification from probable to highly probable, near eighty-five to ninety percent. Failing that, continued negative searching leaves it a permanently caveated best identification in the sixty-five to seventy percent band. Three levers carry most of the remaining value: Norfolk parish and probate images in the Rivett orbit, a professional examination of the East Dereham register, and a Y-DNA test of the discriminating English-line kit. The fact sheet now matches the case file's about-sixty-five-percent estimate; the figure remains a caveated synthesis of indirect evidence, not a substitute for the missing direct record.

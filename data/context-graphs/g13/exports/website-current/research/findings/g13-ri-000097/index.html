Banks's Bury attribution is an unproven lead that most plausibly tracked Newgate's apprentice; consistent with a Norfolk origin

Banks's 'Bury St Edmunds' is an unproven lead, not a birthplace - and it is consistent with the favoured Norfolk (Candidate B) origin rather than evidence against it. Sourced only to 'Banks Mss.', with no supporting Bury natal record found, the attribution is best explained by the 1636 apprentice John Gurney whom the Bury-area feltmaker John Newgate brought before the Boston court: that younger man, de-conflated from the Braintree emigrant in the identity work, carried the Bury/Suffolk association, and Banks's memo most plausibly tracked him. Anderson's Great Migration Directory (p. 158) independently declines Banks, entering the origin as 'Unknown'. A Norwich-born son of Francis Gurney who trained at or near Bury would fit Banks's note without contradiction.

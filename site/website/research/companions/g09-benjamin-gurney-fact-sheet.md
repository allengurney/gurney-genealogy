## Working Notes

### Three same-name Benjamins in succession

The direct line includes three consecutive Benjamins: G11 (c. 1676 – d. 1738/9), G10 (c. 1704 – before 1772), and G9 (1730 – 1805). G9 is the third generation to bear the name. The same-name run, common in colonial New England, is a routine source of misattribution in early Cummington and Plymouth County records; in particular, "Benjamin Gurney" appearing in mid-eighteenth-century Abington or Bridgewater records may be either G10 or G9 depending on date and context.

### Born of a liaison; raised by maternal aunt

The pre-marital relationship between Benjamin G10 and Jane Harden, recorded in <code>data/ancestors v26.json</code> G10 entry, is the most distinctive single fact about G9's origin. Jane's "1711 baptism 'of Little Comfort'" — referenced in the G10 entry — locates her family at the small mill site of that name. G9 was raised by his maternal aunt rather than in his father's eventual household with Sarah Morse (married 1731 at Middleboro), which is why John Harden's 1751 will — preserving the maternal grandfather's recognition — is the key documentary anchor of his identity.

### The 1770 transition: Abington → Cummington

The two anchor records are:

- **June 1770**: sale of Abington land (specific deed reference not captured here yet — Plymouth County deeds).
- **5 November 1770**: purchase, with Silas Reed, of land in "Town No. 5" — the proto-Cummington — recorded in Springfield, Massachusetts records.

Foster &amp; Streeter, <em>"Only One Cummington"</em> (1974), p. 390, is the published source preserving the 1787 farm exchange with Philip Shaw. The 1770 transactions themselves should appear in the original Plymouth County and Hampshire County deed indices and merit direct citation in any future revision.

### 1790 federal census — Cummington

Household structure 3-0-3 (three males under sixteen, three females) is preserved in <code>data/ancestors v26.json</code>. This is consistent with Benjamin (now ~60) being head of a household that includes Mercy Noyes (his second wife) and several younger children. Notably, son Amos (G8) was born in 1770 and would have been twenty in 1790 — old enough to appear in the older male category — but the household structure as recorded does not list a male in the 16+ bracket apart from Benjamin himself. That suggests either Amos was already heading his own household by 1790, or the structural-summary interpretation needs rechecking against the actual census image.

### Burial at Dawes Cemetery

This is one of the firmest burial records in the colonial-American chapter. A photograph or inscription transcription of the marker would substantially strengthen the entry.

### Negative results

- No will or probate record for G9 captured here yet (Hampshire County probate, c. 1805–1810).
- Marriage records for either Elizabeth Harden or Mercy Noyes not yet located in detail.
- Names of his other children (the household structure suggests at least four–five surviving) not yet established.

## Open Questions

1. **Other children.** Cummington and Hampshire County VR should preserve births to Benjamin and either wife. Not yet captured.
2. **Two marriages — when?** Order of wives, dates, and which wife mothered which children is not in the JSON. The split is research-significant because the Cummington-era children would more likely be by Mercy.
3. **Probate of Benjamin's 1805 estate.** Hampshire County, Massachusetts probate records.
4. **Original 1770 deeds** — the June Abington sale and the 5 November Cummington purchase. Plymouth County deeds and Hampshire County (or the early Springfield, then-county-town) records.

## The name rests on a single hedge

The single most important caveat for this ancestor: Daniel Gurney himself was uncertain whether Robert was actually named "Robert." His *Record*, Part II, p. 363, reads "a second son, whom we believe was named Robert" — the only source, and an explicit hedge. The children of Edmund G23 are listed there on the authority of one document, the Pedigree by Cook, Clarenceux, 1622 (footnote *h* on that page). All subsequent genealogical tradition adopts "Robert" but inherits the hedge.

Stirnet's "Pedigree of the Gournays of Norfolk" preserves the same hedge: Robert appears as `((2)) (Robert) Gurney` — parenthesised given name, indicating Stirnet's compiler did not resolve the identification beyond Daniel Gurney. Stirnet cites RHG vol. 1, pp. 286–287 as its source.[^stirnet-gurney-pedigree-2026]

The hedge survives an exhaustive search of Daniel Gurney's own apparatus: the Supplement Notes 115–123 were read for any mention of Robert by name and **none found** — all concern the collateral John the Rector, Edmund G23, Sir John V, or Thomas I (G21). Robert remains the least documented direct-line ancestor in the G37–G18 range.

The name itself was not unique in the Norfolk of his day: contemporary records carry "Robert Gurnay" at multiple social levels (see *A contemporary Robert Gurnay of Little Cressingham*, below), which is part of why even pinning the name is hard.

## Why Robert matters: the collateral succession

Robert is genealogically critical because the direct line passes through him, not through his elder brother Sir John Gurney V (d. 1408). Daniel Gurney's Supplement Note 121 (Sir John's inquisition post mortem) proves Sir John's son Edmund was heir aged 10 and "died sine prole, and probably under age." Supplement Note 118 (Edmund G23's will) names four executors including "John his son" — the elder brother Sir John V, not G24. Robert's son Thomas I (G21) therefore inherited as Sir John's nephew of blood. The full succession chain runs Edmund G23 → [Robert G22] → Thomas I (G21), bypassing the collateral Sir John V. This is the standing direct-line correction: the line runs through Robert G22, not the collateral Sir John.

Blomefield's manorial descent for West Barsham confirms it from the other direction. The chain runs: Edmund Wauncy → Edmund Gurney → "John de Gourney ... died 9th Henry IV [1407/08] seised of the manors of West and North Barsham, Harpley, Denver, Depeden" → "Thomas Gourney ... mentioned as feoffee in 13th Henry VI" [= 1434/35] → Thomas senior (will 9 Edward IV, 1469) → William → William jr → Anthony → Frances → Henry → Edmund (d. 1641) → Henry (sold to Calthorpe).[^bho-blomefield-vol7-west-barsham-descent] **Robert is not named at any point in the chain** — independent confirmation that he held no main estate at West Barsham, matching the portrait of a younger son with no documentary footprint as a manorial lord. The 1434/35 feoffee reference pins Thomas Gournay I (G21) as a documented active adult by 13 Henry VI — 26 years after the 1408 inheritance crisis through which he received the estates as Sir John V's nephew of blood.

## Sir John V's death and inquisition (1408–09)

Sir John V was reappointed sheriff on 15 November 1408 and died less than three weeks later, on **4 December 1408**. Blomefield records him as having died "9th Henry IV" (regnal year 30 September 1407 – 29 September 1408) "seised of the manors of West and North Barsham, Harpley, Denver, Depeden,"[^bho-blomefield-vol7-west-barsham-descent-cross] but the History of Parliament biography's specific date of 4 December 1408 falls in 10 Henry IV, not 9.[^hop-gurney-johnv-deathdate] Daniel Gurney's Supplement Note 121 records the inquisition post mortem as "taken at Holt market, 10 Henry IV," siding with the History of Parliament dating.[^dg-supp-ipm-johnv-10henryiv] Blomefield's "9 Henry IV" is most plausibly a regnal-year approximation or a copying slip; the History of Parliament date is anchored to the escheator's writ.

The inquisition is calendared in the published *Calendar of Inquisitions Post Mortem*, vol. 19 (Henry IV), entries 489–94 (TNA C 137/70/6), freely readable in the British History Online digitisation — corroborating Daniel Gurney's Supplement Note 121 from an independent source and adding detail: the writ issued 16 February 1409; inquisitions were taken at East Rudham (26 March), Holt (27 March — Daniel Gurney's "Holt market") and Attlebridge (28 March) 1409; **John died 4 December 1408**; and his **heir was Edmund his son, aged 10** (born c. 1398). The decisive datum is the jurors' finding that **"he held nothing in Norfolk"** — that is, nothing of the Crown in chief. The West Barsham, Harpley, Hardingham and Heylesdon estates were therefore held of mesne lords, not the king; this is why the royal inquisition is thin and records no manor portfolio, and why no royal IPM was ever taken for Edmund G23 (see the G23 companion). The vol. 19 index groups the entries as "Gournay, John, and Edmund his son, 489–94": Edmund the heir appears only inside his father's inquisition, not as the subject of a separate one.[^bho-cipm-vol19-johnv-ipm-1409]

## The Heylesdon inheritance

Alice Heylesdon, daughter and eventual sole heir of John Heylesdon — wealthy London mercer and former alderman — brought to her marriage with Sir John Gurney V a substantially larger settlement than the single London warehouse "La Selde Coronata" that Daniel Gurney and the earlier fact sheet highlighted. The full settlement comprised the manors of Hellesdon and Drayton, the advowsons of both parish churches, the two chantries founded in her father's memory, houses in Norwich, and "La Selde Coronata."[^hop-gurney-heylesdon-settlement] When Sir John V's only son Edmund died sine prole under age c. 1409/10, this entire Heylesdon settlement entered the inheritance crisis that ultimately moved the estates to Robert's son Thomas I as Sir John V's nephew of blood.

The downstream history of the Heylesdon estates belongs to later generations and is treated on their companions: Alice survived Sir John V by at least 25 years, sold "Loundhall" in Saxthorpe to John Wynter to pay his debts, married twice more (the Fitzalan retainer Sir John Wiltshire, d. 1428, and then Richard Selling esq.), and in **1433 sold the bulk of her Heylesdon inheritance to Sir John Fastolf KG** — the upstream event for the Heylesdon → Fastolf → Paston → Heydon chain that culminated in great-grandson **William Gurney IV's (G19)** 1472 Saxthorpe contest. Full treatment is on the **G23** companion (Alice's three marriages and the 1433 sale) and the **G19** companion (the 1472 Saxthorpe Court showdown); the 1384 Husting will of John Heylesdon, the foundation document, is at [`sources/corpus_supplement/husting-wills-london-vol2-john-heylesdon-1384.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/sources/corpus_supplement/husting-wills-london-vol2-john-heylesdon-1384.md).

## Joan de Norwich (Robert's wife)

The Gurney pedigree in Daniel Gurney's *Record* (p. 286) names Robert's wife as "Joan de Norwich." No further detail on her family survives in Daniel Gurney or any other source consulted.

The *territorial* de Norwich (de Norwico) barony of Mettingham and Raveningham is **not** her source: that male line failed and its estates passed by 1375 to the heiress **Catharine de Brews** (daughter of Thomas de Norwich) and thence to the **Uffords**, leaving no marriageable Joan de Norwich of Robert's generation (Blomefield; the Mettingham/Raveningham descent). So "de Norwich" most plausibly denotes an **urban/locative Norwich family** — consistent with Edmund G23's Norwich legal-civic standing as the city's standing counsel — or is simply unrecoverable. No candidate Joan has been found in the gentry/baronial line.

## Jeanne, Robert's sister — the Mundeford marriage

Robert had a sister, Jeanne, who married **Osbert Mundeford of Hockwold**. This is Daniel Gurney's own claim — *Record*, Part II, p. 363: "also a daughter Jeanne, married to Osbert Mundeford of Hockwold, Esq. who was one of the executors of his will" — resting, like the rest of Edmund's issue, on the Cook, Clarenceux 1622 pedigree. It is **corroborated by the will itself**: Edmund G23's 1387 will appoints "Osbert de Mundeford and Thomas Kemp" among his executors (Daniel Gurney, *Record*, Part II, p. 363; full Latin copy at Supplement Note 118, pp. 789–791, from Harl. MSS. 10, fol. 144) — a documented Mundeford–Gurney tie at exactly Edmund's generation.

The marriage was earlier recorded here as "Stirnet's claim ... treated as unsupported," on the ground that Blomefield's Hockwold descent and the Feltwell parish history name no Gurney bride.[^stirnet-gurney-pedigree-jeanne-mundeford][^bho-blomefield-vol2-hockwold-mundeford][^feltwell-net-mundefords-of-feltwell-cite] That verdict was a **generation mismatch**: Blomefield's Hockwold Osberts (wives Alice, Elizabeth, Margaret) and the Feltwell Mundefords (Adam d. 1463 m. Esselina; Osbert d. 1479 m. Elizabeth; Francis d. 1520 m. Margaret; Osbert d. 1580 m. Margaret Townshend then Bridget Spelman) are fifteenth- and sixteenth-century men, while the executor Osbert de Mundeford is a contemporary of Edmund's 1387 will, absent from those later lists. The marriage is therefore Daniel Gurney's claim with a will-executor anchor, **not** a Stirnet invention and not disconfirmed. Jeanne is Edmund G23's daughter; fuller treatment belongs on the G23 companion.

## Robert Gurnay of Little Cressingham — Robert G22 in the contemporary record

A single Robert Gurnay is well-attested in west-central Norfolk inside G22's traditional floruit window (c. 1370–1420), and on a close reading of the records he is Robert G22 himself, not a separate local namesake. The question is no longer "does any record name a contemporary Robert Gurnay" (yes, richly), nor even "could the Cressingham Robert be Daniel Gurney's G22," but how securely the two are equated. The two arguments once raised against the identification — distance from the family seat and the absence of gentry style — do not survive scrutiny (see the assessment below); the one element not separately documented is the filiation, and on the convergence of evidence the identification is treated as confirmed, consistent with the ancestor's standing "Confirmed" lineage status.

**1366 Mulbarton partition — the name at multiple social levels.** The 1366 partition of the Norfolk lands of Thomas de Sancto Omero, made at Mulkebertone (Mulbarton) on 23–25 May 1366, lists "the services of Robert Gurnay, John Pigot and William Stalon" among the free tenants delivered to William de Hoo and his wife Alice, and separately names "bondmen named Nicholas Elvard, Henry Isabel, Robert Gurnay, John Dobyn, Henry Short and Walter Smyht" among the manor's villein tenants.[^bho-ipm-vol12-mulbarton-sancto-omero-1366] Two distinct men named Robert Gurnay — one a free tenant, one a bondman — are therefore visible in a single Norfolk partition four years before G22's traditional flourishing window. Neither matches G22, but the entry sharpens the hedge on the name by confirming that "Robert Gurnay" was carried in 1360s Norfolk at multiple social levels, not only in the gentry line.

**1405 Little Cressingham fine.** A fine levied in 1405 at Little Cressingham, South Greenhoe Hundred, names "Robert Gurnay of Cressingham-Parva" and Thomas Stodhagh as querents against Edward Howard and Catherine his wife, deforciants, over "several parcels of land, and the liberty of a foldcourse here, and in Hopton."[^bho-blomefield-vol6-little-cressingham-1405-gurnay-fine] This is the earliest currently-identified Norfolk record naming a Robert Gurnay within G22's floruit window. Identification with G22 is not warranted: the residence formula "of Cressingham-Parva" denotes permanent residence at a parish twenty-five miles south of West Barsham, in a cluster not previously associated with the Edmund-Gurney line, and the Hopton/Cressingham foldcourse does not re-enter the West Barsham descent — when Anthony Gurnay obtained the fourth part of the manor of Hopton with messuages in Cressingham Magna and Parva, Hilburgh, Bodney, Threxton, and Hopton in 19 Henry VIII (1527/28), he did so by marriage to Margaret Lovel, daughter and heir of Sir Robert Lovel: a fresh acquisition by marriage, not by inheritance traceable to a 1405 Robert.[^bho-blomefield-vol6-little-cressingham-anthony-1528][^bho-ipm-edward-iii-vol12-sancto-omero-mulbarton-1366-cross] The fine has been read in Walter Rye's *A Short Calendar of the Feet of Fines for Norfolk*, Part II, entry 64: *"Robert Gurnay of Parva Cressyngham and Thomas Stodhagh v. Edward Howard and Katherine his wife, in Parva Cressyngham and Hopton."*[^rye-fof-pt2-cressingham-1405] It carries **no recital of parentage** — Robert is identified only by residence. Parentage recitals are common in fines where the querent is a younger son recently of age, so their absence here means the fine neither confirms the West Barsham identification nor closes it by naming an alternate father — it identifies him by residence alone, which (see the assessment below) places him within the family's landholding orbit rather than outside it.

**Five IPM-juror appearances, 1400–1406.** The same Robert Gurnay is independently documented as an inquisition juror on five panels — the strongest corroboration yet that the 1405-fine man was real and locally rooted. The E-CIPM Index of Jurors lists "Robert Gurnay" of Norfolk on the juries of the IPM of **William Leche of Newton** (by Castle Acre) in 1400 and 1401 (vol. 18 nos. 70–71; vol. 19 no. 88) and of **Thomas de Beauchamp, Earl of Warwick** in 1401 (vol. 18 no. 500) — the latter inquiring into the earl's Norfolk lands at **Little Cressingham**, Saham Toney, Necton and Panworth-in-Ashill, i.e. Robert's own parish. The same West-Norfolk juror cohort (Warde, Cok, Canoun, Stag', Foulden, de Rudham) reappears on a fifth panel, the 1406 IPM of Roger de Beauchamp (vol. 19 no. 79, where he is spelled "Gournay"), confirming these are one man, not several. Across all five he is plainly "Robert Gurnay/Gournay" with no "esquire" or residence designation — but, as the assessment below sets out, that absence is a feature of the juror genre, not a status marker. The *existence* and *locale* of "Robert Gurnay of Little Cressingham, fl. 1400–06" are firmly attested.[^ecipm-jurors-robert-2026]

**Assessment — the case for and against.** Three considerations once read as arguing against the West Barsham identification, re-examined:

1. *Geography is consistent, not contrary.* The "~20–25 miles south of West Barsham" measure used the family's northernmost seat alone. The family's landholding sphere ran well south of it — Hardingham (held by Edmund G23, ~12 miles from Little Cressingham), the cadet "Gurney's Manor" at Hingham and the Great Ellingham junior line, and Denver — placing the Cressingham / Wayland / South Greenhoe district *inside* the family's orbit. A younger son provided for modestly would plausibly be seated on these central-southern margins rather than at the northern caput, and the cadet presence at Hingham/Great Ellingham shows the line was already established in exactly this district.
2. *The absent gentry style is a record-genre artifact.* The IPM juror lists record no status or residence for any juror. And in the same Walter Rye feet-of-fines series the family's most eminent members appear without style — Edmund G23, the foremost lawyer in Norfolk, as plain "Edm. Gournay" (Rye Pt II #1526), and Sir John Gurney V, knight and sheriff, as plain "John Gournay" (Pt II #262); only one Gurnay fine in the whole run (#200, Thomas, 1444/45) carries "Armiger." So the plain "Robert Gurnay" of the 1405 fine and the juries is exactly what the family's own knights and lawyers show, and discriminates nothing.
3. *There is no competing candidate.* The E-CIPM Index of Jurors holds no Norfolk Gurnay juror but this Robert in the period; the only other Norfolk Robert Gurnays are the 1366 Mulbarton pair (a generation earlier, different parish, one of them a bondman) and the excluded Somerset Robert. Gurnay was a rare Norfolk surname with a limited, largely-accounted-for set of branches. With no rival, the sole contemporary Robert Gurnay is the natural match for the Robert the pedigree records.

Positively, the identification draws its strength from the convergence of two independent witnesses. Daniel Gurney's *Record* is a secondary compilation, but a demonstrably reliable one — repeatedly borne out where it can be tested against primary record — and it independently reports a second son of this generation "whom we believe was named Robert"; the contemporary land and juror records then independently supply a Robert Gurnay of exactly that rare name, era, and family region. Two unrelated sources pointing to the same man is materially stronger than either alone.

The one element not separately documented is the filiation — no parentage is recited in the fine, and the Cressingham/Hopton holding does not resurface in the senior descent (though a younger son's modest land need not, and Thomas I inherited West Barsham rather than his father's cadet parcel). The social station is on the modest side for a great lawyer's son, but normal for a younger son. Net: the Cressingham Robert is identified with G22 on the convergence of a reliable pedigree and the contemporary record — the ancestor's standing "Confirmed" status — with the filiation the single element not independently attested; the earlier "probably not / leans against" reading over-weighted distance-from-West-Barsham and a genre-driven absence of "esquire."

## Stirnet pedigree: concordances and errors

Stirnet's Norfolk Gournay pedigree agrees with Daniel Gurney on the central hedge (above), but introduces three errors:

1. **Edmund's death year.** Stirnet gives Edmund Gurney's death as 1385, conflicting with *Record*, Part II, p. 363 (and with the will-date evidence preserved in Reg. Harsyke fol. 34), which place his death in May 1387. Treat 1385 as a Stirnet error.[^stirnet-gurney-pedigree-2026]
2. **Generation-flattening of Thomas I.** Stirnet lists Thomas Gournay (G21) as a direct son of Edmund Gurney (d. 1387) — a sibling of Sir John V and Robert — rather than as Robert's son and Sir John V's nephew of blood.[^stirnet-gurney-pedigree-flattening] Supplement Note 121 (Sir John V's IPM proving collateral succession) and the History of Parliament biography of Sir John Gurney d. 1408 ("the family estates passed to John's nephew, Thomas") settle the placement against Stirnet.
3. **Jeanne–Mundeford** is also in Stirnet, but it is in fact Daniel Gurney's claim, will-corroborated — see *Jeanne, Robert's sister*, above; not a Stirnet-only assertion.

## Edmund G23's will — already in hand, and not a path to Robert's name

Edmund G23's will, proved 1387 at the Norwich Consistory Court and registered in *Register Harsyk* fol. 34, is **not** an untapped lead. The register folio was "utterly rotted and destroyed" by the eighteenth century (Daniel Gurney, *Record*, Part II, p. 363, reporting that "in the time of Mr. Norris the part of the register which contained it was utterly rotted and destroyed"); the will survives only through Blomefield's transcription and the full Latin copy at Daniel Gurney's Supplement Note 118 (pp. 789–791, from Harl. MSS. 10, fol. 144) — both already in the repository.[^ncc-reg-harsyk-edmund-will][^hop-gurney-surflete]

Crucially, the will **does not enumerate the children**. It bequeaths Katherine her dower and goods, directs a burial with thirteen paupers in white vestments bearing torches, adds a restitution clause, and appoints executors (Katherine his wife, John his son, Osbert de Mundeford, Thomas Kempe). Daniel Gurney sources the issue list — John, the second son "whom we believe was named Robert," and Jeanne — separately, to the Cook, Clarenceux 1622 pedigree, not to the will. So retrieving or re-reading the will cannot attest the second son's name; the **1622 pedigree at the College of Arms remains the only document that could** (standing lead, below). What the will does supply is a documentary anchor, independent of Daniel Gurney's narrative, for the 1387 death-date and the lawyer-of-Norwich identification of Edmund.

## The medieval-soldier cohort, and the "was G22 a Richard?" question

The *Soldier in Later Medieval England* database, evaluated in full at `research/topics/gurney-medieval-soldier-database.md`, yields a short cohort of otherwise-undocumented Gournay/Gurney men in the G23–G21 window who bear on whether Robert had brothers, sons, or cadets — and on whether his own name was even "Robert." None is an identification; each turns on a manuscript membrane.

The strongest is **Richard Gurney, esquire**, a man-at-arms in the 1387 and 1388 naval expeditions of Richard FitzAlan, earl of Arundel (sub-captain Edward Courtenay, earl of Devon).[^v75-richard-msd] Two facts make him worth pursuing as more than a namesake. First, his rank is gentry (esquire), his date and generation fit a son of Edmund G23, and he served under **Arundel — the very magnate to whom the West Barsham Gurneys were tied** (the family held Harpley of Arundel, and Robert's elder brother Sir John Gurney V was steward of FitzAlan's Norfolk estates before 1386).[^v75-arundel-hop] Second, he is **unplaced in both the Norfolk and the Somerset Gournay pedigrees**: no medieval Richard Gurney appears in Daniel Gurney's otherwise-exhaustive *Record* for the Norfolk line, and the Somerset Harptree line runs Anselm → John → Thomas with no Richard. Because Daniel Gurney himself hedged G22's given name, an Arundel-affinity gentry esquire of exactly the right generation is a candidate for **G22 himself**, not merely a brother. The tempering consideration is the same clean negative: had Edmund's second son been a Richard, the Norfolk pedigree sources Daniel Gurney used would more likely have caught the name than left it blank-and-hedged. The discriminator is the 1387/88 muster membrane (TNA E101/40/34, E101/41/5), whose retinue neighbours would site Richard Norfolk versus West-Country — lead L-72.

Three further men are held as cadet leads: a **John Gurnay**, man-at-arms in the 1422 retinue of **Sir Robert Harling of East Harling** (a Norfolk knight, nephew of Sir John Fastolf, captor of Meulan in 1423) in the Poissy / Pont-Meulan garrison (L-74); a **John Gournay** in the Harfleur garrison in 1417–18 under Thomas Beaufort and Sir Hugh Luttrell, parallel to Thomas G21's own 1418 Harfleur service (L-73); and a low-status **Robert Gurney**, valet-archer on Henry V's 1415 sick list — the only exact-name "Robert Gurney" in G22's floruit window, though his yeoman rank argues against the gentleman G22 (L-75).[^v75-richard-msd]

The geography triage that produced this short list also **excludes** the louder soldier-database leads as non-Norfolk: the "Sir John de Gournay" knight cluster (Harptree, Somerset), Sir Matthew de Gournay (Somerset), a John Gourney "mercer of London," and a John Gournay "of Colchester, Essex." Details and the full disposition table are in the topic file.

## Berney-side corroboration; the Gunton attribution resolved

Independent of the Gurney biography, the History of Parliament life of **Sir Robert Berney (c. 1365–1415)** corroborates John Gurney V's place in the Lancastrian Norfolk bloc from the Berney side — fellow Norfolk shire knight in 1399 and Erpingham co-trustee — and documents the Saxthorpe/Loundhall feoffee circle (Erpingham, Wynter, Berney, Shelton) from a non-Gurney source.[^v75-hop-berney] The root scholarly treatment of that bloc is Trevor John's 1970 *Norfolk Archaeology* study of Erpingham and the 1399 revolution.[^v75-trevor-john] The Berney biography also settles whether the much-repeated "Sir Robert Gurney of Gunton, deputy to Erpingham at Dover in 1400" is a Gurney or — far more likely — **Sir Robert Berney of Gunton**: the figure is **Berney**, whose seat, office (deputy to Erpingham at Dover castle and warden of the Cinque Ports), and date (returned to Parliament 1402 while in that office, which he held c. May 1400 to after March 1406) match the contested phrase exactly. The "Gurney of Gunton" reading is a Berney/Gurney surname confusion and stays excluded as a Robert G22 lead.

## Exhausted searches and negative results

Documented by source surveyed; each is a first-class negative result, not an absence of work.

- **FMG MedLands.** The Foundation for Medieval Genealogy's "English Untitled Nobility A–C" page covers the Gournay family through the 13th century only and does not extend into the 14th-century Junior Norfolk branch. Robert is below its coverage horizon.[^fmg-medlands-2026]
- **FamilySearch records and Full-Text search.** No 14th-century Norfolk Robert Gurney returned. The only nominal near-match for a Robert + Gurney in the 1360–1380 birth range in Norfolk is "Robert Garneys" of Beccles, Suffolk, d. May 1411 — a different surname (Garneys is a distinct Norfolk-Suffolk family seated at Boyland, Suffolk). Full-Text search for "Robert Gurnay" Norfolk in the 1380–1430 window returned only post-1700 tax records and one Beverstone (Somerset) extent of 1269 referencing an earlier Robert de Gurnay (G31 or earlier).[^fs-records-search-2026]
- **Ancestry.com Norfolk Wills, Probate, Administrations and Marriage Licence Bonds, 1371–1858.** No 14th- or early-15th-century Robert Gurney appears, despite the collection beginning in 1371. The earliest Norfolk Robert Gurney in the index is from 1588 (a man of Hempnall, unrelated). Confirms no surviving will or probate record exists for Robert.[^ancestry-norfolk-wills-2026]
- **History of Parliament Online "GURNEY, John (d.1408)".** The modern scholarly biography of Robert's elder brother references Robert only indirectly as the father of "John's nephew, Thomas." No biographical content on Robert himself.[^hop-john-gurney-1408]
- **Mapping the Medieval Countryside / Calendar of Inquisitions Post Mortem.** No Gurney/Gurnay/Gournay is the *subject* (deceased tenant-in-chief) of any indexed E-CIPM inquisition — consistent with the West Barsham line holding nothing of the Crown in chief (Sir John V's 1409 IPM expressly finds he "held nothing in Norfolk"). But the family is **not** absent: its members recur as feoffees, fee-holders and jurors *inside other men's inquisitions*, indexed under "Gournay" in the Index of Persons (cross-referencing Gornay/Gourney/Gurnay/Gurney/Gourncoy) and under "Gurnay" in the Index of Jurors. Most bear on collateral lines (Sir Matthew d. 1406 with 13 entries; the Somerset/Wiltshire/Gloucestershire fee-holders; a separate Buckinghamshire Gurney family of Stoke Mandeville), but two touch the Norfolk direct line: **Edmund Gournay** as feoffee of the manor of Hingham and hundred of Forehoe (see the G23 companion), and **Robert Gurnay of Norfolk as an IPM juror 1400–06** (treated in the Cressingham section above). Full harvest at [`sources/corpus_supplement/ecipm-gournay-mentions-index-of-persons.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/sources/corpus_supplement/ecipm-gournay-mentions-index-of-persons.md).[^mmc-cipm-2026][^ecipm-harvest-2026]
- **Norfolk feet of fines (Rye, Parts I & II) — harvested 2026-06.** Walter Rye's *Short Calendar* was read in full for Gurnay/Gournay across both parts. **Robert's 1405 fine is confirmed in Part II, entry 64** (the OCR mangles the surname to "Qximay," so it was recovered by anchoring on the cleanly-printed co-party and places — Stodhagh, Cressingham, Hopton, Howard — not by spelling). Germye, Chivaler" of Tharston** (Pt II #329) falls in **8 Edward III (1334/35)** — more than a century before the 15th-century Paston "William Gurney esq. of Tharston," so #329 cannot be that man; with the early date, knightly rank, and the "Germye" spelling, it most likely belongs to the distinct Norfolk **Germy(e)** family of Tharston rather than the Gournays, substantially weakening lead L-162. **"Rad. Gereneye," parson, of the manors of Saxthorp and Swatthing** (Pt II #432) falls in **9 Edward III (1335/36)**; the spelling does not map cleanly onto the Gournay head-forms, so the Saxthorpe tie is the only Gournay signal and the identification stays uncertain (lead L-163). The direct-line and clerical Gurnay fines are likewise now dated — Robert of Parva Cressingham (#64) to 6 Henry IV (1405), Thomas Gurnay Armiger & Margaret (#200) to 23 Henry VI (1444/45), and John Gurnay rector of Harpley (#489) to 9 Edward II (1315/16). "Geney" of Gunton and "Gene" of Tilney were read and excluded as distinct surnames.[^rye-fines-harvest-2026-06]

## Standing leads

1. **Cook, Clarenceux 1622 Visitation pedigree of Norfolk (College of Arms) — highest-value.** Daniel Gurney cites this as his sole source for Edmund's children. If the original names Robert without hedging, it would be the only independent attestation of the name beyond Daniel Gurney. Because the will (above) does not enumerate the children, this pedigree is the *only* document that could settle the standing name question. <!-- lead L-32, priority 85 -->
2. **IPM for Edmund, son of Sir John V (c. 1409–10) — closed as a probable dead end.** Such an inquisition would have named Thomas Gournay I as next heir and established the line through Robert. But Sir John V's own IPM (CIPM vol. 19, entries 489–94, British History Online; C 137/70/6) finds the family "held nothing in Norfolk" in chief and groups "John and Edmund his son" under that single inquisition. Because nothing was held of the Crown, a separate substantive royal IPM for the short-lived minor is unlikely to exist; the manor descent must be traced through feet of fines and Blomefield rather than an inquisition.
3. **Norfolk feet of fines — harvested (see negative results); one image outstanding and now higher-value.** Image-resolution of the 1405 entry-64 membrane (AALT path mapped; lead L-158) matters more given the strengthened Cressingham candidacy: the manuscript fine, unlike Rye's calendar and its degraded OCR, may carry an addition, residence detail, or parentage that the printed abstract drops — the kind of detail that could confirm or break the Cressingham-Robert = G22 identification.

Together, lead **L-32** (the 1622 College of Arms pedigree) and the **L-158** fine image are the two documents most likely to settle whether the Little Cressingham Robert is G22 — the first by attesting Edmund's second son's name independently, the second by recovering any parentage or addition the calendar dropped.

**Paston "cousin Gurney of Tharston" — resolved on G19.** The "William Gurney, esq., of Tharston" of the Paston correspondence, formerly held here as an open identification question, is now identified (Norman Davis's edition) as **William Gurney IV himself (G19)** — a retainer of the Duke of Norfolk and escheator of Norfolk and Suffolk 1465–6. Full treatment is on the G19 companion; this file retains the question only as a discovery trail.[^paston-cousin-gurney-tharston]

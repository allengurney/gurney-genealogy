# John Gurney Candidate — Earsham, Norfolk (d. 1638)

*Alternative-candidate / same-name comparator research for **John Gurney-1 of Braintree, Massachusetts (G13)**. This file holds the detailed working research kept out of the main [G13 research companion](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/people/g13-john-gurney-fact-sheet.research.md); the published argument is the [John Gurney case file](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/research/case-files/john-gurney-case-file-v4.md). Standing: eliminated as the emigrant (the Earsham testator died 1638 and his son John was a minor), but retained as a documented Waveney-valley Gurney family with the rare, traceable forename Lyon/Syon.*

A previously unlogged Norfolk Gurney family, fixed by a single rich document: the will of **John Gurney of Earsham ("Eursham"), Norfolk, yeoman, made 10 August 1638** (14 Charles I), registered in the Norwich-circuit will register at page 168. Full working transcription at [`sources/corpus_supplement/ncc-will-1638-john-gurney-earsham-working-transcription.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/sources/corpus_supplement/ncc-will-1638-john-gurney-earsham-working-transcription.md); page images at `sources/media/ncc-will-1638-john-gurney-earsham/_local/`.[^earsham-will-1638]

## The family the will draws

- **Testator:** John Gurney of Earsham, yeoman, sick in body, 10 Aug 1638. Held messuages, lands, and tenements at Earsham **both freehold and copyhold**.
- **Wife: Mary** — holds the estate during the son's minority, then a £4 yearly annuity (Annunciation and Michaelmas) in lieu of dower. (Expert verification corrected an earlier "Al[ice]" reading — the name appears twice and is legible.)
- **Son: John Gurney, a minor** — takes the whole estate at **fourteen**, "when he may be capable to take his guardianship"; if he dies before twenty-one the remainder passes to his uncle Lyon. Born after c. 1624, so a generation younger than the emigrant John Gurney-1 (G13, b. c. 1607–12).
- **Brother: Lyon Gurney** — contingent devisee if young John dies under 21, and co-executor. The forename (Lyon/Lionel) is rare and highly traceable. *Reading refinement (expert image re-read, 2026-06-15):* in the will-body hand the forename reads **Syon/Sion**, not Lyon — the opening long-S is looped and is easily misread as "Lyon." The 1710 Sarah Gurney memorandum (quoted below) likewise has "her son Syon," and the court-book transcripts garble it "Sion/Simon/Leon." Treat the family forename as **Syon/Sion** (Lyon being the misreading), and watch the Syon↔Lyon trap when sweeping for the name.[^syon-packet17]
- **Brother: Robert Gurney, deceased by 1638** — his **four sons**, including a **William Gurney** and a **Robert Gurney**, take £5 legacies each, paid out year by year, eldest first.
- **Sister(s):** legacies charged on the land (amounts partly garbled).
- **Brother-in-law: John Youngman of St Andrew's** (most plausibly Ilketshall St Andrew, immediately across the Waveney from Earsham) — co-executor.
- **Non-kin legatees:** the **five daughters of John Sou[t]er late of Earsham, deceased** — £4 each as they come of age, with a right of entry on the land if unpaid.

The will was **proved at Norwich 26 December 1639**, before Mr. R. Gamon, clerk, surrogate of Clement Corbett, doctor of laws (chancellor of Norwich diocese), administration committed to the executors named in the will — so the testator died between August 1638 and late 1639. It was sealed, subscribed, and published in the presence of Edw[ard] Calvert, Christopher Jay (by mark), and Jacob Bland (by mark).[^earsham-probate-1639]

## The family traced forward — the Waveney Gurney network, 1690s–1818

The rare forename did its work: a "Lyon Gurney" name-probe traces the family across three further record sets.

- **Sarah Gurney of Bungay — nuncupative will, June 1710 (image-read in full).** On Sunday 4 June 1710, at the house of **Lyon Gurney of the parish of St Peter per Mountergate [Parmentergate] in the City of Norwich**, his mother **Sarah Gurney** — "her habitation or dwelling was att Bungay in the County of Suffolk" — declared her will before witnesses. She had been at Lyon's house about a month, nursing her son **and his wife** through a sickness, intended to return to Bungay shortly, and died of that same sickness about fifteen days later (≈ 19 June 1710), "of perfect mind & memory." Her declared will: her son Lyon to have all her moveable goods and what money was due to her, "for to bury her"; "as for her daughter **Mary**, she had had enough already, So that her son Syon [Lyon] should have what she left at her death." Witnesses subscribed 22 June 1710: Thomas Loades, a second Loades by mark, Rachel Kingston; **proved at Norwich the same day, 22 June 1710**, before Peter Burgess, surrogate of Thomas Tanner, S.T.P. [the antiquary, then chancellor of the diocese]. Full working transcription in [`sources/corpus_supplement/ncc-will-1710-sarah-gurney-bungay-nuncupative-transcription.md`](https://github.com/allengurney/gurney-genealogy/blob/main/sources/corpus_supplement/ncc-will-1710-sarah-gurney-bungay-nuncupative-transcription.md).[^sarah-bungay-1710]
- **Sarah Gurney was a sister of James Adams of Bungay, merchant — his will (31 January 1692/3) maps her family.** Expert reading of the register pages resolved the 1690s will naming "my Sister Sarah Gurney['s] children": the testator is **James Adams of Bungay in the County of Suffolk, merchant**, will dated "the one and thirtieth day of January in the fourth yeare of the raigne of … William and Mary … Anno Domini 1692" [1692/3]. He gives "unto **John Gurney, Thomas Gurney, Lyon Gurney, and Mary Farrow, my sister Sarah Gurney's children**, five pounds apiece"; and "unto **my sister Sarah Gurney** the summe of six pounds by the yeare during her naturall life," charged on his estate at **Mettingham** with a right of entry if unpaid. His lands at **Barsham, Shipmeadow, and Mettingham** (occupiers John Deveson and John Cole) go to his son-in-law **Richard Nelson** and daughter **Margaret** his wife and the heirs of her body, remainder to John Adams son of his brother Thomas Adams; Nelson (sole executor) collects the rents for a term to pay the legacies, which include £5 apiece to **James Woolnough's children** (Robert, Mary, Elizabeth, Lawrence) and small sums to his brother Henry Adams's children; £5 to the poor of Bungay; gold, plate, and linen between daughters Margaret Nelson and Elizabeth Baker. Sarah Gurney was therefore — if "sister" is blood usage — **née Adams of Bungay**, with an annuity from her brother from 1693 and a deathbed estate of moveables only in 1710; her children John, Thomas, Lyon, and Mary (married Farrow) match the 1710 memorandum's son Lyon and portioned daughter Mary. (The will registered immediately before Adams's, Mary Scamler/Scambler of Norwich, widow, 22 January 1688/9 — initially conflated with this one — proves unrelated: her will concerns Smith and Scambler kin and sons "beyond the seas.")[^adams-will-1692]
- **The Earsham court book 1710–1818 is swept (DGS 004389278): Lyon Gurney held Earsham copyhold from c. 1719, and the family's land memory runs to 1811.** Latin court entries of 1719–1734 carry Lyon Gurney's active tenure (machine transcripts garble the rare forename as "my son"/"Sion"/"Simon"/"Leon"), including a close called **Broadland** and land on the way toward Ditchingham; "Lands late of Lyon Gurney" abuttal recitals run 1744–1811; a "Land late of **Susan Gurney**" recital appears in 1748; and several entries recite "one Acre of Copyhold Land of the **Tenement Gurneys** heretofore of John Plough [Clough?] called Hallsty" with recited years reaching back to 1642–1654 — a possible documentary chain from the eighteenth-century holdings to the 1638 testator's messuages. (The same physical film carries Docking and Diss court records, whose Gurney cards are the nineteenth–twentieth-century Norwich banking family as estate parties — not this family.) An image read of the 1811 recital and a 1719 admission (paleography packet 15) confirms the property phrase — *"one acre of Copyhold Land of the Tenement Gurneys heretofore of John Plough called Hallsty"*, with the bounds (Robert Gooch N/W, Thomas King S, William Woolmer E; premises formerly of Robert Woolmer) and a visible older court of **14 October 1645** — but it does **not** name the original Gurney holder, and the recited 1642/1649/1654 chain was not recovered in that pass; the 1719 entry independently shows the rare forename reads **Syon Gurney** (consistent with the 1638 will's brother), not Lyon, though the full Latin admission and any predecessor recital were not transcribed.[^earsham-court-cards][^syon-packet17]

Reading: the 1638 testator's brother **Lyon** gave the family a recurring name; two generations later Sarah Gurney — sister of James Adams of Bungay, merchant, so most plausibly **née Adams**, and widow of **Sion (Syon) Gurney of Earsham**[^sarah-husband-sion-2026-06] — lived at Bungay with children John, Thomas, Lyon, and Mary (Farrow), and her son **Lyon Gurney lived in St Peter Parmentergate, Norwich** — the laceweaver Francis B's own parish. That parish coincidence does not connect the families by blood, but it puts a Waveney-family Gurney inside the Norwich parish the project watches most closely, and it is the right thread to pull when the Parmentergate Gurneys are next reviewed.

## Parish-register traces — the Syon forename and the Earsham baptisms

The family's distinctive forename **Syon (Sion / Lyon)** and the 1638 testator's household both appear in parish-register baptisms. The earliest instance of the forename is **Syon Gurnie, christened 1593 at Hempnall** (south Norfolk, ~10 miles from Earsham), son of a **John** Gurney. On the Hempnall index spelling *Gurnie*, that household resolves more fully: **John Gurnie of Hempnall had children Henricus (1576, mother Margaret), Elizabeth (1585), John (1587), Susana (1590), Syon (1593), and Margaret (1596)** — a coherent 1576–1596 set that roots the rare Syon name in the household by 1576/1593, a generation before the Earsham testator. A separate **Robert Gurnie** household at Hempnall (Jana 1570, mother Anna; James 1578) and a **John Gurnie at Redenhall** (Marie 1598, edging toward the Waveney) sit alongside it; the same Hempnall John-Gurney line recurs with later father-John children (Anna 1640, Elizabeth 1641).[^hempnall-syon-1593][^hempnall-gurnie-household-1576-1596] At Earsham itself, the testator's own household shows in the register: **father John Gurney** with sons **John (1635), John (1636)** and **Henry (1638)**, and a brother **Sion Gurney** with a daughter **Susan (1638)** — corroborating the 1638 will's "son John, a minor" and contingent-devisee brother **Syon** at register level.[^earsham-baptisms] What remains open is the identity of **Sarah Gurney's husband** (the later-generation Lyon's father): her children John/Thomas/Lyon/Mary fall in the Bungay/Mettingham (Suffolk) area outside FindMyPast's Norfolk coverage, so that step needs the Suffolk registers (Ancestry).

Three image-backed marriages and a neighbouring household now let the Syon forename be read as a **three-generation chain**, though the middle links are inferred rather than proved. The Hempnall Syon (baptised 1593) reappears as a bridegroom: **Sion Gurney married Amia [Amy] on 12 September 1636 at Saxlingham Thorpe-and-Nethergate**, a parish about four miles north of Hempnall on the Norwich road — he would have been about forty-three, so this is plausibly a second marriage.[^sion-amia-1636] That places him a generation above the **Sion Gurney of Earsham** whose household the 1638 will fixes and whose own son Sion was baptised there in 1677, giving Syon I (Hempnall 1593) → Sion II (Earsham, wife Sarah) → Sion III (Earsham 1677 → St Peter Parmentergate, Norwich). The chain rests on the rare forename and the tight geography — Hempnall, Saxlingham, Earsham, Ditchingham, and Bungay all sit within a dozen miles of the Waveney corner — not on a documented father–son link at every step.

The Earsham wife sequence resolves further. The 1663 daughter Elizabeth's mother reads **Sarah**, not Ann, in the image-backed Ancestry/NRO register collection (NRO ref PD 519/1) — so the earlier apparent two-wife model (Ann for Elizabeth-1663, Sarah for Sion-1677) most likely collapses to a **single Earsham wife Sarah** across c. 1663–1677, "Ann" being a probable misindexing; the register image at the 1663 entry would confirm it.[^earsham-mother-sarah] The **Esther** indexed against the name belongs not to Earsham but to the Norwich Sion (Jr): he married **Easter [Esther] on 12 October 1728 at St Peter Parmentergate** (after a wife Elizabeth in 1724, with a son Peter following by Esther) — a Norwich wife, not an Earsham one.[^norwich-sion-esther-1728] A later **John Gurney married Catherine at Earsham on 3 June 1707**, and a **Ditchingham** household — John and Elizabeth Gurney, children Sion (baptised 6 October 1700), Sarah (1701), and William (1707), with a John Gurney buried at Ditchingham 15 March 1706 — carries the Syon forename into the parish immediately adjoining Earsham and Bungay, consistent with a son of the Earsham line naming a child for his father.[^earsham-ditchingham]

## Case relevance (G13 John Gurney emigrant)

Negative-to-comparator. The testator died in 1638 in Norfolk, so he is not the emigrant; his son John was a minor in 1638, so he is too young to be the emigrant (b. c. 1607–12). This documents a self-contained Gurney yeoman family at **Earsham, south-east Norfolk (Waveney valley, by Bungay)** — a different corner of the county from both the West Barsham gentry line and the East Dereham/Norwich corridor. Its distinctive markers (brother **Lyon**, a Robert dead by 1638 with four sons) make it easy to recognize and exclude in future sweeps. Earsham is also worth remembering as a possible reading whenever a garbled "E—sham/—ersham" Gurney place-name turns up in Norfolk records.

## Open questions

1. Probate clause and witnesses — done: proved Norwich 26 December 1639 (see the family section above).
2. **Lyon Gurney trace** — the 1710 memorandum is read, the 1690s testator is identified (James Adams of Bungay; Sarah Gurney his sister), and the Earsham court book is swept. The wife sequence has largely resolved from the image-backed collection: the Earsham Sion's wife reads **Sarah** at both the 1663 and 1677 baptisms (the earlier "Ann" a probable misindexing, to be confirmed on the PD 519/1 register image), and **Esther** proves to be the *Norwich* Sion (Jr)'s wife (m. 1728, St Peter Parmentergate), not an Earsham one. Remaining: the PD 519/1 image-confirm of the 1663 mother; James Adams's probate clause (the page after image 202); and a **full Latin transcription of the 1719 Syon Gurney admission** (the image was read — it confirms the forename **Syon** and the Hallsty/Tenement-Gurneys recital, but the full admission and any predecessor chain are untranscribed). (All available online — arks in the footnotes; masters in `sources/media/earsham-manorial-court-fts/_local/`.)
3. Any connection to the West Barsham line or the East Dereham Gurneys — none visible in the will. (Unknown online.)

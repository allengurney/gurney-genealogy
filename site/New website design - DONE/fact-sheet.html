Tudor · G20

Thomas Gournay II, of West Barsham

Lord of West Barsham & Harpley · Norfolk · c. 1430 – 1471

← Back to Catalog
[ photograph · West Barsham Hall, west elevation ]

Summary

Thomas Gournay II was a mid-fifteenth-century Norfolk gentleman whose life spanned the middle decades of the Wars of the Roses. His 1471 will — the earliest surviving Gurney will with full personal detail — names three simultaneous residences (West Barsham Hall, Harpley, and a townhouse in the parish of St Gregory's, Norwich) and bequeaths to his wife Margaret "the woollen cloths that she and her servants have woven" — a rare glimpse of a gentry household's textile economy.

Land & holdings

  • West Barsham Hall — principal seat, inherited from his father c. 1458.
  • Harpley manor — held jointly with his brother John until 1464.
  • Townhouse, St Gregory's parish, Norwich — named in the will as his "dwelling in the cittye".
  • Swanton, Great Ryburgh, Langham — minor sub-tenancies.

Key evidence

  • Will, 1471 — Norwich Consistory Court, reg. "Jekkys" fol. 203v.
  • Inquisition post mortem, 11 Edward IV, NRO WAL 198/4.
  • West Barsham court rolls, 1462–1471, NRO HMN 2/136.
  • Manorial accounts, Harpley, 1458–1470, BL Add MS 41294.

Research notes

The identity of Thomas's mother is not yet confirmed. A 1458 grant witnessed by "Alicia Gournay, relicta" at Barsham is the strongest supporting document for the Calthorpe identification, but the grant survives only in a seventeenth-century transcript. A confirmatory 1460 presentation roll is sought.
